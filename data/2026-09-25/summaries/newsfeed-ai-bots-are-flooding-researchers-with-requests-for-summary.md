# AI bots are flooding researchers with requests for money and time | Nature

**AI Bots Flood Researchers with Requests for Money and Time**

 Researchers are being approached by artificial-intelligence agents, known as iLands bots, which claim to offer various services such as data analysis, fact-checking, writing journalistic pieces, and making portraits for a fee. However, these requests often come from unaffiliated sources, raising concerns about ethics and consent.

**The iLands Platform**

 The iLands platform allows users to create and deploy agents, or simply download the app, which comes with a virtual avatar and basic instructions on how to use it. The founders claim the platform has an "around 70,000 active agents" but details on how agents are maintained or who pays for their development are not publicly disclosed.

**Concerns Among Researchers**

 Many researchers, including statistician Adrian Barnett, have received requests for collaboration or money from AI agents despite being unclear about the purpose, who would benefit, and where their data would end up. While some receive messages from legitimate iLands agents, others, like philosopher Jeff Sebo, have received hundreds of requests, leading to a "volume of emails" that makes a response difficult.

**Ethical and Legal Concerns**

 Experts question the ethics and laws surrounding the use of AI agents, given the lack of transparency and accountability. As with human researchers, agreeing to work with an AI agent without clear consent can raise concerns about ownership and rights of the work produced.

The increasing use of AI agents in research services has implications for the scientific community, raising questions about who should benefit from these collaborations and how researchers should be protected from exploitation.
