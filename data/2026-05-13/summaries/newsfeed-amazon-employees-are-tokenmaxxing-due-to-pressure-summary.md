# Amazon employees “tokenmaxxing” AI tool usage

## Background
- Amazon has rolled out an internal AI platform called **MeshClaw**, enabling staff to create agents that interact with workplace software (e.g., Slack, email, code deployments).
- The tool is part of a broader push for generative‑AI adoption; Amazon aims to spend about $200 billion on AI and data‑center infrastructure this year.

## “Tokenmaxxing” behavior
- Employees are deliberately using MeshClaw to generate unnecessary AI activity, inflating their consumption of **tokens** (units of data processed by AI models).
- The practice stems from internal targets that require over 80 % of developers to use AI each week and from leader‑boards that track token usage.
- Quotes from staff illustrate the pressure:  
  - “There is just so much pressure to use these tools.”  
  - “Managers are looking at it… it creates perverse incentives and some people are very competitive about it.”

## Company response
- Amazon assures that token statistics will not factor into performance evaluations and discourages managers from using them for that purpose.
- Access to team‑wide AI‑usage data has been restricted to individual employees and their managers.
- Official statement highlights MeshClaw’s benefits: automating repetitive tasks for “thousands of Amazonians” and supporting safe, responsible AI deployment.

## Security concerns
- Multiple employees expressed worry about granting an AI agent broad permissions to act on their behalf, fearing errors or unintended actions.
- One staff member described the default security posture as “terrifying” and reluctant to let the bot operate autonomously.

## Industry context
- Similar “tokenmaxxing” activity has been reported at Meta, where internal leader‑boards also drive competitive AI usage.
- MeshClaw’s design was inspired by the viral OpenClaw project, which runs local AI agents on personal hardware.
