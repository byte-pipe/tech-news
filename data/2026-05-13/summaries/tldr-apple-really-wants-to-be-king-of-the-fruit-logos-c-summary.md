# Apple really wants to be king of the fruit logos | Creative Bloq

## Overview
- Apple successfully opposed an EU trademark application by Chinese keyboard maker Yichun Qinningmeng Electronics Co. for a citrus‑inspired logo.  
- The EU Intellectual Property Office (EUIPO) rejected the trademark, citing minor commonalities with Apple’s iconic bitten‑apple logo and the potential for consumer confusion.

## Details of the dispute
- **Yichun Qinningmeng’s logo**: segmented fruit shape, green leaf, “bite” missing, keyboard‑like segments, distinct colour scheme.  
- **Apple’s argument**: the design was intended to evoke Apple’s logo, leveraging the brand’s “immense reputation.”  
- **EUIPO’s stance**: while not fully agreeing with Apple’s claim, the office sided with Apple because of the brand’s notoriety and the likelihood that customers would form a mental link between the two signs.

## Context and precedent
- This is not Apple’s first clash over fruit‑based logos; a previous dispute involved a Pear logo that ended inconclusively.  
- Apple’s aggressive protection of its branding highlights the influence of timeless, recognizable logos in the tech industry.
