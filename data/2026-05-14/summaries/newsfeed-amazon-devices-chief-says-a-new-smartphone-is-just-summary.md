# Amazon devices chief says a new smartphone is “just not the goal”

## Background
- In March, Reuters reported that Amazon was exploring a new smartphone, codenamed **Transformer**, using Alexa as an operating system.  
- The report cited four anonymous sources and noted the project could still be canceled; Amazon declined to comment to Ars Technica.

## Panos Panay’s interview (Financial Times)
- Panay, head of Amazon’s devices and services, said building a new phone “is just not the goal.”  
- He acknowledged the rumors but explained there is “no clear path that makes sense” and that many emerging form factors deserve attention.  
- Panay stressed Amazon will not launch another phone without a compelling reason, saying, “What I won’t ever do again is [go to the customer and say], ‘Here’s another phone, what do you think?’”

## Risks and past experience
- Amazon previously released the Fire Phone in 2014, discontinuing it a year later after poor sales.  
- Introducing a new smartphone would place Amazon in direct competition with Apple and Samsung and revisit a market where it has already failed.  
- The devices business has struggled financially: the Wall Street Journal reported a $25 b loss from 2017‑2021, and Business Insider noted Alexa’s $10 b loss in 2022.

## Profitability focus
- Panay said CEO Andy Jassy tasked him with making devices “one of the next big businesses of Amazon.”  
- The strategy emphasizes driving device usage and encouraging owners to use Amazon services such as Amazon Music and shopping.  
- Panay affirmed, “We want our business to be profitable. I can say that clearly.”

## Conclusion
- Amazon’s current stance is that a new smartphone is not part of its immediate roadmap; the company will concentrate on other device form factors and improving profitability of its devices unit.
