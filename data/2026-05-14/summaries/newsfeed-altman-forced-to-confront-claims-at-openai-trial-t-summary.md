# Altman forced to confront claims at OpenAI trial that he's a prolific liar - Ars Technica

## Overview of the trial
- Elon Musk and Sam Altman testified in a civil trial that will decide OpenAI’s governance, funding sources, and profit structure.  
- Musk’s lawsuit alleges OpenAI abandoned its nonprofit mission and now enriches executives like Altman.  
- Altman testified to refute Musk’s accusations and to demonstrate his commitment to the mission.

## Musk’s testimony
- Spent three days on the stand, often losing his temper as OpenAI counsel William Savitt challenged his claim that OpenAI executives colluded with Microsoft to “steal a charity.”  
- Accused Altman of misleading investors and of restructuring OpenAI for profit.  
- Repeatedly called Altman a liar and tried to prove Altman’s untrustworthiness.

## Altman’s testimony
- Appeared nervous at first but steadied after about four hours.  
- Admitted he once considered leaving OpenAI for a lucrative AI research role at Microsoft after being temporarily ousted in 2023.  
- Described feeling “extremely angry” and “betrayed,” but said he returned because he cared about the mission, not money.  
- Acknowledged hearing that people consider him a liar; maintained he believes himself to be truthful.  
- Declined to fire himself despite concerns about greed, saying he might retire in the future and that board oversight limits his power.  
- Highlighted Musk’s own control‑seeking behavior, citing Musk’s comment that leadership might pass to his children.

## Key exchanges with Musk’s lawyer (Steven Molo)
- Molo asked Altman directly if he is “completely trustworthy.” Altman responded “I believe so,” then amended to “yes.”  
- Pressed Altman on a 52‑page dossier compiled by Ilya Sutskever documenting a “consistent pattern of lying.”  
- Queried whether Altman would ever fire himself for becoming too greedy; Altman said he has no current plans but might retire later.  
- Attempted to expose contradictions in Altman’s control over both for‑profit and nonprofit arms of OpenAI.

## Comparative dynamics
- Both men display similar instincts to retain control of OpenAI; Altman’s “Muskian” moment mirrors Musk’s earlier threats to start rival AI projects.  
- The trial is portrayed less as a mission‑focused dispute and more as an ego battle between two self‑appointed AI moral compasses.

## Conclusions presented by Altman
- He could have earned “a ton of money” at Microsoft but chose to stay because of the mission and the people involved.  
- The board’s past removal of Altman demonstrates his power is not absolute.  
- Musk’s actions, including poaching talent and launching xAI as a for‑profit entity, undermine his claim of altruistic motives.  

## Implications for the jury
- OpenAI’s legal team aims to show Musk’s case is baseless (“a nothing burger”) and that Altman, despite personal flaws, remains committed to the nonprofit mission.  
- The jury must weigh allegations of lying and control‑fights against testimonies of mission‑driven intent.
