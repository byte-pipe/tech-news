# Instructure strikes deal with hackers who breached it twice | TechCrunch

## Overview
- Instructure, creator of the Canvas education platform, announced it has “reached an agreement” with the cybercrime group ShinyHunters that breached its systems twice.
- The breaches exposed personal data of hundreds of millions of students and staff and disrupted thousands of schools.

## Breach timeline
- **April 29 breach**: ShinyHunters claimed to have stolen data for 275 million individuals and announced the compromise of Canvas used by ~9,000 schools.
- **Second breach (late May)**: Hackers defaced Canvas login pages on school sites to pressure Instructure into paying a ransom.

## Terms of the agreement
- ShinyHunters provided evidence that the stolen data was destroyed and promised no further extortion attempts.
- Instructure did not disclose financial details or confirm the amount paid.
- Company spokesperson Brian Watkins declined to comment beyond the official statement.

## Legal and policy context
- U.S. authorities have repeatedly urged victims not to pay ransoms, arguing that payments fund criminal activity.
- Security researchers warn that hackers may lie about deleting data to continue extorting victims.
- The FBI issued a notice about the disruption affecting U.S. schools, advising against paying demands.

## Comparison to similar incidents
- The attack mirrors the 2024 PowerSchool breach, which affected 70 million students and staff; PowerSchool paid ransom but later faced secondary extortion from another group.

## Impact of the stolen data
- Included student names, personal email addresses, and private messages exchanged between teachers and students.
- Some of the data was reviewed by TechCrunch.

## Instructure’s response and ongoing investigation
- The company acknowledges two distinct breach events involving different systems.
- It continues to investigate and validate findings.
- No clear information on who oversees cybersecurity at Instructure or whether CEO Steve Daly will resign.
