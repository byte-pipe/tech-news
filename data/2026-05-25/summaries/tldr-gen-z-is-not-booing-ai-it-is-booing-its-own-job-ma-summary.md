# Gen Z is not booing AI. It is booing its own job market

# Summary of the Article: Boos Not Over AI yet
---------------------------------------------------

Eric Schmidt, former Google CEO, delivered a commencement address at the University of Arizona's spring ceremony, emphasizing the potential impact of artificial intelligence on human adaptation. However, the sentiment remained divided during the event.

Gloria Caulfield, real-estate executive from the University of Central Florida, also used the phrase "the next industrial revolution" in her speech two days earlier. This framing was misinterpreted by some students, leading to boos during her address as well.

A closer examination reveals that the graduating cohort of 2026 is responding differently than previous generations. They are not booing AI technology but rather the announcements and predictions made about their employment opportunities in a rapidly changing labor market.

According to various reports, new-college-graduate unemployment rates reached record highs under the current economic circumstances: up to 30% in two years for AI-related job absorption (Bill McDermott). Furthermore, research projects conducted by major organizations pinpoint that the Gen Z cohort is facing high displacement rates and growing unemployment gaps between entry-level workers and experienced professionals exposed to artificial intelligence substitutions.

Notably, Dario Amodei, chief executive of Anthropic AI, has long predicted AI's potential job elimination effect on up to half of all entry-level white-collar jobs. This forecast echoes findings from the Dallas Federal Reserve regarding unemployment rates widening in occupations with AI substitution post-pandemic.

What sets the 2026 cohort apart is that they are entering a labor market while its displacement is publicly accounted for and quantified across mainstream media: named CEO's statements on the cuts being made by their industries and named targets to be eliminated based on dollar and job losses predictions.
