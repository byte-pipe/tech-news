# ADHD and autism at risk of over‑diagnosis, says government review – BBC News  

## Key findings of the review  
- A rise in ADHD and autism diagnoses over the past 25 years has led to rates that exceed expected prevalence, especially in younger age groups.  
- Economic uncertainty and social‑media use are identified as drivers of increasing mental‑health problems among young people.  
- Over‑diagnosis may label individuals by “difficulties” rather than strengths, harming prospects in education, work and relationships.  
- Children diagnosed before 17 are about four times more likely to be NEET (not in education, employment or training) between ages 16‑24.  

## Problems with current assessment pathways  
- Long waiting times for diagnosis: the Watson family waited three years for an autism assessment for their son Atlas, who was only diagnosed months before school entry.  
- No fast‑track route for children with the most complex needs; families must remain on a generic waiting list.  
- Diagnosis is often required to access support, following a 2014 policy change that tied school assistance to an official ADHD or autism diagnosis.  

## Recommendations from the review  
- Tighten regulation of private firms that provide NHS‑funded assessments, including a ban on advertising and stricter quality controls.  
- Re‑prioritise waiting lists so that those with highest needs are assessed first; consider non‑medical support (school/work adjustments, sleep or anxiety assistance) for others.  
- Decouple support from diagnosis: keep diagnosis essential but create alternative pathways for help.  
- Develop a new mental‑health strategy (expected by year‑end) that outlines a broader model of support for neurodevelopmental conditions.  

## Reactions and commentary  
- Prof Peter Fonagy (review chair) warned the system is “under immense strain” and called for moving away from a “very narrow pathway” to support.  
- Mel Merritt (National Autistic Society) said successive governments have failed autistic people and highlighted the need for a supportive system.  
- Abigail Ampofo (YoungMinds) noted that young people feel blamed for their distress and urged major reform.  
- Health Secretary Yvette Cooper welcomed the report, emphasizing early intervention and the distress caused by delayed or inappropriate diagnoses.
