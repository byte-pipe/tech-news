# A startup founder who served time in prison is looking to court an untapped market: ex‑cons  

## Founder background  
- Richard Bronson previously worked at Stratton Oakmont, the brokerage firm depicted in *The Wolf of Wall Street*.  
- He left Stratton after a year, built a $100 million business taking small‑cap companies public, but did so by violating securities laws.  
- Convicted for those violations, he served a few years in federal prison and lost his freedom and assets.  
- After release, Bronson struggled to find employment; employers treated him as “radioactive.”  

## Inspiration for Commissary Club  
- The lack of support for people re‑entering society motivated Bronson to create a platform aimed at ex‑cons.  
- The name references the prison “commissary,” the store where inmates purchase necessities.  
- The platform combines job‑search tools, housing assistance, benefits navigation, and a social network to combat isolation.  
- It also functions as a referral network and includes a dating component.  

## Team and technology  
- Co‑founder Roman Kissin serves as the technical lead; his background includes roles at eBay, IBM, and a stint as CTO of LexisNexis (2022‑2025).  
- The service uses AI to streamline applications for employment and housing, positioning AI as a creator of jobs for a marginalized group.  

## Business model  
- Initial access is free; users receive the first 10 job applications at no cost.  
- After the free tier, the price is $149 for the next 10 applications, or $14.99 per application.  
- Bronson emphasizes that the venture is for‑profit but driven by a strong mission to help people with criminal records.  

## Market potential and positioning  
- Bronson argues the market of formerly incarcerated individuals is “huge” and largely ignored by existing services.  
- He frames the platform as a win‑win: a revenue‑generating business that also fulfills a social need.  

## Recognition  
- Commissary Club was selected for TechCrunch’s Startup Battlefield 200 competition at TechCrunch Disrupt (October 13‑15, San Francisco).
