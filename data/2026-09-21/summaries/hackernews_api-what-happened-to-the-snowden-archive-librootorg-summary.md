## What Happened to the Snowden Archive

The Snowden archive, a collection of classified documents leaked by Edward Snowden, is no longer publicly available. Initially, The New York Times and ProPublica published a significant batch of documents in August 2015, and later The Guardian and Der Spiegel also contributed.

However, The Intercept, with an exception, ceased publishing its archive in March 2019. Eleven weeks following its closure, the archive was re-released by The Intercept on May 29, 2019. Since then, there have been no other significant releases of the archive.

**Key Points:**

* The archive contained approximately 1 million documents, mostly related to intelligence agencies and the NSA.
* The documents were shared gradually, with each successive release having its own unique details.
* The primary archive contained about 50,000 documents.
* Documents were shared among various journalists and organizations.
* The archive has since been largely shut down, with only a few exceptions.
* There have been no significant releases of the archive since its re-emergence in May 2019.

## The Closure and Its Impact

The closure of the archive had a complex cause, with several factors contributing to its demise.

* **Major Media Outlets Stopped Reporting as Well:** Other major media outlets, such as The Washington Post and CNN, also ceased reporting on the documents.
* **Budget Constraints:** The budget for the archive was significantly reduced following a significant budget cut.
* **Editorial Priorities:** The editorial priorities of various outlets shifted, and some were no longer willing to dedicate significant resources to the archive.
* **Snowden's Intentions:** Snowden has stated that he intended to seek alternative partners and was not enthusiastic about closing the archive.
