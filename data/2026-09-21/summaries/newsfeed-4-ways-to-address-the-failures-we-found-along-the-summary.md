# 4 ways to address the failures we found along the US border’s “virtual wall” | MIT Technology Review

**Failings in the US Border's "Virtual Wall"**

A recent investigation by MIT Technology Review found that the US government's "virtual wall" along the US-Mexico border has led to numerous cases of people dying near surveillance towers. Despite the existence of nearly 600 towers, it appears that many have not been detected by advanced, AI-enabled towers.

**Key Findings**

1. **Broken towers**: Many surveillance towers have been found to be damaged or non-functional.
2. **Algorithmic failures**: Advanced algorithms have failed to detect people in areas covered by these towers.
3. **Lack of response**: Agents were not notified in several cases, indicating a systemic flaw in the virtual wall's management.

**Systemic Flaws**

1. **Incomplete and outdated data**: The US Customs and Border Protection's (CBP) current data suggests that nearly 800 towers operate, but this is an undercount due to limited information.
2. **No comprehensive map**: The CBP does not publish a comprehensive map of its virtual wall, making it difficult to track tower installations and identify areas of concern.
3. **Lack of oversight**: The investigation found that internal auditors did not have access to complete information about tower installations.

**Recommendations**

To address these failures, the US government should take the following steps:

* **Conduct a comprehensive audit**: Analyze and verify tower installations, using external evidence and satellite images to confirm accurate data.
* **Establish a centralized data platform**: Develop a comprehensive database of tower installations, including data on tower location, installation date, and estimated lifespan.
* **Implement stricter controls and procedures**: Ensure that agents are notified of potential alerts and that emergency protocols are in place to address cases where surveillance towers have not detected individuals.
