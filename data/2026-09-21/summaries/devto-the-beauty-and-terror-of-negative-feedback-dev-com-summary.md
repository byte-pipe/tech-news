# Sting of Anonymous Conference Reviews

Giving a first conference talk was exhilarating, with minimal nervousness and unwavering confidence in hard work. The initial mindset was "I'm going to nail this." However, things weren't so rosy. The presentation had a few initial jitters and some screen-sharing issues, but the writer found their rhythm and handled the technical difficulties with humor. The feedback from attendees was mostly positive, with one critique being a 7/10 and encouraging growth. However, the writer's initial reaction was "What's this? No thank you," and they were taken aback by a vague anonymous review that "tapped directly into an insecurity I had harbored."

The anonymous feedback, which criticized the presentation but didn't provide any direct suggestions, left the writer feeling stung and unsure of how to respond. Initially, they were grumpy about the criticism and seemed to accept it as optional feedback. However, their tone shifted after sleeping on it, and they asked deeper questions about the need for improvement.

The writer reflected on their work and recognized the issue of anonymous feedback causing frustration. They realized they had wasted time processing the criticism immediately and were instead motivated to find ways to improve it. The moral of the story is not to accept feedback immediately but to take a step back, investigate the issue, and then respond constructively.

# Key Points:
* Getting anonymous feedback can be stinging and frustrating.
* Recognizing the need for improvement is crucial, not accepting criticism immediately.
* Processing and investigating feedback can help find solutions and grow as a speaker.
* It's essential to take a step back, reflect, and respond thoughtfully to criticism.
