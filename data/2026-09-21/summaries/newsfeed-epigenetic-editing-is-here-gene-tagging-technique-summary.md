# 'Epigenetic' editing is here: gene-tagging technique banishes hepatitis B virus | Nature

* **Background**: Epigenetic therapies for chronic hepatitis B (HBV) may offer hope to cure the disease, but currently, available treatments often fail to eradicate the virus.
* **Current Methods**: Epigenetic therapies, which modify markers attached to DNA, have been explored but can increase cancer risks.
* **New Approach**: Researchers are turning to epigenetic editing, specifically gene-tagging, which modifies genes to shut down viral gene activity.
* **Success in Experiments**: The study tested epigenetic editing in human liver cells and mice, producing minimal and temporary side effects.
* **Clinical Trials**: A single participant in Hong Kong and New Zealand received the treatment, and the firm nChroma Bio launched a clinical trial with multiple doses.
* **Targeting Hidden Copies**: HBV infections can hide viral copies inside liver cells and prevent the immune system from fighting against them, leading to chronic infections.
* **Efficacy**: The therapy showed "quite impressive" efficacy, with minimal side effects in experiments and a promising start to clinical trials.
