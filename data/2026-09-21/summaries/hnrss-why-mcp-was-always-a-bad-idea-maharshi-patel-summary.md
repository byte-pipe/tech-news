# Why MCP Was Always a Bad Idea | Maharshi Patel

Here is a summary of the article in Markdown format:

## Why MCP Was Always a Bad Idea

The article discusses the concept of MCP (Multicasting Protocol) and how it has become a flawed idea despite its initial benefits. The author argues that MCP has always been a bad idea because it was designed for a time when LLMs (Large Language Models) were not as advanced as they are today.

### A Brief History

MCP was released by the Anthropic team in November 2024 as a protocol to help agents connect to external services and data sources. At the time, LLMs were relatively primitive, and MCP was meant to bridge the gap between the two.

### The MCP Industrial Complex

The growth in MCP adoption led to a buildup of context bloat, with multiple tools and services being added to MCP server setups. This made it difficult to manage and maintain the system. Thankfully, users found solutions like Composio, MintMCP, and Pipedream to minimize context bloat.

### The Models Got Better

The author notes that the models behind MCP have become more sophisticated, allowing them to execute code, reason about large codebases, and act autonomously. This has also led to advancements in writing/running scripts, composing services, and calling APIs.

### A Positive Side

However, the author also highlights the positive side of MCP adoption, specifically the improvements in LLMs' capabilities and the development of platforms like Code Mode, which make using MCP easier and more efficient.

### Conclusion

Ultimately, the article concludes that MCP has been a bad idea despite its benefits. It has led to a complex system with problems such as context bloat, and its own limitations, such as the need for more sophisticated models.
