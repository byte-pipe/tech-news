# AT&T loses key ruling in bid to stop offering basic phone service in California - Ars Technica

## California's Ruling on AT&T's Demand for Relief from Basic Phone Service Requirements

California can enforce the rules requiring AT&T to offer basic phone service in its wireline territory despite an ongoing court case. A federal judge has denied AT&T's request for a preliminary injunction allowing it to stop offering basic phone service to new customers and end service for 184,000 residential and 15,000 business customers as of June 1, 2027.

AT&T appealed the ruling but is under California's current orders to keep offering phone service to potential customers while the case continues. The company could appeal later if it loses its underlying claim, but for now, it remains bound by the court's decision.

## Key Points:

* AT&T appealed a preliminary injunction denying its request for relief from basic phone service requirements in California.
* Federal Judge Linda Lopez denied the request, allowing AT&T to continue offering standard phone services to new customers.
* California insisted on maintaining service over copper wires or modernizing it using fiber lines.
* The issue is related to federal regulators' authority to allow carriers to grandfather existing services without making significant upgrades.

## State of Service:

• AT&T aims to end basic phone service for 184,000 residential and 15,000 business customers by June 1, 2027.
• The company wants to stop offering standard phone service but cannot promise to replace old copper lines with fiber in areas where it's not profitable.
• California remains under orders to keep offering basic services without upgrades.

## Future Implications:

• AT&T may appeal a ruling on the preliminary injunction before June 1, 2027 if its claims are unsuccessful.
• If the appeals process is successful, AT&T could lose its case, binding California to require new service providers like it.
