# A Device Hidden in Cars Across the US Leaves Them Vulnerable to Hacking and Paralysis. Patch It Now | WIRED

**Modern Car Systems Hacked by Unsecured Third-Party Component**

Modern cars are now equipped with **multi-ton computers on wheels**, but many drivers may not be aware that they face a significant threat: an insecure third-party component, specifically the KARR Security System, has been introduced into some vehicles.

The KARR alarm system, installed in more than 2 million vehicles globally, can be hacked using Bluetooth commands. Hackers have found a way to send silent unlock commands and control various vehicle functions, including:

* Unlocking the doors with a remote
* Turning off the alarm system
* Honking the horn
* Flashing the car's lights
* Disable ignition

This vulnerability arises from the fact that some manufacturers do not remove car alarms when selling a vehicle to a new owner. Instead, dealerships install these systems as a security feature.

The UC San Diego team has discovered a large number of vehicles (over 2 million) with this unsecured component. To address the issue, they have developed and rolled out an **urgent firmware update** for vulnerable Bluetooth models of KARR alarm devices sold by Acrisure Protection Group.

For car owners with the affected system:

* Manual updates will be available via a smartphone app download process.
* Those who don't download the updated app may need to manually visit their vehicle's manufacturer website or contact dealership support to request a patch.

To mitigate the risk, it is essential for all car owners with KARR Security System-enabled vehicles to identify and check their cars to determine if the system's vulnerability has been exploited.
