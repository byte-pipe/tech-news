# AI adoption correlates with incident frequency, underscoring need for governance

## Dive Brief
- Over 20 % of macOS‑based organizations have suffered financial loss or a cyberattack linked to AI tool usage.  
- Approximately 60 % of these organizations anticipate an AI‑related incident in the near future.  
- Findings are based on interviews with 687 IT and security leaders managing macOS networks.  
- The report outlines administrators’ AI implementation priorities, primary risk areas, and Jamf’s mitigation guidance.

## Dive Insight
- **Adoption levels**: 73 % of macOS enterprises have already deployed AI; another 20 % are exploring deployment.  
- **Shadow AI**: Unapproved, unmanaged AI tools used by employees create visibility gaps that hinder security and governance.  
- **Agentic AI**: Deploying AI agents raises concerns about data exposure and the integrity of code bases when permissions are not tightly controlled.  
- **Vendor proliferation**: The sheer number of AI vendors makes vetting and deployment time‑consuming for IT teams, especially given AI’s rapid evolution.  
- **Usage‑based pricing**: Organizations often lack insight into the number of paid licenses and the cost‑effectiveness of each tool, a problem amplified by weak governance.  
- **Priority ranking**: Survey respondents placed AI governance third (after automating IT management and boosting worker productivity), while AI security improvements ranked fifth.  
- **Incident correlation**: Organizations with deeper AI integration report higher incident rates (27 %) compared to those merely exploring AI (under 20 %).

## Recommendations
- Conduct regular audits to increase visibility of AI tools and usage.  
- Emphasize software governance rather than solely user governance; enforce strict data‑access policies.  
- Integrate governance frameworks from the earliest stages of AI deployment.  
- Leverage built‑in governance tools provided by AI platforms for a streamlined approach.
