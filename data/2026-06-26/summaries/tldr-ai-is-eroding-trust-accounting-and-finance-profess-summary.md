# AI is eroding trust. Accounting and finance professionals can rebuild it

## Current confidence landscape
- Global confidence among accountants hit its third‑lowest point in Q1 2026 (Global Economic Conditions Survey).
- Main headwinds: geopolitical instability (especially Middle‑East conflict), persistent inflation, rising operating costs (69% of respondents).
- Growing worry that customers may go out of business, marking geopolitical risk as the top priority.

## AI’s dual role: risk and opportunity
- Rapid AI adoption adds stress as professionals must learn to integrate, train, and govern new tools.
- In the survey, technology, data, and cybersecurity ranked as the second‑highest risk after geopolitical factors.
- Three core AI concerns emerged:
  1. **Pace of adoption** – implementations often lack controls or clear strategy, leading to over‑reliance.
  2. **Quality of output** – small automation errors can multiply, causing reporting inaccuracies and compliance burdens.
  3. **Erosion of trust** – doubts about testing, bias, and long‑term implications weaken confidence in data and decisions.

## External evidence of trust issues
- University of Melbourne study: < 50% of global respondents willing to trust AI.
- Other research shows customer trust declines when companies disclose AI usage.

## What finance teams must do to rebuild trust
- **Become stewards of compliance**: develop practical AI capabilities, learn real‑world finance use cases (forecasting, auditing, risk management), and master governance, bias detection, and accuracy checks.
- **Go beyond data reporting**: combine AI output with sharp analysis, strategic insight, and sound judgment; act as the human thread that validates recommendations.
- **Upskill without returning to school**: pursue targeted, industry‑validated credentialing programs that deliver immediately applicable skills.
- **Act now**: start building future‑proof skills today to ensure AI is used responsibly and restores confidence in finance functions.
