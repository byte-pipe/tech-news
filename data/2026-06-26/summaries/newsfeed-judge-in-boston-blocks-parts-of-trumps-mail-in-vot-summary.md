# Judge in Boston blocks parts of Trump’s mail-in voting order

## Key points
- U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani in Boston blocked major provisions of President Trump’s executive order that would have limited mail‑in voting.
- The ruling applies to the upcoming general election and earlier races in nearly two dozen states, most of which are led by Democrats, plus Washington, D.C.
- The Trump administration plans to appeal the decision; a separate appeal is already pending in a D.C. case.

## Legal reasoning
- Talwani concluded that the President lacks constitutional authority to direct the Postal Service (USPS) and the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) on election matters; election rules are set by state legislatures and Congress.
- The judge also held that the USPS does not have legal power to control the delivery of mail‑in ballots.
- The order’s directive to create voter lists and restrict ballot delivery to those on the lists exceeds presidential power.

## States affected
- Arizona, California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Illinois, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Nevada, New Jersey, New Mexico, North Carolina, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, Vermont, Virginia, Washington, Wisconsin, and Washington, D.C.

## Reactions
- New York Attorney General Letitia James praised the ruling, saying it protects voting rights from an “unlawful attack.”
- The administration argues the order is necessary for election integrity, while critics warn it would cause chaos for states, election officials, and voters.

## Ongoing litigation
- A federal appeals panel recently rejected a DOJ request for Michigan voter data.
- Another judge permanently blocked key parts of an earlier voting‑related executive order.
- A separate D.C. judge declined an emergency injunction, but Democrats are appealing that decision to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia.
