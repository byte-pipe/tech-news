# All challenges big and small | MIT Technology Review – Summary

## Personal account in Kuwait  
- At 18, the author skipped high‑school graduation to help rebuild Kuwait after the 1991 Gulf War.  
- The country was in chaos: widespread power outages, rubble, unexploded ordnance, and massive oil‑well fires.  
- Worked with a labor crew on quick repairs (windows, doors) while larger tasks, especially extinguishing oil fires, loomed.

## Engineering and environmental challenges  
- Hundreds of oil wells were set ablaze by the retreating Iraqi army, producing thick smoke that darkened the sky and irritated eyes and throats.  
- Carl Sagan warned that the smoke could reach the stratosphere and trigger a “year without a summer” similar to the 1815 Tambora eruption; the plume ultimately stayed lower, causing only regional cooling.  
- Booby traps and mines, including plastic “toe poppers,” littered the landscape; thousands remain undiscovered.  

## International response and innovations  
- Specialist firms such as Red Adair, Boots & Coots, and Bechtel arrived to fight the fires.  
- Engineers repurposed oil pipelines to pump seawater into burning wells.  
- A Hungarian team mounted a firefighting machine (“Big Windby”) on a Soviet T‑34 tank chassis, using two MiG‑21 turbines to spray 220 gallons of water per second (demonstrated only in media).  

## Outcomes and reflections  
- After a 90‑day contract, the author saw visible progress: cleaner air, functional utilities, open markets, and beaches cleared of mines where people could swim.  
- The experience illustrates that while forces beyond our control cause destruction, coordinated human ingenuity can rebuild and improve the world.
