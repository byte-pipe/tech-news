# A Fatal Tesla Crash in Texas Sets Up a Legal Showdown

## Incident Overview
- On a Texas evening, 76‑year‑old Martha Avila was killed when a Tesla Model 3 crashed into her home at over 70 mph.  
- The driver, 44‑year‑old Michael Butler, reported that Tesla’s driver‑assistance features were engaged.  
- Police noted Butler showed “no signs of intoxication.”

## Lawsuit Details
- Avila’s family sued Butler and Tesla in Harris County District Court.  
- The complaint alleges Tesla’s Full Self‑Driving (FSD) system was “defective in design and unreasonably dangerous.”  
- The suit claims the feature required the driver to stay attentive, which the family says did not happen.  
- Justin Barbour, Avila’s son‑in‑law, was also injured in the crash.

## Tesla’s Response
- Tesla did not comment to WIRED.  
- On X, VP of AI software Ashok Elluswamy said data showed Butler manually overrode the system by flooring the accelerator, even after the crash.  
- CEO Elon Musk said speculation that Tesla’s technology caused the crash “makes no sense.”

## Legal Precedents
- A 2022 Florida jury found a Tesla driver mostly responsible for a fatal crash but held Tesla one‑third liable for promoting Autopilot, awarding $200 million in punitive damages and $43 million in compensatory damages.  
- Prior cases show courts may assign partial responsibility to Tesla when drivers rely on the system and lose situational awareness.

## Expert Commentary
- Matthew Wansley (Yeshiva University) warns that if a product leaves drivers vulnerable when the system fails, Tesla could be found responsible.  
- Critics argue that high confidence in FSD can lead drivers to neglect monitoring, increasing risk.

## Regulatory Investigations
- The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) opened an investigation after more than 40 reports of FSD violating traffic laws, including a red‑light run.  
- NHTSA previously compelled a 2023 over‑the‑air recall of Autopilot for encouraging driver inattention.  
- The National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) launched a joint probe with Harris County Sheriff’s Office; details on data received from Tesla are pending.  

## Ongoing Developments
- Additional information from the crash is expected as investigations continue.  
- Avila’s family’s attorney, Ryan Zehl, emphasized the goal of preventing similar tragedies.
