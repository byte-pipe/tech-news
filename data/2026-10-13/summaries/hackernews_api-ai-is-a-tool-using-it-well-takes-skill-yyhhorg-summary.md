# AI Is a Tool. Using It Well Takes Skill. - yyhh.org

**LLMs are Not Automation, But Wielded Tools**

In an opinion piece, Rich Hickey argues that LLMs are not automation tools, but rather wielded tools due to their ability to be directed, interpreted, and modified by humans. I agree, but also note that even by this definition, LLMs can be treated as tools.

**The Ambiguity of the Term "Tool"**

Hickey's distinction between tools and automation creates a false division between the two. Automation can indeed be a feature of tools, as is the case with compilers and spreadsheets. However, the inherent characteristics of a tool, such as intention, feedback, direction, and expertise, are what distinguish it from automation.

**The Importance of Feedback and Direction**

A programmer can control the output of an LLM by asking for specific explanations, testing proposed changes, and refining their requests. This feedback loop directs the instrument towards a purpose, making it a tool that can be used effectively. Expertise is also crucial in using LLMs effectively, as it allows for a deeper understanding of the system and its limitations.

**The Value of Human-LLM Interaction**

While an LLM can learn and adapt, the actual interaction between the user and the system depends on the user's intention, feedback, and direction. A programmer can use an LLM to question their own interpretation, explore an objection, or identify areas for improvement. This level of interaction makes the system more useful and effective.

**The False Division of LLMs**

The term "tool" can be misleading, implying a level of independence and autonomy. However, LLMs are, at core, systems that require human interaction and control. This language risks reinforcing a narrative that makes these systems seem like self-aware, life-like beings.

**Conclusion**

Ultimately, the classification of LLMs as tools or automation is subjective. What matters is how they are used and the skills required to wield them effectively. While there are limitations and complexities inherent to LLMs, the fact remains that they can be treated as tools, and their value lies in their ability to be directed, interpreted, and modified by humans.
