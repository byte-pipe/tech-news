**Cyberattack Cripples European Supermarkets**

A widespread cyberattack has hit major supermarket chains across Europe, affecting refrigeration control systems, deliveries, and in-store pricing powered by electronic shelf labels. The attack, which has been reported to be ongoing, has reportedly seen units set to continuously defrost at 50 degrees Celsius.

**Key Details**

* Several major retailers, including Danfoss-made refrigeration controls used by French supermarkets Intermarché, Leclerc, and Coopérative U.
* Cyberattack affects several stores in the network, with claims of losses ranging from 300,000 to several million euros.

**Impact**

* Automatically defrosting systems activated, with temperatures set to 50 degrees Celsius.
* Compromised electronic shelf labels, disrupting in-store pricing and deliveries.
* Cybersecurity incident reported on Sunday afternoon, with multiple chains hit, including:
	+ Albert Heijn in the Netherlands
	+ Delhaize chain in Belgium

**Authoritative Statements**

* Dominique Schelcher, president of Coopérative U, has confirmed the incident and assured teams are mobilizing to fix the issue.
* Albert Heijn has stated they have taken measures to protect their IT environment, including temporary shutdowns of systems.

**Follow-up**

* A full investigation is underway to uncover the extent of the incident and whether the attacks are related.
* Ongoing losses are expected to continue unless the issue is resolved.
