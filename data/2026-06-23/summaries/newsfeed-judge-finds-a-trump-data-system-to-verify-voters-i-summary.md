# Judge finds a Trump data system to verify voters is unlawful

## Background
- The SAVE (System for Award Management Verification) tool, run by USCIS, was originally used for individual checks of foreign‑born individuals’ eligibility for certain benefits.  
- In 2025‑2026 the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) overhauled SAVE to allow bulk checks, linking it to Social Security Administration data and adding records of U.S.-born citizens.  

## Scope of the Overhaul
- More than 60 million voter records were run through the revamped SAVE system.  
- The tool is intended to flag potential non‑citizen or deceased voters, but it mistakenly flagged U.S. citizens, especially foreign‑born citizens, as potential non‑citizens.  
- Approximately 21,000 records (less than 1 %) were flagged as potential non‑citizens.  

## Legal Challenge and Ruling
- A federal lawsuit, brought by groups including the League of Women Voters, argued that the overhaul violated the Privacy Act, the Social Security Act, and the Administrative Procedure Act.  
- U.S. District Court Judge Sparkle Sooknanan issued a 75‑page ruling declaring the expanded SAVE system unlawful and ordering that it may not be used in its current form.  
- The judge stated that the government “knowingly trampled on the privacy rights of American citizens” and that the agencies lacked statutory authority for the changes.  

## Immediate Effects
- The overhauled SAVE tool must be suspended pending further action.  
- The federal government may appeal the decision.  
- State officials who had already used SAVE for voter‑list checks must halt reliance on its results.  

## Reactions
- **League of Women Voters (Marcia Johnson):** Called the decision “a resounding victory for voters” and warned that a federal voter database threatens democratic rights.  
- **Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics (Nikhel Sus):** Noted that agencies ignored thousands of negative public comments and that the court’s ruling validates those concerns.  
- **DHS General Counsel (James Percival):** Criticized the ruling on social media, claiming it hinders efforts to address “alien voting.”  

## Notable Individual Impact
- **Anthony Nel:** A naturalized U.S. citizen born in South Africa was flagged as a potential non‑citizen after Texas ran its voter list through SAVE. He was removed from the rolls, later proved citizenship, and was reinstated, voting in the 2026 primary. He described the ruling as “a step in the right direction” for voting and privacy rights.  

## Next Steps
- The federal government must decide whether to appeal or to seek congressional authorization before any future use of SAVE for voter verification.  
- States will need alternative methods to verify voter eligibility that comply with privacy and procedural statutes.  
- Ongoing monitoring of the impact on foreign‑born citizens and potential non‑citizen voting will continue as the legal and policy debate evolves.
