## Jürgen Schmidhuber’s 1991 Timeline

- **26 March 1991** – Introduced the first “unnormalized linear Transformer,” an efficient linear‑cost alternative to later quadratic Transformers.  
- **30 April 1991** – Published two breakthroughs:  
  - Unsupervised pre‑training for deep neural networks, enabling very deep architectures.  
  - Neural network distillation, a technique later used in large‑scale LLMs such as DeepSeek “Sputnik.”  
- **15 June 1991** – Developed deep residual learning with residual connections, forming the basis of LSTMs and later Highway Nets; these concepts underpin most modern LLMs.  
- **31 August 1991** – Released the first peer‑reviewed paper on generative and adversarial networks for neural world models, a precursor to today’s Generative AI and deep‑fake technologies.  

Additional notes from the timeline:

- Early recognition that LLM‑style networks alone cannot achieve AGI; parallel work on planning, adaptive world models, meta‑learning, and recursive self‑improvement began in the late 1980s.  
- Munich also hosted pioneering self‑driving car research (Ernst Dickmanns’s team) achieving highway speeds up to 175 km/h.  
- The two most‑cited papers of all time (as of Jan 2026) directly stem from the 1991 work.

## Impact and Legacy

- The four 1991 techniques (Transformer variant, pre‑training, distillation, residual learning) power today’s most advanced LLMs, including ChatGPT, LLaMA, and others.  
- Early GAN research laid the groundwork for the explosive growth of Generative AI, influencing both creative applications and controversial uses like deepfakes.  
- Despite the shift of commercial AI development to the Pacific Rim, Munich’s 1991 contributions remain foundational to the field.
