# A Source of Mysterious Repeating Radio Signals From Space Has Been Identified

## Background
- Long‑period radio transients (LPTs) are strong radio bursts that repeat every few minutes to several hours; only about a dozen have been found in the Milky Way.  
- Earlier hypotheses suggested either slowly rotating magnetars or white‑dwarf binary systems as the source.  
- The magnetar idea conflicted with theoretical models, and no white‑dwarf binary had yet shown direct evidence of active accretion.

## New Observation
- An international team led by the University of Sydney used the ASKAP radio telescope to study the object **ASKAP J1745‑08.9‑505149**.  
- Spectroscopic data revealed hydrogen Balmer lines and strong He II emission, signatures of magnetic cataclysmic variables (a white dwarf accreting from a companion).  
- Radial‑velocity measurements gave an orbital period of **≈ 1.368 hours**, matching the radio pulse repetition period of **≈ 1.345 hours**.  
- The companion star’s mass is estimated at **≈ 0.096 M☉** and radius **≈ 0.13 R☉**, classifying it as an M6‑type red dwarf.

## Multi‑wavelength Findings
- X‑ray observations from China’s Einstein Probe show a periodicity of **≈ 1.32 hours** with large amplitude variations, indicating a changing accretion rate onto the white dwarf.  
- Radio bursts and X‑ray flares originate in different regions: X‑rays from heated accreting gas, radio bursts from the magnetic interaction zone; their peaks are out of phase.  
- Radio pulses are elliptically polarized, and the upper frequency limit oscillates with a longer‑period “beat,” possibly caused by misalignment between the white dwarf’s spin and orbital motion.  
- The system exhibits “modulation lanes” (striped intensity patterns), the first such detection in a binary other than the Jupiter‑Io system.

## Significance
- ASKAP J1745‑5051 is the first LPT where both stars and the accretion process have been directly observed, providing a concrete example of a white‑dwarf binary origin.  
- Researchers view it as a “Rosetta stone” for deciphering whether other LPTs arise from neutron‑star pulsars or similar white‑dwarf systems.  
- The binary serves as a natural laboratory for studying matter under extreme magnetic fields and strong gravity.

## Future Work
- Ongoing observations across radio, optical, and X‑ray wavelengths aim to clarify the precise mechanism that generates the repeating radio transients.
