# Arrive three hours before flight home, airline boss tells UK holidaymakers

## Background on the new EU border system
- The Entry Exit System (EES) requires non‑EU travellers to register biometric data (fingerprints) on entry and checks it on exit.  
- Since October, almost 80 million entries and exits have been recorded, with 35 000 refusals.  
- Full implementation across the Schengen zone is scheduled for 10 April.  
- Greece has temporarily suspended biometric checks for British citizens to avoid summer disruptions.

## Impact on travellers
- Long queues at passport control are causing passengers to miss return or connecting flights, especially at “hotspots” such as Spain, Portugal and France.  
- ACI Europe’s survey of 45 airports in 20 EU states reported waiting times of up to three and a half hours.  
- Portugal announced an extra 360 border officers for July to ease delays.  

## Advice from Wizz Air UK managing director Yvonne Moynihan
- Normal recommendation of arriving two hours before departure is increased to three hours for the summer period.  
- Passengers should bring a portable charger and water in case of extended waits.  
- Those with connecting flights should allow “a number of hours” between flights.  
- Border officials may proactively suspend EES checks when queues become excessive.  

## Responses from authorities and industry bodies
- The European Commission states EES is functioning well at “almost all border crossing points” and can be temporarily suspended in exceptional cases.  
- ACI Europe warns the situation could become unmanageable as passenger volumes rise and calls for resolution of IT stability and staffing issues.  
- The Commission emphasizes that member states must ensure proper implementation and adequate border‑guard staffing.  

## Outlook for the summer travel season
- Despite initial glitches, improvements have been made, but higher summer traffic will test the system further.  
- Wizz Air expects no schedule cuts, no fuel shortages, and continues to offer low fares to stimulate demand.  
- Airline bosses (Wizz Air, EasyJet, Jet2) encourage passengers to book with confidence, noting that any fare increases would likely be linked to sustained high oil prices.
