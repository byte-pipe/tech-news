# Anger at decision not to prosecute Canadian suicide kit supplier in UK

## Background and legal proceedings
- Kenneth Law, a 60‑year‑old Canadian, pleaded guilty in an Ontario court to 14 counts of aiding suicide and knowingly sending products abroad for that purpose.  
- A plea agreement withdrew earlier murder charges; sentencing is pending in Canada.  
- The UK National Crime Agency (NCA) investigation identified 286 UK recipients of Law’s suicide kits, linked to 112 deaths (79 confirmed in the Canadian statement of facts).

## Decision by the NCA and Crown Prosecution Service (CPS)
- The NCA and CPS announced they will not seek Law’s extradition to the UK after his Canadian proceedings conclude.  
- Their joint statement says the full extent of his offending will be addressed in a single Canadian sentencing process, a practice they deem normal for serious cross‑border crimes.  
- They acknowledge the decision may be painful for victims and families who hoped for a separate UK prosecution.

## Reactions from bereaved families
- Adele Zeynep Walton (sister of Aimee Walton, who died in 2022 after buying a kit) called the decision “insane” and “insulting,” warning it sends a dangerous message to potential offenders.  
- David Parfett (father of Thomas Parfett) expressed anger and urged a public inquiry, noting existing measures are insufficient.  
- Andy Burrows, chief executive of the Molly Rose Foundation, described the lack of UK prosecution as a “bitter blow” and highlighted ongoing risk while pro‑suicide forums remain online.  
- Families plan to meet with their lawyers at Leigh Day to discuss further actions and continue campaigning for justice.

## Government and prosecutorial rationale
- CPS specialist prosecutor Andrew Hudson argued extradition would be unnecessary and could trigger double‑jeopardy issues, as the conduct would already be punished in Canada.  
- He emphasized that the agreed approach ensures the quickest possible justice for UK victims.  
- Deputy Director of NCA investigations Craig Turner noted the UK investigation’s depth allowed its inclusion in the Canadian case, making the UK the only country with a sufficiently mature investigation to be incorporated.

## Next steps
- Law’s Canadian court will document the scale of his operations, including the 286 UK shipments and 79 UK deaths attributed to his kits.  
- Bereaved families will continue legal consultations and advocacy to prevent future incidents and seek accountability.
