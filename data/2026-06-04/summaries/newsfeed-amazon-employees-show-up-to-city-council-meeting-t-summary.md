# Amazon Employees Show Up to City Council Meeting to Demand Limits on Data Centers | WIRED

## Overview
- Two Amazon software engineers, Liesl Wigand and Patrick Schloesser, testified at a Seattle city hearing urging regulation of new data centers.
- Their remarks highlight concerns that rapid AI‑related data‑center expansion threatens the region’s environment, economy, and safety.

## Employees’ Main Demands
- **Local control:** City governments, together with community stakeholders, should set the terms for data‑center development.
- **Renewable energy:** Data centers must supply more renewable power than they consume and provide grid‑supporting storage.
- **Taxation and oversight:** Impose new taxes on tech firms and create worker‑led safety committees reporting to the city about risky AI tools.
- **Pause on permits:** Support a one‑year moratorium on data‑center permits to allow time for rulemaking.

## Collective Action
- Both speakers belong to **Amazon Employees for Climate Justice**, a group of current and former workers advocating for stronger environmental policies at Amazon.
- The collective plans additional testimony at upcoming city hearings where the moratorium ordinance will be debated.
- They call for city officials to consult frontline‑worker groups, such as labor unions, when shaping data‑center rules.

## Industry Context
- Tech companies and developers plan to spend hundreds of billions building dozens of data centers across the United States to meet AI demand.
- Communities nationwide have organized against these projects, citing electricity and water usage, toxic waste, emissions, noise, tax breaks, and doubts about AI’s societal value.
- Microsoft and Google have recently pledged greater transparency and environmental safeguards to pre‑empt backlash.

## Seattle’s Position
- The city currently lacks specific regulations for data centers, though it hosts several small facilities and anticipates interest in “large‑scale” projects.
- Officials are weighing a one‑year pause on permits to develop appropriate regulations, fearing higher water and power costs and increased carbon emissions.
- The Land Use and Sustainability Committee, after a public comment period with about 30 speakers (including laid‑off tech workers), voted to advance the moratorium proposal.

## Broader Worker Concerns
- Over 1,000 Amazon employees signed an anonymous open letter last year warning about the company’s “warp‑speed” AI development approach.
- Microsoft, Meta, and Google workers have voiced similar worries about AI’s environmental impact, military uses, and labor‑related issues.
- Amazon Employees for Climate Justice note that this is the first public, explicit call for data‑center regulation from workers at any major tech firm.

## External Perspectives
- A former Amazon manager in Seattle questioned the logic of locating data centers in urban areas given that newer technologies allow remote placement, while supporting workers’ right to speak without retaliation.
