# Amazon will show AI product images when you search for some reason

## Overview
- Amazon announced a new feature that displays AI‑generated product images in its shopping app based on users’ search queries.  
- The move is criticized as a questionable use of AI because it presents “fake” photos instead of real product images.

## How the feature works
- Users enter a search term; below the autocomplete suggestions, a row of AI‑generated images appears.  
- Example: searching “blue gingham dress” shows several dress variations (different sleeves, lengths, etc.).  
- Clicking an image takes the user to search results that match the visual style, leveraging Amazon’s visual search technology.  
- Amazon frames the feature as helping shoppers who can’t find the right descriptive term (e.g., “cowl neck” or “rattan”).

## Concerns and criticism
- Potentially misleading: shoppers might think the displayed image represents an actual product they can purchase, leading to disappointment.  
- Questioned necessity: Amazon already hosts real photographs of real products, which shoppers typically prefer.  
- The practice of creating fictitious product images to steer users is seen as “bananas” by the author.

- Audio product summaries where AI experts describe highlights in a podcast‑style format.

- Amazon Lens Live: visual search by scanning items with a camera.
