# State of the CIO, 2026: CIOs set the course for AI ROI

## Overview
- CIOs are under pressure to turn AI experimentation into measurable business value.  
- Only 19% of surveyed leaders say AI initiatives have met or exceeded goals; 18% report fewer than one‑third of use cases meet expectations.  
- Lack of clear ROI metrics, undefined corporate AI strategy, and insufficient in‑house expertise are the top barriers.

## Organizational structures for AI
- 83% of IT leaders have or plan cross‑functional steering committees or task forces to prioritize AI use cases.  
- IT dominates these committees, with participation from corporate leadership, security, finance, legal, and HR.  
- Formal AI project approval processes exist for 53% of respondents; another 28% plan to implement one within 12 months.  
- Only 47% have defined AI KPIs; 34% intend to create them this year.  
- Most common KPIs: operational efficiency (40%), employee productivity (34%), cost reduction (30%); revenue impact is tracked by only 27%.

## Case study highlights
- **First Student**: Established an AI council and innovation framework; uses metrics‑driven business cases to fast‑track value‑focused deployments.  
- **TIAA**: Built a comprehensive AI platform and governance (CoE, training, performance goals) but still struggles with ROI due to hidden operational costs (e.g., token usage, traffic handling).  

## Recommendations for achieving AI ROI
1. **Joint accountability** – Assign both a technical and a business sponsor to each project, sharing outcome ownership.  
2. **Embedded AI squads** – Move from a centralized CoE to AI teams placed within business units to ensure direct impact responsibility.  
3. **Stage‑gated funding** – Tie financing to outcome milestones (e.g., “reduce returns by 8%”) with regular checkpoints; terminate projects that miss multiple checkpoints.  
4. **Workflow integration** – Design AI experiences that fit seamlessly into day‑to‑day tasks of end users; otherwise adoption stalls.

## The CIO as chief AI orchestrator
- 46% of respondents view the CIO as a proactive business leader who identifies needs and aligns technology solutions with goals.  
- 83% see CIOs as change agents driving AI adoption across functions.  
- CIOs’ deep knowledge of the technology stack and proven cross‑functional collaboration make them the natural leaders for AI governance, metric definition, and execution.  

## Key takeaways
- Prioritizing high‑impact AI use cases and establishing clear, outcome‑based metrics are essential for ROI.  
- Structured governance (steering committees, approval processes, KPIs) is rapidly becoming standard practice.  
- Embedding AI expertise within business units and enforcing stage‑gated funding improve accountability and reduce waste.  
- CIOs are increasingly expected to lead AI strategy, align it with business objectives, and champion organization‑wide change.
