# Uber Caps Usage of AI Tools Like Claude Code to Manage Costs

## Background
- The author previously wrote about Uber exhausting its 2026 AI budget within four months, a result of budgeting based on 2025 expectations before the surge in token‑burning coding agents.

## New Policy
- Uber limits each employee to **$1,500** in monthly token spending for each AI coding tool.
- The limit applies only to agentic coding software such as **Cursor** or **Anthropic PBC’s Claude Code**.
- Spending on one tool does not affect the budget for another tool.

## Rationale
- The $1,500 per‑tool cap is presented as a rational response to prior overspending.
- It is considered more sensible than internal “token‑maxxing” leaderboards that encouraged competition for higher AI usage.

## Financial Impact
- Assuming an engineer actively uses two tools, the yearly cap per engineer is:
  - $1,500 × 2 tools × 12 months = **$36,000**.
- Median yearly compensation for Uber software engineers in the U.S. is **$330,000** (per Levels.fyi).
- The AI spending cap therefore represents roughly **11 %** of an engineer’s compensation.
- The author’s personal usage is about **$1,000/month** per provider (Anthropic and OpenAI), costing **$100** per provider thanks to subsidized individual plans—plans no longer available to large companies like Uber.
- If the author worked at Uber, they would have roughly **$500/month** of token budget remaining for each tool under the new cap.

## Implications
- The policy reveals the dollar value Uber assigns to AI coding tools.
- Larger enterprises lose access to the generous subsidized pricing that individual subscribers enjoy.
