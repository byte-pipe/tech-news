# GOV.UK goes Dutch on payments as it dumps Stripe

# Summary of “GOV.UK goes Dutch on payments as it dumps Stripe”

## Contract overview
- The Government Digital Service (GDS) has replaced Stripe with Dutch payments provider **Adyen** for many GOV.UK Pay transactions.  
- The new three‑year agreement is worth up to **£25.3 million** (initially estimated at a maximum of £49 million).  
- Adyen will handle card payments for local authorities, police forces, and armed forces units, as well as “pay‑by‑bank” services.  
- The contract covers roughly **17 % of all GOV.UK Pay transactions** but more than **70 % of the organisations** using the service.  
- It includes a “one‑working‑day” start‑up option for new services.

## Migration and impact
- GDS plans to migrate about **1,000 services** to Adyen.  
- The migration will comply with Know Your Customer (KYC) regulations to protect against fraud.  
- According to senior content designer Alan Maddrell, users should notice **no difference in the payment experience** and there will be **no loss of functionality**.  
- The switch enables the introduction of “pay‑by‑bank”, which moves money directly between bank accounts via open‑banking APIs, eliminating the need to enter card details.  
- Payments for central government, linked organisations, and NHS bodies will continue to be processed by **WorldPay**.

## Service details and statistics
- GOV.UK Pay was created to reduce the cost and effort for public services to set up online payments; organisations are only charged transaction fees.  
- Since its launch in 2016, GOV.UK Pay has processed **137.5 million transactions**, totalling around **£9.2 billion**.  
- The platform now supports **1,718 services**, including **662 for local government** and **256 for police forces**, serving **608 organisations** (e.g., RAF Air Cadets, Yeovil Town Council).
