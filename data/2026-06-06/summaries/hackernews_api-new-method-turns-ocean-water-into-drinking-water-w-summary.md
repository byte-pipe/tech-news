# New method turns ocean water into drinking water, without waste

## Context and problem
- 2.2 billion people lack safely managed drinking water; many regions rely on desalination.
- Conventional methods (reverse osmosis, thermal distillation) are energy‑intensive and produce brine, which harms marine ecosystems.
- Existing solar‑thermal desalination works with simple NaCl solutions but clogs when exposed to real seawater containing diverse salts.

## Key innovations
- Solar‑powered device uses black metal panels etched with femtosecond lasers to create a super‑light‑absorbing, super‑wicking surface.
- The panel has an “active” region that draws a thin water film, absorbs sunlight, evaporates water, and a “passive” region where salts are deposited, preventing clogging.
- Design exploits the coffee‑ring effect to drive salts toward the passive region, making the surface self‑cleaning.
- No chemical additives or pre‑treatment are required; the process extracts nearly 100 % of salts as solid material instead of brine.

## Technical approach
- Laser‑etched grooves on the black metal surface guide water flow and salt migration.
- Passive region collects solid salts, which can later be harvested.
- Embedded hydrogen‑titanate nanoparticles in the grooves selectively isolate lithium from other salts.

## Performance and results
- Tested with water samples from the Pacific, Atlantic, and Indian Oceans; the system continuously produced fresh water while self‑cleaning.
- Demonstrated extraction of solid salts and recovery of lithium (≈ 50 % of lithium in Great Salt Lake samples).
- Produced potable water and solid mineral residues without loss of efficiency.

## Implications and support
- The technology is inherently scalable, offering a potential solution to global water scarcity and sustainable mineral supply chains.
- Funding and support came from the National Science Foundation, the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation, and the Worldwide Universities Network.
- Researchers involved include Prof. Chunlei Guo, Senior Scientist Subash Singh, and graduate students Luheng Tang, Tainshu Xu, among others.
