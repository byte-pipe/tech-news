# Anthropic urges AI labs to pause development

## Background and proposal
- Anthropic calls for a coordinated, verifiable pause in the development of advanced AI systems, warning that rapid progress could lead to humans losing control.  
- The company’s internal research institute will work with others to explore the issue and develop mechanisms for a credible slowdown, without detailing specific actions.

## OpenAI’s contrasting view
- OpenAI argues that democratic governments, not private companies, should set the rules, safeguards, and accountability mechanisms for AI.  
- It stresses that decisions about the pace of AI innovation should not be left to any single lab, company, or special‑interest group.

## Technical concerns driving the pause
- AI models are becoming faster at tasks such as autonomous coding, raising the possibility of “recursive self‑improvement,” where an AI could design its own successor.  
- While self‑building AI could bring benefits in science and healthcare, it also heightens the risk of losing human control.  
- Researchers at the University of Toronto demonstrated an AI‑driven “worm” that adapts its hacking strategy as it spreads, underscoring security risks beyond the largest language models.

## Intended purpose of a coordinated slowdown
- Provide time for “societal structures and alignment research” to keep pace with AI advances; alignment means ensuring AI behaves according to human values and intentions.  
- Enable verification that all major AI labs have genuinely slowed or stopped work, preventing a “least cautious” player from gaining an advantage.  
- Address security concerns highlighted by Anthropic’s own Mythos model, which exposed vulnerabilities in existing code.

## Regulatory context
- Regulation, especially in the United States, has been slow; a recent Trump‑administration executive order asks labs to voluntarily submit their most capable models for government cybersecurity testing before public release.  
- Earlier AI‑pause efforts, such as the 2023 Future of Life Institute campaign backed by Elon Musk, achieved limited success.

## Anthropic’s safety stance and market moves
- Anthropic has positioned itself as a safety‑focused lab, refusing U.S. military use of its models for domestic surveillance and autonomous weapons, leading to a national‑security blacklist effective later in 2026.  
- The pause proposal comes as Anthropic and OpenAI prepare for IPOs, with Anthropic’s valuation potentially approaching a trillion dollars.
