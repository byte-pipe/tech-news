# Amazon's new Protein warehouse robot is fully autonomous

## Overview
- Amazon introduced an upgraded version of its Proteus robot that can be directed by employees using plain conversational language, thanks to an AI enhancement.
- The robot resembles a heavy‑duty Roomba and is built to move heavy carts and travel long distances inside fulfillment centers.

## New Capabilities
- Workers can assign tasks to the robot with natural language, eliminating the need for custom software.
- The AI determines task priority, optimal routes, and timing autonomously.
- Expanded operational range: the robot can now work throughout the warehouse, not just in dock areas, handling container transport, workstation transfers, and employee assistance.

## Deployment Plans
- Pilot testing is occurring in Amazon labs.
- Full rollout in Europe is planned for the first half of 2027.
- Amazon also intends to broaden use of its Vulcan touch‑sensitive robot and launch a new precision robot for smaller containers called Stark.

## Impact on Workforce
- Amazon states the robots will let employees focus on higher‑skill tasks such as inventory flow management and quality control, improving safety and reducing repetitive work.
- The company emphasizes that it has not replaced human jobs and plans to add 25,000 warehouse workers in Europe, noting that robotics have coincided with hiring hundreds of thousands globally.
- Contrastingly, Amazon has laid off nearly 30,000 workers across various divisions in the past year and has faced criticism for safety, accounting for a disproportionate share of serious warehouse injuries in 2024.
