# "We pissed off a lot of people": Giant data center plan cut 50% amid protests - Ars Technica

# Summary of ““We pissed off a lot of people”: Giant data center plan cut 50% amid protests”

## Overview
- The Stratos data center, originally planned to cover ~40,000 acres in Box Elder County, Utah—nearly three times the size of Manhattan—has been scaled back to about 20,000 acres after intense local opposition.
- The controversy centers on water use, electricity costs, air quality, wildlife impacts, and land stewardship.

## Project Scope and Environmental Concerns
- Proposed transfer of 1,900 acre‑feet of water from a ranch to the data center sparked a $15 fee‑based comment campaign by residents.
- Critics fear depletion of the Great Salt Lake and increased strain on local water resources.
- Additional worries include higher electricity bills, potential air‑quality degradation, and threats to local wildlife and habitats.

## Kevin O’Leary’s Response
- Venture capitalist Kevin O’Leary, chair of O’Leary Digital, acknowledged mishandling of public outreach and expressed regret for “screwing it up.”
- He confirmed the reduction of the project’s footprint by 50 % and pledged full transparency in permitting and environmental reviews.
- O’Leary said he will personally manage communications, criticizing earlier information sources as unreliable.

## Political Involvement
- Utah Senate President Stuart Adams (R) sent a letter demanding a 75 % cut; the compromise was celebrated by Adams as a potential model for responsible data‑center development.
- Adams’ push for transparency is linked to his upcoming primary election, with O’Leary suggesting political motives.
- Nationally, public opinion has shifted, with recent polls showing roughly 70 % of Americans opposing data centers near their homes, influencing Republican and Democratic strategies.

## Local Criticism and Skepticism
- Community activist Brenna Williams labeled the scaled‑back agreement “excellent performance art,” doubting any real mitigation of water and environmental impacts.
- The Salt Lake Tribune warned that the damaged trust may linger even if the project proceeds.
- Uncertainty remains about whether the nine‑gigawatt capacity will be altered.

## Broader Impact on Data‑Center Development
- HeatMap reports that at least 20 data‑center projects were canceled in Q1 2024 due to public backlash, more than double the previous quarter.
- Activist Erin Brockovich launched a tracking resource for data‑center status, reflecting growing public scrutiny.
- Other states, such as Illinois, are reconsidering tax incentives for data centers pending stronger regulatory frameworks.
