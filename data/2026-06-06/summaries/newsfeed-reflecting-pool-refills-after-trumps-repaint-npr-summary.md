# Reflecting Pool refills after Trump’s repaint

## Project overview
- The Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool was closed for weeks while its basin was resurfaced and painted a darker shade the president calls “American flag blue.”
- Water began refilling the 2,028‑foot pool on Friday; a stripe of water runs down the middle, reflecting the Washington Monument.
- President Trump announced the completion time and described the result as “beautiful,” while Interior Secretary Doug Burgum posted a video of water bubbling up through a grate on the newly painted floor.

## Public reaction
- Visitors noted that the pool’s color looks similar to its previous gray; many could not tell a difference once the water level rose.
- Some observers described the hue as closer to black or gray rather than blue.
- A few praised the cleaner appearance and the return of ducks, while others expressed concern that the project might have made the historic landmark look more like a swimming pool.
- Comments included:
  - “I’m just happy it’s not that bright blue… if it stays like this, it’s fine.” – Luisa Córdoba, D.C. runner
  - “I’m colorblind, so it doesn’t look blue — yet.” – Terry Barzanti, Maryland resident
  - “I’m not colorblind and it doesn’t look blue.” – Edgar Sadsad, coworker

## Funding and legal issues
- Trump initially estimated the project would cost $2 million and be finished in one to two weeks; the Department of the Interior later said it would take about a month.
- Interior Department records show a contract of $13.1 million with Atlantic Industrial Coatings, far exceeding the president’s figure.
- The nonprofit Cultural Landscape Foundation sued to halt the work, claiming the administration bypassed required historic preservation reviews; a judge had not ruled by the time work was declared complete.
- Funding sources appear to include “fee dollars” from national park visitors, with a sign stating the improvements are paid for by park fees. The administration also cited endowment funds and revenue from park pass sales.

## Broader D.C. renovation agenda
- Trump’s administration is pursuing multiple changes in Washington, D.C., ranging from a proposed triumphal arch to new statues, fountain restorations, graffiti removal, and debris cleanup.
- The Interior Department referenced a White House post listing accomplishments such as 500 graffiti removals, 134 rat‑resistant trash cans installed, and 250 truckloads of pond debris removed.
- National Guard troops are being deployed to assist with the projects ahead of the nation’s 250th‑birthday celebrations around July 4, which serves as a deadline for many renovations.
