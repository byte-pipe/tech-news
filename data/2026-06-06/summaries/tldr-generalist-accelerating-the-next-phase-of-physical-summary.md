# Accelerating the next phase of physical AI

## Introduction
- Millions of robots are already deployed; billions more are expected across factories, warehouses, labs, restaurants, farms, homes, and space.  
- All these robots need intelligence that can understand and act in the physical world.  

## Funding and Investors
- Announced $400 million in new funding, bringing total capital raised to over $500 million.  
- Lead investor: Radical Ventures; other new investors: 8VC, Union Square Ventures, Hanabi Capital, Norwest.  
- Existing investors continued participation: NVIDIA, Boldstart Ventures, Spark Capital, Bezos Expeditions, NFDG.  
- New angel investors include Bin Lin, Fei‑Fei Li, and Naval Ravikant.  

## Frontier Lab for Robot Intelligence
- **GEN‑0 (Nov 2023)** introduced large‑scale pretraining for robotics, showing scaling laws: more real‑world data and larger models yield more capable systems.  
- **GEN‑1 (Apr 2024)** moved toward commercial viability with:
  - 99 % reliability across diverse tasks.  
  - Execution speed up to 3× faster than previous state‑of‑the‑art.  
  - Ability to learn complex new physical skills.  
  - Emergent improvisational intelligence enabling creative problem solving.  

## Vision and Strategy
- Success stems from thousands of decisions across data, models, hardware, infrastructure, operations, and deployment by a world‑class team.  
- Funding will support:
  - Development of next‑generation models.  
  - Expansion of the physical data engine.  
  - Scaling compute and training infrastructure.  
  - Partnerships with industries to bring systems into everyday use.  
- Commitment to building whatever technology is needed for physical AGI, without being tied to a single method or label.  

## Future Outlook
- Intelligence must work across varied form factors (humanoids, robotic arms, mobile warehouse robots, space autonomous systems).  
- Early stage of the journey; breakthroughs and product inflections are expected to compound.  
- A “flywheel” is emerging:
  1. Scaling robot learning → better models.  
  2. Better models → more useful physical work.  
  3. Real‑world data from businesses → next‑generation models.  
- General intelligence will arise from systems that learn by acting, improve through experience, and become useful alongside people.
