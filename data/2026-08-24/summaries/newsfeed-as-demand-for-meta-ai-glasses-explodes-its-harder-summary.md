# Summary of “As demand for Meta AI glasses explodes, it’s harder to avoid creepy recordings”

## Overview
- Smart‑glasses sales are soaring, with Meta’s AI glasses leading the market.  
- Their ubiquity raises the risk of being recorded without consent in everyday settings.

## Privacy concerns
- Potential future features such as facial‑recognition could enable deepfakes and non‑consensual intimate imagery.  
- EFF warns that without a private right to sue for biometric‑privacy violations, manufacturers may add invasive capabilities.  
- Meta’s only built‑in safety indicator is a tiny LED that can be covered, offering minimal protection.

## Bans and enforcement
- Schools, courts, restaurants, entertainment venues, and events like DEF CON 2026 have begun banning smart glasses.  
- Enforcement relies on staff spotting the devices or users voluntarily disclosing them; both are imperfect.  
- Bans create social friction but cannot stop covert recording on their own.

## Meta’s planned “super‑sensing” features
- Reports indicate Meta is testing glasses that continuously capture video and audio, with plans to disable the LED indicator during such use.  
- Public backlash could influence whether these features are released.  
- Meta previously removed an unreleased facial‑recognition system from its app after criticism.

## EFF guidance for users
- Publishes a guide on resisting facial‑recognition, recording responsibly, and blurring strangers’ faces before sharing videos.  
- Emphasizes that current LED warnings are “better than nothing, but not by much.”

## Hobbyist detection apps
- Developers have created Bluetooth‑based apps (e.g., Nearby Glasses, Antizuck Smart Glasses Scanner) that alert users to nearby smart glasses.  
- Apps cannot confirm active recording and may produce false positives; they advise against confrontation.  
- Despite limitations, the apps have been downloaded over 100,000 times and are seen as a supplemental defense.

## Public reaction
- Users express strong revulsion toward non‑consensual recording and seek any tool to detect glasses.  
- Teachers and other professionals worry about unwanted recordings in classrooms and other sensitive environments.  
- Developers stress that detection tools are imperfect and should not give a false sense of security.
