# The End of an Athlon – Summary

## Incident Description
- While researching CPUID quirks on Athlon MP and XP CPUs, the author removed a heatsink from an Athlon XP.
- A large chunk of silicon detached from the processor and remained glued to the heatsink.
- The CPU continued to function despite the missing material.

## Analysis of Damage
- The detached piece suggests a pre‑existing long, straight micro‑crack that did not affect operation.
- Applying modest force caused the micro‑crack to propagate, resulting in the loss of a sizable silicon fragment.
- Close‑up photos show a straight fracture on the right side and typical fracture marks on the left.

## Packaging Context
- Around 2000, both Intel and AMD employed flip‑chip PGA packaging to improve cooling as TDP rose to 70–80 W.
- Intel quickly abandoned the approach, using it only on some PIII models before switching to lidded CPUs for the P4 line and PIII‑S.
- AMD continued flip‑chip packaging for PGA Athlons but not for Opterons.

## Impact on CPU Durability
- Exposed silicon in flip‑chip designs is fragile; uneven heatsink pressure can crack the die.
- Many vintage flip‑chip CPUs exhibit chipped corners, usually without functional impact.
- Lidded and later pin‑less LGA packages are sturdier, reducing mechanical damage to the die (though the socket becomes the new weak point).
