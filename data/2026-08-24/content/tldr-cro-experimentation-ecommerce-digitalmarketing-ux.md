description: 'The term ''Conversion Rate Optimisation (CRO)'' is the worst possible name for the concept it is meant to describe. Some people believe it is merely a matter of semantics and that the name is unimportant. However, it is indeed significant, as it lies at the core of the profound misunderstandings that pervade our industry. Here is why: 1) Digital experimentation does not have to focus solely (or even at all) on ''conversion rate''. This metric was initially adopted as a useful comparative measure for the user experience of your website. Nevertheless, there are numerous goals and objectives that can be improved through experimentation (not necessarily A/B testing). The term ''CRO'' severely restricts the practice of experimentation to a specific outcome (website UX for e-commerce). 2) Digital experimentation will not necessarily tangibly increase your conversion rate. This metric is a leading indicator of numerous factors related to your company, product, and market. While informed
  decision-making will yield incremental revenue benefits, there is a possibility that your conversion rate could decrease due to other factors. This name is central to a significant misunderstanding in this area. 3) The term ''optimisation'' suggests refining and finalising minor details, rather than implementing substantial, significant changes. This leads people to perceive it as an exercise in polishing, whereas more critical elements are simply matters of opinion and belief. This could not be further from the truth. 4) The use of a ''metric'' to describe an entire practice leads people to believe that the sole purpose is to alter that metric. This is what causes people to completely disregard the research and development and learning aspects of their work. 5) Furthermore, improving your conversion rate can be done in myriad ways that could actually hurt you or even destroy your business, for example by destroying your brand, credibility, reputation etc. These things cannot be measured
  with metrics! The most significant problem is that senior individuals responsible for budgets, hiring, objectives, and so on, will never take the time to understand the practice at a sufficiently deep level; they possess only a superficial understanding. A considerable part of that understanding stems from the name of the practice. The term ''CRO'' could not be less appropriate for what we do, and it is fundamentally at the heart of many of the challenges faced by anyone in this field. Unfortunately, though, it is one of those terms that is unlikely to be replaced in the near future! #cro #experimentation #ecommerce #digitalmarketing #ux #userexperience #productmanagement | 30 comments on LinkedIn'
