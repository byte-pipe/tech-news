# 'Stunningly beautiful' blue sea creatures appear on Welsh beaches

## Main event
- Hundreds of blue sea creatures washed up on beaches in Anglesey, Gwynedd and Tenby.  
- They are identified as *Velella velella*, commonly called “by‑the‑wind sailors”.

## Public reaction
- Maxine Allinson, 49, spotted one on Tenby South beach and described it as “fantastic” and “beautiful”.  
- Another beach‑goer compared the creature to a crystal.

## Description of the creatures
- Small hydrozoans with a sail‑like structure that catches wind and drifts on the ocean surface.  
- About 7 cm long, bright blue, and form large groups that strand together when currents change.  
- Related to Portuguese man‑of‑war, sea anemones and corals; they are colonies of animals.

## Expert insight (Frankie Hobro, Anglesey Sea Zoo)
- Appear most often in autumn and winter, carried by ocean currents.  
- Generally harmless compared with Portuguese man‑of‑war; sting is “very mild”.  
- Stinging cells can remain active even after the animals appear dead.  
- Advice: admire from a distance; avoid touching lips, face or other sensitive areas, as contact may cause a painful tingling sensation.

## Seasonal and environmental context
- Large groups strand together because they travel in the same currents and are deposited on coastlines simultaneously.  
- Storms or shifts in currents often “dump” them ashore.
