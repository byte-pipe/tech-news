# Summary of “My brother hid in a rice sack” – The refugee stars at the World Cup  

## Key players and their refugee backgrounds  
- Antonio Rüdiger (Germany, Real Madrid) – born in Berlin after his family fled the Sierra Leone civil war; his uncle hid children in a rice sack to escape rebel recruitment.  
- Alphonso Davies (Canada, Bayern Munich) – spent early childhood in a Ghanaian refugee camp after his parents fled Liberia’s war; later moved to Canada and credits the country for his education and football development.  
- Nestory Irankunda (Australia, Watford) – 20‑year‑old forward, born in a refugee camp and became the Socceroos’ youngest World Cup scorer.  
- Mohamed Toure and Awer Mabil (Australia) – both grew up in African refugee camps before representing Australia.  

## UNHCR “game‑changing team” and the broader refugee narrative  
- Rüdiger, Davies and several other internationals (Eduardo Camavinga, Victor Moses, Asmir Begović, Ali Al‑Hamadi) have joined a UNHCR campaign showcasing athletes who were displaced by war or persecution.  
- The campaign aims to highlight what is possible when displaced youth receive safety, opportunity and welcome.  
- Rüdiger warns that public empathy for refugees is waning and stresses that negative actions of a few should not tarnish the whole group.  

## Australia’s multicultural squad and its promotion  
- Australia’s national team features a trio of forwards with refugee backgrounds (Irankunda, Toure, Mabil).  
- The Professional Footballers’ Association produced a video in which each player states their place of birth or family heritage, celebrating immigration’s benefits.  

## US refugee policy context affecting the World Cup narrative  
- In January 2025, President Donald Trump signed an order suspending the US Refugee Admissions Programme (USRAP), cutting annual admissions to 7,500 and prioritising white South Africans.  
- From October 2024 to April 2025, only 6,069 refugees were admitted, 99.95 % from South Africa, compared with over 100,000 in the previous fiscal year under President Joe Biden.  
- Critics argue the policy contradicts the World Cup’s message of inclusion and opportunity, especially as the tournament is hosted partly in the US.  

## Implications and commentary  
- The presence of refugee‑origin players on the world stage underscores the positive outcomes of generous asylum policies.  
- UNHCR and player advocates hope the tournament will shift global perceptions, emphasizing resilience and contribution rather than blame.  
- Ongoing political decisions, particularly in the US, risk undermining the narrative of hope that the refugee athletes embody.
