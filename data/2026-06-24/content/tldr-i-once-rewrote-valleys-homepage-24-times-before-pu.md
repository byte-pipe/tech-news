description: 'I once rewrote Valley''s homepage 24 times before publishing it. I thought I was being obsessive. Then I read this: 5x more people read your headline than your body copy. So by the time you''ve written the headline, you''ve already spent 80 cents of every dollar. ( Ogilvy ) I wasn''t being obsessive. I was underspending. Here are the copywriting rules I keep coming back to, mostly from Harry Dry & the greats he channels: + Run every sentence through 3 tests: Can I visualize it? Can I falsify it? Can nobody else say this? Three yeses = keep. Three nos = rubbish. + "Don''t just get a job. Change an entire industry." Fails all 3. "Worn by supermodels in London and dads in Ohio." Passes all 3. That''s the whole game. + Concrete beats abstract. Your brain remembers pictures. "Couch to 5K" beats "regain fitness." "1,000 songs in your pocket" beats "5GB MP3 player." + The zoom-in trick: write the abstract word, then rewrite it until you''ve got something you could drop on your foot.
  + Falsifiable beats safe. "Galileo got house-arrested for saying the Earth spins around the Sun. If he''d said the Earth had a harmonious connection with celestial objects, they''d have sent him to the pub." + Volvo line: "Your car has 5 numbers on the speedometer. Volvo has 6." Visual. Falsifiable. Only Volvo can say it. + If a competitor could have written your sentence, cut it. Hinge owns "Designed to be deleted." Nobody else can say it now. Find your version. + Write the way you talk. Contractions. Starting sentences with And. But. So. Read it aloud. If your partner cringes, rewrite. + Kill landing-page words: unlock, unleash, empower, supercharge, seamless. Real people don''t talk like that. + Kill adverbs and adjectives. "Incredibly delicious meal" loses to "the steak fell off the bone." + More periods. Fewer commas. + The burrito test: paragraphs should fit in 2 lines, and pulling any sentence should make the whole thing fall apart. + Slippery slide: the only job of the headline
  is to get the first sentence read. Same for every sentence after. + Bold claim up top. Then spend the rest backing it up with an absurd amount of proof. + Call-to-Value beats Call-to-Action. "Create Your Website" beats "Sign up now." + Write for one reader. Not a thousand. One. + Your customers wrote your copy already. Ask them what''s frustrating, steal the exact phrase from the email. + Blair Warren''s 27 words: people will do anything for those who encourage their dreams, justify their failures, allay their fears, confirm their suspicions, and help them throw rocks at their enemies. Hit one, you punch above your weight. + AI can''t write copy. AI has no taste, no conviction, no lived experience. It rearranges words humans already laid down. Great copy is laid down in an order nobody has used. + Great copy isn''t written. It''s rewritten. 20 drafts, minimum. The bar: if a competitor could have written it, cut it. If a 9 out of 10 isn''t your floor, don''t ship. If not, rewrite. | 11
