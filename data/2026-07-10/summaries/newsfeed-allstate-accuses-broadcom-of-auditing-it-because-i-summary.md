# Allstate accuses Broadcom of auditing it because it quit VMware, CA - Ars Technica

# Summary of “Allstate accuses Broadcom of auditing it because it quit VMware, CA – Ars Technica”

## Background
- Allstate Insurance claims Broadcom launched audits as retaliation after Allstate decided not to renew contracts with VMware and CA Technologies.  
- The allegation appears in the context of a lawsuit that VMware filed against Allstate in December 2025.  
- Broadcom asserts that Allstate violated license‑audit obligations stipulated in its contract.

## Allstate’s Position
- In a filing dated June 12, Allstate stated that Broadcom’s audits were “haphazard” and were initiated only after Allstate announced it would end business with VMware and its sister company CA.  
- The filing quotes Allstate: “This case is about VMware’s decision to initiate a haphazard ‘audit’ of Allstate, once it was aware that Allstate did not intend to renew its contracts with VMware or its sister company, CA.”  
- Allstate is listed among “thousands of companies” migrating away from VMware after Broadcom’s takeover, alongside T‑Mobile, Tesco, and Western Union.  
- Allstate did not provide a comment to The Register; Broadcom declined comment to Ars Technica.

## VMware Lawsuit Details
- VMware and Allstate have been partners since 2008.  
- VMware’s complaint (PDF) alleges it issued a formal audit notice in March 2025; Allstate acknowledged receipt on May 7 2025 but allegedly “stone‑walled” and withheld required materials.  
- VMware claims Allstate removed VMware from all devices on September 12 2025, preventing execution of audit scripts that depend on VMware components.  
- Allstate’s response (June filing) asserts it complied “substantially and in good faith” with audit and reporting requirements and that VMware’s claims are unfounded.

## CA Technologies Lawsuit
- A separate lawsuit filed by CA Technologies in May 2025 accuses Allstate of copyright infringement and breach of contract.  
- The claim centers on Allstate’s sale of its Employer Voluntary Benefits business and associated Symantec products to StanCorp Financial Group, without sending the required notice to CA.  
- CA alleges Allstate sent a notice to the now‑defunct Symantec but not to CA.

## Procedural Timeline
- Both the VMware and CA lawsuits allow parties to file dispositive motions until May 17 2027, seeking resolution without a trial.

## Implications
- The cases illustrate Broadcom’s aggressive litigation strategy toward dissatisfied VMware customers.  
- Allstate has not disclosed how dependent it was on VMware or what virtualization technology it now employs.  
- The situation reflects a broader trend of large enterprises moving away from VMware and challenging Broadcom’s post‑acquisition business practices in court.
