# A Judge Has Blocked the Pentagon’s Attempt to Blacklist Anthropic | WIRED

# Summary of “A Judge Has Blocked the Pentagon’s Attempt to Blacklist Anthropic”

## Court Decision
- U.S. District Judge Rita Lin (California) ruled that the Trump administration’s designation of Anthropic as a “national security risk” was unconstitutional retaliation.
- The ruling vacated Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s February 27 decision labeling Anthropic a “supply‑chain risk” and barred the company from federal contracts.
- Lin described the additional punitive measure—preventing contractors or suppliers to the military from doing business with Anthropic—as “arbitrary, capricious, an abuse of discretion, and otherwise not in accordance with law.”
- Nine agencies (Pentagon, Treasury, State, DHS, etc.) were found to have improperly imposed sanctions; those penalties were removed.
- The decision does **not** require the Pentagon to use Anthropic’s models, nor does it prevent the Pentagon from choosing other AI providers. An appeal is expected.

## Background Dispute
- The conflict began over a $200 million contract for Anthropic’s Claude models in military applications.
- Reports surfaced that Claude was used in the operation to capture Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro, prompting internal concerns.
- Anthropic demanded limits on the use of its models (e.g., prohibitions on lethal autonomous weapons and mass‑surveillance systems). Hegseth rejected any curbs, insisting the contract allowed “all lawful use.”
- After negotiations collapsed in February, Hegseth labeled Anthropic a “supply‑chain risk,” effectively blacklisting the company.

## Pentagon’s Position
- The Pentagon argued the supply‑chain risk label was necessary because granting Anthropic access to classified systems could pose “unacceptable risk” if the AI lab could disable or alter its technology during wartime.
- Despite the ruling, the Pentagon retains the freedom to select any AI vendor and may continue using other models.

## Anthropic’s Response
- Anthropic spokesperson Danielle Cohen welcomed the ruling, calling the supply‑chain risk designation unlawful, and affirmed the company’s intent to work productively with the government on national‑security AI applications.

## Ongoing Legal Actions
- Anthropic filed two lawsuits: the California federal district case (now decided) and a separate case in the U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit, alleging First‑ and Fifth‑Amendment violations on ideological grounds. The D.C. case remains pending.

## Implications
- The judge’s decision highlights limits on executive authority to blacklist private firms without clear legal justification.
- It underscores the tension between national‑security concerns and corporate control over AI deployment.
- The ruling may affect how future AI contracts and supply‑chain risk designations are handled across federal agencies.
