## Main observations
- I have been testing **gpt‑5.6‑luna**, finding it surprisingly capable, fast (≈100 tps), and able to navigate my codebase, email, and knowledge base.  
- The primary drawback is cost; even modest research runs can generate API bills in the tens of cents.

## Why consumer AI is still scarce
- Traditional consumer‑app playbooks relied on cheap hosting, virality, fundraising, and ad‑based monetization.  
- Adding AI introduces per‑request inference costs, dramatically raising the capital needed to scale.  
- Example: a daily personalized news site built with a Sonnet‑class model would cost ~ $1 per user per day, making a $30/mo subscription untenable.  
- With luna, the same workflow averages about **$0.10** per user, opening a viable business case.

## Business implications
- My Segment co‑founder Peter distinguishes two work categories:  
  1. **“IQ 180” work** – breakthrough, high‑impact problem solving.  
  2. **“Token‑spewer” work** – high‑volume, fast‑response tasks (calls, nudges, coordination).  
- Although Peter’s companies rely on the first type for survival, roughly **95 %** of his daily effort falls into the second category.  
- Frontier‑level models will remain essential for research‑intensive fields (engineering, hard science, model training).  
- Simultaneously, demand for **fast/cheap/good‑enough** models is poised to explode, mirroring how most human labor is allocated in organizations today.

## Path forward
- Realizing the potential of inexpensive, responsive models requires:
  - New tooling and harnesses for deployment.  
  - Robust prompt‑injection safety mechanisms.  
  - Role‑based access and permission frameworks.  
- I invite anyone experimenting with small models to reach out and collaborate.
