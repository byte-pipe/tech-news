# 2027 Nissan Rogue Hybrid: Drives like an EV, but there's no plug involved – Ars Technica

## Overview of the powertrain
- Nissan’s new Rogue Hybrid uses a **series‑hybrid** layout (e‑Power), where the 1.5 L turbo three‑cylinder engine acts only as a generator for a 1.67 kWh lithium‑ion traction battery.  
- Engine output: 148 hp (110 kW), limited to 5,200 rpm, achieving ~42 % thermal efficiency.  
- Battery powers two permanent‑magnet synchronous motors:  
  - Front motor – 202 hp (150 kW), 243 lb‑ft (330 Nm)  
  - Rear motor – 134 hp (100 kW), 144 lb‑ft (195 Nm)  
  - Combined system rating limited to 225 hp (168 kW) by battery capacity.  
- Regenerative braking is strong enough that Nissan demonstrated a “no‑spill” obstacle test, outperforming the Toyota RAV4 hybrid.

## Design and efficiency
- Exterior styling features a slim horizontal light strip and a new large Nissan grille, giving a subtle Range‑Rover vibe.  
- Aero‑optimized bodywork improves fuel economy.  
- Interior emphasizes touch controls; the Tech package adds a 14.3‑inch Android Automotive display with Google services and Apple CarPlay.  
- Seats use Nissan’s “Zero Gravity” design, inspired by puffer‑jackets, offering long‑drive comfort.

## Driving experience
- Acceleration feels electric‑like with immediate throttle response; 0‑60 mph in about 6.4 seconds.  
- One‑pedal driving (e‑Pedal) is available, providing smooth deceleration via regen.  
- Engine noise is minimal; it shuts off whenever possible, making cabin noise dominated by wind and road.  
- Ride quality is comfortable over bumps but shows some under‑steer on low‑traction, low‑rolling‑resistance tires.  
- Steering feedback is praised, and overall feel is pleasant for daily use.

## Practicality and fuel economy
- EPA‑like ratings: 41 mpg city, 36 mpg highway, 38 mpg combined (≈5.7 L/100 km city).  
- Cargo space: 34.4 cu ft (974 L) with seats up, 65.9 cu ft (1,866 L) with seats folded.  
- Only the SR trim with Tech Package is offered initially, priced at **$41,490**.  
- Non‑hybrid Rogue will not appear until the 2028 model year.

## Verdict
- The series‑hybrid e‑Power system delivers an EV‑like driving feel without a plug, satisfying enthusiasts who prefer pure electric dynamics.  
- Fuel efficiency is competitive within the compact‑SUV segment, though slightly behind the RAV4 hybrid.  
- Strong interior tech, comfortable seating, and refined driving dynamics make the 2027 Rogue Hybrid a compelling choice for buyers seeking a hybrid SUV with electric‑style performance.
