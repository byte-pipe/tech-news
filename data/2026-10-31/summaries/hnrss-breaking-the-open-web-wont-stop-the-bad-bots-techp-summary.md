# Breaking the Open Web Won’t Stop the Bad Bots – Summary  

## Background  
- Over 300 media representatives recently urged Congress to pass the Stealth Bot Prohibition Act.  
- The surge in automated traffic, largely from AI developers scraping content, threatens referral traffic and ad revenue for news sites.  

## Issues with the Proposed Bills  
- Both the federal Stealth Bot Prohibition Act and New York’s Stealth Crawler Prohibition Act would require bots to disclose their identity and purpose.  
- The federal bill allows the FTC to impose penalties up to $53,000 for “reasonably likely” damage, but provides no clear definition of that standard.  
- The New York bill lets a publisher subpoena a crawler’s identity without showing any actual harm.  
- Disclosure of identity does not stop high‑frequency, costly crawling, and it can chill legitimate public‑interest activities.  

## Importance of Anonymous Crawling  
- Investigative journalism relies on large‑scale scraping (e.g., WSJ’s analysis of 31,000 financial disclosures).  
- Archival projects like the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine have preserved over one trillion webpages, supporting news reporting, legal evidence, and public memory.  
- Researchers, disability advocates, public‑health analysts, cybersecurity agencies, and environmental investigators use crawlers, often needing anonymity to protect the integrity and safety of their work.  

## Legal Context  
- The First Amendment protects against government‑imposed identification requirements for receiving information (Lamont v. Postmaster General).  
- Existing privacy, copyright, and consumer‑protection laws already address many harms associated with abusive scraping.  

## Policy Recommendations  
- Encourage development of standardized, interoperable technical solutions (e.g., IETF protocols) for managing automated traffic and enabling anonymous attestation of good bot behavior.  
- Avoid premature legislation that harms “good” bots while “bad” bots can simply evade new rules.  
- If new laws are deemed necessary, liability should focus on conduct that actually causes damage, not merely on anonymity.  

## Conclusion  
- Unmasking bots does not reduce request volume, compensate publishers, or restore lost referral traffic.  
- Targeted, technology‑focused approaches, combined with existing legal tools, are more effective than broad identification mandates that threaten the open web and essential public‑interest work.
