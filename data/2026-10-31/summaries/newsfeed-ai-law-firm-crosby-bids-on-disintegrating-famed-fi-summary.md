# AI law firm Crosby bids on disintegrating, famed firm Weil Gotshal

## Overview
- Crosby, a two‑year‑old AI‑first law firm, has submitted a bid to acquire assets of Weil, Gotshal & Manges, including its trademark, brand, and unspecified internal data.  
- The bid also aims to hire some of Weil’s 1,200 lawyers.  

## Context of Weil Gotshal’s Collapse
- Weil Gotshal confirmed to the New York Times it will vote on dissolution in the coming days.  
- Contributing factors: the presumed leadership heir declined the role, several top‑producing lawyers left, and a planned merger with rival Willkie Farr & Gallagher fell through.  

## Crosby’s Business Model and Funding
- Crosby operates as a full‑service law firm that leverages its internally developed AI to deliver legal services at unprecedented speed.  
- Funding: roughly $85 million total, including a $60 million Series B round in March led by Lux and Index, with participation from seed‑lead Sequoia, Elad Gil, 01 Advisors, and Bain Capital Ventures.  
- The firm emerged from stealth in 2025 after raising $5.8 million in seed capital.  

## Implications
- No direct link has been made between the rise of legal AI and Weil Gotshal’s downfall, but AI is viewed as a key driver for the legal industry’s future.  
- Terms of Crosby’s bid were not disclosed, and there is no guarantee of success; however, a successful acquisition could see Weil’s legacy transformed under an AI‑first firm.
