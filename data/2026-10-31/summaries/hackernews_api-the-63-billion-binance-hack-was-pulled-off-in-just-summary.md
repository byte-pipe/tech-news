# The $6.3 Billion Binance Hack – Summary

## Overview
- On October 29, hackers drained roughly $6.3 billion in crypto from Binance wallets across at least ten blockchains (Ethereum, Bitcoin, Tron, Solana, etc.).
- The theft occurred in about 20 minutes, starting around 4 p.m. ET.
- Binance immediately halted all deposits and withdrawals and began an investigation.

## Timeline & Method
- **Preparation:** At least 36 hours before the attack, the perpetrators tested small withdrawals on three wallets and rehearsed the final code on a fourth.
- **Execution:** Four hours after the rehearsal, the malicious code was installed on a Binance hot wallet, allowing rapid draining of funds.
- **Hot wallets:** Internet‑connected wallets used for customer withdrawals were the primary target.
- **Automation:** Thousands of rapid transactions suggest the use of automated tools to obscure fund trails.

## Attribution
- Crypto‑tracing firms (Elliptic, TRM Labs, Chainalysis) link the hack to the North Korean hacking group known as **TraderTraitor**.
- Indicators include:
  - Similar tactics to previous North Korean attacks (e.g., compromised engineer laptop, use of Tornado Cash for mixing).
  - Overlap of stolen funds with those from the recent Bitget exploit, also attributed to North Korean actors.
- Binance and law‑enforcement have not officially confirmed any link to North Korea.

## Response & Recovery
- Binance states the incident involved a compromised laptop and additional breach points; it will cover all losses.
- Crypto firms have frozen about $3 billion of the stolen assets (≈44 % of the total), mainly stablecoins.
- The remaining $3.3 billion, primarily Bitcoin, Ether, and XRP, cannot be frozen until it reaches an exchange or service; only a modest increase in frozen assets is expected.

## Current Status
- Approximately half of the stolen crypto is currently immobilized; the rest remains in circulation.
- Chainalysis reports new laundering patterns and heavy use of automation to hide fund movements.
- Ongoing investigations by Binance, crypto tracing firms, and law‑enforcement aim to track and recover the remaining assets.
