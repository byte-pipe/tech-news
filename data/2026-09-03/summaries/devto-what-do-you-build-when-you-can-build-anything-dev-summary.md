# What do you build when you can build anything? – Summary

## The Cost of Building Everything
- Building every idea wastes resources: time, money, data‑center bytes, and often results in products no one uses.  
- The crucial skill today is learning **what not to build** amid limitless possibilities.  
- The author’s photo‑walk app example shows how a simple concept quickly expands into many user needs, prompting the question of real value versus novelty.

## Welcome to Agency Hell
- Historically, technical and logistical constraints filtered ideas; many never left the sketch stage, which was beneficial.  
- AI removes most barriers: an idea can become a prototype within hours, with code, design, copy, and deployment generated automatically.  
- This freedom creates a new trap: constant pressure to build because “everything is possible,” leading to a cycle of copying trends and chasing quick wins.

## What Do You Actually Care About?
- The core question shifts from “what can I build?” to “what do I care about?”  
- Personal relevance (family, hobbies, friends) surfaces problems that matter and are worth solving.  
- Passion drives the ability to notice niche pain points that others overlook; without it, AI‑generated projects become empty.

## Possibility Isn’t Purpose
- AI lowers execution costs and speeds experimentation but does **not** identify valuable ideas.  
- Time, audience size, and problem significance remain limited resources.  
- Building without genuine interest leads to “endless misery” despite technical ease.

## Takeaway & Discussion
- Selectivity is essential: prioritize ideas that align with personal values or real user needs.  
- AI is a tool for faster creation, not a substitute for purpose.  
- The author invites readers to share experiences of duplicated ideas and to reflect on how to discern worthwhile projects.
