# Malta's Yorgen Fenech acquitted in case of Daphne Caruana Galizia killing

## Background
- Daphne Caruana Galizia, a 53‑year‑old investigative journalist, was killed by a car bomb on 16 October 2017 while driving near her home.  
- Her reporting exposed alleged corruption in Malta’s political and business circles, including figures close to then‑Prime Minister Joseph Muscat and offshore companies revealed in the Panama Papers.  
- At the time of her death she faced more than 40 libel suits.  
- The murder shocked Europe, sparked mass protests, and contributed to Muscat’s resignation, highlighting a culture of impunity in the EU’s smallest member state.

## Trial outcome
- Yorgen Fenech, a prominent Maltese businessman, was indicted in 2021 on charges of complicity and criminal conspiracy, accused of being the mastermind who ordered and paid for the car‑bomb attack.  
- On 2 September 2026 a Maltese jury found Fenech **not guilty** on both counts, making him the last of a half‑dozen defendants to be tried in the case.  
- No cameras were permitted inside the courtroom, as is customary in Maltese criminal trials.

## Immediate aftermath
- After the verdict, Fenech exited the Valletta courtroom to a chaotic scene with hecklers shouting; he entered a waiting car and made no comment.  
- Caruana Galizia’s sons were present in the courtroom but did not issue a statement.

## Family’s response
- The family posted a statement on Facebook saying the verdict “denied Caruana Galizia the justice she deserves.”  
- They called for reforms, stating that nine years after the assassination “the institutional failures that enabled her murder remain unaddressed and unreformed” and that lasting justice means no one should face similar risks again.

## Other convictions related to the murder
- **2022:** George Degiorgio and Alfred Degiorgio pleaded guilty to carrying out the attack; each received a 40‑year prison sentence.  
- **2025:** Jamie Vella and Robert Agius were convicted of complicity for supplying the bomb and were sentenced to life imprisonment, the maximum penalty.  
- **2021:** Vincent Muscat pleaded guilty for his role and was sentenced to 15 years.  
- **2019:** Taxi driver Melvin Theuma, who acted as a middleman, received a presidential pardon in exchange for testimony.

## Significance
- A 2021 independent inquiry concluded that the Maltese state bears responsibility for the assassination due to a pervasive culture of impunity at the highest levels of government.  
- The case continues to underscore the need for institutional reforms and stronger protections for journalists in Malta.
