# AI‑generated videos are already displacing actors and livestreamers across China's entertainment industry

## Main points
- Since ByteDance launched Seedance 2.0, AI‑generated digital performers can create short videos faster, cheaper, and with higher quality than human actors.  
- In Q1 2026 China released about 128 000 short dramas—three times the total for all of 2025—and 95 % of them were produced by AI, according to the China Netcasting Services Association.  
- The sector employs roughly 690 000 people directly; about 15 million people list livestreaming as their main occupation.  
- Production cost for one minute of AI video is now US $90‑$120, roughly 10 % of the cost of comparable human‑acted footage (Tsinghua professor Shen Yang, FT).  
- Some performers are compelled to “distil” their voice and likeness into AI tools before being dismissed, leading to a rise in AI‑related labor disputes over the past two‑three years.  
- Successive upgrades—Seedance 2.5 and Wan 3.0—have further boosted AI video quality, prompting more realistic replacements of actors, influencers, and livestreamers.  

## Illustrative example
- A video featured by Hubei Daily demonstrates how AI can replicate a human presenter with convincing realism; additional examples show even more lifelike results.  

## Industry impact
- The cost advantage and speed of AI production are driving rapid adoption, threatening traditional roles in acting, influencing, and livestreaming.  
- Legal and ethical challenges are emerging as workers confront forced digitisation of their personas and subsequent job loss.
