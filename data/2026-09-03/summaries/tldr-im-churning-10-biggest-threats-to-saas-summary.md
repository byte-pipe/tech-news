# “I’m Churning” | 10 Biggest Threats to SaaS  

## Overview  
- The author observes a recent rebound in SaaS stock performance but questions whether it is sustainable.  
- Drawing from personal experience with SaaS renewals in 2026, the author lists the ten most common reasons they have said “I am churning” this year.  
- Companies that can neutralize or turn these threats into advantages are likely to thrive.

## 1. Increasing AI spend squeezes the SaaS budget  
- CFOs must cut other expenses when AI budgets rise, often targeting existing SaaS spend.  
- Boards and investors are impatient with higher AI costs unless they are paired with clear revenue growth or efficiency gains.

## 2. ROI expectations are sky‑high after Claude  
- Paying twice as much for a tool compared to Claude triggers intense scrutiny of price‑to‑value ratios.  
- Buyers now demand stronger justification for every dollar spent on SaaS products.

## 3. Exponential growth in competition  
- AI enables new entrants to reach feature parity quickly.  
- Advantages for newcomers include AI‑native architecture and lower capital requirements, allowing more aggressive pricing.

## 4. Decline in switching costs  
- Implementation friction has dropped; customers no longer tolerate long, risky onboarding processes.  
- Vendors with lengthy implementations risk losing customers (“ngmi”).

## 5. Vendors expanding into each other’s territory  
- AI‑driven roadmap acceleration creates product overlap among vendors.  
- Free or low‑cost additions from larger vendors make point solutions harder to justify.

## 6. Vibecoding pressure  
- Threatening to build a solution internally (e.g., in Claude) often forces vendors to offer special pricing.  
- Early renewal conversations (4+ months out) increase the chance of securing better terms.

## 7. New pricing models feel “stupid”  
- Many vendors are experimenting with usage‑based AI pricing.  
- Buyers are skeptical and look for clear, predictable pricing that doesn’t appear to “screw” them.

## 8. AI changes what customers value  
- UI importance has dropped; interaction via AI agents is now preferred.  
- Tools that lack pre‑built AI connectors or MCPs are at risk of churn.

## 9. Vendor not accelerating product roadmap  
- Regular demos help assess whether a vendor keeps pace with AI advancements.  
- Stagnant roadmaps push customers toward more AI‑native alternatives.

## 10. AI reduces headcount/seats  
- Automation cuts the number of user seats needed, directly impacting SaaS revenue.  
- Vendors must avoid reactionary pricing changes that could alienate remaining customers.
