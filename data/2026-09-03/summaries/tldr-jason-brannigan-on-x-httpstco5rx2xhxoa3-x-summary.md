# Summary of “AI labs are the new crypto L1s”

## Core Thesis
- The author likens today’s AI labs (e.g., OpenAI, Anthropic) to the previous wave of crypto Layer‑1 (L1) protocols.  
- Both are being funded on the premise that the “winner‑take‑all” infrastructure will capture the majority of economic value, but history shows that marginal technical advantages become less decisive as the ecosystem matures.

## Valuations vs. Sustainable Moats
- OpenAI and Anthropic have near‑$1 trillion valuations based on current technical leads and massive user bases.  
- Their recent financing rounds show huge committed capital, but operating losses are widening (e.g., OpenAI’s Q2 2026 loss grew to $12.3 bn).  
- The author argues that a durable moat will depend less on raw model superiority and more on sticky products, distribution channels, and ownership of end‑to‑end workflows.

## Lessons from Crypto L1s
- Early crypto L1s raised capital on the promise of faster, cheaper infrastructure.  
- As dozens of reliable L1s and L2s emerged, marginal performance gains mattered less; users became chain‑agnostic, and value shifted to applications, liquidity, and ecosystem design.  
- Survivors like Solana and Base succeeded by turning performance into real‑world applications, not merely by being the fastest chain.

## Commoditisation of AI Capability
- Once multiple models meet the quality bar for routine tasks (email, support, coding), buyers will route work to the cheapest or fastest option.  
- Open models (e.g., DeepSeek) already demonstrate cost‑efficiency advantages over frontier models on many benchmarks.  
- The “frontier premium” will be confined to niche tasks where small capability differences materially affect outcomes (e.g., safety‑critical systems).

## Diverging Price Trends
- The price for a fixed benchmark performance has fallen 5‑10× per year (2024‑2025).  
- Conversely, the cost to run the best available model has risen 3‑18× per year, creating a market where users pay more for frontier capability while paying less for baseline intelligence.

## Routing as the New Bridge Layer
- Model routers (e.g., OpenRouter) aggregate dozens of models behind a single API, allowing per‑task model selection with a simple string edit.  
- Stripe’s planned acquisition of OpenRouter signals the strategic importance of this abstraction layer.  
- Routing enables firms to reserve expensive frontier models for a few high‑value tasks and route the bulk of work to cheaper, “good‑enough” models, reducing vendor lock‑in.

## Shift of Durable Value to Applications
- As intelligence becomes cheaper and interchangeable, the primary source of lasting value will be applications that own the customer relationship and the surrounding infrastructure.  
- Similar to crypto bridges and aggregators that abstract away chain loyalty, AI routers abstract away model loyalty, allowing multichain‑style flexibility in AI services.

## Implications for Investors
- Investing in AI labs solely on the premise of infrastructure dominance mirrors the over‑optimistic crypto L1 thesis.  
- Future returns are more likely tied to companies that embed models into sticky products, control distribution, and manage end‑to‑end workflows rather than those that merely hold the current technical edge.
