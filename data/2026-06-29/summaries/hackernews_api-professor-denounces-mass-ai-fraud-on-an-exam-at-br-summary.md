# Professor denounces mass AI fraud on an exam at Brown University: ‘Academic integrity is at risk’

## Main incident
- Professor Roberto Serrano, Harrison S. Kravis University Professor of Economics at Brown, discovered that at least 50 of the 89 students in his ECON 1170 midterm (March 5) used AI tools such as ChatGPT to answer questions.  
- The midterm, a take‑home closed‑book exam, yielded an average score of 96/100, with 40 perfect scores; graders noticed passages matching AI‑generated output.  
- Serrano warned that the final exam would be in‑person; the average then fell to 48/100, and only 59 students attended. Among the 27 absent, 22 had earned perfect midterm scores.

## Institutional response
- Initial reaction from Brown’s president and dean was silence; the dean only responded after the case was brought before the Academic Code Committee.  
- The committee sent a note calling the episode “a wake‑up call,” which Serrano deemed insufficient.  
- He argues that the university must publicly acknowledge the seriousness of AI‑driven cheating and open a broad debate.

## Proposed policy changes
- For the next academic year Serrano will:  
  - Remove weekly exercises from the final grade calculation, citing AI‑facilitated completion.  
  - Eliminate all take‑home exams, regardless of pedagogical suitability.  
  - Keep in‑person exams for high‑stakes assessment.

## Background on Professor Serrano
- Spanish‑born economist, blind since age 17 due to retinal dystrophy; learned Braille and continued his academic career.  
- Holds a bachelor’s from Universidad Complutense de Madrid, a PhD from Harvard, and has been at Brown for 34 years.  
- Recognized with awards such as the King of Spain Prize for Economics (2024) and an honorary doctorate from his alma mater.  
- Known for applying game theory to market analysis.

## Contextual events influencing the semester
- A campus shooting on December 13 2022 (two dead, nine injured) deeply affected Serrano and his students; one victim, Ella Cook, had recently met him.  
- The trauma contributed to his decision to adopt take‑home exams for that semester, aiming to reduce student anxiety.

## Serrano’s broader concerns
- He warns that academic integrity is at risk if faculty are left to combat AI fraud alone.  
- He suspects that university administration may be reluctant to act decisively because of financial ties to wealthy donors whose children could be implicated.  
- Emphasizes the need for collective action to preserve the prestige and utility of higher education.
