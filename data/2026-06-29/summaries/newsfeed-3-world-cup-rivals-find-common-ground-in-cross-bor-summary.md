# 3 World Cup rivals find 'Common Ground' in cross‑border beer  

## Overview  
- Three craft breweries—Rey Árbol (Mexico), Headlands Brewing (USA), and Cabin Brewing (Canada)—joined forces to create a single beer for the 2026 World Cup.  
- The brew is a “dry‑hopped Mexican lager” that blends each nation’s brewing traditions while each brewery uses its own label design.  

## Collaboration details  
- Development took place via video calls and emails months before the tournament.  
- All three breweries follow the identical recipe; Headlands produced about 130 cases, priced at $15.99 for a four‑pack.  
- The beer is marketed under the name **Common Ground** to emphasize connection rather than commercial gain.  

## Motivations and messages  
- Brewers describe the project as building bridges: “When I go to California or Canada, they will treat me like family,” said Alejandro Gomez (Rey Árbol).  
- They view soccer and beer as shared passions that can shrink perceived distances between the countries.  

## Geopolitical context  
- The tournament’s official narrative promotes unity, yet trade tensions over tariffs and auto‑industry standards have risen among the host nations.  
- Rising costs for aluminum cans and European hops (subject to a 15 % tariff) directly affect the breweries.  
- President Trump’s 2025 executive order hinted that cross‑border rivalry might add excitement to the event.  

## Cultural and symbolic significance  
- Scholar Andrés Martinez notes that the original 2017 joint bid highlighted strong trilateral bonds, which have since cooled.  
- The collaborative brew serves as a “bittersweet reminder” of the cooperation once envisioned.  

## Public reception  
- At Headlands Brewing’s Berkeley patio, fans watched the Mexico vs. South Africa match while enjoying the beer.  
- Soccer fan Roberto Mandujano summed up the experiment: “Three different ways, three different taste buds come together to make something cool.”  
- He downplayed political friction, saying the common love of soccer is the real common ground.
