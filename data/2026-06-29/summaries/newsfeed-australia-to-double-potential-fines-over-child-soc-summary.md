# Australia to double potential fines over child social media accounts

## Background
- Australia enacted a ban on children under 16 holding social‑media accounts, effective 10 December 2025.  
- The initial legislation passed in 2024 gave platforms more than 12 months to implement age‑restriction measures.  

## Enforcement Challenges
- Despite the ban, eSafety data in March showed that seven out of ten children with accounts on restricted platforms remained active on Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat and TikTok.  
- eSafety Commissioner Julie Inman Grant is considering court action against these platforms and YouTube for not taking reasonable steps to exclude children.  
- Platforms such as X, Kick, Reddit, Threads and Twitch were judged to be making satisfactory progress.  

## Government Response
- Communications Minister Anika Wells announced draft legislation that would double the maximum fine for non‑compliant platforms to **AU$99 million** (≈US$68 million).  
- The amendments would expand the eSafety Commissioner’s powers to:
  - Request information and documents directly from social‑media companies.  
  - Obtain data from third‑party age‑verification technology providers to verify platform claims.  
- Wells emphasized that monthly updates from eSafety show no improvement, prompting the need for stronger tools.  

## Political Reaction
- Opposition lawmaker Jane Hume (Liberal) described the original law as “undercooked” and said her party would consider supporting the reforms because the ban was not working under the current framework.  

## Reported Outcomes
- The government initially reported that more than 5 million child accounts had been removed, deactivated, or restricted after the ban took effect.  
- Ongoing monitoring indicates that the majority of children continue to hold accounts on major platforms, highlighting enforcement gaps.  

## Expected Impact
- Higher fines and enhanced oversight aim to compel platforms to enforce the under‑16 ban more effectively.  
- Strengthened powers for the eSafety Commissioner are intended to ensure compliance and allow timely legal action when necessary.
