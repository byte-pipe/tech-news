# A heat wave is expected across much of the U.S. this week

## Overview
- The National Weather Service (NWS) warns of dangerous, potentially record‑setting heat across the eastern two‑thirds of the United States through the July 4 weekend.  
- Heat indices are expected to reach 100‑110 °F in many areas, especially in Ohio, parts of North Carolina, and Washington, D.C.  
- Central and eastern states are under a Moderate to Major Heat Risk, posing health threats to the elderly, people with respiratory issues, and anyone without adequate hydration or cooling.

## Heat dome explanation
- A “heat dome” is a massive, hot air mass that becomes trapped under a high‑pressure “lid,” keeping temperatures elevated over a region.  
- Forecaster Bryan Putnam notes that temperatures in the 90s to low 100 °F combined with high humidity will make the perceived heat feel well into the 100s.  
- The dome’s effects will linger after July 4, with daytime feels of 100‑105 °F and limited nighttime relief, especially in the West.

## Health risks and recommendations
- Heat‑related illnesses include heat exhaustion, heat rash, and heat stroke; symptoms can involve nausea, headache, confusion, dizziness, slurred speech, and loss of consciousness.  
- The CDC emphasizes that heat stroke can be fatal or cause permanent disability without prompt emergency care.  
- Officials advise:
  - Limit prolonged outdoor exposure and plan activities for cooler times of day.  
  - Stay hydrated, use air conditioning, and check on vulnerable neighbors.  
  - Seek immediate medical attention if heat‑illness symptoms appear.  
  - Be especially vigilant during evening outdoor events, such as July 4 fireworks, as high humidity can keep heat indices elevated after sunset.

## Outlook
- Extreme heat warnings are in effect for parts of Iowa, Missouri, and Kansas.  
- Risks are expected to continue into the weekend and into the western United States, with limited overnight cooling.  
- Residents should prepare now for several days of high temperatures and follow public health guidance to reduce heat‑related impacts.
