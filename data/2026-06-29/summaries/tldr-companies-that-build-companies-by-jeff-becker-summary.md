# Companies That Build Companies - by Jeff Becker

# Companies Building Companies

## TL;DR
- A new class of firms sells the ability to start and run other companies using swarms of AI agents.  
- The model tolerates massive “slop” (failed or abandoned ventures) and bets on a small percentage of real winners.

## Polsia – the flagship example
- Marketed as “AI Slop” backwards; raised $30 M at a $250 M valuation with a single human founder and no employees.  
- Offers a service: for $49 / month plus 20 % of revenue, a stack of AI agents handles research, coding, payments, ads, support, and sales.  
- Claims $10 M annualized revenue after five months and 7,600 business customers.  
- Founder Ben Cera says the AI ran the fundraising process, preparing data rooms and handling diligence.  
- Reality check: Trustpilot score 2.0, users report unfinished work; “zero employees” appears partly marketing.

## The broader cohort
- YC‑backed “Thomas”: an AI‑driven virtual founder that creates and runs its own companies, keeping profits while the human behind it now only signs legal documents.  
- Other YC companies build AI‑run consumer brands, AI‑only consulting firms, and more.  
- The emerging category: companies whose product *is* the ability to spin up other companies.

## Business model mechanics
- Analogous to Shopify: millions of stores, but only a tiny fraction succeed; the platform thrives on the few that do.  
- Expect ~95 % of AI‑spun ventures to be “slop” – abandoned drafts, half‑shipped products, forgotten subscriptions.  
- The model’s profit comes from the 5 % that become viable, scalable businesses; the slop is the cost of distribution.  
- Starting a company (paperwork, landing page, first build, payment integration) is becoming cheap and instant, shifting competitive advantage to the human layer: creativity, deep problem understanding, relationship building.

## Implications for founders and investors
- Founders must focus on the hard 5 %: obsessiveness, work ethic, first‑principles thinking, durability.  
- Investors are betting on the platform itself, underwriting the small slice of winners while tolerating the large slice of failures.  
- The platform will continue to get faster, cheaper, and more powerful, but it cannot yet replace the uniquely human elements that create “legendary” companies.

## Takeaway
- The rise of AI‑run “company‑building” platforms is inevitable and accelerating.  
- Success will still be defined by the few human‑driven ventures that break through the noise.  
- Watching the 5 % that truly matters remains the real story.
