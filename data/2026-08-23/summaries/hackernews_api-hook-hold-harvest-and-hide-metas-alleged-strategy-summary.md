# Hook, hold, harvest and hide: Meta’s alleged strategy laid out in first week of landmark trial  

## Overview  
- The trial began in Oakland, California, with California and 28 other states suing Meta for alleged harmful design of its platforms.  
- Prosecutors describe Meta’s business model in four steps: “hook” users, “hold” them, “harvest” their data, and “hide” the truth.  

## Allegations  
- The states claim Meta designed addictive products that especially harm children.  
- Federal child‑privacy laws and state consumer‑protection laws were allegedly violated by collecting data on users under 13 without parental consent.  
- The lawsuit seeks damages up to $200 billion and demands redesign of Meta’s products to make them safer for children.  

## Legal Proceedings  
- Eight jurors heard opening statements from state attorneys and Meta’s lawyers.  
- The jury’s role is advisory; Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers will issue the final verdict and damages.  
- The trial is expected to last six to eight weeks and is part of thousands of similar lawsuits across the United States.  

## Key Testimonies  
- **Arturo Béjar**, former Meta safety engineer, testified about his daughter’s negative experiences on Instagram and described a “don’t ask, don’t tell” approach to child safety.  
- Béjar presented an email to Mark Zuckerberg showing that 51 % of teen respondents reported harmful experiences, yet only 0.02 % of offending content was removed.  
- Former user‑experience researchers Elena Davis and Natalie Troxel gave recorded depositions.  
- Psychology professor Jean Twenge testified briefly, with further testimony scheduled.  

## Potential Outcomes  
- If found liable, Meta could face up to $200 billion in damages, roughly equal to its projected 2025 revenue.  
- The court could order permanent changes to Meta’s product design, affecting how Facebook, Instagram and other services operate.  

## Meta’s Response  
- Meta denies all allegations, with spokesperson Liza Crenshaw calling the states’ payout demands “outlandish.”  
- Lead attorney Paul Schmidt acknowledged social‑media challenges but emphasized Meta’s tools to address them and noted the company blocks accounts of users under 13, having disabled over one million such accounts.  
- Meta unsuccessfully attempted to prevent Béjar from testifying, arguing his credibility was inflated.
