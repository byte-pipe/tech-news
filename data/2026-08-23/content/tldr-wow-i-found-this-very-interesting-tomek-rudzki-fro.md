description: 'Wow, I found this VERY interesting: Tomek Rudzki from Peec AI just shared some brand new data with me regarding ChatGPT''s evolving search behavior with the launch of 5.6. Earlier this week, I published a Substack about changes I’ve been seeing in ChatGPT’s fan-out queries (link in comments). One hypothesis I raised was that ChatGPT may be adjusting its search behavior to mitigate some of the spammy, GEO-focused content that has flooded the search results - content built specifically to win AI citations and brand mentions. The new data Tomek shared with me appears to support this theory. For one, we’re not only seeing a huge surge in site: searches and modifiers like “official.” Peec’s data also shows which terms have declined the most as a share of total fan-out queries: - vs - comparison - top - best - reviews My first thought when I saw that list: these look *exactly* like the page types companies have been building (often at scale) to try to win AI citations and brand mentions
  in the past year or two. (I’ve been sharing examples of this in my talks all year.) There are two primary types of pages I''ve seen companies scaling this year to drive AI search visibility: 1. Listicles — “Top 10 [brands],” “8 best [products],” etc. 2. Comparison pages — “[company X] vs. [company Y],” “[brand] comparison,” “[company] alternatives,” etc. Notice how the terms included in those page types are *all the same terms* that dramatically dropped off in ChatGPT''s fan-outs? But wait, there''s more: Tomek also shared with me how different page types are appearing in citations since ChatGPT 5.6 was released. Two huge developments: Fall of listicles The share of citations classified as LISTICLE dropped from 15.77% (PRE) to 7.80% (POST), a -50.5% relative decline. Fall of comparisons The share of citations classified as COMPARISON dropped from 9.08% (PRE) to 6.17% (POST), a -32.1% relative decline. ChatGPT is issuing fewer fan-out queries containing terms like “best,” “top,” “vs,” and
  “comparison," and, at the same time, listicles and comparison pages are making up a dramatically smaller share of its citations. I see this as further evidence that ChatGPT is changing its retrieval behavior in ways that reduce the visibility of some of the content formats that have been most heavily exploited for GEO. Some methodology notes: * Share of total is the primary metric here. It answers: “What percentage of ChatGPT’s current search/citation activity does this represent?” It’s the same basic framing as asking what percentage of AI answers mention a brand, except applied to query terms and URL categories. * Per-chat is a diagnostic metric, not the headline number. Peec uses it to understand why share moved: did the underlying behavior actually change, or was the category simply diluted because the overall query pool got larger? *The second image is an AI-generated image that visualizes Peec AI''s new data but was not made by Peec! | 67 comments on LinkedIn'
