# Ultra-Pure Quantum Crystals from an Abandoned Mine in the Atacama Desert

## Introduction
- I am Dr. Aaron Breidenbach, a former Stanford PhD student who grew Zn‑Barlowite and Herbertsmithite crystals, candidates for the quantum spin liquid (QSL) state.  
- Recent Nature Physics paper (with Young Lee’s Lab) provides the strongest evidence yet for QSL in these materials, suggesting huge potential for future quantum computers.  
- Unlike most quantum‑active crystals, Herbertsmithite also occurs naturally—a rare anomaly, possibly existing in the crust for millions of years.  
- This post is the first of a two‑part series: the first focuses on scientific promise, the second on ecological destruction caused by mining in the Atacama Desert.  

## The Discovery and the Promise
- Together with anthropologist Vicente Carrasola Vega, I located natural Herbertsmithite crystals in the waste tailings of the abandoned San Francisco mine (Sierra Gorda, Chile).  
- Identity confirmed by X‑ray scattering with Prof. Joseline Tapia’s team at Universidad Católica del Norte.  
- Visual estimate: at least 10 g of hexagonal green crystals, likely dominated by Herbertsmithite (~65 % by composition), plus several grams of Atacamite and Zn‑Paratacamite.  
- Laboratory synthesis is labor‑intensive: ~1 week prep, 9 months growth, $10 k equipment, $100 chemicals per attempt, yields 1–2 g per tube with ~45 % success. Our field find yields roughly ten times that amount using only $15 pickaxes.  

### Purity Advantage
- Electron microprobe analysis by the late Michael Scott (University of Arizona) shows natural crystals have a Cu:Zn ratio of 2.98:1.02, much closer to the ideal 3:1 than synthetic crystals (3.15:0.85).  
- Higher purity reduces magnetic impurities that occupy Zn spacer layers, preserving the two‑dimensional Kagome lattice essential for QSL behavior.  
- Reduced impurity levels could eliminate the magnetic “talk” between layers that many physicists believe destroys or masks the QSL state.  

## Implications for Quantum Physics
- Cleaner natural Herbertsmithite offers a more faithful platform to study QSL phenomena, potentially accelerating the development of quantum technologies.  
- The abundance and low‑cost extraction method could democratize access to high‑quality quantum materials, contrasting sharply with the expensive, low‑yield laboratory routes.  

## Outlook
- Ongoing work will separate and quantify the crystals, perform detailed magnetic measurements, and compare their performance to synthetic counterparts.  
- The second part of this series will address the severe ecological impact of mining activities threatening these rare natural quantum crystals.
