# As WHO sounds alarm over Ebola in DRC, what can be learned from previous outbreaks?

## Background
- The current Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) is caused by the Bundibugyo variant, a rare strain previously seen only in 2007 and 2012.
- From 2018 to 2020, the DRC experienced its largest Ebola outbreak in the northern Kivu province, marked by conflict, political tension, and community mistrust.
- Social practices such as close contact with the dead and the use of bodily fluids for traditional rites accelerated transmission in eastern Kivu and Ituri provinces.

## Differences in the current outbreak
- No licensed vaccine or specific therapeutic exists for the Bundibugyo variant, unlike the 2018‑2020 outbreak where a vaccine was available.
- The World Health Organization (WHO) declared a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) within 48 hours of detection, a stark contrast to the four‑month delay in 2018.
- Confirmed cases have already been reported in both the DRC and neighboring Uganda, prompting rapid international attention.

## Lessons from the 2018‑2020 outbreak
- **Conflict and insecurity**: Armed attacks on health workers and Ebola clinics disrupted response efforts and heightened community fear.
- **Mistrust and misinformation**: Political actors exploited the crisis, spreading denialist narratives and accusing outsiders of introducing the virus.
- **Population mobility**: Displacement and cross‑border movement complicated surveillance, contact tracing, and delivery of care.
- **Vaccination challenges**: Even with a vaccine, insecurity and mistrust repeatedly hampered vaccination campaigns.

## Challenges specific to the Bundibugyo variant
- The strain is the least well‑known of the three Ebola virus species and has a case‑fatality rate of about 30 %.
- No vaccine is in late‑stage clinical development that can be deployed quickly.
- Early cases may have been missed, allowing undetected transmission for several weeks before formal recognition.

## WHO response and the PHEIC declaration
- WHO Director‑General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus acted without convening an emergency committee, citing the rapid spread and cross‑border risk.
- The PHEIC aims to mobilise international resources, coordinate response, and signal the global nature of the threat.
- The last Ebola‑related PHEIC was declared in July 2019 during the 2018‑2020 DRC epidemic after it reached the urban centre of Goma.

## Expert commentary
- Daniela Manno (London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine) highlighted ongoing transmission before detection, insecurity, displacement, and the lack of a vaccine as major hurdles.
- Anne Cori (Imperial College London) noted that the international spread to Uganda likely accelerated the swift PHEIC declaration.

## Outlook
- The DRC now has stronger outbreak‑response capacity than a decade ago, built from experience with previous epidemics.
- Continued challenges include maintaining community trust, ensuring the safety of health workers, and finding effective medical countermeasures for the Bundibugyo variant.
