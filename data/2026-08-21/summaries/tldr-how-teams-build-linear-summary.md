# How teams build – Linear

## AI adoption by function
- From Jan 2026 to Jun 2026 the proportion of users who used Linear AI features doubled in every role.  
- Fastest growth was in Product (12 % → 34 %, +22 pp); Go‑to‑Market rose from 5 % to 18 % (+13 pp).  
- Overall active AI users (last 30 days) in Jan 2026: Founder 14 %, Engineering 12 %, Product 12 %, Design 6 %, GTM 5 %.  
- By Jun 2026: Founder 30 %, Engineering 30 %, Product 34 %, Design 22 %, GTM 18 %.

## Adoption by executive team
- Executives match or exceed their teams’ AI usage.  
- CEOs in companies with ≥ 201 employees jumped from 9 % to 36 % (+27 pp).  
- CPOs rose from 3 % to 24 % (+21 pp); CTOs from 11 % to 35 % (+24 pp).  
- Founders across all sizes increased from ~15 % to 31‑36 % (≈+16 pp).

## Adoption by company size
- AI usage roughly tripled regardless of organization size.  
- 1‑50 FTE: 8 % → 23 % (+14 pp)  
- 51‑200 FTE: 9 % → 25 % (+16 pp)  
- 201‑1000 FTE: 9 % → 27 % (+19 pp)  
- ≥ 1001 FTE: 8 % → 25 % (+17 pp)

## Time spent in Linear (June 2025 vs June 2026)
- **Create & triage**: Engineering +5 m, Design +3 m, GTM +4 m, Founders +17 m (Product –1 m).  
- **Assign & update**: Engineering +3 m, Design +3 m, GTM +3 m, Founders +7 m (Product 0).  
- **Comment**: Engineering +5 m, Product +1 m, Design +2 m, GTM +6 m, Founders +26 m.  
- Average minutes per user rose across all functions, indicating more coordination and context for AI agents.

## AI‑generated issue creation
- Two years ago AI authored <0.1 % of issues; by mid‑2026 AI writes just under 50 % of new issues.  
- Weekly issue volume (thousands) shows AI agents (Agents & MCP) growing from 0 to >1 k per week, while human‑generated issues remain around 600‑750 k per week.  
- At the current growth rate AI‑authored issues are projected to surpass combined human and integration contributions soon.

## Overall insight
- AI adoption is widespread across roles, seniority levels, and company sizes.  
- Increased AI use correlates with higher time spent on creation, triage, assignment, and commenting, suggesting AI is becoming a core part of the product‑development workflow.  
- The shift toward AI‑authored issues marks a fundamental change in how software teams generate and manage work items within Linear.
