# ARR Doesn't Mean What It Used To – Summary  

## Main insight  
- Venture capitalists increasingly distrust ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue) because the term now covers many inconsistent definitions and often does not reflect true recurring revenue.  

## Why ARR is unreliable  
- **Varied definitions** – could be revenue, GMV, quarterly revenue multiplied by 4, or daily revenue annualized.  
- **Non‑recurring nature** – many fast‑growing AI startups have revenue that spikes in a year but isn’t durable.  
- **Lack of clarity** – investors want founders to state explicitly how ARR is calculated (actual recurring revenue vs. annualized figure).  

## Contracted/Committed ARR (CARR) concerns  
- Seen as an even weaker proxy for real revenue; signed bookings may not convert due to onboarding delays, customer churn, or unpredictable implementation timelines.  
- Some investors prefer live ARR and direct customer validation over contracted numbers.  

## Pricing model complications  
- **Blended recurring + consumption pricing** – annualizing consumption revenue before it stabilizes is misleading, especially for PLG/consumer AI products.  
- **Capital‑intensive AI infrastructure** – headline ARR can hide cash‑flow issues caused by financing, equipment leases, or revenue‑sharing deals; investors look for free‑cash‑flow after financing costs.  
- **Lumpy, month‑specific spikes** – annualizing a single hot month inflates ARR for AI‑native services.  

## Gross margin pitfalls  
- Compute‑heavy, usage‑based products often show high gross margins only because variable costs (e.g., compute) are hidden in operating expenses or assumed low usage.  
- Mis‑allocation of Front‑End Development/Integration (FDE) costs can inflate reported margins; true margins may drop sharply once product usage scales.  

## Net Revenue Retention (NRR) skepticism  
- Companies may boost NRR by counting small POCs that later become contracted ARR, creating an apples‑to‑oranges comparison with traditional NRR calculations.  

## Structural shift in metric‑based investing  
- In the AI era, traditional benchmarks (growth, NRR, gross margin, burn) are less reliable; investors rely more on qualitative judgments about durability and business model resilience.  

## Takeaway for founders  
- Clearly define and explain any ARR‑related metric on the first slide.  
- Emphasize live, recurring revenue and realistic usage assumptions.  
- Be transparent about cost structures and cash‑conversion dynamics.  

These points reflect the collective view of 15 VCs who no longer trust ARR and related metrics at face value.
