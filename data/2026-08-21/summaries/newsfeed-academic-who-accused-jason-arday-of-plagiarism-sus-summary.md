# Academic who accused Jason Arday of plagiarism suspended by university

## Main events
- Nathan Cofnas, a postdoctoral researcher at Ghent University who describes himself as a “race realist,” was suspended after the university opened a preliminary disciplinary investigation for “discriminating against” the late Cambridge professor Jason Arday.  
- The suspension was described as a precautionary measure; the university reiterated its commitment to human dignity and its stance against discrimination, hatred, and racism.

## Background of the plagiarism dispute
- Cofnas claimed to have run Arday’s work through plagiarism‑detection software, finding many passages copied with minimal editing and even retaining original copy‑editing mistakes.  
- A retired professor had previously raised similar concerns, prompting two journals to issue correction notes on Arday’s articles.  
- Cofnas also questioned Arday’s public statements about extraordinary marathon and charity‑fundraising feats (e.g., 30 marathons in 35 days, 300 miles in three days, 600 miles on a treadmill in six days).

## Jason Arday’s situation
- Arday, who became Cambridge’s youngest Black professor in 2023, faced weeks of media scrutiny over the plagiarism allegations and the marathon claims.  
- He denied the accusations, resigned from his professorship a week before his death, and was found dead in Battersea, south London, on 14 August 2026.

## Cofnas’s prior controversies
- Cofnas lost a position at a Cambridge college in 2024 after backlash to a statement that, under a “true meritocracy,” Black people would “disappear from almost all high‑profile positions outside of sports and entertainment.”

## University’s response
- Rector Petra De Sutter and Vice‑Rector Herwig Reynaert said the recent public statements were taken “very seriously” and that “appropriate action” had been taken.  
- They emphasized the importance of academic freedom paired with responsibility, noting that freedom is not unlimited and may be restricted to protect others’ rights.  
- The university called for reflection on how academics treat one another, especially when individuals become subjects of public controversy.
