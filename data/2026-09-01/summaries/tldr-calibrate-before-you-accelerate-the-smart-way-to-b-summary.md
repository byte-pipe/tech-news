# Calibrate Before You Accelerate: The Smart Way to Bias Toward Action in a New Role | Joshua Tucker

# Summary of “Calibrate Before You Accelerate: The Smart Way to Bias Toward Action in a New Role”

## Overview
- Starting a new job often triggers a strong urge to prove oneself immediately.  
- Action is valuable only when it is informed by context; otherwise it becomes noise.  
- The article proposes a three‑phase approach to channel the bias toward action responsibly.

## Phase 1: The Collection Period
- Treat deliberate listening as an active effort.  
- **Map the terrain:** Identify key stakeholders and observe team dynamics before proposing changes.  
- **Investigate the “why”:** Apply Chesterton’s Fence—don’t discard a legacy process until you understand its purpose.  
- **Gather data:** Review historical documents, shadow peers, and hold discovery‑focused 1:1 meetings.

## Phase 2: The Synthesis Phase
- Bridge information gathering and execution through analysis.  
- **Connect the dots:** Spot recurring pain points mentioned by multiple stakeholders.  
- **Categorize opportunities:** Separate quick, low‑risk wins from complex, systemic issues that need long‑term strategies.

## Phase 3: Strategic Acceleration
- Deploy the bias toward action safely and effectively.  
- **Start small and public:** Deliver a quick win that eases someone else’s work, building political capital.  
- **Share your hypothesis:** Draft a one‑pager outlining the planned action and solicit feedback before launching a major project.  
- **Shift gears:** Gradually move from a 90 % listening / 10 % doing ratio to 20 % listening / 80 % doing.

## Takeaway
- The goal isn’t to move slowly, but to ensure that every action pushes on a genuine need.  
- In a new environment, resist the impulse to fix everything immediately; first study the “blueprints,” then pick up the “sledgehammer” when ready.
