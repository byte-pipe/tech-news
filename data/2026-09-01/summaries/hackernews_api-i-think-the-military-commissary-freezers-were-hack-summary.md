# I Think The Military Commissary’s Freezers Were Hacked

## Overview
- Multiple U.S. military installations reported simultaneous refrigeration failures in August 2026.  
- The Pentagon has acknowledged a “possible refrigeration disruption” at several Defense Commissary Agency (DeCA) sites.  
- The author suspects a cyber‑related cause but has no direct evidence of a hack.

## Fort Huachuca Case Study
- Official Facebook post (Aug 27) reported an overnight equipment failure that forced **all** commissary freezers into defrost mode, spoiling the contents.  
- The base clarified that power did **not** outage; the freezers actively heated during defrost.  
- An unverified comment suggested a “network issue” and noted that the newer refrigeration system is remotely controlled through DeCA.

## DeCA and the Remote Monitoring Control System (RMCS)
- DeCA runs ~235 commissaries worldwide; refrigeration is not locally isolated.  
- In March 2026 DeCA issued a procurement for “Facilities Maintenance, Call Center Support, and Remote Monitoring Control System (RMCS) Management” covering ~182 locations, including all sites on the author’s list.  
- RMCS documentation states: **“Defrost shall be controlled through the RMCS.”**  
- Contracts describe RMCS units at each commissary with 24/7 remote alarm monitoring and a “master control system” located in the continental United States.  
- The extent of remote command (e.g., ability to trigger defrost at multiple sites simultaneously) is not publicly detailed.

## Possible Explanations & Implications
- **Aging equipment** could have failed in a coordinated manner, but the uniform entry into defrost mode is atypical for independent mechanical failures.  
- **Network‑based manipulation** is technically feasible given RMCS remote control capabilities; a malicious actor could potentially issue a defrost command across multiple sites.  
- If a cyber incident is responsible, the impact extends beyond spoiled food to concerns about broader DoD infrastructure security and supply‑chain resilience.  
- The author emphasizes the lack of concrete proof of a hack while highlighting the need for further investigation into the RMCS architecture and its security posture.
