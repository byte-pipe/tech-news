## Cells and Genomes
- Cells are the fundamental units of life; every cell contains a genome encoded in DNA.
- Genes are the “recipes” that encode proteins, the functional molecules of the cell.
- **Bakery analogy**: the genome is a master recipe book (~20,000 recipes); proteins are the cakes produced. Humans have two copies of each recipe, and different cell types bake different assortments of cakes.

## A Mental Model for DNA
- DNA can be imagined as a ~3 billion‑character string composed of the letters A, C, T, and G.
- The molecule consists of two complementary strands; bases pair uniquely (A‑T, G‑C) forming a double‑helix resembling a spiral staircase.
- During cell division the helix unwinds, strands separate, and each serves as a template for accurate replication, introducing very few variations.

## Physical Structure
- DNA is packaged into large sequences called chromosomes, which reside in the nucleus.
- Chromosomes are paired (one from each parent) and wrapped around histone proteins for compact storage and gene regulation.
- Humans normally have 22 pairs of autosomes (numbered by size) plus one pair of sex chromosomes (XX or XY), totaling 23 chromosome pairs.

## Conclusion
- The genome is a vast biochemical database that, when accessed correctly, reveals how bodies function and differ.
- Natural genetic variation underlies individual traits; adverse mutations can cause cancer and other genetic diseases.
- Linking genotype to phenotype enables clinicians to develop personalized treatments based on an individual’s genetic makeup.
