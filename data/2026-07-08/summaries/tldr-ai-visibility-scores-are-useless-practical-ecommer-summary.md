# AI Visibility Scores Are Useless

## Main Critique of Visibility Scores
- AI visibility‑tracking tools such as Profound and Peec AI assign scores based on how often a business appears in responses to specific prompts.  
- Scores are entirely dependent on the prompts and can be easily manipulated (e.g., embedding the business name in the prompt yields a 100 % visibility score).  
- These scores do not reflect what consumers actually search for or enter.

## More Useful AI Tracking Approaches

### Most cited domains
- Instead of chasing every citation, analyze citations across multiple generative‑AI platforms.  
- Focus on:  
  * User‑generated content and social media platforms that influence AI answers.  
  * Publications trusted by large language models.  
  * Competitors that are frequently cited for benchmarking.

### Competitor citations
- AI tracking can reveal content gaps beyond keyword gaps.  
- Identify competitors that effectively address shopper concerns and locate their pages in AI responses.  
- Use these insights to craft a content strategy that directly answers the target audience’s questions.

### Cited but not visible
- “Invisible citations” are links shown without naming the source; they tend to receive few clicks.  
- “Visible citations” name the brand and have a stronger impact on buying decisions.  
- Invisible citations represent opportunities to add branded content so generative AI can display the brand name alongside the link.

### Branded prompts
- Supplying generative‑AI platforms with clear, brand‑specific prompts is the key to visibility.  
- Example prompts to test:  
  * What is [your business name]?  
  * [Your business name] versus [competitor’s name].  
  * [Your business name] reviews and complaints.  
  * Is [your business name] trustworthy?

## Takeaway
- Visibility scores are misleading and can be gamed.  
- Prioritize metrics that reflect real consumer queries, analyze cross‑platform citations, and use branded prompts to improve generative‑AI visibility.
