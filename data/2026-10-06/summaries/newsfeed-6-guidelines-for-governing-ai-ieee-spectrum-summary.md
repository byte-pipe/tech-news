# Summary of “6 Guidelines for Governing AI”

## Guideline 1 – Keep Humans in the Loop  
- Design AI systems so that critical decisions involve human judgment.  
- Provide operators with clear, actionable information about the AI’s reasoning.  
- Implement override mechanisms that allow humans to halt or modify AI actions when needed.

## Guideline 2 – Ensure Transparency and Explainability  
- Document model architecture, training data, and performance metrics.  
- Offer understandable explanations for AI outputs, especially in high‑stakes contexts.  
- Make documentation accessible to stakeholders, regulators, and end‑users.

## Guideline 3 – Promote Fairness and Mitigate Bias  
- Conduct regular bias audits across demographic groups.  
- Use diverse, representative datasets and apply techniques to reduce disparate impact.  
- Establish governance processes to review and remediate unfair outcomes.

## Guideline 4 – Prioritize Safety and Reliability  
- Test AI systems under a wide range of scenarios, including edge cases.  
- Deploy monitoring tools that detect anomalies, drift, or degradation over time.  
- Define clear safety thresholds and contingency plans for failure modes.

## Guideline 5 – Protect Privacy and Data Governance  
- Apply data minimization, encryption, and anonymization where feasible.  
- Enforce strict access controls and audit trails for data handling.  
- Align data practices with relevant regulations (e.g., GDPR, CCPA).

## Guideline 6 – Establish Accountability and Oversight  
- Assign clear responsibility for AI decisions to individuals or teams.  
- Create independent review boards or ethics committees to evaluate AI deployments.  
- Maintain records of model versions, changes, and impact assessments for auditability.
