# AI glasses face their first major government crackdown – Ars Technica

## Background
- Norway becomes the first major nation to propose a temporary ban on AI‑glasses in selected public places.
- Targeted locations include parks, beaches, schools, and kindergartens.
- The move follows growing privacy worries about wearable tech that can record people without consent.

## Government Action
- Norway’s centre‑left government will introduce a draft law to parliament soon, outlining a temporary ban.
- An expert group will be created to develop permanent national regulations.
- Digitalisation minister Torgeir Micaelsen emphasized the need to prevent hidden photography, audio recording, and filming that make public spaces feel unsafe.
- The ban is described as a “high priority” but not a total prohibition; appropriate use where no consent risk exists is still allowed.

## Domestic Reactions
- Oslo schools have already banned AI glasses.
- Equinor, Norway’s largest oil and gas company, prohibits them in offices and offshore facilities.

## International Context
- Australia is reviewing a similar ban for certain public spaces.
- Several countries have banned smart glasses in courtrooms.
- Global debate continues on how to regulate devices from Meta, Google, and cheaper copy‑cat models.

## Industry Perspective
- US tech firms (Meta, Google, OpenAI) view smart glasses as a future primary interface for AI, potentially replacing smartphones.
- Counterpoint Research projects consumer spending on wearables to exceed $1 trillion between 2026 and 2032.

## Privacy Concerns
- Critics label the technology “pervert technology” due to fears of covert recording, especially of children and women in private settings such as changing rooms.
- The Norwegian proposal aims to give time for thorough assessment and informed public debate before permanent rules are set.
