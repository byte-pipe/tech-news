# Summary of “The lamps in my house”

## Introduction
- The author is passionate about designer lamps, especially vintage pieces, and shares them on X and Instagram.  
- Designed a lamp personally and decided to give a tour of the lamps owned.

## Collecting perspective
- The lamps are expensive; they may not be worth the price objectively, but they are priceless to the author.  
- Collecting becomes a hobby distinct from simply using a lamp for illumination.  
- Modern, well‑designed lamps are available at low cost (e.g., IKEA).  
- Vintage stores and fairs can provide good deals on many of the featured pieces.

## Lamp collection (no particular order)

### Artemide Tolomeo Mini
- Designed by Michele De Lucchi & Giancarlo Fassina, 1987; won Compasso d’Oro in 1989.  
- Iconic, widely copied desk lamp; simple, portable, excellent for work desks.  
- Author created a custom adaptor to attach it to a USM Haller desk.

### Tizio Micro
- Designed by Richard Sapper, 1972; Sapper also created the first IBM ThinkPad and Alessi’s 9090 espresso maker.  
- Arms conduct electricity, balanced by counterweights; moves with one finger and stays in place.  
- Small red details are Sapper’s signature; the Micro is a compact version of the original Tizio.

### Akari 1A
- Made of traditional Japanese washi paper; designed by Isamu Noguchi in 1951.  
- Provides a soft, warm glow; considered more a sculptural object than a lamp.  

### Akari 3A
- Taller version of the 1A (56 cm), with wooden trim at top and bottom.  
- Best placed on a low table, sideboard, or cabinet due to its intermediate size.

### Akari 16A
- First Akari purchased; moved from office to bedroom.  
- Hand‑made at Ozeki workshop in Gifu, Japan, using traditional methods.

### Akari 75A
- Largest Akari in the house (≈75 cm across); fills the room with presence.  
- Authentic pieces carry a small red sun‑and‑moon stamp; Noguchi designed over 100 Akari models.

### Louis Poulsen PH 5
- Designed by Poul Henningsen, 1958; one of the most famous Danish lamps.  
- Soft light without direct view of the bulb; “5” refers to the 50 cm shade; red and blue inserts warm the light.  
- Notable appearances: film *Another Round* and Dieter Rams’ home.

### Louis Poulsen PH 3/2
- First lamp to use Henningsen’s three‑shade system (1925); won a gold medal at the Paris exhibition.  
- Mouth‑blown glass, shiny exterior, matte interior; fragile (author broke one and waited months for a replacement).

### AJ Wall Lamp
- Designed by Arne Jacobsen, 1957 for the SAS Royal Hotel in Copenhagen.  
- Tiltable; can direct light toward a wall for indirect illumination or toward a book for reading.  
- Related cutlery design appeared in *2001: A Space Odyssey*.

### Lampe de Bureau (Jean Prouvé)
- Designed in 1930 for student residences in Nancy, France.  
- Constructed from bent sheet steel; exhibits a brutal, engineered aesthetic.  
- Available today from Vitra in original colors.

### Chispa (Marset)
- Designed by Joan Gaspar, 2020; inspired by vintage garage lamps with metal cages.  
- Rechargeable, water‑resistant, three light levels; used both indoors and in the garden.

### Kismas Lamp 01
- Created by the Lithuanian studio Kismas; “Kismas” means change or transformation.  
- Incorporates real Soviet‑era glass blocks; includes optional film/theater filters for color changes (considered cumbersome).  
- Artistic focus, less functional.

### Tokio Lamp
- Designed by Shigeaki Asahara, 1980 for Italian company Stilnovo; mailbox‑shaped shade.  
- Revived in 2020 by the Danish brand “Please Wait to be Seated.”  
- Transported from Denmark to Germany, then to Ankara, Turkey.

### Vision 20/20 Floor
- Produced by DCW éditions, Paris; named after the eye‑test chart.  
- Base slides under a sofa; features two lights—one upward, one downward.
