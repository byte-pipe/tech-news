# A Guided Tour of Donald Trump’s Renovated Washington, DC | WIRED

## Union Station  
- Union Station’s surrounding Columbus Circle was renovated with an $11.8 million upgrade, restoring fountains and cleaning marble sculptures.  
- Homeless encampments were cleared by the National Guard, who later moved to the Reflecting Pool.  
- The improvements have been positively received by commuters and local staff.

## Department of Justice and Labor Banners  
- Large Trump‑face banners (≈ $6,000 each) now hang on the Labor Department and Justice Department buildings, the latter displaying the slogan “Make America Safe Again.”  
- The banners symbolize Trump’s push to dominate federal agencies, including the DOJ that previously indicted him.

## The White House  
- Ongoing construction on the East Wing includes a ballroom, an underground bunker, and a planned rooftop drone port.  
- Project costs have risen from $200 million to $400 million, with Trump requesting up to $1 billion from Congress; funds have been re‑appropriated from the Secret Service budget.  
- The site is obscured by temporary staff parking, a massive American flag, and a black‑granite colonnade renovation costing about $700,000 to taxpayers.

## The Reflecting Pool  
- A $14.2 million renovation painted the pool floor “American‑flag blue,” but the work suffered algae growth, sealant tears, and peeling paint.  
- National Guard troops patrol the area and are ordered to detain anyone who touches the water, with potential vandalism charges.

## “Arc de Trump”  
- A proposed 250‑foot triumphal arch with gilt statues, including a 60‑foot Lady Liberty, will sit on Memorial Bridge, blocking the view of Arlington House.  
- The arch is intended to include an observation deck but faces strong public opposition (52 % against, 21 % in favor).

## Kennedy Center and United States Institute of Peace  
- The US Institute of Peace now bears Trump’s name after the agency was shuttered and staff fired.  
- The Kennedy Center’s Trump signage was ordered removed by a federal judge; a tarp currently covers the former name, with removal still pending.
