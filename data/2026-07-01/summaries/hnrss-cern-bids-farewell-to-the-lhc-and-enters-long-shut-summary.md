# CERN bids farewell to the LHC and enters Long Shutdown 3

## Overview
- The Large Hadron Collider (LHC) has been switched off after its final physics run to start CERN’s Long Shutdown 3 (LS3).
- LS3 is a comprehensive programme of maintenance, consolidation, and upgrades that will prepare the accelerator for the High‑Luminosity LHC (HiLumi LHC), slated to start operation in 2030.
- The shutdown involves dismantling 1.2 km of accelerator components and installing new HiLumi equipment, including a new cryogenic line delivered last spring.

## Historical achievements of the LHC
- First beams circulated in September 2008; first proton collisions in 2009.
- Delivered three operational periods (Runs 1–3) with unprecedented data volumes.
- Most celebrated result: discovery of the Higgs boson on 4 July 2012 by ATLAS and CMS.
- Additional contributions: discovery of >85 hadrons, exclusion limits on new particles, studies of matter‑antimatter asymmetry, quark‑gluon plasma research, and measurements relevant to astrophysics.
- Stimulated advances in accelerator science, superconducting technology, computing, and international collaboration.

## HiLumi LHC goals
- Increase luminosity up to ten times the original design.
- Enable much larger data sets for precision Higgs studies and searches for physics beyond the Standard Model.
- Expected to begin physics runs around 2030, with a gradual restart of the accelerator complex from 2028.

## Scope of Long Shutdown 3
- Largest intervention on CERN’s accelerator complex since the LHC construction.
- Involves thousands of specialists from CERN and partner institutes worldwide.
- Key projects:
  - Removal and replacement of 1.2 km of LHC magnets and components.
  - Consolidation of the Super Proton Synchrotron (SPS) North Area.
  - Dismantling of the CERN Neutrinos to Gran Sasso (CNGS) target area.
  - Conversion of Experimental Cavern North 3 (ECN3) into a high‑intensity fixed‑target facility.
  - Renovation of the ISOLDE facility.
  - Upgrades to personnel safety systems, electrical network, and technical galleries.

## Upgrades to ATLAS and CMS
- Complete replacement of trigger systems to handle 140–200 proton‑proton collisions per bunch crossing (vs. ~60 in the last run).
- Installation of new detector technologies:
  - All‑silicon tracking systems with billions of readout channels.
  - High‑precision timing detectors (tens of picoseconds resolution).
  - Calorimeter systems capable of megahertz‑rate operation.
- Aim: select the most interesting events from >5 billion interactions each second.

## Scientific activity during LS3
- No circulating beams, but data analysis continues on the large LHC datasets.
- Thousands of researchers extract new physics results while preparing for the upgraded experiments.

## Outlook
- The HiLumi LHC will build on the LHC legacy, offering unprecedented opportunities to deepen our understanding of the Universe.
- The transition marks both a farewell to the LHC as it has been known and the beginning of a new era in high‑energy physics.
