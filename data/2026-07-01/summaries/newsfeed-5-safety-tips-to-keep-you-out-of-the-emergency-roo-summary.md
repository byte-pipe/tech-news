# 5 safety tips to keep you out of the emergency room this summer

## 1. Learn five basic water‑safety skills  
- Be comfortable jumping in, submerging fully, and surfacing.  
- Float or tread water for at least one minute while staying relaxed.  
- Turn around in the water and locate an exit; exiting is as important as entering.  
- Swim 25 yards to an exit without stopping, using any stroke you prefer.  
- Exit the water without a ladder, using the “elbow, elbow, tummy, knee, knee” technique taught by Olympic swimmer Cullen Jones.

## 2. Designate a “water‑watcher”  
- Assign a sober adult to monitor swimmers and keep a phone nearby for emergencies.  
- Rotate the water‑watcher every 15 minutes or so to stay focused.  
- The watcher should know CPR and have a flotation device ready for rescue.

## 3. Recognize and respond to heat exhaustion  
- Watch for excessive sweating, weakness, dizziness, fainting, pale skin, nausea, vomiting, or fatigue.  
- Pay extra attention to seniors, children, and people with heart disease, diabetes, or certain medications.  
- If symptoms appear, move the person to an air‑conditioned space; if no cooler place is available, call 911.  
- Seek medical care if symptoms persist after cooling down.

## 4. Perform a tick check after outdoor activities  
- Cover skin with long sleeves, long pants tucked into socks, and apply EPA‑approved repellents (permethrin on clothing, DEET on exposed skin).  
- Stay on trails and avoid tall grass in tick‑prone areas such as the Northeast.  
- Upon returning home, dry clothes on high heat to kill ticks and inspect the body immediately.  
- Remove ticks promptly to reduce disease transmission risk.

## 5. Grill safely in an open space  
- Read the grill’s manual and set it up away from the house, patio roofs, or balconies.  
- Ensure ample open air for heat and smoke to disperse.  
- If a flare‑up occurs, close the lid and vents to cut off oxygen; keep a fire extinguisher nearby.
