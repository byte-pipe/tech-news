# Voter roll purges banned 90 days before federal elections

## Overview
- Federal law (National Voter Registration Act of 1993) prohibits systematic voter‑roll purges within 90 days of a federal election (“quiet period”).
- Republicans are seeking to have courts reinterpret or narrow this protection.
- The issue will be reviewed by the Supreme Court in the upcoming term via the case **RNC v. Mi Familia Vota** (Arizona).

## Legal background
- The 90‑day ban applies to the 44 states covered by the NVRA and Washington, D.C.
- Purpose: give mistakenly purged eligible voters time to resolve issues before Election Day.
- The ban covers any program “the purpose of which is to systematically remove the names of ineligible voters” from official lists.

## Recent court actions
- **2024 Virginia purge**: Republican governor Glenn Youngkin ordered daily voter‑roll updates targeting “non‑citizen registration” during the quiet period; federal judges found the program likely violated the NVRA, but the Supreme Court allowed it to continue without explanation.
- **Supreme Court (2024)**: granted the Republican National Committee’s request to hear the Arizona case; decision expected next year.
- **9th Circuit (2025)**: rejected Arizona’s argument that its citizenship‑based cancellations were “individualized,” ruling the program was systematic and thus barred by the NVRA.

## Republican arguments
1. **Non‑citizen exception** – the quiet period’s ban applies only to removing ineligible voters, not to removing non‑citizens, who are deemed ineligible.
2. **Individualized removals** – states claim their actions are case‑by‑case checks, not systematic programs, and therefore not covered by the ban.

## Judicial responses
- Courts are focusing on the definition of “systematic” and whether database‑driven mass checks constitute a prohibited program.
- Legal scholars warn the dispute is becoming a “textualist, semantic game” that could expand state authority beyond congressional intent.
- The Supreme Court’s “shadow docket” decisions raise concerns about limited remedies for unlawful purges during the quiet period.

## Implications
- A narrowing of the quiet period could allow states to conduct aggressive voter‑roll clean‑ups close to elections, increasing the risk of eligible voters being removed.
- Ongoing litigation may set precedents affecting all 44 NVRA states and D.C. for future federal elections.
- The outcome will influence the balance between election integrity efforts and protections for voter participation.
