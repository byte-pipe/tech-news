# AST SpaceMobile Faces Lawsuit Over Its Competitiveness With Starlink Mobile

## Lawsuit Details
- Investor Edward Hunter filed a class‑action lawsuit alleging that AST SpaceMobile made materially false and misleading statements about its ability to compete with SpaceX’s Starlink Mobile.  
- The 39‑page complaint claims the company overstated the durability of its position in the satellite direct‑to‑cell (D2C) market and failed to disclose that its competitive standing was weaker than presented.  
- Hunter purchased 18 shares in November 2025 and additional shares in June 2026 at roughly $74 each; the stock briefly rose to $133 in May before falling to $58, which he says reflects an artificially inflated price.  
- The suit seeks damages for investors who bought shares between March 4 2025 and July 15 2026, citing violations of federal securities laws.

## AST SpaceMobile Business Overview
- Texas‑based AST is developing a constellation of “BlueBird” satellites to provide space‑based phone connectivity for partners such as AT&T and Verizon, aiming to eliminate cellular dead zones.  
- The company targets “over 150 Mbps per coverage cell” and had planned to operate at least 45 satellites by the end of 2026.  
- As of the article, AST has 13 BlueBird satellites in orbit plus a prototype BlueWalker 3 satellite.  
- Service launch has been delayed to early 2027 after launch provider Blue Origin experienced a New Glenn rocket explosion during a ground test.

## Competition with Starlink Mobile
- SpaceX’s Starlink Mobile, already available through T‑Mobile, offers satellite‑to‑phone connectivity and plans to provide 150 Mbps per user with next‑generation satellites launching next year.  
- SpaceX is also exploring the use of ground‑based femtocells to compete directly with the major U.S. carriers.  
- AST positions itself as an alternative that AT&T, T‑Mobile, and Verizon could leverage through a joint venture for satellite‑to‑phone services.

## Additional Context
- The complaint also alleges that AST misled investors about rising capital requirements, increasing debt, and resulting share dilution that harmed shareholders.  
- AST SpaceMobile did not immediately respond to requests for comment.
