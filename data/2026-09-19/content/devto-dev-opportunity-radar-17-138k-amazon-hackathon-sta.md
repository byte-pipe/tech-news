### 📌 Code in Place X

Who it's for:Anyone 18+ who wants to learn programming, or people who already know Python and want to volunteer as a teacher.

What it's about:Code in Place Xis a new free course from Stanford that teaches programming while also helping students practice explaining what they build.

The course issix weeks longand you'll meet every week on Zoom with a small group of students and a volunteer teacher who will guide you through the material. You don't need any previous programming experience, and the course is completely free.

I wanted to include this one because I like that it is not only about writing code. Students also get to practiceexplaining their thinking and what they built, which is a useful skill whether you're learning programming for the first time or already working with AI coding tools.

The course also has an interesting approach to AI. Students can use AI as part of their learning, but they are expected to come to their weekly section ready to show what they learned and share how they are using AI. So the course is also exploring what it means to learn programming when AI can already write a lot of code for us.

There is also avolunteer teacheroption. If you already know how to write a simple Python program and can explain your thinking, you can apply to lead a small group. You don't need to be a professional engineer or have teaching experience. Teachers spend around4 hours per weekand receive lesson plans and section problems to work with.

The course itself takes around7 hours per weekfor students and begins onOctober 12, 2026.

Time Commitment:Around 7 hours/week for students | Around 4 hours/week for volunteer teachers

Cost:Free

Eligibility:18+ | No prior programming experience required for students

### 📌 Build, Ship, Shape: Amazon Developer Hackathon

Who it's for:Developers, students, AI builders, and anyone interested in building applications for Amazon devices and services.

What it's about:TheBuild, Ship, Shape: Amazon Developer HackathonbringsFire TV, Alexa+, Bee, and Ringtogether in one global hackathon. You can choose one of the four tracks and build a working project using the developer tools, APIs, SDKs, or simulators for that track.

I wanted to include this one because there are quite a few different ways to approach the challenge. You can build forFire TV, Alexa+, Bee, or Ring, depending on what you're interested in. There are also additionalAWS BuilderandOpen Sourcemini challenges that you can enter alongside your main track.

For example, the Fire TV track lets you build an app using React Native, web technologies, Android, or another framework that runs on Fire OS or Vega OS. The Alexa+ track focuses on building anMCP server or Agent Skill, while Bee projects use data from a Bee device or Apple Watch running Bee software. Ring projects can use Ring APIs, SDKs, simulators, or devices.

You also don't necessarily need to already have experience with Amazon's developer tools. The hackathon provides documentation, sample code, simulators, and live office hours to help participants get started.

The submission needs to include aworking project, GitHub repository, and a demo video under three minutes, along with product feedback on the Amazon tools you used.

There is a$138,000 cash prize pool, with prizes across the four main tracks and the AWS Builder and Open Source mini challenges. Track winners also receive a1:1 meeting with the Amazon Developer team, depending on the track.

One thing to check before choosing your project is the specific requirements for each track. Some tracks require you to actually use the relevant technology in your code or demo, so simply mentioning a particular Amazon service isn't enough.

Mini Challenges:AWS Builder, Open Source

### 📌 Dev3Pack Hackathon

Who it's for:University students and student builders interested in building on Solana. You can participate solo or in a team of up to four.

What it's about:Dev3Pack Hackathonis a three-day hybrid hackathon where students from around the world can build onSolana, either online or at one of the local build stations.

The hackathon runs fromOctober 30 to November 1, 2026, but there is also a freeSolana Rust Bootcamprunning from October 6 to 30. So if you're new to Rust or Solana, you can use the weeks before the hackathon to learn and get ready.

I wanted to include this one because it has a little more of acommunity and learning sidethan just the three-day hackathon. You can join a local build station if there is one near you, meet other students and mentors, or participate completely online if you prefer.

There are currently40+ build stations listed around the world, including Mumbai, along with locations across Asia, Africa, Europe, and the Americas.

The project needs to beopen sourceand must use Solana if you want to be eligible for prizes and the accelerator. You can also use AI coding tools while building, as long as the project is yours and you understand and can explain what you built.

After the hackathon, top teams can be invited to theBridge Accelerator, which runs from November through January. The exact prize tracks and amounts for this edition are still to be announced.

There are also some builder perks available to participants, includingNoah AI credits, v0 credits, and Cursor Pro for select countries, along with other perks listed in the participant dashboard.

### Coding Interview University

If you're preparing for software engineering interviews and don't know where to start,Coding Interview Universityis a free study plan that puts a lot of the topics you need to learn in one place.

It covers things likedata structures, algorithms, Big-O, trees, graphs, sorting, dynamic programming, networking, and coding interview practice. It also has sections on resumes, finding a job, interview preparation, and other parts of the interview process.

What I like about this resource is that it gives you a path to follow instead of just giving you a list of random topics. You can go through the topics step by step and practice coding problems along the way.

There are also a lot of free resources linked throughout the plan, including videos, books, courses, and coding practice sites.

It is a pretty big study plan though, so you definitely don't need to finish everything. You can pick the parts that make sense for where you are in your interview preparation.

Format:Free, self-paced study plan

Covers:Data structures, algorithms, coding problems, computer science topics, and interview preparation

### Dogfood | 72-Hour Hackathon

Shared byDhruv Jani (@dj29).

Dhruv shared theDogfood | 72-Hour Hackathonby Hackathon Raptors, and I thought this was an interesting one to include because the challenge itself is quite different from a usual hackathon.

Instead of letting everyone build different projects, everyone is working on thesame product: an open-source, self-hostable platform for running hackathons, including registration, team formation, submissions, judging, scoring, and results.

What I found interesting is that the goal isn't just to build a working demo. Thewinning project will be forked, self-hosted, and used for future Hackathon Raptors events. Teams also keep ownership of their work.

The technical requirements are quite specific too. The platform needs to run locally withdocker compose upand work without a hosted database, external API, cloud account, or network connection. The challenge also covers things like role isolation, judging, score normalization, voting, APIs, testing, and documentation.

There are four tiers to work through, but you don't have to try to build everything. The organizers mention that a clean and correct lower tier is better than a broken higher tier, which I thought was a good approach for a 72-hour challenge.

Who it's for:Developers, students, engineers, open-source contributors, and anyone interested in software development, backend systems, APIs, DevOps, security, or data and algorithms.

Format:Online

Duration:72 hours

Event Date:September 25–28, 2026

Team Size:1 to 4 participants

Prize Pool:₹2,50,000
