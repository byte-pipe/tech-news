# NASA figured out how to keep its 48-year-old Voyager 2 probe running for yet another year | Space

**NASA Adjusts Voyager 2's Power Supply**

NASA has made an adjustment to the power supply of its Voyager 2 spacecraft, ensuring it can continue operating for another year.


The decision came after a reduction in power requirements due to the dwindling supply of plutonium on each probe. The mission is managing the "Big Bang" power shift with reduced non-science devices and alternatives that are still functional.

**Key Points:**

* Voyager 2 and its twin, Voyager 1, have a "Big Bang" power shift planned in the coming months.
* The initial plan was to shut down two science instruments for nearly another year.
* NASA has identified options to reduce power requirements without impacting essential functions.
* The adjustment is crucial due to the dwindling supply of plutonium on each probe.


**Science Impact:**

* Both Voyager spacecraft are experiencing a measurable science impact from their reduced power supplies.
* Initially launched with 10 instruments, Voyager 2 now operates only three.
* A one-way signal to each spacecraft takes nearly 24 hours (or one light-day) to reach the other side of the solar system.


**Background:**

* NASA's Voyager 1 and 2 were launched in 1977 as part of a pioneering mission to explore our solar system.
* The twin spacecraft flew past Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune, capturing unprecedented imagery of the outer planets.
