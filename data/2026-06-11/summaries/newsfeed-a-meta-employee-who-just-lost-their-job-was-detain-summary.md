# A Meta Employee Who Just Lost Their Job Was Detained by Immigration Agents

## Incident Overview
- A former Meta employee, laid off on May 20 during a 10 % workforce reduction, was detained by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) in recent days.  
- The detention was reported on an internal Meta immigration‑focused messaging board, marked “urgent,” and tagged two Meta executives responsible for immigration and employee risk.  
- The worker’s current status and location are unknown; internal messages suggest the detention occurred in El Paso, Texas, near the U.S.–Mexico border crossing to Ciudad Juárez.  

## Visa and Legal Context
- Many international tech workers at U.S. companies hold H‑1B visas, which are tied to a specific employer.  
- After a layoff, workers must adjust their immigration paperwork, sometimes by leaving and re‑entering the country, creating vulnerability to enforcement actions.  
- WIRED could not confirm the detained employee’s nationality or the exact visa type used.  

## Company Response
- Meta spokesperson Dave Arnold declined to comment.  
- ICE and the Department of Homeland Security did not provide statements before publication.  
- Meta’s internal community has called for stronger protection for immigrant employees, including legal‑fee assistance and flexible work arrangements on days when immigration officials are present.  

## Employee Advocacy and Support Efforts
- A small group of Meta staff is organizing financial and logistical aid for colleagues facing immigration issues.  
- Workers have urged the company to help pay legal costs and to allow remote work to avoid potential raids.  

## Broader Enforcement Landscape
- The detention is a rare publicly known case of a corporate tech worker being taken into immigration custody since the intensified enforcement campaign launched by the Trump administration in early 2022.  
- Under that administration, tens of thousands of people were arrested each month, with about 60 000 individuals in detention centers as of early April 2024.  
- Tech offices have generally not been primary targets, though in January immigration authorities arrested two workers traveling to a Meta data‑center construction site.  

## Meta Layoffs Context
- In May, Meta cut roughly 8 000 jobs (≈10 % of its workforce) to improve efficiency and offset heavy AI‑infrastructure investments.  
- Several of those laid‑off employees were on work visas, increasing their exposure to immigration enforcement.
