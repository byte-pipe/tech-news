# Judge Rejects Google’s Attempt To DMCA Its Way Out Of Being Scraped | Techdirt

### The Implications of Google's Case

The US Copyright Act imposes an exception to the DMCA in cases where a technological measure controls access to copyrighted material but does not protect the underlying work itself. This is often referred to as the "public domain" or "non-copyrighted" exception.

A recent case, SearchGuard, dealt with Google's attempts to claim ownership of search results that do not contain any copyrighted content. The court ruled that this is a matter of law subject-to-dismissal where the results are exempt from copyright protection due to non-protection under the Copyright Act.

While this case appears straightforward, its implications can extend beyond SearchGuard to other areas, especially for non-copyrighted content such as large language models (LLMs) output. The same reasoning could apply if those outputs contain copyrighted data used or created by others.

### Potential Implications for Non-Copyrighted Content

The outcome of such cases could have significant effects on the scraping and use of non-copyrighted content, including:

*   Larger organizations like tech companies that regularly scrape and process data might find themselves more exposed to copyright claims due to their extensive work in this area.
*   Developing new technologies in artificial intelligence or NLP could also put these platforms at risk if they inadvertently rely on copyrighted input data.
*   As other non-copyrighted sources and services emerge, the ability of scrapers to claim public domain status in such cases may dwindle.
