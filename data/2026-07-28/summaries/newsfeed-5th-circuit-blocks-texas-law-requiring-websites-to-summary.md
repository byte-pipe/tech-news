# 5th Circuit blocks Texas law requiring websites to filter "harmful" speech - Ars Technica

Here is a concise and thorough summary of the article:

**Harmful Speech Protections in Texas**

* US Court of Appeals for the 5th Circuit rules that Texas's law requiring websites to filter "harmful" speech from children is preempted by federal Section 230 of the Communications Decency Act.
* The 5th Circuit panel argues that Section 230 allows website publishers to control content and prevent unwanted exposure, while also providing liability protection for internet service providers.

**Background**

* Texas law requires websites to block minors and monitor online interactions to comply with obscenity laws.
* Challenged groups, including the Computer & Communications Industry Association (CCIA) and NetChoice, argue that this law is overly broad and infringes on freedom of expression.

**Key Points**

* The 5th Circuit panel agrees that Section 230 prevents website publishers from filtering content they have not created themselves.
* This interpretation allows websites to control their own content creation and censorship but also shields internet service providers from liability for creating or distributing unwanted material.
* The law is designed to protect parents' rights in making informed decisions about what is suitable for their families.

**Conclusion**

* The 5th Circuit upholds its previous decision blocking the filtering mandate, finding that it goes too far.
* Judges conclude that Section 230 allows website publishers to control content while shielding internet service providers from liability.
