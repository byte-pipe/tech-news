### Knock, Knock: The First Domino

A few months ago, my DEV buddy@francistrdevinvited me to join DEVengers. Of course, I accepted. It's a great team full of cool folks, they're all stars and it's a real honor to be among them.

The thing is, a team like this only stays alive if people keep showing up and writing. This is my first post under DEVengers, so I wanted it to be special: easy to read, but with some depth. Consider it the first domino. I'm knocking it over, and I fully expect the rest of the team to follow. (No pressure, folks.)

### The Numbers and a Plot Twist

So here I am: thousands of followers, almost 50 articles, 2 hackathon wins, 6 Top 7 of the Week features, and that's only scratching the surface of what I've accomplished since I joined this amazing community.

You might think I've been creating content here for years. Nuh-uh. It's been just a year and a half since I started writing for real.

I first heard about DEV back in 2017. Yes, some of you were probably still in high school. That was also around the time I started my professional software engineering career. A year later, in 2018, I registered, buying the ticket in 2018 and boarding the train seven years later.

### How Did I End Up Here?

There's no funny story about how I discovered DEV. No viral post, no friend or colleague dragging me in.

So instead, I'm going to do what Ted Mosby did in How I Met Your MotherTV show. He sat his kids down to tell them how he met their mother, and ended up telling them his entire life story first.

This is the story of how I met DEV, and all the doors I had to go through before I finally stepped through this one.

Don't worry, though. Unlike Ted, I promise it won't take nine seasons.

### Door #1: Let Go for Being Too Good

Before I landed my first job as a junior software engineer, I worked in network and IT support while studying at university.

That was the reality in my home country. You can't just focus on your studies and then glide smoothly into a job. Most students are broke as hell, and I definitely was one of them. I barely had enough money for public transport and food.

I still performed like crazy, because that's just who I am. I don't know how to do things halfway, even things I can stand.

And then I was let go. I was "overqualified", according to the CEO, or whatever he called himself.
