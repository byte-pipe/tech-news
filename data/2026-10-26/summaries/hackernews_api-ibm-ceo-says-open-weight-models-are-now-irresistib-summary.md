# IBM CEO says open weight models are now irresistibly good and cheap on-prem

# IBM CEO Says Open Weight Models Make Brilliant Sense for On-Prem Enterprise Use

IBM CEO Arvind Krishna has expressed optimism about the adoption of open weight models for on-prem enterprises, citing their superiority over closed frontier models.

According to Krishna, in 2024, closed frontier models remained the best choice, with a top score of 70, compared to the open alternative's top score of 20-30. However, this gap between the two options began to close in late 2025, with the open model scoring around 70-50.

Krishna believes that businesses will choose the open model primarily due to its lower costs, particularly in terms of token cost. Currently, open weight models deliver anywhere from 25-10% of the token cost of frontier models, which is attractive given their 90% effectiveness.

The CEO acknowledges that not all tasks are suitable for the open model, citing various examples such as summarizing emails, examining contracts, and answering customer calls. However, he believes that these tasks can often be performed similarly using the much cheaper open alternative.

Krishna notes that the success of the open model depends on the number of tasks required to justify the higher costs. He predicts that the majority of businesses with significant AI infrastructure needs will choose not to incur those costs.

"The question is: how do you do that?" Krishna asked. "The question that enterprises struggle with is: *where are my kernel engineers*? *where are the people who worry about the underlying network?* *who can make sure that the model is clustered on GPUs that are close to each other in network proximity?*?"

In response, IBM will produce software that eases AI deployments, while also indicating demand for on-prem AI infrastructure will drive sales of Big Blue's Spyre mainframe accelerator cards.
