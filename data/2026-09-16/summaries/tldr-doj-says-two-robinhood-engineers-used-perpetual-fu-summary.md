# DOJ Says Two Robinhood Engineers Used Perpetual Futures to Trade Ahead of Its Crypto Listings

## Key Points
- The U.S. Attorney’s Office for the Southern District of New York unsealed criminal complaints against Robinhood engineers Hefu Chai and Huaisong Xiang, charging each with wire fraud and commodities fraud.  
- Both engineers had access to nonpublic information about upcoming token listings on Robinhood Crypto.  
- Between 2025 and 2026 they allegedly bought perpetual futures tied to specific tokens on the decentralized exchange Hyperliquid shortly before Robinhood announced those listings, earning more than $50,000 each from the trades.  
- The case mirrors earlier insider‑trading prosecutions, such as the 2022 case against former Coinbase manager Ishan Wahi, but is distinguished by the use of perpetual futures—a derivative that never expires.  
- Prosecutors applied the Commodity Exchange Act to the derivatives trades, emphasizing that corporate insiders cannot evade securities and commodities laws by using instruments like perpetual futures or tokenized securities.  
- Each defendant faces one count under the Commodity Exchange Act (up to 10 years imprisonment) and one count of wire fraud (up to 20 years imprisonment).  
- Robinhood cooperated with the investigation; the charges remain allegations and the engineers are presumed innocent.
