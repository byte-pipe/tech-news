# why I can't stop thinking about Papua New Guinea and what I think everyone should know about it

## Commonly heard “facts” about PNG
- Small fraction of Denisovan ancestry (≈4.8%).  
- About 1 000 languages are spoken – ~12 % of all world languages, the highest linguistic diversity on Earth.  
- Population estimates vary widely (10–17 million).  
- Bow‑and‑arrow warfare persisted into the 1960s; arrows are still shot in some urban areas.  
- The capital, Port Moresby, is often labeled the world’s most dangerous city.  
- Historical reports of cannibalism, kuru disease, and alleged murders (e.g., Michael Rockefeller).  
- Jared Diamond has suggested PNG peoples may be genetically “smarter” than Europeans.  

## The discovery that changed everything
- Until 1930 the interior “highlands” were assumed to be empty, uninhabited mountains.  
- Gold finds in 1926 spurred Australian prospectors inland; Mick Leahy, chasing gold, accidentally entered the eastern highlands in 1930.  
- He found lush valleys inhabited by roughly one million people who had never seen outsiders.  

## What the book *First Contact* documents
- Leahy’s expedition was recorded with high‑quality cameras and film, producing thousands of photos and hours of footage.  
- Interviews from the 1970s let highland survivors recount their first‑contact experience.  
- Early reactions: highlanders thought the white men were ghosts of dead relatives, then tested them by spying on their waste.  
- Demonstrations of “strength” (pig‑shooting, record‑playing, mirrors, airplanes) were interpreted as spiritual or supernatural power.  
- Europeans traded shells (the highland “currency”) for food and labor, causing hyperinflation: shells went from rare prestige items to worthless mass‑distributed tokens.  

## Historical background of the island
- First human arrival ≈ 50 000 years ago via land bridges and island‑hopping from Southeast Asia.  
- Those migrants later gave rise to Australian Aboriginal peoples when New Guinea and Australia were connected.  
- Around 10 000 years ago agriculture independently arose in the highlands, allowing dense valley settlements.  
- The highlands became isolated from coastal groups, remaining so for roughly 10 000 years until the 1930 contact.  

## Why the story fascinates me
- The extreme insularity: a large population living without external contact for tens of thousands of years, diverging culturally and genetically from the rest of humanity.  
- The rarity of a documented first‑contact event on such a scale, captured in extensive photographic and film records.  
- The rapid cultural and economic disruption caused by the introduction of foreign goods (e.g., shells) and technology.
