# Voters mostly don’t like AI and data centers, but neither party seems to have an edge  

## Key findings  
- 61 % of 1,503 likely voters oppose building AI data centers; only 14 % strongly support them.  
- Opposition is strongest among voters who backed Kamala Harris (74 %) and non‑voters (67 %); Trump supporters are split 49 % support vs 45 % oppose.  
- Of those opposed, 56 % favor limits on data centers, while 38 % prefer a total ban.  

## Reasons for opposition  
- Environmental and water‑usage concerns – 32 %  
- Negative impact on local communities – 21 %  
- General distrust or dislike of AI – 18 %  
- Regulation worries – 10 %  
- Economic concerns – 9 %  

## Relative importance to voters  
- AI and data centers rank below 1 % of issues influencing midterm votes for most demographics.  
- Younger voters (18‑29) list AI/data centers at 3 % of top concerns, still far behind economy, foreign policy, inflation, and anti‑Trump sentiment.  

## Party trust and voter alignment  
- Voters overwhelmingly trust their own party to handle AI/data‑center issues.  
- Overall, 40 % of respondents favor Democrats, 42 % favor Republicans, and 18 % are undecided or refuse to answer.  

## Context from other polls  
- NBC News (March) showed AI polling lower than ICE.  
- Gallup (May) found 70 % of Americans oppose local AI data‑center construction.  

## Takeaway  
Despite strong public opposition to AI data centers, the issue remains low‑priority in electoral decision‑making, and neither political party has a clear advantage in addressing voter concerns.
