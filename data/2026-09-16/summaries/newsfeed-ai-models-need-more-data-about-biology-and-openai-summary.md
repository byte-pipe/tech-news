# AI models need more data about biology, and OpenAI is paying to create it | MIT Technology Review

# Summary of “AI models need more data about biology, and OpenAI is paying to create it”

## Background
- Ruxandra Teslo, a policy analyst focused on clinical trials, suggested using data from failed biotech companies to accelerate medical AI.
- She proposed bidding in bankruptcy proceedings to obtain regulatory filings, manufacturing strategies, and safety data—information usually treated as trade secrets.
- Teslo called this collection “biotech’s lost archive,” envisioning it as training material for AI copilots in the drug‑approval process.

## OpenAI Foundation Initiative
- The OpenAI Foundation, the nonprofit arm of OpenAI, launched “Public Data for Health” to fund the creation of high‑quality scientific datasets for AI in medicine.
- The initiative is based on the premise that AI breakthroughs in disease treatment require far more data than currently available.
- Morgan Levine (former VP for computation at Altos Labs) noted that data is the biggest bottleneck for applying AI to biology.

## Funding Details
- Initial round of grants includes:
  - $40 million for a University of North Carolina program to collect data on novel cancer vaccines.
  - Support for OpenAdmet, which runs competitions to predict drug effects.
  - $500 000 for Teslo’s biotech archive idea, administered by the advocacy group 1Day Sooner.
- The OpenAI Foundation aims to give away $1 billion by the end of the year.
- The foundation holds a 26 % equity stake in OpenAI, potentially making it the world’s richest charitable organization (≈$250 billion in stock value).

## Role of 1Day Sooner and the Biotech Archive
- 1Day Sooner, representing clinical‑trial volunteers, will test whether bankrupt biotech data can be acquired cheaply (estimated “a few tens of thousands of dollars” per dataset).
- The group already possesses three datasets, two donated by Lumen Bioscience, which used Chapter 11 to access another company’s drug‑development data.
- Attempts to acquire additional drug‑company files this year failed when bids were not accepted.
- The targeted documents are “common technical documents,” containing regulator‑company communications, scientific measurements, and comprehensive drug information.
- Teslo believes a large stockpile of such files could turn an AI into a regulatory expert, speeding the path from discovery to market.

## Broader Context and Concerns
- OpenAI’s charitable work occurs alongside heightened fears of AI‑driven existential risk, including scenarios where AI creates bioweapons.
- Prominent AI leaders (e.g., Sam Altman, Elon Musk) have endorsed calls to slow AI capability development to allow risk mitigation.
- Jacob Trefethen, an OpenAI Foundation executive, emphasizes the foundation’s separate operation but shared mission to ensure AI benefits all humanity.

## Future Outlook
- Bankruptcies may become a “new land grab” for AI training data, as illustrated by Google’s acquisition of Spirit Airlines’ corporate data.
- Successful acquisition and integration of biotech “lost archives” could make AI a powerful tool for navigating the opaque, costly regulatory landscape of drug development.
- The foundation’s substantial financial resources position it to shape the direction of AI‑driven health research, provided it can manage grantmaking effectively and address ethical concerns about data ownership and privacy.
