# AI doom, a guide for the perplexed

## Overview
- The author reflects on the rapid progress of AI since 2016 (AlphaGo, ALE, AlexNet) and argues that current discussions of AI risk are riddled with misconceptions.  
- Two primary “doom” scenarios are highlighted:
  1. **Universal job‑loss AI** – machines that can perform any computer‑based task, eventually extending to physical work via robotics, creating massive unemployment, political marginalisation, and entrenched oligarchy.  
  2. **Recursive self‑improving AI** – systems that can improve their own intelligence and potentially escape human control, posing an existential threat.

## Misguided Approaches
- Debates often start with abstract philosophy or metaphysics (the “Maimonides” warning) instead of concrete analysis of the technology.  
- Media focus on surface‑level concerns (e.g., universal basic income) ignores deeper power‑concentration dynamics.  
- Skeptics dismiss existential risk by citing historical survival, while optimists claim precise failure pathways; the author prefers converging, overlapping models rather than single‑step narratives.

## Scenario 1: Universal Job‑Loss AI
- **Metric for automation:** “Could this job be done from home?” → roughly 50 % of task‑level work is automatable today; a slightly smaller share of whole jobs.  
- **Future trajectory:** Advances in robotics will extend automation to most remaining occupations.  
- **Societal impact:**  
  - Ordinary people become a “surplus population” with little industrial power.  
  - Warfare and repression become automated, reinforcing elite control.  
  - UBI, even if implemented, only addresses subsistence, not the concentration of political/economic power.  
  - The Gulf States illustrate societies where labour power is minimal; AI could create a similar “resource curse” for workers worldwide.

## Scenario 2: Recursive Self‑Improvement AI
- **Core risk:** Machines capable of recursively enhancing their own intelligence may outpace human understanding and act against our interests.  
- **Plausibility:** The author finds the scenario both possible and, in some moods, probable, though acknowledges extreme uncertainty.  
- **Approach to prediction:** Favor multiple, converging lines of evidence rather than deterministic step‑by‑step forecasts or blanket denial.

## Recent AI Achievements (Illustrating Progress)
- **Winograd Schema** (2019): Early test of commonsense reasoning; later easily solved by modern models.  
- **Mathematical proofs:** AI generated solutions to Navier–Stokes‑type problems, hinting at capability to tackle Millennium‑Prize level questions.  
- **Creative outputs:** AI won the Commonwealth short‑story prize (Granta) and published a philosophy article, though both were met with criticism once the AI origin was revealed.  
- **Common criticism:** Achievements are labeled “defective,” “intellectual theft,” or “trash,” often as a defensive reaction to AI’s growing competence.

## Core Argument
- While AI’s accomplishments may be imperfect, the tendency to downplay them as “defective” is likely exaggerated and serves as a coping mechanism.  
- Recognising the genuine capabilities of AI is essential to addressing the two primary doom scenarios.

## Way Forward (Proposed Actions)
- **Prevent rapid AGI creation** by a small, power‑seeking minority operating under competitive “race” pressures.  
- **Raise awareness** of both scenarios by dismantling preconceptions that block understanding.  
- **Develop policies** that address not only subsistence (e.g., UBI) but also the redistribution of political and economic power in a post‑labour world.  
- **Foster interdisciplinary scrutiny** that starts with concrete technological analysis before moving to philosophical speculation.
