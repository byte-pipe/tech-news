# Summary of “AI app ads promoting ‘objectification of women’ banned by watchdog – BBC News”

## Key points
- The UK Advertising Standards Authority (ASA) banned five AI‑app adverts on Meta platforms for sexualising and objectifying women, including a girl under 18.
- The ads promoted AI tools that could create sexually explicit or “sizzling” videos from users’ images, often without the subjects’ consent.
- ASA’s AI‑driven “Active Ad Monitoring System” identified the ads, describing them as likely to cause serious or widespread offence.
- The regulator warned that such content can cause real‑world harm and may be reported to platforms and law enforcement when illegal.
- Meta had already removed the ads under its own rules, but the ASA’s action underscores a “zero‑tolerance” stance on such advertising.

## Details of the banned advertisements
- **Animcha Ltd AI companion app** – featured a synthetic female character presented as a customizable “crush,” combining sexualised imagery with child‑like clothing; also used an under‑18 girl’s sexualised image.
- **Nexaipic portrait generator** – invited users to upload a “crush” and generate explicit videos.
- **KH31 DD22 image‑to‑video app** – showed a clothed woman being turned into a topless video, implying the tool could expose women’s bodies.
- **Rusto AI image generator** – encouraged creation of sexualised videos from photographs, condoning manipulation of women’s images.
- **PictoPop image‑to‑video app** – used sexualised imagery, depicting a woman in a submissive pose to demonstrate the tool.

## Regulatory context
- The ASA’s ban follows recent UK legislation outlawing non‑consensual deep‑fake nude imagery.
- The ASA noted that the law changed during its investigation; future ads will be judged against both the law and ASA rules.
- When ads are deemed illegal, the ASA may notify platforms and law‑enforcement agencies.

## Meta’s response and broader landscape
- Meta removed the offending ads before the ASA’s ruling, citing breaches of its own policies.
- Earlier research identified over 11,000 similar ads on Meta platforms; most were automatically removed, but many persisted.
- Meta claims to have used new detection technology and taken legal action, reporting the removal of more than 344,000 nudification‑related ads between November 2025 and January 2026.
