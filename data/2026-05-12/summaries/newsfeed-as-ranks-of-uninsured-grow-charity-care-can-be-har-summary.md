# As ranks of uninsured grow, charity care can be hard to come by at many hospitals

## Overview
- Investigation by KFF Health News and the Minnesota Star Tribune examined charity‑care programs at every Minnesota hospital from 2020 to 2024.  
- Minnesota hospitals allocate far less of their operating budgets to charity care than hospitals in most other states.

## Key Findings
- National average charity‑care spending is about 2.4 % of hospital operating budgets; Minnesota’s average is roughly one‑third of that, around 0.8 %.  
- Of Minnesota’s 123 general hospitals, 62 spent less than 0.5 % of their budgets on charity care.  
- CentraCare’s flagship St. Cloud Hospital spent less than 0.25 % of its budget on charity care—about $25 in patient aid for every $10,000 of operating expenses.  
- Eligibility standards differ widely: some hospitals set the income threshold for free care at about $15,000, while others allow patients earning up to $47,000.  
- Applications often require detailed personal financial information, including bank statements, retirement accounts, mortgage documents and asset estimates.

## Patient Example: Cori Roberts
- Diagnosed with early‑stage cervical cancer; earned $41,000 per year and had insurance.  
- Received more than $8,000 in medical bills from CentraCare, which denied her charity‑care eligibility because her income was deemed too high.  
- Paid over $6,000 over two years, cutting back on groceries and gifts, and was later sued for the remaining balance; the lawsuit was dropped after she took a loan against her retirement plan.  
- Nearby hospitals with different charity‑care policies would have offered her more generous assistance, but she was unaware of those options.

## Context and Implications
- The uninsured and underinsured populations are growing; about 100 million Americans carry healthcare debt, much of it from hospital bills.  
- Charity care, originally intended for the uninsured, is now essential for many insured patients who cannot afford high copays and deductibles.  
- Hospital officials argue that financial strain limits their ability to expand aid; the Minnesota Hospital Association says the need far exceeds what hospitals can provide.  
- Minnesota Attorney General Keith Ellison contends that nonprofit hospitals must increase charitable assistance to match the tax benefits they receive.

## Factors Behind Low Aid in Minnesota
- High rates of employer‑based insurance and an expanded Medicaid program give the impression that fewer people need charity care.  
- Absence of statewide standards leads to inconsistent eligibility criteria and patient experiences.  
- Burdensome application processes discourage eligible patients from seeking assistance.

## Responses and Statements
- Erin Hartung, director of legal services at Cancer Legal Care, described the system as “not working” and highlighted the disproportionate burden on the most vulnerable.  
- CentraCare’s spokesperson cited medical‑privacy laws and declined to discuss specific charity‑care spending or the Roberts case.
