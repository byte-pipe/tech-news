# Software engineering may no longer be a lifetime career

## Argument presented by some engineers
- Claim: Using AI to solve tasks reduces learning from those tasks.  
- Consequence: Engineers who rely on AI will see their technical skills atrophy.  
- Conclusion: AI should not be used in software work.

## Author’s response to the claim
- Disagrees with the step that skill atrophy necessarily follows AI use.  
- Points out past technology shifts (e.g., assembly → C) changed effectiveness in mixed ways; AI may be a larger shift but its impact is uncertain.  
- Emphasizes that until ~2024, “learning by doing” was the primary path to competence, a fortunate coincidence rather than a permanent rule.

## Market forces and labor dynamics
- Engineers who accept AI’s short‑term gains may outcompete those who reject it, leading to market pressure to adopt AI.  
- Labor unions could theoretically slow this trend, but the author is skeptical about their effectiveness in the high‑pay, globally distributed tech sector.

## Implications and recommendations
- Acknowledge the possibility that AI could reduce long‑term cognitive or technical ability.  
- Recognize that refusing AI may limit earning potential and job opportunities.  
- Plan for a career that does not rely solely on sustained technical skill, similar to athletes preparing for post‑competition life.  
- Stay aware of emerging techniques (e.g., tools to keep engineers mentally engaged) that could mitigate skill erosion.
