# The air is full of DNA — here’s what scientists are using it for

## Main points
- Airborne environmental DNA (eDNA) is now being recognized as a valuable source of genetic information, complementing traditional water and soil samples.  
- Current uses focus on monitoring individual species by detecting their DNA fragments in the air.  
- Researchers anticipate broader applications, such as:  
  - Assessing the effectiveness of conservation programs.  
  - Detecting invasive species or wildlife migrations.  
  - Identifying biological‑weapon attacks through unusual DNA signatures.  
- Major knowledge gaps remain, including how far DNA travels in the atmosphere, its degradation rate, and optimal sampling techniques.  
- Ethical considerations arise because airborne sampling could unintentionally capture human genetic material, raising privacy and consent issues.
