# Fable 5 Vs Opus 4.8: Outcomes‑Based Assessments Are A Massive Warning For Frontier AI Labs

## Overview
- The article argues that current AI benchmarks are “vibe‑based” and do not reflect real business outcomes.  
- The author uses a concrete benchmark: rebuilding his personal website to improve conversion rates.  
- The central claim: frontier models (Fable 5, Opus 4.8) look impressive on artifact metrics but fail when measured against outcome metrics.

## Benchmark Results
- Both Fable 5 and Opus 4.8 produced functional websites, but neither improved conversion rates; they scored **0** on the outcomes‑based metric.  
- Key failures:
  - No tracking or event‑logging hooks were included, preventing measurement of conversions.  
  - Security features were missing; Fable 5 even refused to add them.  
  - Required manual fixes and additional prompts for basic elements (sitemap, SEO metadata).  
- When the same prompt was given to the open‑source model Gemma 4, it delivered a comparable site at zero token cost, with the same need for manual adjustments.

## Why Models Miss Outcomes
- Models only implement outcomes that are explicitly described in the prompt (e.g., tracking, security).  
- Adding more context diminishes the advantage of “advanced” reasoning; simpler models perform equally well.  
- The gap between frontier model capabilities and enterprise requirements is largely due to missing outcome‑oriented instructions.

## Enterprise Evidence
- Eli Lilly ran an outcomes‑based benchmark on frontier models, found them unsatisfactory, and continued to rely on smaller, purpose‑built models trained on proprietary data.  
- Despite a 2024 partnership with OpenAI, Lilly has not expanded the relationship, indicating limited trust in frontier models for high‑end research.  
- The article cites the CEO of Palo Alto Networks, Nikesh Arora, claiming “infinite” AI demand, but contrasts this with real‑world reluctance to adopt frontier models for critical workloads.

## Market Implications
- Mature AI adopters are shifting toward:
  - Smaller, open‑source or domain‑specific models.  
  - Information flywheels and purpose‑built agents.  
  - Local AI strategies that keep data in‑house and avoid token‑based costs.  
- Frontier labs risk “spending cliffs” if they continue to prioritize hype over outcomes.  
- Alex Karp (Palantir) publicly criticized the reliance on vibe‑based benchmarks, urging a pivot to outcomes‑centric products.

## Advice for the C‑Suite
- Reframe AI investments around concrete business outcomes rather than token consumption or model prestige.  
- Ask critical questions such as: “If a model could generate a billion dollars for us, why would it charge per token?”  
- Prioritize partnerships with vendors that can demonstrate measurable ROI and support outcomes‑based pricing models.  
- Use the article’s benchmark as a template to evaluate AI solutions within your own organization.
