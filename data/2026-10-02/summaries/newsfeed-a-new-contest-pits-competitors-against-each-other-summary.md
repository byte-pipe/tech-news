# A new contest pits competitors against each other in a race to biological youth | MIT Technology Review

**Younger Contest**

The Younger Contest is a unique competition that rewards individuals for getting younger by measuring biological age. The competition, led by neuroscientist Christin Glorioso, aims to create "the world's most comprehensive clock dataset" using various tools to assess biological age.

**Key Points**

* The Younger Contest will involve 500 participants who will submit blood samples and undergo cognitive tasks for brain age evaluation.
* A leaderboard will rank participants based on their changes in chronological age and biological age.
* Winners will receive rewards, including the person showing the greatest biological age difference and the person who reverses their biological age the most.
* The goal of the contest is to provide a better understanding of biological age.

**The Concept of Biological Age**

Biological age is often measured using various tools, including chemical signatures, proteins, lipids, functional health markers, and overall pace of aging. However, these methods are not yet accurate enough to estimate biological age for individual people.

**The Contest's Approach**

The Younger Contest uses a different approach, involving multiple clocks to assess biological age. The contest's approach may help generate a comprehensive clock dataset, which could improve our understanding of biological age.

**The Participants' Participation**

Participants will send blood samples, complete cognitive tasks, and upload selfies to an app for facial age analysis. The contest aims to create a standardized approach to measuring biological age, providing more accurate results.

**The Potential Impact**

The Younger Contest may benefit the field of aging research by providing a more comprehensive clock dataset. This could lead to new discoveries and a better understanding of the biological processes underlying aging.

**The Challenge**

One of the challenges associated with biological age assessment is understanding what each clock is capturing. More research is needed to develop accurate and reliable tools for measuring biological age.
