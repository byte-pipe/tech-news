# With most information hidden, the game Stratego had stumped AI—until now - Ars Technica

**Ataraxos Breaks Struggle of Classic Game Strategoh**

A team of researchers from Carnegie Mellon, MIT, New York University, and Stanford University has achieved a groundbreaking victory in the classic strategy game Strategoh. In a self-play session, Ataraxos, a sophisticated artificial intelligence system, defeated a professional player, Pim Niemeijer, a legendary Strategoh player, by a score of 15 to 1, with four draws. This victory demonstrates the AI's unparalleled understanding of the game.

**Key Contributions**

Ataraxos's approach differs significantly from earlier AI systems like DeepNash, which relied solely on brute force calculation. To counter this, the research team implemented a more adaptive strategy, incorporating "big, bold changes" in the strategy early in training and "small, careful ones" later. This innovative method allowed Ataraxos to think ahead, anticipating the opponent's moves and adjusting accordingly.

**Insights from the Game**

The game Stratoholds a fascinating paradox. On one hand, it requires players to hide information, making it an imperfect game. However, the game's length and complexity create an environment where AIs must balance stealth with clever bluffing, as revealed by the team's observations and experiments (e.g., 40 pieces on the board with a vast number of possible setups, bluffing moves that are difficult to detect, and the game's length, which requires a high level of calculation).
