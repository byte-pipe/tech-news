description: 'Your LinkedIn industry field is probably wrong. And it''s deciding who sees your posts. I found out mine was when I dug into how LinkedIn''s new system decides who sees your posts. 4 years ago, I worked at a medtech company. So I probably changed it to "Technology, Information and Internet" back then and never opened that setting again. Four years later, I run a content agency. And my profile still told LinkedIn I was in tech. That one dropdown matters more than most of your profile. When LinkedIn decides who sees your post, it doesn''t send your whole profile along with it. Only five fields travel with your content into someone else''s feed. LinkedIn calls this your author data: Name Headline Company Industry Title That''s it. Your skills, job history, education, and About section don''t travel with your posts. They live on your profile page. They help someone decide to work with you once they land there. They play no part in who the post reaches. So all those hours people
  spend rewriting their About section help convince a visitor who''s already looking at them. That section has no say in who the post reaches in the first place. Industry is the field almost nobody checks. You set it once when you sign up, usually based on wherever you worked at the time, and forget it exists. Mine said tech. I changed it to Marketing Services. Took two minutes. Go check yours right now. Open your intro, look at the industry field, and ask if it matches the people you want reading your posts. If it still says whatever industry you were in years ago, you''ve been fighting the algorithm on a setting you forgot existed. Little low hanging fruit for y''all there. | 80 comments on LinkedIn'
