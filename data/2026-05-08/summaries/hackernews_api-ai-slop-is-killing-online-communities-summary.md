# AI Slop is Killing Online Communities

## Prologue – I ❤️ AI
- The author is not an AI‑hater; they see AI as a useful tool.
- They are frustrated by the flood of low‑effort AI‑generated content that clutters the communities they care about.

## The Problem – AI Slop Overruns Communities
- People rapidly share anything they can generate with a prompt (“I rewrote Kafka in COBOL”, AI‑written blog posts, videos, self‑published ebooks) without considering usefulness.
- Typical sharing workflow:
  1. Discover a flashy AI‑coding feature.  
  2. Push a project to GitHub.  
  3. Let an LLM write a hype‑filled post and blast it to every subreddit or Slack.
- The author urges a pause after step 2 to ask:
  - Is the project actually useful, documented, reusable?
  - Will the author maintain it, answer issues, or improve it?
  - Does the content add genuine value or is it just AI‑filled noise?
- Resulting “AI slop” drowns out organic contributions, making signal hard to find, driving members away, and risking community death or a sterile AI‑only environment.

## Good AI Slop vs. Bad AI Slop
- **Good AI‑assisted content**: Enables people to do things they couldn’t before, created with care and clear human intent, and contributes positively to the community.
- **Bad AI slop**: Low‑effort, spammy, engagement‑farming material that offers no benefit and merely adds noise.

## Who Sets the Standards?
- The author claims “common decency and taste” as the guiding principle.
- Sharing should be purposeful: know what you’re sharing, to whom, and why.
- Historical analogy: not every Geocities homepage deserved internet‑wide exposure; similarly, not every AI‑generated app or post deserves mass distribution.

## Recommendations – What to Share
- **Built with AI, not by AI**: Use AI as a tool while providing the thinking, instruction, and verification yourself.
- **Contribution matters**: Ensure the output adds something new to the community beyond a reproducible prompt result.
- **Quality over quantity**: Prioritize well‑documented, maintainable projects and thoughtful writings over flashy but shallow AI outputs.

## Takeaway
- AI can be a powerful ally, but indiscriminate sharing of AI‑generated “slop” harms online communities.
- Creators should self‑filter, share only when their work genuinely enriches the community, and uphold standards of usefulness, effort, and responsibility.
