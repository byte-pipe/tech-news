# Old matchboxes are igniting new ideas for these Indian creatives

## Overview
- The article explores how three Indian design projects reinterpret the traditional matchbox, a medium that historically reflected visual culture, social narratives, and advertising in India.  
- Inspired by archival collections such as Gautam Hemmady’s phillumenist archive and global initiatives like *The Matchbook Book*, the creators use the matchbox as a democratic canvas for contemporary commentary.  

## Maachis
- Founded by designer Sonal Nagwani (originally with Kevin Thomas) after discovering extensive Indian matchbox label archives.  
- Goal: continue the matchbox’s legacy of social commentary by embedding modern themes—queer rights, body positivity, anti‑evil‑eye humor, female autonomy—into collectible objects.  
- Product: wooden matchboxes with magnets at both ends; designs are created by Sonal and collaborating artists on a royalty basis.  
- Future directions include city‑specific subculture series, lifestyle/home products, and large‑scale installations that amplify the matchbox’s maximalist visual language.  

## Matchbox Comix
- Produced by Studio Kokaachi (Tina Thomas and Pratheek Thomas), evolving from a 2013 idea to package short comics inside matchboxes.  
- Format: six boxes per set, each containing an accordion‑style three‑panel comic that tells a complete, quirky story appealing to all ages without being preachy.  
- Creative brief emphasizes humor, absurdity, and universal appeal; the process of finding suitable stories is described as “surprisingly tough.”  
- Packaging evolves from minimal labels to story‑specific designs, with upcoming volumes aiming for a vintage aesthetic reminiscent of historic Indian matchboxes.  

## Matchbox Momentos
- Interactive game created by artist Harshit Agrawal in partnership with Google Arts & Culture, Museum of Art & Photography, and Tasveer Ghar.  
- Inspired by his grandfather’s stamp collection and Hemmady’s matchbox archives, the project treats matchboxes as carriers of cultural memory.  
- Users build personal matchbox art collections, exploring India’s pop‑culture and social change through visual data and machine‑learning analysis.  

## Conclusion
- All three projects demonstrate how the humble matchbox can serve as a versatile platform for maximalist design, storytelling, and cultural preservation, linking historic visual grammar with contemporary artistic practice.
