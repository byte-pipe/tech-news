# AI: Finance adopts AI at 2x the Pace of Its Regulators

## Key Findings from the CCAF 2026 Report
- 4 in 5 financial firms have deployed AI in some capacity.  
- 76 % of firms rely on OpenAI as their primary foundation model.  
- The three leading cloud providers serve over 80 % of the industry.  
- 43 % of financial regulators are not collecting AI‑adoption data and have no near‑term plans to start.

## Regulator Capability Gap
- Regulators move roughly half as fast as the firms they supervise.  
- 46 % of surveyed regulators lack any cloud infrastructure; 24 % do not use foundation models.  
- Main constraints cited by regulators:  
  - 48 % lack training and capacity building.  
  - 47 % lack talent.  
  - 45 % lack technology and infrastructure.  
- Existing prudential frameworks (capital adequacy, liquidity, operational resilience) do not capture AI‑related risks.

## Information Problem
- Regulation intentionally lags technology, but it still requires observation.  
- With 43 % of regulators not tracking AI use, a critical information gap emerges.  
- The Financial Stability Board (FSB) highlights data gaps: undefined AI terminology, difficulty assessing AI criticality, and no consistent tracking of third‑party dependencies.  
- FSB’s four systemic vulnerability categories:  
  1. Third‑party dependencies and service‑provider concentration.  
  2. Market correlations from common models.  
  3. Cyber risks.  
  4. Model risk, data quality, and governance failures.

## Divergent Regulatory Approaches
| Jurisdiction | Regulatory Stance | Notable Measures |
|--------------|-------------------|------------------|
| **EU** | Prescriptive | AI Act obligations for credit scoring, insurance pricing, AML profiling effective 2 Aug 2026; fines up to 7 % of global turnover or €35 M. |
| **UK** | Flexible / experimental | FCA avoids AI‑specific rules; uses Supercharged Sandbox, AI Live Testing, AI LAB for supervised experimentation; Treasury Select Committee calls for practical guidance by end‑2026. |
| **US** | Examination‑focused | SEC 2026 Examination Priorities target AI in fraud detection, AML, trading, portfolio management, customer service; only 40 % of S&P 500 disclose AI use, 15 % disclose board oversight. |

## Concentration Risks
- **Model providers:** OpenAI used by 76 % of firms (48 % of regulators), Google 57 %, Anthropic 35 %, DeepSeek 15 %.  
- **Cloud providers:** AWS powers 46 % of industry workloads; Azure leads among regulators using cloud (39 %).  
- Potential impact of a sustained outage at a dominant provider: stalled credit decisions, halted AML alerts, non‑functional chatbots, loss of AI‑driven trading signals, disabled compliance automation.

## Systemic Risk Insights (ESRB Report)
- Five AI features that can amplify systemic risk: concentration, model uniformity, monitoring challenges, over‑reliance, speed.  
- Proposed policy responses:  
  1. Adjust capital and liquidity requirements.  
  2. Impose “skin‑in‑the‑game” and sophistication standards on AI deployment.  
  3. Enhance supervision with transparency labeling of AI‑enabled financial products.  

## Emerging Regulatory Tools
- The UK’s Critical Third Parties Regime gives the FCA and Bank of England investigative and enforcement powers over essential service providers, including AI and cloud vendors, though its effectiveness remains to be demonstrated.
