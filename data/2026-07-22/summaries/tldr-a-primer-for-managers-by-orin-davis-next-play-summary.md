# A Primer for Managers – Summary

## TL;DR Recommendations
- Seek out and normalize celebration of excellence; it signals that people and their work matter.  
- Begin with a listening tour, then hold bi‑weekly (or weekly) 1‑on‑1s that cover work and growth; skipping them is risky.  
- Require curated attendee lists, agendas, and prep for all meetings; meet only for sensitive, creative, or complex decisions that need quick resolution.  
- Start and end each day with a standing meeting: the morning sets goals and allocates resources, the evening shares progress and gives high‑fives.  
- Track the team’s activities with a system that combines dashboards and detailed records; administrative oversight is a core manager duty.  
- Grant employees control over where, when, and how they work; ensure mutual understanding of what “done, well, on time, with the resources given” looks like.  
- Prepare hard conversations carefully and adopt a CORGI mindset (Curiosity, Optimism, Respect, Good Intentions); remember that rude behavior often stems from time pressure.  
- Trust yourself and be patient; mastering management skills takes time.

## Introduction Highlights
- **Celebrate yourself first.** Acknowledge the promotion, the salary bump, and give yourself a virtual high‑five; self‑celebration is the foundation for celebrating others.  
- **Normalize win‑celebration for the team.** Overcome the taboo of bragging by encouraging personal and collective shout‑outs, high‑fives, and “strengths spotting” (recognizing when team members use their character strengths).  
- **Use 1‑on‑1s to know your people.** Without deep knowledge of each team member, you cannot coach, motivate, or develop them effectively.

## Detailed Practices

### Celebrate Wins and Strengths
- Permit yourself to feel proud and notice internal barriers to celebration.  
- Track wins publicly; make high‑fives and shout‑outs routine.  
- Practice “strengths spotting” by catching teammates using their best traits, reinforcing that their work matters.

### Consistent 1‑on‑1 Meetings
- Schedule bi‑weekly (or weekly) 1‑on‑1s with every direct report (15 min weekly, 30 min bi‑weekly).  
- For large teams, use team leads to cascade 1‑on‑1s and rotate additional meetings to keep the load manageable.  
- **Listening tour:** In the first 4–6 weeks, hold longer (60 + min) 1‑on‑1s to learn backgrounds, motivations, strengths, and work context.  
- Structure each 1‑on‑1 into four parts: casual chat, top‑of‑mind items, work check‑ins, and growth/development discussion.  
- Never skip these meetings except for genuine emergencies.

### Meeting Hygiene
- Only meet when the topic is sensitive, creative, or requires rapid decision‑making.  
- Curate attendee lists, prepare agendas, and require pre‑meeting prep.  

### Daily Stand‑Ups
- **Morning stand‑up:** set daily goals, allocate resources, clarify priorities.  
- **Evening stand‑up:** report progress, acknowledge achievements, share quick feedback.

### Team Tracking System
- Implement a combined view: high‑level dashboards for quick status, plus detailed logs for deeper insight.  
- Treat administrative tracking as a core managerial responsibility.

### Autonomy and Clear Expectations
- Offer flexibility on location, schedule, and work methods.  
- When assigning tasks, co‑define what “done, well, on time, with the resources given” means.

### Hard Conversations (CORGI Approach)
- Prepare thoroughly: gather facts, outline objectives, anticipate reactions.  
- Approach with Curiosity, Optimism, Respect, and assume Good Intentions.  
- Recognize that time pressure often fuels negative behavior; manage workload to reduce tension.

### Self‑Trust and Patience
- Accept that management competence develops over months and years.  
- Give yourself grace while iterating on the practices above.
