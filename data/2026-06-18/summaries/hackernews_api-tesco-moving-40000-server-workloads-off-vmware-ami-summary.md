# Tesco moving 40,000 server workloads off VMware amid Broadcom's “abusive conduct” - Ars Technica

**Tesco Moves 40,000 Server Workloads Off VMware Amid Broadcom's "Abusive Conduct"**
=====================================================================

Tesco, a UK-based retail conglomerate, is relocating approximately 40,000 server workloads to VMware after Broadcom's alleged abusive conduct in the contract dispute. 

**Background**
-------------

In January 2021, Tesco signed contracts with Broadcom and VMware involving perpetual licenses for vSphere Foundation and Cloud Foundation, support services through 2026, and the option to extend support for four additional years.

However, when Broadcom took over VMware in November 2023, it failed to honor its contractual obligations. The new company refused to renew support services or upgrade software while maintaining pre-existing subscriptions, which is deemed abusive conduct by Tesco.

**Tikc**    
Tesco has since started migrating away from VMware and Broadcom's mainframe products and began paying for third-party support.

**Milestones**
---------------

- As of January, Broadcom stopped supporting Tesco’s VMware products.
- In its initial complaint to The Register, Tesco alleged:
  + Refusing payments for virtualization software
  + Forcing the purchase of "duplicative subscription-based licenses" without allowing for a more competitive payment plan
- Despite efforts otherwise,  **Tesco reports** that it will migrate off VMware by the end of December 2027 at the earliest.

**Counterpoints**
----------------

* Tesco reportedly noted that if they expedite the migration process, it will be completed by December 2026.
* However, this process poses significant operational and commercial risk to Tesco.
* The company faces challenges regarding data security as its new software is incompatible with other products.
