# How to Share Your Point of View Despite Fears of Being Wrong

## 1. The Burden of Proof Matters
The more controversial the idea, the higher the burden of proof.

* This principle emphasizes that sharing your perspective requires taking responsibility for its merits.
* It encourages you to think critically about the strength of your argument and the potential impact on others.

## 2. Update Your Assumptions About Value
Update your assumptions about how you add value to a team or organization.

* This principle challenges you to consider whether your unique insights and experiences hold value.
* It invites you to explore new ways of thinking and challenging yourself to contribute more effectively.

## 3. Share Where Your Hunch is Coming From
Deserve the right to be heard by sharing where your hunch comes from, beyond just your intuition.

* This principle empowers you to articulate your thought process and emotions behind a particular idea.
* It helps clarify why you approached your perspective in the first place and what drives it.

## 4. Explain Why You're Speaking Up
Make it clear for others that it's not personal but rather about the issue at hand.

* This principle encourages you to take ownership of your concerns and articulate them clearly.
* It builds trust with fellow team members by explaining why they need to listen and respond

## 5. Don't Rely on Credentials Alone
Just because your expertise or credentials are impressive, does not necessarily mean your idea should be the first one presented.

* This principle reminds you that a well-thought-out argument based on data and evidence is more compelling than relying solely on personal experience.
* It promotes a culture of objectivity and encourages diverse perspectives within organizations

## 6. Speak with Conviction (But Not Confidently)
Don't be dismissive: instead express confidence in your idea while remaining open to feedback and revision.

* This principle highlights the importance of maintaining credibility without being overly confident.
* By sharing your original intention while being receptive to others' views, you foster a collaborative conversation that leads to better solutions.
