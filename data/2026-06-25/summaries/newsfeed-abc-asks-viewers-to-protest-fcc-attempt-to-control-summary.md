# ABC asks viewers to protest FCC attempt to “control who is allowed” on The View

## Main call to action
- ABC aired a commercial urging viewers to write to the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) and ask it to stop trying to “control who is allowed to appear” on *The View*.
- The ad references the FCC’s request for public comment on whether the talk‑show qualifies as a “bona fide news interview program.”

## Historical FCC treatment of talk shows
- For decades the FCC has classified late‑night and daytime entertainment talk shows as bona‑fide news for their interview segments, exempting them from the equal‑time rule that requires equal airtime for opposing political candidates on non‑news programming.
- Exemptions have been granted to programs such as *Phil Donahue*, *Sally Jessy Raphael*, *Jerry Springer*, *Bill Maher*, *Jay Leno*, and *Howard Stern*.
- *The View* received its own bona‑fide news exemption in 2002 during the first term of President George W. Bush.

## Actions taken by FCC Chairman Brendan Carr
- Carr opened a proceeding that could force *The View* to comply with equal‑time requirements, citing an interview with Texas Democratic Senate candidate James Talarico as the trigger.
- The FCC issued a broad warning to broadcast stations that talk‑show interview segments should not be used for “partisan political purposes.”
- Carr also initiated an unusual review of Disney’s eight broadcast‑TV station licenses, alleging that Disney’s diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI) practices violate anti‑discrimination rules—a move ABC characterizes as retaliation for editorial choices.

## ABC/Disney’s response
- ABC’s ad frames the FCC’s move as an attempt to “control who is allowed on the show” and calls on viewers to voice opposition.
- The FCC labeled the ad as “misinformation” and asked ABC to clarify which part of the advertisement is misleading; the article notes that a response from the FCC’s public‑relations office is pending.

## Carr’s public reply
- In an X post, Carr asked the public whether *The View* should be classified as a bona‑fide news program, implying that such a classification would exempt the show from equal‑time rules.
- The post omitted the fact that *The View* already holds a 2002 declaratory ruling confirming its bona‑fide news status, which ABC reaffirmed in a May 7 filing.

## Political backlash
- Carr’s threats toward ABC and other broadcasters have drawn bipartisan criticism.
- Senators Ted Cruz (R‑TX) and Ron Wyden (D‑OR) recently proposed legislation to prevent federal officials from coercing broadcasters or tech platforms into restricting speech.
