# 7 Mistakes Founders Make When Chasing Their First Deals

## 1. Don’t Wait for Perfection Before You Start Selling
- Begin with a hypothesis about a real problem; a flawless product or polished deck isn’t required.  
- Treat selling as a contact sport: talk to customers early, gather feedback, and iterate.  
- Early signals help diagnose mis‑fits and accelerate product‑market fit.  

## 2. Stop Underpricing Yourself
- Charge amounts that make you slightly uncomfortable; low prices signal low value.  
- If the price is too low, consider a design partnership for feedback, case studies, and references instead of cash.  
- Ask prospects what they consider fair when they push back.  

## 3. Find the Person Who Holds the Budget
- Identify the Economic Buyer early, not just an enthusiastic internal champion.  
- Ask “Who else needs to weigh in?” and “Whose budget would this come from?” to avoid dead‑end conversations.  

## 4. Listen More Than You Talk
- Apply an 80/20 rule: 80 % listening, 20 % speaking.  
- Use open‑ended questions (“What’s the process for signing?” “How high a priority is this problem?”).  
- Capture exact customer wording to tailor a resonant pitch and build trust.  

## 5. Don’t Fall in Love with Just One Customer
- Run multiple design‑partner conversations in parallel; a single hot lead isn’t a reliable fundraising foundation.  
- Embrace “no” as a filter that frees you to focus on better prospects.  
- Spread effort across a broader pipeline and celebrate rejections as learning opportunities.  

## 6. Your Pitch Should Evolve with Every Conversation
- Treat the pitch as a living document that improves after each call.  
- Note what excites, confuses, or prompts questions from prospects; adjust accordingly.  
- Spend ~60 seconds post‑call to record insights and refine the story for the next interaction.  

## 7. Don’t Hire a Salesperson Too Early
- Founder‑led sales are critical for discovering the sales motion and influencing product roadmap.  
- CEOs selling themselves convey vision and credibility that a hired salesperson can’t yet match.  
- Only bring on a salesperson after you’ve closed repeatable deals and documented a playbook.
