# Quaise Energy achieves 100 meters of drilling using millimeter wave technology

## Key Milestone
- Quaise Energy successfully drilled to a depth of **100 meters** at a field site in Central Texas using its proprietary millimeter‑wave drilling technology.  
- This marks the first field‑scale demonstration; previously the technology had only been proven in the laboratory, with MIT’s early system reaching only a few centimeters.

## Technology Overview
- The system relies on a high‑power gyrotron that **ablates rock with millimeter‑wave energy**, eliminating the need for any downhole hardware.  
- Developed after more than a decade of research at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT).  
- Capable of penetrating hard, hot rocks such as granite and basalt, reaching temperatures around **752 °F (≈400 °C)** that are typical of super‑hot geothermal reservoirs.

## Significance & Future Plans
- The granite drilled in the test is the same hard rock that blankets the Earth’s basement layer, demonstrating relevance to commercial geothermal targets.  
- Quaise plans to upgrade to a gyrotron with **10 × more power** to accelerate progress toward commercial depths.  
- Goal: complete a pilot geothermal power plant in the western United States **by 2028**.  
- Earlier this year the company performed a full‑scale demonstration on a Nabors oil rig, drilling through a ten‑foot granite column, showing rapid scaling from the initial test.

## Executive Quote
- “Our progress this year has exceeded all expectations,” said **Carlos Araque**, CEO and President of Quaise Energy. “We’re drilling faster and deeper at this point than anyone believed possible, proving that millimeter‑wave technology is the only tool capable of reaching the super‑hot rock needed for next‑generation geothermal power. We are opening up a path to a new energy frontier.”
