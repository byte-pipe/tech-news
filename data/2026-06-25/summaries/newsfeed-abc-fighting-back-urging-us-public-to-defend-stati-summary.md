# ABC ‘fighting back’, urging US public to defend stations amid FCC scrutiny  

## Background  
- The Trump administration has pressured Disney‑owned ABC, prompting the network to launch an on‑air campaign asking viewers to support its eight owned‑and‑operated stations.  
- The call to action is tied to a public comment period on the FCC’s early licence‑renewal review, which ends on 29 July.  

## FCC actions  
- In April, the Federal Communications Commission ordered ABC to file early licence renewals for stations in New York (WABC), Houston (KTRK), San Francisco, Chicago and Los Angeles.  
- Licence renewals assess whether stations serve the public interest; critics say the move is intended to appease President Trump.  
- FCC Chairman Brendan Carr has publicly backed Trump’s efforts to “reshape and silence ABC.”  
- Commissioner Anna Gomez warned that using licences as leverage against a parent company is a “dangerous misapplication” of FCC authority.  

## ABC’s campaign  
- Local ABC stations have posted on‑air messages and website notices urging viewers to submit comments defending the stations.  
- The network argues the administration’s actions violate its First‑Amendment rights.  

## Press‑freedom and advocacy response  
- Seth Stern (Freedom of the Press Foundation) praised ABC for “fighting back” and called the FCC’s actions “abuse of power.”  
- Advocacy groups contend the FCC is selectively enforcing rules, targeting ABC while ignoring similar practices by other broadcasters.  

## Trump’s criticism  
- Trump used Truth Social to attack ABC News for reporting on alleged vandalism at the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool, accusing the network of “FAKE NEWS.”  
- He referenced a prior $16 million settlement of his defamation suit against ABC and hinted at filing another lawsuit.  

## Additional pressures on ABC  
- Earlier, ABC removed (and later reinstated) Jimmy Kimmel after comments on the death of activist Charlie Kirk.  
- In February, the FCC opened an investigation into “The View” for possible violations of equal‑time rules.  
- In March 2025, Carr launched an inquiry into Disney’s diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI) practices, threatening licence revocation.  

## Industry context  
- The FCC approved the TEGNA‑Nexstar merger; Nexstar’s NewsNation has faced criticism for shifting rightward to appease the president, though its CEO claims continued DEI commitment.  

## Current status  
- ABC has not responded to Al Jazeera’s request for comment.  
- The public comment window remains open until 29 July, after which the FCC will decide on the licence renewals.
