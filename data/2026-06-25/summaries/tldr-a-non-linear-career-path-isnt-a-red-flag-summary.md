# “A non‑linear career path isn’t a red flag”

## Overview  
- The piece is a response in the weekly **Creative Career Conundrums** column (If You Could Jobs).  
- Kat Wong, founder of the career‑change platform **Oh Yeah**, answers the question: *What does being a career zigzagger look like in the creative industry?*  

## Core Message  
- In the creative sector a non‑linear, “squiggly” career is **an asset**, not a warning sign.  
- The constant throughout Kat’s 30‑year journey has been **giving herself permission to explore and experiment**, rather than conforming to others’ expectations.  

## How to Approach a Zig‑zag Career  
- Treat each move as a **deliberate choice**: ask how it nourishes you, builds skill, or expands your network.  
- Build a **proven track record** of delivering impact and cultivating trusted relationships.  
- Use the breadth of experiences to develop confidence, clarify values, and make intentional next‑step decisions.  

## When Frequent Changes May Raise Concerns  
- Red‑flag appears when an experienced professional **stays less than a year** in multiple roles over several years.  
- This pattern can suggest under‑delivery or difficulty forming lasting work relationships.  

## Benefits Specific to the Creative Industry  
- Exposure to diverse perspectives and platforms enhances creativity and problem‑solving.  
- Artists, musicians, and actors routinely reinvent themselves; creatives can adopt the same mindset without being pigeonholed.  
- A strong network and reputation are “gold” because projects often rely on trusted collaborations.  

## Practical Takeaways  
- **Own your story**: frame your career as intentional experimentation rather than random hopping.  
- **Focus on impact**: let the quality of work and relationships speak louder than the number of job titles.
