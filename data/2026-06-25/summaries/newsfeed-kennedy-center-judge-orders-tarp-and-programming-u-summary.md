# Kennedy Center Judge Orders Tarp and Programming Update

## Court Order and Reporting Deadline
- Judge Christopher R. Cooper required the Kennedy Center to submit a status report on its operations, programming, and public access.
- The report must include an explanation of the tarp and scaffolding placed over the front signage.
- Deadline: within seven days after the July board meeting or by July 31, whichever comes first.
- The Center’s request for an extension was denied.

## Renovation and Closure Options
- President Trump, now chairman, announced a July 5 closure for major renovations.
- President‑and‑CEO Matt Floca presented three options to the board:
  1. Complete closure for extensive renovations.  
  2. Partial closure allowing limited public access and programming while renovations continue.  
  3. Phased, highly limited closures targeting critical infrastructure, maintaining a full programming slate.

## Tarp, Scaffolding, and Signage
- The tarp and scaffolding were erected on June 13 after the Center delayed the court‑mandated removal of President Trump’s name from the façade.
- Workers removed the lettering overnight, then covered the sign with a tarpaulin; the sign remains hidden.
- Trump’s name was removed from all digital content on June 4 following an internal memo.

## Programming and Staffing Challenges
- Many prominent artists have canceled appearances, citing politicization of the venue.
- Most programming staff have left through layoffs or resignations.
- Matt Floca’s background is in facilities management (construction management degree) with no prior experience in artistic direction, fundraising, or arts administration.
- Typical season announcements occur in mid‑spring for performances from September through the following summer; the Center’s future schedule is uncertain.

## Ongoing Legal Disputes
- The lawsuit was filed by Rep. Joyce Beatty (D‑Ohio) against President Trump and the Kennedy Center board; the judge ruled Beatty may attend board meetings.
- The Washington National Opera sued the Center on June 12 for $17 million, alleging withheld donor gifts, bequests, and endowment funds intended for the opera.

## Current Activity Snapshot
- Only a few outdoor free movie screenings and children’s workshops are listed on the Center’s calendar.
- Historically, the Kennedy Center hosted over 2,000 arts and education events annually.
