# Nature Index: A Database of Author Affiliations and Institutional Relationships
===========================================================

## What is the Nature Index?
-------------------------

The Nature Index is a database that tracks contributions to research articles published in high-quality natural-science and health-science journals.

## Key Points
-------------

* The database provides absolute and fractional counts of article publication at the institutional and national level.
* The Nature Index is an indicator of global high-quality research output and collaboration.
* The database is updated regularly, with the most recent 12 months made available.
* The database is compiled by Nature Portfolio.

## Nature Index Metrics
--------------------

The Nature Index uses two metrics to track research output: Count and Share.

* Count: A country/territory or institution is given a Count of 1 for each article that has at least one author from that country/territory or institution.
* Share: A fractional count that takes into account the share of authorship on each article. The total Share available per article is 1.

## How Does it Work?
-------------------

The Nature Index uses Share to account for duplicate authorship. It takes into account the share of authorship on each article and splits the author's Share equally among each affiliated institution.

## Adjusted Share
----------------

Adjusted Share accounts for the annual variation in the total number of articles in the Nature Index journals. It is calculated as the percentage difference in the total number of articles in the Index in a given year relative to the number of articles in a base year and adjusted to the base year levels.

## Bilateral Collaboration Score (CS)
--------------------------------

The bilateral collaboration score (CS) between two institutions A+B is the sum of each of their Shares on the papers to which both have contributed.

## Conclusion
----------

The Nature Index provides a valuable dataset for researchers, policymakers, and the general public to track high-quality research output and collaboration worldwide.
