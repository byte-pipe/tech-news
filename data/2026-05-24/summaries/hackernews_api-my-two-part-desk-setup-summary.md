# My two-part desk setup

## Motivation for change
- Previously the desk faced the wall; after noticing museum desks facing the room in Hamburg, I rotated my desk to face the room.
- The new orientation gives a broader field of view, a sense of openness, and the door is now in front of me.
- Over time I reconsidered what I need from a desk, moving away from a single tech‑only surface.

## New layout
- Adopted a single 200 × 75 cm USM Haller desk split into two distinct zones: a digital side and an analog side.
- The split creates separate mental contexts while sharing one piece of furniture, avoiding the need for two separate desks.

## Digital side
- Located by the windows; contains the Studio Display, Mac, and a custom split keyboard.
- Used mainly for writing, coding, and calls; kept intentionally minimal.
- New items are allowed only if they are used regularly; otherwise they are moved to the analog side or removed.

## Analog side
- Holds non‑screen tools: notebook or planner, fountain pens, current books, loose sketches, and a desk lamp (Artemides Tolomeo Mini with custom adaptor).
- Functions for reading, journaling, planning, sketching, small DIY projects, and shared activities with my kids (e.g., LEGO, drawing).
- Designed to be functional and inviting rather than minimal; items can stay out for extended periods.

## Living with the setup
- After nine to ten months I am very satisfied with the arrangement.
- Facing the room adds depth and comfort; seeing the door improves a sense of safety.
- The clear boundary between digital and analog zones lets me shift context simply by moving the chair.
- The desk now serves as a workspace, a thinking area, and a family interaction spot, and I do not plan to revert to a tech‑only desk.
