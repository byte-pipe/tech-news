# Australia battling its largest diphtheria outbreak in living memory

## Overview
- Diphtheria cases have surged in Australia, the first significant rise since the 1930s vaccination programme.
- 230 confirmed cases and one adult death have been recorded this year.
- The outbreak is concentrated in Indigenous communities in remote parts of the Northern Territory and Western Australia, with smaller clusters in Queensland and South Australia.

## Causes and risk factors
- The outbreak likely began with a Queensland case in 2022 that was acquired overseas and then spread across state borders.
- Contributing factors include:
  - Gaps in vaccination coverage, especially low booster uptake among adolescents.
  - High mobility between remote communities and crowded housing conditions.
  - Increased vaccine misinformation and hesitancy following the COVID‑19 pandemic.
  - Shortages of nurses and doctors in remote health services.

## Vaccination gaps
- About 92 % of 5‑year‑olds in the Northern Territory are vaccinated against diphtheria.
- Only around 67 % of 13‑year‑olds receive the recommended school‑based booster.
- Immunity wanes over time, making boosters essential for adolescents and young adults.

## Clinical picture
- Caused by *Corynebacterium diphtheriae*, which can infect skin or respiratory tract.
- Symptoms include skin ulcers or a thick grey membrane in the throat, leading to breathing difficulty.
- Toxin entering the bloodstream can cause heart failure or paralysis.
- Treatment involves antibiotics and an antitoxin, but mortality still occurs.

## Government and health response
- The Australian government has allocated A$7.2 million to contain the outbreak.
- Additional doctors and nurses are being deployed to affected regions to deliver boosters and treatment.
- Community demand for vaccination is reported to be strong.

## Historical context
- Prior to vaccination, diphtheria caused thousands of deaths in Australia in the late 1920s and early 1930s.
- The largest post‑vaccination diphtheria epidemic occurred after the Soviet Union’s collapse in 1991, with over 140,000 cases and 5,000 deaths, illustrating the risk when vaccination programmes are disrupted.

## Ongoing challenges
- Maintaining high booster coverage among adolescents.
- Combating misinformation and vaccine hesitancy.
- Addressing healthcare staffing shortages in remote Indigenous communities.
