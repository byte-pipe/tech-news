# Attacks on Ebola centres intensify in eastern DRC amid outbreak fears

## Overview
- Violence against Ebola treatment facilities is increasing in eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC).
- The World Health Organization warns the outbreak could spread to DRC and Uganda.
- As of late May 2026, nearly 180 deaths and about 800 confirmed cases have been reported.

## Recent incidents
- **Rwampara health centre**: Residents stormed the centre demanding the bodies of relatives who died from Ebola.
- **Mongbwalu hospital (Ituri province)**: A tent provided by Doctors Without Borders (MSF) was set on fire after a patient’s body was prepared for burial.
- Tensions during body retrieval led to the burning of two tents, though staff secured the body for a safe burial.

## Community mistrust and cultural concerns
- Fear, rumours, and mistrust fuel resistance to strict burial protocols that require protective handling of bodies.
- Residents suspect Ebola response teams of organ trafficking and view the disease as a profit‑making scheme.
- Traditional practices involve close contact with the deceased; families report being denied final rites, causing emotional distress.

## Health impact and challenges
- Attacks and patient flight from treatment centres risk accelerating transmission.
- Health workers face shortages of resources; the national treasury now funds a large share of the response amid declining international aid.
- Officials describe treatment centres as overwhelmed and under‑resourced.

## Government and agency response
- The Congolese health minister emphasizes that only trained teams in protective gear may handle Ebola bodies.
- Authorities have limited public gatherings, suspended wake services, and banned the movement of bodies.
- Public health messages urge hand‑washing, physical distancing, proper cooking, avoidance of self‑medication, and trust in response teams.

## Regional coordination
- Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention placed ten African countries on high alert, including South Sudan, Rwanda, Kenya, Tanzania, Ethiopia, Republic of Congo, Burundi, Angola, Central African Republic, and Zambia.
- DRC, Uganda, and South Sudan agreed to strengthen cross‑border surveillance, early warning systems, border monitoring, and laboratory capacity.
