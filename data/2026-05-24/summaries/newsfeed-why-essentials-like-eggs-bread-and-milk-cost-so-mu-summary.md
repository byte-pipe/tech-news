# Why essentials like eggs, bread and milk cost so much more now

# Summary of “Why essentials like eggs, bread and milk cost so much more now”

## Price increases since 2022
- **Eggs:** £1 for a box of six (2022) → £1.80 now.  
- **Milk:** £1.29 for four pints (2022) → £1.65 now.  
- **Bread:** 65 p for a medium white loaf (2022) → 74 p now.  

## Main drivers of higher costs
- **Avian flu (2021‑2023):** Massive culling of hens reduced supply, forcing supermarkets to limit purchases and raise prices.  
- **Energy and grain price spikes:**  
  - Russia’s full‑scale invasion of Ukraine (2022) raised grain costs (Ukraine is a key grain exporter) and lifted energy prices.  
  - Ongoing conflict in the Middle East adds further energy pressure.  
- **Production expenses:**  
  - Eggs: grain feed, heating sheds, transport.  
  - Milk: milking, processing, transport energy‑intensive.  
  - Bread: wheat price increases after the Ukraine war, now stabilising.  
- **High demand:** Continued popularity of protein‑rich diets keeps egg demand strong.  

## Impact on producers and farmers
- Input costs for producers rose 7.7 % in the year to April (ONS), the biggest rise in over three years.  
- Factory‑gate prices to retailers increased only 4 %, creating a cost squeeze.  
- Dairy farmers receive about 25 % less per litre, many operating at a loss.  
- Contracts between producers and supermarkets are set in advance, limiting mid‑contract price adjustments; producers absorb some cost spikes.  

## Supermarket profitability and competition
- Total supermarket sales grew from ~£130 bn (2020) to ~£160 bn (2024).  
- Profit margins have not risen over the past 20 years; many staples are sold at a loss to attract shoppers.  
- The Competition and Markets Authority (July 2024) found no evidence of artificial price inflation.  
- Retailers claim the UK remains one of the most affordable Western European grocery markets, with some items sold below cost to protect customers.  

## Outlook
- Energy and raw‑material cost pressures remain a “perfect storm” of higher expenses for raw materials, energy, labour, and packaging regulation.  
- While wheat price pressures have levelled, ongoing geopolitical tensions keep the broader cost environment uncertain.  
- Supermarkets are likely to continue absorbing losses on key staples, keeping margins tight but competitive.
