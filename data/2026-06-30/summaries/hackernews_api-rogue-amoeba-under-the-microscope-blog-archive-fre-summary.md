### Apple's Shape Restriction in MacOS App Icons

In recent MacOS versions, Apple has implemented shape restrictions on app icons by allowing only certain, predetermined shapes. While this may seem like a natural fit for maintaining visual consistency and aesthetic standards, it results in blurry and unappealing icons due to the elimination of distinct shapes.

#### **Tahoe Release: Changes from Tahoe**

Before the latest Tahoe release ("Golden Gate"), Apple's app icon choices were marked by design missteps, such as introducing "Liquid Glass" effects that compromised the image quality. The Golden Gate release brought significant improvements, allowing app icons to retain their earlier, sharper form.

#### **Preventing App Icon Shape Restrictions (Recommended Changes)**

To prevent potential issues similar to those observed during Tahoe, Apple should reconsider its approach. Instead of mandating specific shapes for every third-party icon, let developers decide on their desired icon styles, providing flexibility and creative freedom while ensuring usability and accessibility.

#### **Benefits of Respecting Developer Choice**

Allowing app icons to retain distinct shapes supports various design considerations:

*   Providing a clear visual identity for individual apps
*   Aiding in color accuracy and contrast sensitivity
*   Enhancing user experience by allowing for customization

The original article from "Basic Apple Guy" expresses their desire for an improvement, pointing out that the "dictated squirling" style during Tahoe resulted in bland grayscale backgrounds to accommodate specific shapes. This indicates that there are valid issues present within the current shape limitations imposed upon app icons.

While this change may introduce some upfront maintenance and technical considerations, developers will be able to adapt their icon styles without requiring drastic transformations of standard shapes. In contrast, forcing uniformity could lead to a homogenized aesthetic for users with diverse visual needs or abilities.
