# My Media Diet: Why is everyone watching old TV again?

### "I have work, and then I have a dinner thing. And then I am busy, trying to become who I am."

Raised on Social by Goat
May 26, 2026
Share

Author: Liv Molho, Strategy Manager,The Goat Agency

Like many, I just finished Famesick by Lena Dunham. Required reading, for sure. And the timing feels right. Ever since COVID, her showGirls- which aired from 2012 to 2017 on HBO - has had a total resurgence.

In the same way Sex and the City became such a rewatch staple that it cemented itself as a cultural landmark (and required viewing), Girls has proven its staying power too. The show feels incredibly of its time, and also totally timeless.

But it’s not just Girls.

I feel like everyone around me is watching (or rewatching) Mad Men, The Sopranos, Seinfeld, Breaking Bad, The Nanny, etc. Not in a passive “I threw this on in the background while I’m throwing dinner together” kind of way. People are watching for the first time - discovering it like it’s new. Going to drinks or dinner and talking about what everyone is watching has become a total time warp.

My thesis? In a time where the biggest influx of media is reality TV, and the actual world feels increasingly scary and unstable, people need escapism more than ever. But non-fiction media doesn’t really provide escape anymore. If anything, it feels like an extension of the same social internet exhaustion we’re already trapped in all day.The people yearn for fiction.

“Going to drinks or dinner and talking about what everyone is watching has become a total time warp.”

Not just any fiction, either. They yearn for shows that remind them of a different time. A simpler time, a funnier time, a more hopeful time. Or at least a version of time that feels coherent and less confusing. A world with structure. Stakes that resolve themselves in 30 minutes or 10 episodes. Even the anxiety in these older shows feels cleaner somehow. More contained. Less algorithmic. Less existential.

These days, it feels like every streaming platform debuts a new opportunity to watch hot randos make out every six months. It was fun for a while, but reality TV has almost become its own dominant cinematic universe. Bravo has never been more culturally powerful, and Hulu is now franchising its breakout hit The Secret Lives of Mormon Wives into its own mini Bravo-verse with the newly announced Orange County offshoot.

Meanwhile, movies are increasingly sequels, remakes, spin-offs, or IP extensions. The combination of audiences craving fiction and studios fearing failed investments has basically pulled culture into one giant feedback loop, and pushed everyone into the past.

And honestly? Sometimes it’s nice. There’s something genuinely communal and comforting about realizing everyone you know is watching Tony Soprano have a panic attack in widescreen again.

But I also can’t shake the feeling that this mass cultural retreat into old TV is maybe a tiny cry for help. Like collectively, we are all searching for worlds that feel more emotionally familiar than our own.
