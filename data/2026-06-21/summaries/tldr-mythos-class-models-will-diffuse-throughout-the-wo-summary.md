# Mythos-class models will diffuse throughout the world by 2029 — Saagar Pateder

# Model capabilities improve over time, but open-weight models lag the frontier

## Overview
- Personal use of newer models (e.g., Claude Fable 5) shows only modest gains over previous versions for everyday tasks like cooking or planning a date night.
- Diminishing marginal returns apply both to human workers and to AI models: beyond a certain intelligence level, additional capability yields little practical benefit for many tasks.
- The Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (AAII) and Epoch Capabilities Index provide useful, though imperfect, benchmarks for tracking model progress.
- Frontier models have steadily improved and are expected to keep advancing, pushing more tasks toward the performance asymptote where extra intelligence matters less.

## Open‑weight models vs. frontier models
- Open‑weight models are publicly runnable given sufficient hardware, are cheaper, but typically lag behind closed‑weight frontier models by an estimated 4 months on benchmark scores.
- Model size correlates with capability; larger open‑weight families (e.g., Google’s Gemma 4 series) are more capable but require more compute.
- Two additional performance curves can be imagined: one for the cutting‑edge open‑weight models and another for models that can run on an average laptop.

- A table (not reproduced here) predicts when models comparable to Fable 5 will be small enough to run on devices ranging from $1,000 laptops to $5,000 workstations.
- Estimates assume benchmark parity; real‑world parity is expected 6–12 months later.

## Consumer implications
- Most consumers prioritize ease of use, memory, and multimodal features over raw model intelligence.
- Ads and occasional rate limits are acceptable trade‑offs for free or low‑cost tiers.
- On‑device models are unlikely to be a major selling point for the average user.

## Enterprise implications
- Enterprises evaluate AI based on ROI; the 90th‑percentile spends about $7,200 per employee per year on AI.
- Open‑weight or local models that cost ~20 % of current spend could become attractive if they meet performance needs.
- Frontier models may retain value in high‑stakes domains (life sciences, healthcare, finance, law, engineering) where superior accuracy justifies cost.
- For many routine business tasks, a sufficiently capable open‑weight model on a modest laptop could be more cost‑effective than licensing premium closed‑weight services.

## Security concerns
- Easy deployment of powerful open‑weight models (e.g., Mythos‑class) raises the risk of malicious actors leveraging advanced capabilities for cyber‑attacks.
- The accessibility of sophisticated models underscores the need for robust safeguards and responsible deployment practices.
