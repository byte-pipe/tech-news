# Summary of “As global warming threatens corals, scientists search for reefs that can take the heat”

## Context and problem
- Record‑breaking marine heat waves since 2023 have caused the most severe global coral bleaching event, affecting >80 % of reefs in 83 countries.  
- Stressed corals expel their symbiotic algae, turn pale, and many fail to recover.  
- Scientists suspect that some reefs possess an extraordinary ability to endure extreme temperatures.

## The “Super Reefs” research program
- Initiated by marine scientist Anne Cohen (Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution) in 2018 to locate reefs that remain healthy despite nearby bleaching.  
- 2021: a joint global initiative with The Nature Conservancy and Stanford University to find and protect heat‑tolerant coral communities.  
- Definition of a “super reef”: scientifically proven resilience to higher temperatures (via genetic adaptation or favorable local conditions) and the capacity to reseed other reefs.  
- Targeted regions with existing or planned marine‑protected areas: Belize, Hawaii, and the Marshall Islands.  
- Goal: protect resilient reefs from additional threats such as dredging, runoff, plastic pollution, bottom trawling, and dynamite fishing.

## Field work in the Marshall Islands
- Cohen’s seventh visit to Majuro, using the autonomous surface vehicle “Yellowfin” to locate a reef she has monitored for years.  
- The observed reef displayed vibrant tabletop and staghorn corals, indicating strong health despite regional heat stress.  
- The Marshall Islands’ land and culture are built on coral; the nation’s atolls are essentially massive coral structures.

## Challenges and future directions
- Human activities continue to degrade reefs; >50 % of global reefs already lost, with projections of >90 % disappearance in 25 years without intervention.  
- Need to identify more super reefs beyond those already documented.  
- Cohen proposes a “super reef blue corridor” linking the Marshall Islands, Kiribati, and Tuvalu, using ocean currents to facilitate larval exchange and regional reef recovery.  
- Ongoing testing of new technologies (e.g., autonomous vehicles) aims to accelerate the discovery and protection of resilient reefs.
