Earlier this week,Anthropic stated in their S1that they “may imminently meet” the threshold of “dramatically accelerated progress due to AI” as defined in their Responsible Scaling Policy. Intheir latest RSP (effective July 2026), say this threshold will be met when:

1. They observe or expect overall capabilities progress to be going twice as fast as the fastest sustained rate before AI was contributing much to AI R&D.
2. This is plausibly attributable to AI automating AI R&D, rather than acceleration in compute or other inputs.

For the last few months, some of my colleagues at METR have been working on a deep-access investigation to understand how much Anthropic is being accelerated by its AI systems.From my reading oftheir reportand Anthropic’s RSP, it looks like the “dramatic acceleration” threshold has already been crossed.

The report authors found that:

1. Between October 2025 and October 2026, capabilities progress at Anthropic has been going about twice as fast as it had been over the previous twelve months. The measurement they report is an internal version of theEpoch Capabilities Indexcalled AECI; a wide range of other supporting evidence for acceleration has been redacted. They said that this redacted evidence was "a majority of the evidence [they] found most informative."
2. While the pace may have initially started picking up for a mix of reasons, AI automation is probably the large majority of the reason why capabilities arestillimproving at this faster pace today. Their reasons for believing this have been redacted.

This is a much bigger deal than it might sound at first. I think this report is the most important update we have gotten about AI risk in the last couple of years — much bigger thanthe Hugging Face attack. Naively, it points toward much shorter timelines than most external researchers (includingthe AI 2027 team) have projected.

Importantly,if AIis roughly doubling capabilities progress overall, it must be speeding up human researchers bymuchmore than that.
