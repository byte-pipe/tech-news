## Don't Make the Same Mistake I Did

I have plenty of regrets, and one of them is not starting to write my own content years ago. Better late than never, as they say, but still.

I want to make sure you don't make the same mistake. Every week I see more people joining this amazing community, and it reminds me how excited I was when I first signed up back in 2018. (If you're curious how I ended up here, I wrote about it inmy previous article.)

Back then, I thought writing was only for seasoned veterans. I couldn't have been more wrong. I know plenty of engineers with years of experience who still can't write a single technical article. Not because they have nothing to say, but because they never tried.

## Time to Take the Leap

Trust me, you don't need decades of experience. You're fine.

Your first article will probably feel tricky, awkward and hard. Speaking from experience, though, it gets so much easier after that. And your first one will always be special, no matter how good or bad it turns out. You might cringe at it in a year or two, but that's a special kind of cringe. It's proof of how far you've come a long way since then.

## Not Sure What to Write About?

You don't need a groundbreaking idea. Some of the best first articles are simple:

* A bug that took you way too long to fix, and what finally solved it
* Something you learned this week that you wish you'd known earlier
* How you got into programming
* A small project you built, even if nobody uses it
* A concept that finally clicked for you, explained the way you wish someone had explained it to you

And so many other ideas that you can think of.

If it was interesting to you, it'll be interesting to someone else.
