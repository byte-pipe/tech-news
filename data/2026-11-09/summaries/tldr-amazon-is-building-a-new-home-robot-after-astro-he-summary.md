# Amazon is building a new home robot after Astro. Here’s what we know

## Background
- Jeff Bezos championed a household robot in 2017, leading to the $1,450 Astro device unveiled in 2021.  
- Astro functioned as an Alexa‑enabled tablet on wheels, capable of autonomous navigation, face recognition, and security monitoring.  
- The product remained invite‑only, shipped to only a few thousand customers, and was later discontinued for both consumers and businesses.

## New Robot Initiative
- Recent job postings analyzed by CNBC reveal a newly formed “Personal Robotics Group” working on a “new‑to‑world robot product.”  
- Listings describe a robot that can navigate dynamic home environments and operate autonomously alongside family members.  
- Amazon’s CEO Andy Jassy highlighted artificial intelligence and robotics as major inflection points in his latest annual letter.

## Acquisitions & Team Expansion
- In March, Amazon acquired New York‑based Fauna Robotics, creators of the 3.5‑foot humanoid “Sprout.”  
- Fauna’s staff joined the Personal Robotics Group, alongside engineers redeployed from the Astro team and a MIT roboticist.  
- Job ads mention roles such as Field Support Engineer and User Experience Designer focused on making robots “delightful” and “friendly” for everyday spaces.

## Market Outlook
- Barclays projects the global humanoid market, currently valued at $2‑3 billion, could reach about $40 billion by 2035, or up to $200 billion in optimistic scenarios.  
- Analysts expect a surge in consumer‑focused humanoid robots as AI and hardware capabilities improve.

## Challenges & Expert Opinions
- Home environments present unique obstacles: varied layouts, stairs, children, pets, and unpredictable lighting.  
- Colin Angle (co‑founder of iRobot) noted that the tooling available to the Astro team was “primitive” compared with today’s advanced robot development kits.  
- Jeff Burnstein of the Association for Advancing Automation emphasized the difficulty of creating walking robots that can handle the diversity of real homes.

## User Experience with Astro
- Emily Lugo Velez, a North Carolina resident, has used Astro since 2023 mainly for pet and appliance checks; she describes it as a “party trick” and a “trip hazard.”  
- Former VP of consumer robotics Ken Washington revealed internal discussions about adding a manipulator arm and pursuing a bipedal design, which were rejected by leadership.

## Outlook
- Amazon’s acquisition of Fauna and the formation of a dedicated robotics division suggest a renewed, better‑resourced attempt to bring a functional, appealing home robot to market.  
- The company acknowledges the gap between laboratory prototypes and reliable operation in everyday living spaces, and is actively collaborating with the broader robotics community to bridge it.
