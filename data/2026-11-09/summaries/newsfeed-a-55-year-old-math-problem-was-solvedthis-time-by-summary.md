## Background and Graham’s Conjecture  
- Ronald Graham, a renowned mathematician and avid juggler, asked in 1971 whether any non‑zero set of integers modulo a prime p can be reordered so that all partial sums are distinct.  
- The problem is equivalent to arranging juggling throws so that no two balls land on the same beat when each ball has a different “air time.”  
- Trivial for all‑positive or mixed‑sign sets, the conjecture concerns the finite, cyclic setting of clock arithmetic.  

## Early Partial Results  
- **Large sets (almost all residues):** Alp Müyesser and Alexey Pokrovskiy (2022) used a random permutation, removed a few “spare” numbers, and repaired zero‑sum intervals by inserting those spares.  
- **Small sets (few residues relative to p):** Noah Kravitz and Benjamin Bedert (Sept 2024) tackled the opposite extreme, proving the conjecture when the chosen set is much smaller than p.  

## Bridging the Gap  
- Kravitz, Bedert, Müyesser, and additional collaborators combined the large‑set and small‑set techniques (Aug 2025) to cover many intermediate cases, but a substantial “medium‑size” range remained unresolved.  

## Final Resolution

- Their work, the fourth in a series of papers, employed probabilistic methods and new combinatorial constructions to handle the remaining medium‑size cases, thereby confirming Graham’s conjecture for all possible sets and primes.
