# A global atmospheric methane record from a tropical ice core | Nature

Here's a concise and informative summary of the article:

**Introduction to Tropical Ice Core Methane Record**

A global atmospheric methane record from a tropical ice core provides insights into the largest natural source of methane (CH4) in the atmosphere. However, inconsistencies in historical reconstructions have led to uncertainty about the contribution of tropical wetlands to CH4 production.

**New Analysis and Results**

A 2,000-year record of CH4 concentrations was reconstructed from ice cores at the South Peak summit of Nevado Huascarán (Summit Core A, SCA). The results show a consistent trend with polar records, but a significant increase since 1750 CE.

**Isotopic Measurements and Model Validation**

δ13C-CH4 measurements from the ice core confirm a dominant tropical CH4 source, with equatorial emissions rising by 160% since 1750 CE. Introduction of the new record into an atmospheric four-box model suggests sustained equatorial CH4 dominance.

**Implications and Insights**

The tropical ice core methane record highlights the importance of equatorial emissions on the global methane budget and supports the long-standing hypothesis that low-latitude CH4 emissions dominated pre-industrial variability.

**Comparison with Historical Records**

Previous estimates of CH4 emissions were based on polar ice core data, leading to large variations in modelled emissions. The new record from a tropical ice core provides a more accurate and consistent picture of CH4 variability and emissions.

### Key Findings

* Atmospheric CH4 concentrations varied between 350 and 800 ppb throughout the past 800 years.
* Previous estimates of past CH4 emissions still contain uncertainty.
* The tropical ice core record provides a more accurate and consistent picture of CH4 variability and emissions.
* Equatorial CH4 dominance was confirmed with δ13C-CH4 measurements.
