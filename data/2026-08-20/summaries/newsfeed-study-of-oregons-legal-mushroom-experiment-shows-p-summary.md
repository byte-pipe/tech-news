# Study of Oregon's legal mushroom experiment shows promise

## Overview
- Oregon launched the first U.S. state‑run, non‑medical psilocybin program in 2020, allowing adults 21+ to use magic mushrooms under supervised facilitators.
- A recent real‑world study analyzed almost 350 participants who received psilocybin at state‑licensed centers, representing a subset of the >20,000 users since the program’s inception.

## Health Findings
- Most participants described their experience as beneficial; over half rated it among the most meaningful moments of their lives.
- More than half reported improvement in mental‑health symptoms after the session.
- The average dose administered was ~30 mg, slightly higher than typical clinical trial doses.
- Positive changes in well‑being were comparable to effects reported from meditation studies.

## Safety and Adverse Events
- Serious adverse reactions were rare: only four participants (first‑time users) experienced events classified as serious (e.g., violent reaction, severe anxiety requiring emergency care).
- Behavioral health issues were the primary safety concerns; physical health problems were minimal.
- One week after sessions, three participants reported new thoughts of death or suicide, rising to six by the three‑month follow‑up.

## Demographics & Limitations
- Study participants were predominantly white, college‑educated, with about one‑third earning > $200,000 annually.
- The sample mirrors the broader Oregon psilocybin user base concentrated around Portland but remains a small, possibly non‑representative group.
- Researchers caution that findings may not generalize to all users; anecdotal reports suggest some individuals experience lingering difficulties.

## Future Directions
- The research team plans to expand monitoring through the Open Psychedelic Evaluation Nexus (OPEN) to include a larger, more diverse cohort.
- Positive early results support the viability of a regulated psychedelic industry and suggest psilocybin may address a range of mental‑health concerns beyond the initial indication.
