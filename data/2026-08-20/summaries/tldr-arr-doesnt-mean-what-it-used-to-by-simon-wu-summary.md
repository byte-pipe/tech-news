# ARR Doesn't Mean What It Used To

## Overview
- The article surveys 15 venture capitalists about metrics they no longer trust at face value.  
- Focus is on how ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue) and related metrics have become ambiguous, especially in AI‑driven businesses.  
- The author, Simon Wu, highlights the need for clearer definitions and deeper qualitative assessment.

## ARR – the most mistrusted metric
- **Definition drift**: VCs see ARR used for revenue, GMV, quarterly‑times‑4, or even 365‑day daily revenue calculations.  
- **Lack of recurrence**: Many companies report ARR that is not truly recurring; growth can be a one‑off spike.  
- **Recommendation**: Founders should spell out their ARR calculation on the first deck slide to build credibility.

## CARR (Contracted/Committed ARR) – the runner‑up concern
- **Over‑reliance on contracts**: CARR multiples are viewed as “fake” valuation tools because signed contracts often do not convert to cash.  
- **Conversion risk**: Customer onboarding delays, implementation uncertainties, and changing mindsets cause large gaps between CARR and realized revenue.  
- **Alternative focus**: Some VCs prefer live ARR and direct customer validation over contracted figures.

## Consumption‑pricing wrinkle
- **Blended models**: PLG and consumer AI firms mix recurring subscriptions with usage‑based pricing, making simple annualization misleading.  
- **Capital intensity**: For AI infrastructure businesses, headline ARR can hide financing costs, revenue‑sharing deals, and heavy capex, obscuring true free‑cash‑flow generation.  
- **Seasonality/lumpiness**: Annualizing revenue from a single hot month can inflate ARR for AI‑native services.

## Gross margin skepticism
- **Compute cost mismatch**: Flat pricing with usage‑scaled costs leads to gross margins that assume low customer consumption, which often collapses as the product gains traction.  
- **Hidden costs**: Some AI firms push front‑end delivery (FDE) expenses into OpEx, inflating gross margin figures; true margins may be far lower once integration costs are accounted for.

## NRR (Net Revenue Retention) doubts
- **POC to ARR conversion**: Companies start with small proofs of concept, convert them to contracted ARR, and then report inflated NRR that isn’t comparable to traditional definitions.

## Structural perspective on metrics‑based investing
- **Shift from benchmarks**: Pre‑AI investing relied on clear benchmarks (growth, NRR, GRR, burn). AI’s rapid, uneven growth makes pure metric‑driven decisions harder.  
- **Qualitative judgment**: VCs now place more weight on durability, product‑market fit, and operational realities than on headline numbers.

## Beyond SaaS – other industries feel the same strain
- The same metric‑obfuscation appears in non‑software sectors, just wrapped in different KPI terminology.

## Takeaway
- Metrics like ARR, CARR, gross margin, and NRR have become less reliable signals in the AI era.  
- Clear definitions, transparent calculations, and deeper qualitative analysis are essential for founders seeking investment and for VCs evaluating opportunities.
