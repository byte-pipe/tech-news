- Journals and integrity staff are unprepared for the surge of AI‑generated content; more resources are needed to detect and filter distortions.

- Chemists Anthony Cheetham and Ram Seshadri found many of DeepMind’s claimed novel crystal structures to be minor variations of known materials, urging more human validation.  
- AI‑generated “slop” – papers written by models with fake citations or phantom results – is increasingly detected; a study by Prof Dmitry Kobak found AI fingerprints in ~90 % of biomedical papers by late 2023.  

## Core concerns  
- **Trust:** AI‑driven hallucinations may blur the line between genuine discovery and fabricated claims, threatening the foundation of scientific credibility.  
- **Verification:** Human oversight remains essential to confirm AI outputs, especially when models may overstate significance or produce erroneous data.  
- **Policy:** Publishers and research institutions need stronger detection tools and stricter review processes to safeguard the integrity of the scientific record.
