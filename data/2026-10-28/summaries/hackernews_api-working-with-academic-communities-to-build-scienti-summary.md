# Working with academic communities to build scientific understanding

## Overview
- OpenAI announces three AI‑generated discoveries in mathematics, computer science, and physics.
- Introduces a new release model that partners external expert “shepherds” with academic communities.
- Three distinct release strategies are being trialed, one for each discipline.

## Release strategies
- **Mathematics** – Publish the AI output directly; researchers may organize their own efforts to interpret the result.  
- **Computer science** – Form an external collaboration to explain the result while also making the AI output publicly available for anyone to examine and build upon.  
- **Physics** – Form an external collaboration and defer all release decisions to that group.

## Principles for external collaborations
1. **Transparency** – No nondisclosure agreements; participants can discuss, share intermediate products, and publish conclusions.  
2. **No academic credit** – OpenAI does not claim credit for the discoveries; inviting researchers does not imply they made the original finding.  
3. **Recognition** – The collaborating group receives credit for the understanding, write‑ups, and any additional materials they produce.  
4. **Independence** – Participation is voluntary and unpaid; members speak for themselves, not for OpenAI, and vice‑versa.

## Mathematics: Local smoothing and the Kakeya conjecture
- AI produced a proof of the local smoothing conjecture in every dimension, which simultaneously resolves the Kakeya conjecture and Stein’s restriction conjecture.
- The proof relies on a novel auxiliary partial differential equation.
- AI output generated on 18 Oct 2026, formalized in Lean on 19 Oct 2026, and both the natural‑language write‑up and Lean code were released on OpenAI’s public GitHub repository.

## Computer science: Refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH)
- AI disproved the longstanding ETH; the disproof was verified in Lean.
- Collaboration led by Russell Impagliazzo (UC San Diego), Ramamohan Paturi (UC San Diego), and Ryan Williams (MIT), all key contributors to ETH research.
- Raw data and proofs are publicly available; OpenAI will fund a conference to explore the implications for algorithms, verification, and research practice.
- AI natural‑language disproof produced on 16 Oct 2026, Lean formalization completed on 17 Oct 2026; both released on the public GitHub repository.

## Physics: Quantum field theory result
- AI generated a new result in quantum field theory.
- The external collaboration chose to withhold public release of AI‑produced artifacts while they prepare a written exposition.
- All materials will be handed over to the collaborating group for further development.

## Trade‑offs and feedback request
- The shepherd model empowers experts to unpack and contextualize results but may create information asymmetry by selecting initial participants.
- OpenAI invites feedback on how to broaden participation, improve transparency, and make the process more useful for the wider academic community.
