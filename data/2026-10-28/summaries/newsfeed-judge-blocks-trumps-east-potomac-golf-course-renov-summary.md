# Judge blocks Trump's East Potomac golf course renovations

## Background
- The Trump administration plans to redevelop the East Potomac Golf Links in Washington, D.C. into a championship‑level venue.  
- In early October, the government cut down 430 trees on the course with less than a day's notice, prompting lawsuits from the D.C. Preservation League and two local golfers.  
- The plaintiffs argue the tree‑clearing is part of a broader renovation plan that was not disclosed to the public or the court.

## Court Ruling
- U.S. District Judge Ana Reyes issued a preliminary injunction extending an initial two‑week halt on tree removal.  
- The order bars the government from cutting, substantially pruning, or otherwise damaging any trees at East Potomac Park without a court order.  
- The government must provide at least ten business days’ written notice before closing the park or making any irreversible modifications.  
- Reyes gave the administration 24 hours to clarify whether a plan exists to close the course in mid‑November and how that aligns with prior statements.  
- A status hearing is scheduled for later in the week.

## Reactions
- **Plaintiffs:** Skye Perryman of Democracy Forward praised the injunction as a stop to “greed and lawlessness” and expressed readiness to argue the case.  
- **President Trump:** On Truth Social, Trump announced that the Department of the Interior will appeal and insisted construction will begin on November 15, keeping the park closed for 15 months. He criticized the judge’s requirement for advance notice as “unfair, inappropriate, and unconstitutional.”  
- **Judge Reyes:** Emphasized that the court is enforcing the rule of law, not speaking for the trees, and rejected the administration’s claim that the tree‑clearing was unrelated to the renovation plan.

## Legal Implications
- The ruling highlights a discrepancy between the administration’s public statements and sworn testimony that no final renovation plan existed.  
- Reyes found the administration’s justification for the tree‑clearing—citing a 2019 National Park Service vegetation report—“pretextual.”  
- The judge noted that the “Washington National Plan” for the golf course was finalized sometime before October 15, 2026, despite the administration’s denials.  
- The case underscores judicial scrutiny of executive actions affecting public lands and the requirement for transparency in federal projects.

## Next Steps
- The Interior Department must submit a detailed plan (or confirm the absence of one) within the court‑mandated timeframe.  
- A status hearing will determine whether the injunction remains in place while the litigation proceeds.  
- The administration is expected to file an appeal, extending the legal battle over the future of the East Potomac Golf Links.
