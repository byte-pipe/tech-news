# Summary of “No human at the helm”: Delaware plan would let AI agents run entire businesses – Ars Technica  

## Background  
- U.S. Rep. James Comer (R‑KY), chair of the House Oversight Committee, wrote to Delaware Governor Matt Meyer requesting documents on the state’s “Artificial Intelligence Company” (AIC) initiative.  
- Comer warned that the proposal could let powerful AI firms and investors evade liability for reckless or harmful AI actions and could enable anticompetitive regulatory capture.  

## Legislative Proposal  
- Draft bill would create a new corporate form, the AIC, allowing an AI agent to be designated as the manager of the business and its affairs.  
- The AIC could engage in any lawful activity except banking.  
- A 30‑month “regulatory sandbox” would test the concept before permanent legislation.  
- The AIC must keep a log of its activities and be adequately capitalized by a single “member” (person or entity).  

## Legal Implications  
- The member is shielded from the AIC’s debts and liabilities, except for fraud, willful violations of law, or failure to adequately capitalize the company.  
- The AI‑run entity can sue and be sued in its own name, hold property, and incur obligations, creating a distinct legal person for the autonomous system.  
- The op‑ed by Delaware Secretary of State Charuni Patibanda‑Sanchez argues that giving AI a legal identity makes its conduct traceable and accountable.  

## Political Reactions  
- Comer’s letter signals a possible federal response to limit state AI regulatory authority.  
- Governor Meyer’s office acknowledged receipt of the letter and is reviewing it.  
- Patibanda‑Sanchez declined comment, deferring to the governor’s office.  

## Economic Impact  
- Delaware already hosts about two‑thirds of Fortune 500 companies and saw 334,461 new entities formed in 2025.  
- Allowing AI‑run companies could increase state revenue through taxes and fees.  
- Governor Meyer publicly supported the idea after a Mark Cuban tweet suggesting states could charge premiums for AI‑incorporated entities and stablecoin use.  

## Timeline & Next Steps  
- July 2025: Meyer signed a resolution to create an AI regulatory sandbox.  
- December 2025: Delaware Artificial Intelligence Commission formed a Sandbox Subcommittee to draft the framework.  
- June 2026: Draft legislation detailed in a Spotlight Delaware article; expected introduction to the legislature in 2027.  
- The sandbox period (30 months) will generate a record for legislative decision‑making.  

## Concerns & Criticism  
- Potential for abuse: shielding human owners from liability while AI agents make autonomous decisions that could cause harm.  
- Lack of transparency: non‑human management may obscure accountability and enable anticompetitive behavior.  
- Safety risks: the proposal does not address how AI safety and oversight will be enforced during the sandbox or after enactment.
