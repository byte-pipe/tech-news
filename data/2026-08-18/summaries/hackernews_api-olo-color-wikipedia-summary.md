# Olo (color)

## Definition
- Olo is an imaginary color that can only be perceived when the M‑cone cells of the retina are activated in isolation.  
- Normal viewing conditions cannot produce olo because the spectral sensitivities of M cones overlap with those of S and L cones, preventing a monochromatic stimulus that excites only M cones.  
- Researchers mapped individual cone types on a retinal patch and used targeted laser pulses to stimulate M cones while avoiding S and L cones.  
- The closest representation of olo within the sRGB gamut is the hexadecimal colour #00FFCC.

## Discovery
- The colour was reported on 18 April 2025 by a team at the University of California, Berkeley.  
- Its name derives from its theoretical LMS colour‑space coordinates (0, 1, 0), which read “olo” in leet‑speak.  
- Only five participants in the Berkeley experiment have officially seen olo.  
- Participants described it as a blue‑green hue of unprecedented saturation, “more saturated than any colour you can see in the real world.”  
- The researchers are investigating whether the laser‑stimulus technique could be adapted to improve colour perception for people with colour‑blindness or to enable a form of enhanced vision (tetrachromacy).

## Legitimacy and Reception
- The method of precisely stimulating individual photoreceptors with lasers is regarded as a significant technical achievement.  
- Some scientists, such as Prof. John Barbur (University of London), question whether olo truly constitutes a new colour, suggesting its status is open to debate.  
- The concept has attracted interest beyond academia, with artists exploring paint formulations inspired by olo and media outlets seeking demonstrations of the phenomenon.
