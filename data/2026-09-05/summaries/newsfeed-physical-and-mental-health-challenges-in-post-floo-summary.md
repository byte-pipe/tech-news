# Physical and mental health challenges in post‑flood Nepal  

## Overview  
- Catastrophic flash floods in August 2026 devastated the Bidur municipality and surrounding areas, wiping out homes, businesses, and critical infrastructure.  
- Over 5,600 people remain missing; at least 1,200 deaths were reported in Nepal and neighboring Tibet.  

## Damage to health infrastructure  
- Seven hospitals and health centres were completely destroyed or severely damaged.  
- Remaining facilities face blocked roads, disrupted electricity, and limited access; the bridge to Trishuli hospital now requires a 10‑hour trek.  
- Routine medical services, including prenatal care, are interrupted; UNICEF is deploying medical tents and distributing hygiene kits and bed nets to 19,000 people.  

## Risks to physical health  
- Chronic‑disease patients (diabetes, hypertension, cancer) lack medication and guidance, worsening their conditions.  
- Contaminated water supplies raise the threat of water‑borne epidemics such as cholera and diarrheal disease.  
- Overcrowded temporary shelters increase the likelihood of airborne illnesses like tuberculosis; the Ministry of Health has sent technical staff to strengthen disease surveillance.  

## Emerging mental‑health crisis  
- Approximately 2,500–3,000 survivors are housed in temporary holding centers, many experiencing profound grief and trauma.  
- Children repeatedly look to rescue helicopters, hoping for missing family members; some report sleeplessness and severe emotional distress.  
- Survivors express shock and loss (“The dead are gone, I am the one alive,” “I lost my children”).  
- Save the Children and other NGOs are providing counselors, but mental‑health support is often deprioritized and may be withdrawn before long‑term needs are met.  

## Expert observations  
- Shushma Bhusal (IFRC) describes the area as “nothing left there,” highlighting total community devastation.  
- Padam Simkhada (University of Chester) states the Nepalese health system was unprepared for a disaster of this magnitude.  
- WHO representative Allison Gocotano emphasizes ongoing challenges with access, electricity, and disease surveillance.  

## Immediate response needs  
- Restore transportation routes and bridge access to reconnect communities with health services.  
- Ensure continuous supply of essential medicines and clear dosage instructions for chronic‑illness patients.  
- Implement rapid water‑sanitation interventions to prevent epidemics.  
- Prioritize sustained mental‑health services in shelters and displaced‑person camps.
