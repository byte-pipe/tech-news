# Report finds Trump’s labor secretary oversaw hostile work environment  

## Overview  
- The Labor Department’s Office of Inspector General released a report concluding that former Labor Secretary **Lori Chavez‑DeRemer** violated the department’s anti‑harassment policy.  
- The investigation began after an anonymous complaint and included interviews with 53 current and former employees; Chavez‑DeRemer was not interviewed and resigned a day before she was scheduled to be.  
- Her attorney noted the report found no criminal violations, only breaches of internal policy.  

## Key Findings  

### 1. Awareness of harassing behavior without intervention  
- Staff described a “toxic, humiliating and intimidating” atmosphere in the Office of the Secretary.  
- Chief of staff Jihun Han and deputy chief of staff Rebecca Wright used threatening, demeaning, and abusive language, commented on appearance and hygiene, and threatened termination.  
- Wright allegedly hired based on looks, told an employee not to cut her hair “to avoid turning into a lesbian,” and moved a “fat person” away from the front office.  
- Investigators concluded Chavez‑DeRemer knew of this conduct and failed to stop it or discipline the perpetrators.  

### 2. Inappropriate relationship with a security detail member and employee pressure  
- Evidence showed Chavez‑DeRemer maintained an unprofessional, overly close relationship with a member of her security detail (ASAIC 1).  
- She asked her driver to accompany the agent and a strip‑club performer, gave the driver money to tip the performer, and pressured the agent to comply despite his reluctance.  

### 3. Use of staff for personal errands  
- A personal aide and executive assistant used department transportation to clean Chavez‑DeRemer’s closet at her home during work hours, handling laundry, dry‑cleaning, and organizing clothing and accessories.  
- The report deemed these tasks outside official duties and a violation of ethical standards.  

### 4. Misuse of official travel for personal purposes  
- Chavez‑DeRemer’s “America at Work” tour was sometimes used to mask personal trips.  
- Deputy chief of staff Wright added official engagements to trips that were primarily for Chavez‑DeRemer’s personal reasons, such as attending a rodeo in Las Vegas after receiving complimentary tickets from a lobbyist.  
- The tickets, valued at about $100 each, were not reported as required by ethics rules.  

## Additional Context  
- The investigation was led by former Republican Congressman Anthony D’Esposito.  
- After Chavez‑DeRemer’s resignation, deputy Keith Sonderling became acting Labor Secretary.
