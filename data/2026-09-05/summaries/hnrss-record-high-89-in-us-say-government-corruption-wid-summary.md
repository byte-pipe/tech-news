# Record-High 89% in U.S. Say Government Corruption Widespread

# Summary of “Record-High 89% in U.S. Say Government Corruption Widespread”

## Main Findings
- 89 % of U.S. adults surveyed in 2026 say government corruption is widespread, the highest level in the past 20 years and a 10‑point rise from the previous year.  
- The perception has been above 72‑79 % for most of the 2010‑2025 period.  

## Partisan Trends
- Democrats’ perception rose sharply: 57 % in 2024 (Biden’s final year), 76 % in 2025 (first year of Trump’s second term), and 91 % in 2026.  
- Independents reported 90 % in 2026, closely matching Democrats (91 %).  
- Republicans remained relatively stable, around 80‑87 % over the same period; 83 % said corruption is widespread in 2026.  
- The convergence of Democratic and Republican views contributed to the overall record high.  

## International Comparison
- Among the 38 OECD member countries, the median perception of government corruption fell from 69 % in 2009 to below 60 % since 2022.  
- In 2025 the OECD median was 59 %; the U.S. at 79 % was 20 points higher, placing it at the top of the OECD rankings.  
- Only four of 132 countries surveyed by Gallup since 2023 have ever exceeded the U.S. 89 % level (Lebanon 92 % in 2024, Peru 92 % in 2025, Ghana and Nigeria 90 % in 2024).  

## Government vs. Business Corruption Perceptions
- Perceived business corruption in the U.S. rose to 71 % in 2026, eight points higher than the previous year.  
- The gap between government (89 %) and business (71 %) corruption perceptions is 18 %, the largest observed in the U.S. since 2006.  
- Historically, the gap averaged 11 % and few countries have shown a larger divergence.  

## Survey Methodology
- Telephone interviews conducted May 1–June 10 2026 with a random sample of 1,000 U.S. adults (aged 15+).  
- Margin of sampling error: ±4.1 % at the 95 % confidence level, including design effects for weighting.  
- Additional sources of error may arise from question wording and practical survey challenges.  

## Bottom Line
- U.S. perception of government corruption has reached a two‑decade high and now exceeds that of any OECD country.  
- While business corruption is also viewed as widespread, the public sees government corruption as markedly more pervasive.  
- The near‑parity of Democratic and Republican concerns underscores a bipartisan alarm over corruption in the United States.
