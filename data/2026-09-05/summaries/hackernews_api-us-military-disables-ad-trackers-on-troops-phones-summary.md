# US military disables ad trackers on troops’ phones amid security fears

## Background
- Reports indicated that commercial location‑data brokers were able to track U.S. forces deployed in the Middle East.  
- Senator Ron Wyden (D‑OR) has been urging the Pentagon for months to address the risk posed by advertising identifiers (MAIDs) that reveal device location.

## Actions taken by the services
- **Air Force:** Disabled advertising identifiers on computers and mobile phones two months ago.  
- **U.S. Special Operations Command:** Recently turned off MAIDs on its Windows devices.  
- **Army:**  
  - Advertising IDs on Windows computers blocked “since before 2021”.  
  - Android and Apple mobile devices had MAIDs disabled by default “since at least February 2026”.  
- **Navy:** Sent a letter confirming disabling of advertising IDs, but gave no timeline; no comment was returned to Reuters.

## Legislative response
- Wyden and Rep. Pat Harrigan (R‑NC) sent a joint letter to the Pentagon requesting an investigation into whether the military has adequately mitigated the location‑data threat.  
- Wyden stated the military’s measures “have not been effective at neutralizing this threat”.  
- Harrigan warned that adversaries could “pull out a credit card and buy information that helps them track American troops”.  
- The Pentagon replied that it will respond directly to the lawmakers.

## Security implications
- Mobile advertising IDs are unique device identifiers that can be used to track a person’s movements across apps and infer physical location.  
- Disabling MAIDs reduces the amount of location data that can be sold in bulk by data‑broker firms.  
- Experts note that troops could still be tracked through other methods, such as triangulating network data or app‑level information.

## Expert commentary
- Zach Edwards, co‑founder of privacy‑focused ad‑tech firm DecryptAds, called the MAID‑disablement “definitely a positive thing”.  
- He cautioned that more sophisticated tracking techniques could still expose device locations despite the new restrictions.
