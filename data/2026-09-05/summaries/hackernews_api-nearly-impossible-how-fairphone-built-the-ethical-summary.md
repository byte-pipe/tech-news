# Nearly impossible? How Fairphone built the ethical, repairable Fairphone Gen 6+

## Overview
- Fairphone, a Dutch maker now in the US, focuses on longevity and repairability rather than frequent upgrades.  
- The Gen 6+ costs $650 and aims for a minimum five‑year usage, tracked as a key performance indicator.  
- The phone is modular, allowing users to replace up to 12 components with a single T5 Torx screwdriver.

## Design for Repair
- Uses plastic housing with an internal metal frame, making it slightly thicker than typical glass‑and‑aluminum phones.  
- No glue; parts are secured with screws and flat board‑to‑board connectors that can be popped off with a spudger or fingernail.  
- Battery, camera modules, USB‑C port, display, and other major parts are user‑replaceable.  
- Disassembly takes about 15–20 minutes for a novice after consulting Fairphone’s documentation.  
- Tests are performed during development to ensure robustness after repeated disassembly and reassembly, including tolerance for over‑tightened screws.

## Sustainability & Ethics
- Emphasizes conflict‑free minerals, recycled materials, and living‑wage labor.  
- Designed to reduce e‑waste by extending device life and enabling component swaps instead of full replacements.  
- The company rejects planned obsolescence; the business model relies on selling a single durable phone rather than frequent new models.

- **Memory / Storage:** 12 GB RAM, 256 GB internal + microSD slot

## User Experience
- The only glass component is the OLED screen, which is fully replaceable via a few screws and a long ribbon cable.  
- Screen brightness is adequate for outdoor use but lower than premium flagship displays.  
- Retains features often removed for space savings: microSD slot and physical SIM tray.  
- The modular approach adds a millimeter or two to thickness but maintains a solid feel.

## Pros and Cons
- **Pros:** High repairability, ethical sourcing, long‑term support, modular upgrades, transparent sustainability goals.  
- **Cons:** Slightly thicker and heavier than glued‑in competitors, lower peak brightness, higher repair cost for some parts compared to buying a new flagship.  

## Conclusion
Fairphone’s Gen 6+ demonstrates that a high‑performing, modular smartphone can be built without sacrificing core functionality. While trade‑offs exist—chiefly in size, weight, and display brightness—the device offers a compelling option for consumers who prioritize durability, repairability, and ethical manufacturing over the latest design trends.
