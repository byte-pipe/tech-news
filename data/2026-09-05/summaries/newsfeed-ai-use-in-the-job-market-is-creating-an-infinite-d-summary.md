# AI Use in the Job Market Is Creating an Infinite Doom Loop

## Overview
- Job‑search platforms increasingly claim to optimize résumés for applicant tracking systems (ATS) using AI.
- The belief that most companies rely on AI to rank candidates creates a feedback loop where both seekers and employers double‑down on automation, often to their own detriment.

## The ATS and AI Optimization Tools
- Tools such as Jobscan compare a résumé to a job description and score the match, suggesting changes (e.g., length, use of symbols, consistency of initials).
- Companies’ ATS vary widely; some incorporate AI, others rely on manual review, and features can change rapidly.
- Candidates often assume AI is involved and spend time “gaming” the system, even when the employer may not use AI at all.
- Subscription costs for optimization services can be $30‑$50 per month, creating a financial incentive for the service providers to keep users dependent.

## The “Doom Loop” Phenomenon
- Daniel Chait, CEO of Greenhouse, describes a tragic cycle: each side uses AI to solve a perceived problem, which amplifies the original issue and leads to more AI use without benefiting anyone.
- Low‑hire economies, scarce postings, and fraudulent job ads exacerbate mistrust; applicants feel they are sending applications into a black box, while employers receive near‑identical submissions.

## Human‑Centric Hiring vs. AI Ranking
- Some recruiters rely entirely on human review (e.g., Toshiba’s Kim Jones), focusing on job requirements, salary expectations, and cultural fit rather than keyword scores.
- Others experiment with AI ranking but find it unreliable; Doist’s experiments showed that candidates hired by humans were often excluded from AI shortlists.
- Unwanted AI use can appear in interviews, where candidates rely on AI‑generated responses that feel inauthentic.

## Case Studies
- **Jodi Beggs**: A data scientist whose résumé was penalized for being two pages and inconsistent use of a middle initial; minor changes (e.g., using “%”) improved her score, highlighting the granular focus of optimization tools.
- **Kim Jones (Toshiba)**: Emphasizes human review of every application; sees AI‑polished materials as irrelevant to ATS passage and worries about AI‑generated interview answers.
- **Nadia Vatalidis (Doist)**: Tested AI shortlisting on previously filled roles; the actual hires never appeared on AI shortlists, suggesting the algorithm missed strong candidates.
- **James Jacobsen**: Built a personal AI‑driven job‑search tracker that filtered listings, scored opportunities, and revised his portfolio; despite increased efficiency, he still received no offers, illustrating limits of AI assistance.

## Recommendations for Job Seekers
- Stop “spray‑and‑pray” applications; focus on deep research of target companies, even lesser‑known ones.
- Submit a cover letter when possible; it can differentiate candidates in human‑review processes.
- Leverage networking and personal connections rather than relying solely on algorithmic matching.
- Accept that systemic issues, not personal shortcomings, often hinder progress.

## Conclusions
- The current reliance on AI for résumé optimization and candidate ranking creates an “infinite doom loop” that harms both job seekers and employers.
- A balanced approach that combines thoughtful human evaluation with selective, purposeful AI use may break the cycle and restore trust in the hiring process.
