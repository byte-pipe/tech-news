# New immigration court locations throw chaos into due process

## Overview
- Immigrants have been given last‑minute notices to appear at previously undisclosed courtroom locations.
- Five immigration attorneys who reviewed the notices or observed the hearings told NPR the practice appears to be part of a broader Trump‑administration strategy to limit due‑process opportunities and accelerate removals.

## Tactic and its consequences
- Hearings originally scheduled at the Hyattsville and Baltimore courts (Maryland) and the Sterling court (Virginia) were reassigned to two buildings: one in Washington, D.C., and another in Falls Church, Va.
- Neither building is listed as an in‑person immigration court; the D.C. site is identified as a Federal Emergency Management Agency office.
- Notices were sent only days before the new dates, and in at least two cases no notice was sent at all.
- Immigrants who miss a hearing can receive an “in‑absentia order of removal,” allowing immediate detention and deportation.

## Legal and procedural concerns
- Kel White, officer of the D.C. chapter of the American Immigration Lawyers Association, called the practice “the most unusual thing” she has seen in 15 years of practice.
- She cited a Justice Department policy manual requiring at least ten days’ notice for a hearing change, which was not met.
- Attorneys say the notices target unrepresented immigrants, who make up the majority of those trying to argue to stay in the U.S.

## EOIR response
- The Executive Office for Immigration Review (EOIR) said it continues to add judges and adjust scheduling, including opening new hearing locations, to handle cases “in a timely and lawful manner.”
- EOIR did not explain why immigrants were directed to the unfamiliar sites or confirm that the sites are new immigration courts.

## Trump administration context
- The administration has pursued a goal of reducing the 3.5 million‑case backlog, framing immigration courts as obstacles to rapid deportation.
- An April White House press release highlighted an “aggressive and successful immigration enforcement overhaul” that extends to immigration courts.

## Specific site observations
- **Falls Church, Va.**: The hearing took place in a government building that houses EOIR headquarters and the Board of Immigration Appeals, but not a regular courtroom. Judges and prosecutors appeared remotely; several attendees were ordered deported for not appearing.
- **Washington, D.C.**: Scheduled to begin hearings on Sept. 9 at Federal Center Plaza (400 C St SW), a location with no DOJ courtrooms. The General Services Administration (GSA) did not comment on the use of the space.

## GSA leasing surge
- Sources report the GSA issued a public request for lease proposals for 18 potential locations, requiring space for at least 10 courtrooms, with a target to secure space by December.
- No public announcement has been made about the acquisition or conversion of the D.C. or Falls Church sites.

## Immigrant experience
- An individual who relied on the MigraConnect app discovered a hearing date change from December to Sept. 2 with only a two‑week notice; without the app they would have missed the hearing and faced removal.
