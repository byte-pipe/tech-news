### 📌 HackerRank Orchestrate

Who it's for:Developers, students, and builders who want to try building an AI agent in a short, focused challenge. It's open to anyone, and the challenge issolo.

What stands out:HackerRank Orchestrateis a 24-hour hackathon where you'll receive a real-world problem and build an agent-based solution for it. The challenge starts onSeptember 12 at 6:00 PM IST, and you'll have 24 hours to build and submit your solution.

I wanted to include this one because it's a little different from the usual hackathons where you know the problem before you start. Here, the challenge is sent to you when the hackathon begins, so you'll need to figure out the architecture, retrieval strategy, model choice, prompts, and the rest of the approach yourself.

You can also use the tools you already work with, includingClaude Code, Cursor, Codex, or Copilot. The submission includes your code, the agent's output, and your AI chat transcript, so they're looking at more than just the final result.

There's also an interesting part after submission: a30-minute voice interview with an AI judge, where you'll walk through your architecture, decisions, tradeoffs, safety choices, and code. So if you're curious about what it feels like to have your AI project evaluated beyond a demo, this could be an interesting challenge to try.

There are cash prizes too, with$1,200 for first placeand$300 for places 2–5, along with other rewards. The top three winners who build with Codex can also receive additional Codex credits.

One thing worth knowing before signing up is that this is a24-hour challenge, and the problem itself isn't revealed until the start. So it may be more suited to people who enjoy working under a tight deadline rather than those looking for a longer, more relaxed hackathon.

Prize:$1,200 for 1st place | $300 for 2nd–5th place

Format:Online |Duration:24 hours |Team:Solo

Challenge Starts:September 12, 2026 at 6:00 PM IST

What you need to build:An AI agent that solves a real-world problem provided when the challenge begins.

Evaluation:Code, agent output, AI chat transcript, and a 30-minute AI judge interview.

Eligibility:Anyone can participate | No prior AI experience required

### 📌 Claude Campus Ambassador Program

Who it's for:Students who want to help other students learn about and use AI on their campus. There are separate tracks forundergraduates, master's students, and PhD students/postdocs, with different responsibilities for each.

What stands out:TheClaude Campus Ambassador programfromAnthropicis back for the 2026–27 school year, and applications are currently open.

I wanted to include this because it's a little different from the usual student programs I come across. Instead of being focused mainly on learning or completing a project, you're helping build an AI community on your campus and organizing activities for other students.

Forundergraduate students, the program involves leading aClaude Builder Club, organizing technical and non-technical workshops, running hackathons or demo nights, and sharing resources with members. Selected ambassadors receive a$3,600 cash stipend.

Formaster's students, there's aClaude Campus Conversationstrack focused more on discussions around AI and its impact on education and society. You'd organize discussion groups and events, while also helping students share and discuss the AI projects they're building.

ForPhD students and postdocs, theClaude Sciencetrack is focused on researchers using AI in their work. This includes leading workshops for other researchers, organizing Claude for Science sessions, and engaging with Anthropic's science teams.

All three tracks also give ambassadors opportunities to participate in workshops led by Anthropic, connect with other ambassadors, and share feedback with Anthropic's teams.

A few things are worth knowing before applying. The program runs for thefull school year, from September 2026 through June 2027, so this is more of an ongoing commitment than a short-term program. You also need to be18 or olderand have work authorization in your country of study.

The program is open to studentsanywhere in the world, although the initial pilot for the graduate and PhD tracks may select from a smaller group of schools. You don't need to be an AI expert, but Anthropic does expect some level of AI fluency and familiarity with Claude.

Stipend:$3,600

Format:Campus-based |Program Duration:September 2026 – June 2027

Tracks:Claude Builder Club (Undergraduate), Claude Campus Conversations (Master's), and Claude Science (PhD/Postdocs)

Eligibility:Students worldwide |Age:18+

Application Deadline:September 12, 2026

Cost:Free to apply and participate

### 📌 Bharat Builds Tour

Who it's for:University students in India who want to build projects through a series of online and in-person hackathons. You must be18 or olderto register on your own.

What stands out:TheBharat Builds Tour, organized byWeMakeDevs in collaboration with AWS Builder Center, is a six-stop hackathon series where students can participate online from anywhere in India. Each stop has its own hackathon, and you can take part in as many of the six as you'd like.

I wanted to include this one even though it's geographically limited because there is ahiring componentthat I don't come across very often in student hackathons.

For students graduating in2027 or 2028, some of the top projects can be eligible for afast-track interview opportunity with Amazon's University Talent Acquisition Teamfor available internship and full-time roles. The fast-track means the usual screening round is skipped and the project is used in its place.

That doesn't mean winning a hackathon automatically gets you an Amazon interview, though. The rules say that being shortlisted for a prize and being put forward for an interview are separate decisions, and the interview opportunity is also subject to Amazon's eligibility requirements, available roles, and business needs. So I'd definitely read the terms before counting on the hiring part.

The tour itself includessix hackathons, with the first one,First Commit, taking place September 17–20. Every stop has an online option for students across India, while the city hosting that particular stop also has limited in-person seats.

You can participatesolo or in teams of up to four, and you don't need previous cloud experience. Each project does need to use AWS, and submissions include a public repository, a short demo video, and a write-up explaining the project and where AWS is used.

There are also prizes across the tour, with a total prize value of₹1 crore in gadgets and cloud credits. Participants can also get access to AWS learning resources, credits, a certification voucher, mentors, and a WeMakeDevs certificate.

One thing worth knowing is thatone registration covers all six stops, but you still need to check in to each hackathon as it opens. You'll also need an AWS Builder Center profile and verified student status before your entry can be scored.

Prize Pool:Prizes worth ₹1 crore across six hackathons

Format:Online across India + limited in-person seats

First Hackathon:September 17–20, 2026

Team Size:Solo or up to 4 people

Eligibility:University students in India, 18+

Hiring:Top projects from eligible 2027 and 2028 students may receive a fast-track interview opportunity with Amazon

Cost:Free

### The System Design Primer

If system design is something you're trying to learn, this is one resource I came across that is worth keeping around.

TheSystem Design Primeris a free, open-source GitHub repository with a collection of system design topics, interview questions, real-world architectures, and other resources. It also has a study guide that gives you an idea of what to focus on depending on how much time you have.

It covers things likescalability, latency, availability, consistency, DNS, CDNs, load balancers, databases, caching, microservices, and distributed systems.

What I liked here is that it also has actual system design questions to work through, such asPastebin, Twitter, a web crawler, a social network, and scaling a system to millions of users on AWS.

There is also a section on how to approach a system design interview, starting with requirements and constraints, then moving into the high-level design, core components, scaling, and trade-offs.

So if you're just getting started with system design, or preparing for interviews and don't know what topics to go through first, this could be a good place to start.

Format:Free, open-source GitHub repository

Covers:System design, scalability, databases, caching, distributed systems, networking, microservices, and more.

Also includes:System design interview questions, real-world architectures, and Anki flashcards.

### The NASA Space Apps Challenge

Shared byL. Cordero (@earlgreyhot1701d).

L. Cordero shared theNASA Space Apps Challenge, a global hackathon happening onNovember 14–15, 2026, with450+ local eventsto choose from when you register.

The challenge brings people together to work on real-world problems usingNASA data and Space Agency Partner data. The challenges can cover things like software development, storytelling, astrophysics, space exploration, agriculture, and more.

I wanted to include this because I like thecommunity sideof this one. You can choose a local event and meet other people interested in technology, science, and space, and it is open to people from different backgrounds and skill levels.

### The Build with React Image Editor Challenge

Shared byKonark Sharma (@konark_13).

Konark shared theBuild with React Image Editor ChallengefromUnlayer, where you build a GTA VI-inspired experience usingReact Image Editor.

The idea is to build something where users can edit or customize at least one visual, use React Image Editor as a core part of the project, and then publish the source code in a public GitHub repository and deploy the project.

I wanted to include this because it's a little different from the usual hackathons I come across. The GTA VI theme gives you a starting point, but there is still quite a bit of room for what you can actually build around it.

There is$1,000 in prizes, with $500 for first place, $300 for second, and $200 for third. Projects are judged on creativity, visual execution, use of React Image Editor, and the overall experience.

Format:Online

Deadline:September 24, 2026 at 11:59 PM UTC

Prize Pool:$1,000

What you need to build:A GTA VI-inspired experience using React Image Editor, with at least one visual that users can edit or customize.

Submission:Public GitHub repository + deployed project
