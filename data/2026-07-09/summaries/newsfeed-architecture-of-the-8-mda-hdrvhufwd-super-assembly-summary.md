# Architecture of the 8 MDa Hdr–Vhu–Fwd super‑assembly in class I methanogens  

## Abstract  
- Methanogens drive global carbon cycling and are major biogenic sources of methane.  
- The Hdr–Vhu–Fwd super‑assembly couples H₂ oxidation to CO₂ reduction via flavin‑based electron bifurcation.  
- Cryo‑EM reveals an 8 MDa complex composed of 252 polypeptide chains and >600 redox cofactors.  
- Cryo‑ET shows the intact super‑assembly within the cytoplasm of whole cells.  
- Architecture: two hexameric HdrABC–Vhu rings linked by a tetrameric FwdF core, forming a circular electron conduit.  
- Twelve VhuB polyferredoxin subunits bridge Vhu–Hdr and Fwd, directly connecting the first and last steps of methanogenesis.  
- A modular variant replaces Vhu with tungsten‑containing formate dehydrogenase (FdhAB), indicating flexible electron‑input integration.  
- Taxonomic analysis shows this architecture is specific to class I methanogens and differs from the smaller Hdr–Fmd complex of class II.  

## Background  
- Hydrogenotrophic methanogenesis reduces CO₂ to CH₄ using electrons from H₂ or formate, completing the Wolfe cycle.  
- Electron bifurcation distributes energy asymmetrically: a low‑potential electron reduces CO₂, a high‑potential electron reduces the heterodisulfide CoM‑S‑S‑CoB.  
- Prior structural knowledge was limited to a single hexameric Hdr–Fmd complex from *Methanospirillum hungatei*.  
- Class I and class II methanogens diverge phylogenetically, with distinct physiological traits and genome sizes.  

## Structural Findings  
- The *M. maripaludis* super‑assembly contains 12 Hdr–Vhu dimers arranged in two symmetric hexameric rings.  
- Three hourglass‑shaped tetrameric Fwd(ABCDFG) complexes sit between the rings, creating a continuous electron pathway.  
- Twelve VhuB polyferredoxin subunits link the Vhu–Hdr and Fwd modules, enabling direct coupling of electron bifurcation to CO₂ reduction.  
- An alternative assembly incorporates FdhAB instead of Vhu, demonstrating modular substitution of the electron‑input module.  

## Functional Analysis  
- In situ infrared spectroscopy confirms electronic coupling among all catalytic subunits within the super‑assembly.  
- The arrangement allows the low‑potential electrons from VhuB/FdhB to reach the Fwd active site for CO₂ reduction, while high‑potential electrons reduce CoM‑S‑S‑CoB via HdrABC.  

## Evolutionary and Taxonomic Implications  
- Conservation of Hdr, Fwd/Fmd, and VhuB components across class I methanogens suggests the observed architecture is representative of this lineage.  
- The distinct architecture from class II Hdr–Fmd complexes indicates divergent evolutionary solutions for energy conservation in methanogenesis.  

## Isolation and Purification  
- Hdr complex was overexpressed in *M. maripaludis* using a Twin‑Strep‑tagged HdrB2 subunit and purified by single‑step affinity chromatography to preserve the fragile assembly.  
- Mass spectrometry confirmed the presence of HdrABC, VhuADGU, FwdABCDFG, VhuB, and the alternative electron‑input module FdhA2B2.  

## Conclusions  
- The 8 MDa Hdr–Vhu–Fwd super‑assembly represents a modular, adaptable bioenergetic machine specific to class I methanogens.  
- Its architecture links the initial CO₂ reduction step directly to the final heterodisulfide reduction, optimizing energy conservation under diverse anaerobic conditions.  
- Structural divergence between class I and class II methanogens underscores the evolutionary flexibility of archaeal methanogenic pathways.
