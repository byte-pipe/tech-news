# Art school students and Bob Design brand refresh a 750‑year‑old London market with collaged pictograms  

## Project overview  
- Surrey Street Market, a 750‑year‑old London market, celebrated its septuacentennial (plus 50 years) with a visual identity refresh.  
- Female‑owned graphic design practice **Bob Design** partnered with students from **Croydon School of Art**.  
- The initiative is part of a Greater London Authority‑funded programme covering green infrastructure, cultural programming and activation of vacant units in London markets.  

## Collaboration process  
- Bob Design facilitated participatory workshops, public consultations and teaching sessions with a full year group led by lecturer Levi Goff.  
- Students worked on‑site in aprons, using paint and tools, and later road‑tested the identity kit in the market.  
- Seven students were offered internships after the project’s completion.  

## Design elements  
- **Typeface**: Inspired by historic market signage found in archives; includes informal quirks to convey randomness and grassroots creativity.  
- **Wayfinding**: Arrows and directional graphics adopt wiggly, wacky shapes such as stairs and spiky waveforms.  
- **Pictograms**: Collaged cut‑outs transformed into flexible, inclusive geometric icons, reflecting dialogue with local stakeholders.  
- **Visual toolkit**: A set of typeforms, colours and shapes that can be applied across banners, awnings, street furniture and new gateways.  

## Outcomes & impact  
- Launched the new identity alongside the mayor during pop‑up printing workshops at a Saturday market.  
- Integrated architectural angles into the branding, linking heritage market elements with contemporary design.  
- Demonstrated how student‑led creativity can contribute to large‑scale public‑realm rebranding projects.
