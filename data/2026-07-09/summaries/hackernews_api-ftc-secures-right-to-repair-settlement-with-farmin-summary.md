# FTC secures right‑to‑repair settlement with John Deere

## Settlement details
- The Federal Trade Commission and attorneys general from Arizona, Illinois, Michigan, Minnesota and Wisconsin reached a settlement with Deere & Co. requiring the company to provide diagnostic and repair tools to equipment owners and independent repair shops.  
- The order bars Deere dealers from retaliating against owners or shops that choose to repair equipment themselves.  
- The settlement is pending approval by Judge Iain D. Johnston.

## Background
- John Deere has long been criticized for withholding software needed for repairs and forcing customers to use authorized dealers.  
- This is the second right‑to‑repair settlement for Deere in 2024, following a $99 million class‑action settlement with farmers in April that compensated consumers but did not change repair practices.  
- The FTC’s antitrust lawsuit, filed in January 2025, alleged that Deere’s restrictions illegally limited competition in the farm‑equipment repair market.

## Reactions
- Arizona Attorney General Kris Mayes said farmers and independent mechanics have been “at the mercy of Deere’s monopoly over repair tools.”  
- Denver Caldwell, vice president of aftermarket and customer support at Deere, called the agreement “good news for our customers” and said it supports more flexible repair options.

## Financial and compliance terms
- Deere will pay a collective $1 million to the five states to cover antitrust‑enforcement costs.  
- The company will be subject to strict compliance oversight for the next ten years.

## Company statement
- Deere maintains that it remains committed to independent repair and views the settlement as reinforcing its innovation in offering flexible repair solutions.
