# Appeals court denies Trump’s request to halt removal of his name from the Kennedy Center

## Key points
- The U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit rejected all of President Trump’s arguments to stop the removal of his name from the Kennedy Center.
- Judges found Trump failed to demonstrate that the Center would suffer “irreparable injury” without his name on the building.
- The court noted that the name had already been removed, so a stay would not prevent the alleged harms.

## Court reasoning
- Judges Patricia Millett, Robert Wilkins, and Gregory Katsas wrote that the claim of irreparable harm was unsupported because the signage had already been taken down.
- Trump’s assertion that the removal would jeopardize future fundraising and cause financial decline was dismissed for lacking specific evidence.
- The decision emphasized that the appellants offered only conclusory statements from the Center’s executive director without factual backing.

## Context of the litigation
- The case is part of ongoing litigation filed by Rep. Joyce Beatty (D‑Ohio) against Trump and the Kennedy Center board.
- Beatty’s statement called the ruling a reaffirmation that the administration’s attempt to rename the Center was unlawful and urged removal of the tarps covering the building.

## Additional court orders
- Presiding judge Christopher R. Cooper ordered the Kennedy Center to submit a status report on its operations and programming by the end of the month.
- Current programming includes limited outdoor movies, children’s workshops, and five free live performances on the Millennium Stage for July, a reduction from the Center’s usual schedule of over 2,000 annual events.
