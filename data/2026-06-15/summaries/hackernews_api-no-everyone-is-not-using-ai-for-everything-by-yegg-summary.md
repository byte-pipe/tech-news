# No, everyone is not using AI for everything

## Key claims  
- The notion that “everyone is using AI for everything” is inaccurate; most people use AI only occasionally or not at all.  
- AI adoption in the United States roughly splits into three equal groups: active users, occasional users, and non‑users.  
- Negative sentiment toward AI has risen, driven by concerns about jobs, privacy, and misinformation.  

## Data on AI adoption  
- **Gallup (Gen Z, 2025/2026)**  
  - 79 %/81 % use AI at least rarely  
  - 32 %/31 % use AI only monthly or less  
  - 21 %/19 % never use AI  
  - Anxiety about AI: 41 %/42 %  
  - Anger about AI: 22 %/31 %  
- **Microsoft US AI Diffusion (telemetry)**  
  - >30 % of working‑age adults use AI (≈70 % do not)  
  - Definition: ≥90 minutes of engagement with major AI services in a month.  
- **Datos (June 2025)**  
  - 21 % of desktop devices visit “AI Tools” ≥10 times/month  
  - 62 % visit 0 times/month  
  - 17 % fall in between.  
- **Searchlight Institute**  
  - 58 % have used AI (ChatGPT, Claude, etc.)  
  - Split evenly between regular (≥few times/month) and infrequent (≤once/month) users.  
- **The Argument survey**  
  - Most Americans use AI once a week or less.  

Overall: ~1/3 active users, ~1/3 occasional users, ~1/3 non‑users.  

## Reasons people limit AI use  
- **Top concerns (Searchlight)**  
  1. Job displacement (42 %)  
  2. Privacy violations (35 %)  
  3. Spread of misinformation (33 %)  
- Strong support for safety/privacy regulation, even if it slows U.S. AI development.  
- Skepticism about AI’s net societal benefit: only +8 % net positive rating, comparable to social media and far below phones (+68 %), internet (+67 %), solar (+65 %).  
- The Argument study finds broad doubt about the bullish claims made by CEOs and AI promoters.  

## Meat‑consumption analogy  
- AI usage is likened to protein intake: not everyone consumes meat, many reduce red‑meat, and a minority avoid meat entirely.  
- U.S. meat‑consumption stats (various studies):  
  - 95 % eat meat (2023)  
  - 70 % reducing red meat (2024)  
  - 30 % eat meat only rarely/occasionally (2020)  
  - 12 % avoid red meat (2026)  
  - 4 % vegetarian, 1 % vegan (2023)  
- Parallel motivations (health, cost, environment, ethics) mirror AI concerns (privacy, job impact, misinformation).  

## Implications for AI providers  
- Recognize the continuum of user attitudes rather than assuming universal adoption.  
- Offer optional, privacy‑focused AI features (e.g., DuckDuckGo’s duck.ai) to address concerns.  
- Provide a range of “menu” options: full‑feature AI, limited/opt‑in AI, and the ability to turn AI off entirely, catering to users across the spectrum.
