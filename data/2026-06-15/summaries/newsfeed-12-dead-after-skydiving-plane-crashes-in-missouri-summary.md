# 12 dead after skydiving plane crashes in Missouri, authorities say

## Key Facts
- A private skydiving plane crashed near Butler Memorial Airport in Butler, Missouri, killing the pilot and 11 passengers.  
- The crash occurred around 11:30 a.m. on Sunday; the aircraft landed nose‑first in a field and caught fire.  
- Emergency responders extinguished the fire and secured the area; no one attempted to jump before the crash.  

## Aircraft and Operator
- The aircraft was a Pacific Aerospace 750XL, a single‑engine turboprop capable of carrying up to 17 skydivers; it was manufactured in 2010.  
- The plane was operated by Skydive Kansas City, which uses the small airport that hosts about 30 privately owned aircraft.  

## Response and Investigation
- Missouri State Highway Patrol, Butler Police Department, and Bates County Sheriff’s Office responded to the scene.  
- Teams from the National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) and the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) were dispatched to investigate.  
- The cause of the crash is still unknown; investigators will examine factors such as possible loss of power and aircraft stall.  

## Context and Safety Concerns
- Skydiving operations in the region run eight to nine months per year, typically from late March/early April through October/November.  
- Aviation safety expert Jeff Guzzetti noted that skydiving planes are subject only to standard private‑plane FAA regulations, which can lead to inadequate maintenance and safety culture compared with stricter charter or airline standards.  

## Additional Information
- The small town of Butler has a population of roughly 4,300 and lies about 65 miles south of Kansas City.
