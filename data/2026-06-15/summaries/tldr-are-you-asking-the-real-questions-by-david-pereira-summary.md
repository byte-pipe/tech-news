# Are You Asking the REAL Questions? – David Pereira

## Theory vs. Reality
- LinkedIn is full of stories about building and shipping products in hours with AI, but most focus on new products, not on improving existing ones with legacy customers.
- Optimizing established products requires maintaining satisfaction, reducing churn, and growing revenue, which is a different challenge than launching fresh ideas.
- Example: A Meta PM using AI for internal tools (no customer impact) can ship without engineers, but the same approach does not apply to revenue‑critical products like Ads.
- When hearing AI‑driven building claims, consider the product context; the hype may not reflect the true constraints you face.

## Questions People Are Ignoring
- Many product managers have shifted to a “builder” mindset, turning product teams into feature factories.
- The core role of a PM should be to define strategy, uncover business opportunities, and ensure outcomes, not just output.
- AI changes the game, but the focus must remain on strategic questions that drive real value.

### 1. How easy is it for AI to replicate your core functionalities?
- Intercom’s co‑founder created “Fin,” an AI support assistant, to pre‑empt AI competition; it now accounts for ~25 % of revenue.
- Assess how readily AI could copy your product’s core features; the harder to replicate, the stronger your moat.
- Transactional products need additional safeguards; relationship‑based businesses rely on more than feature duplication.

### 2. How easily can customers switch to competitors?
- True stickiness comes from the value customers would lose by leaving, not merely the time spent learning a new tool.
- Personal example: staying on Substack because of network‑driven recommendations that would disappear on another platform.
- Evaluate what substantive value customers would forfeit if they migrated; if only time is the barrier, the risk of disruption is high.

### 3. How fast can you move from idea to value?
- AI enables rapid testing of multiple solutions, but many teams produce half‑baked features that never reach activation.
- Activation is becoming harder as feature velocity outpaces user capacity to adopt.
- Review the last 3‑5 ideas and measure the time from concept to delivered value; speed of value delivery is the key competitive advantage.
