## Early life, aspirations, and repeated rejections
- Anil Menon, a NASA flight surgeon, applied to become an astronaut four times; each time he reached the final round but was rejected.  
- At 39, he was older than the typical astronaut‑candidate age and felt his dream was slipping away.  
- His background includes emergency medicine on Mount Everest, disaster relief in Haiti, and search‑and‑rescue missions in Afghanistan.  
- After the fourth rejection, he journaled about his purpose and chose to focus on space medicine, deciding that if he could not fly, he would help others fly.

## Decision to join SpaceX
- Menon held a medical degree, a mechanical‑engineering degree, and served as a flight surgeon at NASA, supporting six long‑duration ISS missions launched on Russian Soyuz.  
- In 2018 he accepted a position at SpaceX as a physician for the upcoming Crew Dragon Demo‑2 mission, despite a stable NASA job and his wife Anna’s career as a flight controller at Johnson Space Center.  
- The move required a family relocation from Houston to California and was described as a “big gamble.”  
- At SpaceX he was encouraged to be entrepreneurial, working directly with astronauts Doug Hurley and Bob Behnken to ensure crew health for Demo‑2.

## Demo‑2, COVID‑19, and expanded responsibilities
- As the Demo‑2 mission entered its final phase in early 2020, the COVID‑19 pandemic hit.  
- SpaceX President Gwynne Shotwell communicated that the company would stay safe while continuing its mission, providing Menon’s contact info for employee health questions.  
- Menon’s role grew to include leading SpaceX’s internal pandemic response: antibody testing, collaborations with Harvard, two Nature publications, manufacturing ventilator components for Medtronic, and producing 55,000 masks for Los Angeles emergency departments.  
- He was present at launch, accompanied the crew during their final minutes on Earth, and greeted them after splashdown, describing the experience as the “busiest and most fun time” of his life.

## Re‑applying to NASA during the pandemic
- While managing pandemic duties, Menon learned NASA had opened applications for its 23rd astronaut group.  
- At age early‑40s, after moving his family cross‑country, he decided to apply again, motivated by the question of future regret.  
- He prepared by meditating for confidence and improving interview skills, addressing weaknesses that had hindered earlier attempts.

## Unexpected call and the path to becoming an astronaut
- About a year and a half later, while at a weekend retreat in Big Bear Lake, Menon received a call from Reid Wiseman, chief of the Astronaut Office.  
- The call, initially about a technical issue (“What’s going on with Dragon’s toilet?”), signaled that Menon’s astronaut selection was imminent, despite pandemic‑related delays.  
- This moment marked the transition from being a support physician to becoming one of the selected astronauts, a culmination of his perseverance and the risks taken with his family.
