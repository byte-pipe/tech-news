### 📌 The Bridge by Entrepreneurs First

Who it's for:Ambitious founders, engineers, researchers, product builders, and future startup CEOs based outside the US who want to build a venture-scale company. You don't need to have a co-founder or even a startup idea before applying.

What stands out:The Bridge is an8-week, fully funded residencyinSan Franciscodesigned for exceptional international founders at the very beginning of their startup journey. Participants live and build together, explore ideas, meet potential co-founders, talk to users, and receive hands-on support from theEntrepreneurs Firstteam.

I wanted to include this because most founder programs expect you to already have a company, traction, or a complete founding team. The Bridge is different. It's designed for people who are still figuring out what to build and who to build it with, while giving them the environment and support to make meaningful progress.

Another thing I like is that the residency is only the beginning. At the end of the eight weeks, teams that demonstrate strong potential can pitch to EF's Investment Committee. Selected teams receive a$250,000 pre-seed investment(in exchange for equity) and continue building in San Francisco for another three months with ongoing support from the EF team.

Duration:8-week residency (plus an additional 3-month company-building program for funded teams)

Location:San Francisco, California

Cost:Free (housing, workspace, food, and the program are covered)

Who can apply:Exceptional founders based outside the US. You don't need an idea or a co-founder to apply.

Application Deadline:August 30, 2026

### 📌 Frontier AI Security Residency

Who it's for:Security engineers, cybersecurity professionals, hardware and embedded systems specialists, cryptography researchers, AI safety practitioners, and experienced software engineers interested in securing frontier AI systems. Prior research experience is helpful but not required, though the applied track generally expects around five or more years of industry experience.

What stands out:TheFrontier AI Security Residency (FASR)is an8-week, fully funded, in-personresearch and engineering residency inCambridge, UK, run byERA. Residents work on real-world challenges at the intersection ofAI security, cybersecurity, and hardware verification, tackling problems such as securing frontier AI systems, verifying AI compute, and building mechanisms that make AI systems more trustworthy and verifiable.

I wanted to include this because most AI programs focus on building more capable models. This residency focuses on something just as important but discussed far less often: making advanced AI systems secure, verifiable, and resilient before their capabilities outpace our ability to protect them. If you're interested in the security side of AI rather than model development, opportunities like this are still relatively rare.

Another thing I like is how comprehensive the support is. The residency coverstravel, accommodation, visas, workspace, and meals during working hours, pairs each resident with an experienced mentor and a Research Manager, and culminates in a research symposium where participants present their work. Residents also receive apro-rated salary(based on an annual rate of£34,125), making it possible to focus entirely on the research without worrying about day-to-day expenses.

Duration:8 weeks (October 12 – December 4, 2026)

Location:Cambridge, UK (primarily in person)

Funding:Fully funded, including travel, accommodation, visa support, workspace, and meals during working hours. Residents also receive a pro-rated salary.

Who can apply:Applicants worldwide aged 18 or older. The program values demonstrated technical ability over formal credentials. Prior research experience is not required, although the applied track generally expects experienced professionals.

Application Deadline:August 2, 2026

### 📌 Figma Campus Leader Program 2026–2027

Who it's for:University and college students in theUnited States, Canada, and Indiawho have at least one academic year remaining, actively use Figma, and enjoy building communities around design, creativity, and product development. Students fromany majorare welcome.

What stands out:TheFigma Campus Leader Programgives students the opportunity to become the official Figma ambassadors at their university. Campus Leaders work with theFigma for Educationteam to grow their campus design community by hosting workshops, events, and creative programs while helping other students learn and use Figma.

I wanted to include this because it's more than a typical campus ambassador program. Rather than focusing on promotion alone, it gives students the resources to build a lasting design community at their university while developing leadership, event planning, and community-building experience that carries well beyond college.

Another thing I like is thatIndia is joining the program for the first timethis year, making this a great opportunity for Indian students who previously weren't eligible. Campus Leaders also receive acommunity budgetfor events,exclusive mentorship and training,direct access to the Figma team,custom Figma swag, and acomplimentary ticket to Config, Figma's annual conference for product builders.

Program Duration:August 2026 – June 2027

Time Commitment:Approximately 2–5 hours per week

Benefits:Community budget, Config ticket, exclusive swag, mentorship, portfolio reviews, training workshops, and direct access to the Figma team.

Who can apply:Students in the United States, Canada, and India with at least one academic year remaining who are active Figma users and passionate about design, creativity, and community building.

Application Deadline:August 2, 2026 (11:59 PM PDT)

### The Missing Semester of Your CS Education

Who it's for:Computer science students, self-taught developers, early-career software engineers, and anyone who wants to become more confident with the tools they use every day while building software.

What stands out:Every computer science degree teaches things like algorithms, operating systems, databases, and programming languages. But very few teach you how to use the tools you'll spend hours with every single day. That's exactly whatThe Missing Semester of Your CS Education, created by instructors atMIT, is all about. Instead of focusing on another programming language or framework, the course covers practical topics like the command line, Git, debugging, development environments, SSH, code quality, documentation, packaging, and modern AI-assisted development workflows.

I wanted to include this because I think it's one of those resources that's easy to overlook. We spend so much time trying to learn the latest framework, library, or AI tool that we sometimes forget the skills that make us more productive no matter what we're building.

One thing I really liked is how practical the course is. It's full of the little things experienced developers often take for granted but that can make a huge difference once you learn them. If you've ever wondered how someone is so fast with Git, the terminal, debugging, or navigating a large codebase, there's a good chance they've picked up many of the skills this course teaches.

I also like that the course has continued to evolve alongside the software industry. The latest edition introducesAgentic Codingand naturally incorporates AI-assisted development throughout the lectures, while still emphasizing the fundamentals that every developer should know.

Topics Covered:Shell, development environments, debugging, Git, packaging, code quality, AI-assisted development, documentation, and more.

Cost:Free

Format:Online lectures, notes, and exercises
