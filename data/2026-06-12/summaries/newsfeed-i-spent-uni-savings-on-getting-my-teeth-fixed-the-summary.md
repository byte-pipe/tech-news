# 'I spent uni savings on getting my teeth fixed' - the real cost of NHS dentist shortage

Deacon Galloway, a university student from North Yorkshire, has had to turn to private dentists due to the NHS being out of reach for him. Costly treatment and lack of government support have put pressure on families to spend their university savings.

Here's how it works:

* Deacon's grandparents saved up before he went to uni and estimated £800.
* He needed dental work, which cost nearly 1/3 of what his grandparents had saved.
* A private dentist quoted him around £800 for two fillings and replacement ones. If NHS would have covered this, but Deacon is under 19 in full-time education.

## Unprecedented costs

The situation isn't just one family dealing with this issue - many others face a similar dilemma as the cost of dental treatment rises rapidly, prompting families to invest their University of Manchester student's savings into private dentists.

Key statistics:

* 1/3 of people get dental treatment privately in the UK
* £80 average for initial consultations after two years
* £139 average for simple extractions; prices vary widely by clinic
* Root canal charge at NHS averages vs. £660 for some clinics

## What's causing this shortage?

MyTribe Insurance analysis found:

* Initial consultation charges have risen by 23% in 2 years to £80 on average
* Simple extraction costs now stand at £139, an increase of 32% since two years ago

The lack of NHS access and rising private prices create significant challenges for patients seeking treatment. Some are calling out dentists for "exploiting" the situation by charging excessively.

In response to these concerns, a Competition and Markets Authority inquiry is underway, along with price reductions in the public sector dental treatment pricing and greater transparency from dentistry about their costs.
