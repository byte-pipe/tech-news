# Are we really headed for a ‘super’ El Niño? What the science says

## Forecast and likelihood
- NOAA’s 14 May report gives an 82 % chance of an El Niño developing between May and July 2024 and a 96 % chance of it being present by December.  
- The agency estimates only a 37 % probability that the event will reach the “very strong” category (central‑eastern Pacific sea‑surface temperatures more than 2 °C above average).

## Current ocean conditions
- Sea‑surface temperatures in the central and eastern tropical Pacific have risen up to 1 °C above the long‑term average, especially off the western coast of South America.  
- Multiple climate models suggest the upcoming El Niño could peak stronger than the 2023‑24 event.

## Definitions and historical context
- The term “super” El Niño is applied when Pacific SST anomalies exceed +2 °C relative to baseline.  
- The most recent super El Niño occurred in 2015‑16.

## Model predictions
- The European Centre for Medium‑Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) projected that Pacific SST anomalies could reach +3 °C above average by November 2024.  

## Potential impacts
- A strong El Niño may trigger floods, droughts, and other extreme weather worldwide.  
- It could help push global temperatures to new record highs, potentially influencing the 2027 temperature peak.

## Key uncertainties
- The ultimate intensity depends on how wind patterns and other atmospheric factors interact with the warm ocean surface; they could either amplify or dampen the warming, leaving the final strength uncertain.
