# Ask HN: How to be SOC2 Type 2 compliant as a solo-entreprenuer? | Hacker News

# Summary of “Ask HN: How to be SOC2 Type 2 compliant as a solo‑entrepreneur?”

## Question
- The original poster (sochix) asks whether a solo‑founder can obtain SOC 2 Type 2 compliance without spending $20k+ on auditors.
- Customers are demanding proof that the app “Perfect Wiki” can be trusted.

## Main viewpoints from the discussion

### 1. Don’t pursue SOC 2 as a solo‑founder
- **tptacek**: SOC 2 is a “secret handshake” used by larger firms; it’s not worth pursuing speculatively.  
- **jwr**: Solo businesses usually cannot meet the many required controls; a single “NO” answer can disqualify you.

### 2. Provide alternative documentation instead
- **codegeek**: Share detailed security and data‑policy documents with customers; works ~99 % of the time.  
- **homeonthemtn**: A recent penetration test can serve as a practical proof of security for small firms.  
- **ozim**: ISO 27001 documentation can satisfy many enterprise customers; SOC 2 may be added later if needed.

### 3. Expect extensive questionnaires anyway
- **spydum**: Without SOC 2 you’ll face 200+‑question security questionnaires; LLM tools can help generate answers.  
- **mikeocool**: Some enterprises still require questionnaires even if you have a SOC 2 report.  
- **yeutterg**: SOC 2 does not eliminate the need for questionnaires.

### 4. Cost‑benefit considerations
- **parliament32**: If competitors have SOC 2, lacking it can cost sales; otherwise, the impact is limited.  
- **AndrewKemendo**: Avoid extra effort until a concrete requirement forces you to invest.  
- **tortilla**: Enterprise accounts may not be worth the overhead for a solo‑founder.

### 5. Tools and shortcuts
- **bdangubic** & **spydum**: Use modern LLMs to draft questionnaire responses and security policies, then review manually.

## Common themes
- **SOC 2 is expensive and geared toward larger organizations.**  
- **Documentation, ISO 27001, and penetration tests are often sufficient for SMB customers.**  
- **Enterprise buyers may still demand questionnaires regardless of certifications.**  
- **Cost‑effective alternatives (LLMs, pen‑tests) can reduce the workload.**  
- **Pursue SOC 2 only when a specific contract or revenue opportunity justifies the expense.**  

## Practical takeaways for a solo‑entrepreneur
- Start with a solid internal security policy and a recent pen‑test.  
- Offer that documentation to prospects; it satisfies most inquiries.  
- Be prepared to answer detailed questionnaires; leverage LLMs to draft responses.  
- Re‑evaluate the need for SOC 2 only when a paying customer explicitly requires it and the potential revenue outweighs the audit cost.
