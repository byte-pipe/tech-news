# U.S. DOJ Demands Apple, Google, Amazon, and Walmart Reveal Data on EZ Lynk Auto Agent Users  

## Background  
- The DOJ sued Cayman‑based EZ Lynk in 2021, alleging violation of the Clean Air Act by selling “defeat devices” that let diesel owners bypass factory emissions controls.  
- EZ Lynk claims its Auto Agent app and OBD dongle are intended for legitimate vehicle monitoring, software updates, and diagnostics, and that any emissions‑related misuse is the user’s responsibility.  

## Scope of the Subpoenas  
- In March–April 2026 the DOJ issued subpoenas to Apple and Google for download and account information of anyone who installed the Auto Agent app.  
- Parallel subpoenas were sent to Amazon and Walmart for purchase records of the EZ Lynk hardware dongle.  
- Estimates reported by Gizmodo suggest the requests could cover more than 100,000 individual users.  
- The government says the data will help identify and interview witnesses about how the tools were actually used, citing forum posts and social‑media evidence of emissions‑control disabling.  

## Privacy Concerns and Pushback  
- EZ Lynk’s attorneys label the subpoenas “overreach,” arguing they exceed what is needed and raise Fourth‑Amendment issues.  
- Apple and Google are reportedly preparing legal challenges to the demands.  
- Privacy groups such as the Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF) and the Electronic Privacy Information Center (EPIC) criticize the broad collection of personally identifiable information, noting many users may have simply read trouble codes or performed routine diagnostics.  

## Legal Developments and Next Steps  
- A 2025 ruling rejected EZ Lynk’s attempt to invoke Section 230 immunity, allowing the case to proceed.  
- The outcome of challenges to the subpoenas could set precedent for how app‑store data is used in regulatory enforcement.  
- No public comment has been made by Apple, Google, Amazon, Walmart, or the DOJ beyond the court filings.
