# AI-driven layoffs aren’t making business sense

## Key findings
- 80 % of large enterprises surveyed by Gartner reported workforce reductions after launching AI or automation projects, with cuts ranging from 1 % to 15 %.
- Gartner’s analysis shows no correlation between the size or speed of layoffs and the return on investment (ROI) from AI initiatives.
- Short‑term profit gains from layoffs do not translate into the expected long‑term AI ROI.

## Drivers of AI ROI
- **Workforce upskilling** – Companies that invest in training employees to build and manage AI agents achieve the highest ROI.
- **New AI‑related roles** – Leading firms create positions such as AI‑orchestration specialists for existing staff, turning displaced workers into AI enablers.
- **Automate‑to‑augment** – Focusing on automating low‑risk, routine tasks frees staff to deliver higher‑value services or explore new markets.

## Expert perspectives
- **Helen Poitevin (Gartner)**: ROI comes from reinvesting in people, not from replacing them; AI will ultimately create more jobs than it eliminates.
- **Brian Behe (RIIG Technology)**: Cutting staff first removes institutional knowledge that AI needs; giving knowledgeable employees AI tools yields real returns.
- **Andy Williamson (ONLC Training)**: Layoffs are often used as a proxy for AI progress, but the real work is building operating models that deliver value; human talent remains a critical asset.

## Risks of layoff‑focused strategies
- Loss of institutional knowledge hampers AI effectiveness.
- Short‑term cost reductions overlook the long‑term human impact and potential talent loss.
- Misjudging AI’s ability to replace workers can lead to rehiring cycles and wasted resources.

## Recommended approach
- Adopt an “automate‑to‑augment” mindset: automate repetitive tasks while upskilling staff to handle higher‑order responsibilities.
- Develop clear career pathways for employees whose roles are affected, including training for AI orchestration and development.
- Measure AI success by value creation and operational improvements rather than headcount reductions.
