# Asteroid set to fly very close to Earth – summary

## Key details
- Asteroid **2026 JH2** will pass Earth on 18 May 2026 at 09:38 UTC, at a distance of about 90 917 km (≈¼ the Earth‑Moon distance).  
- Estimated size: 16–36 m in diameter.  
- Relative speed: ~9.17 km s⁻¹, crossing the sky as fast as artificial satellites.  

## Observation and visibility
- Discovered this week by the Mount Lemmon Survey (Arizona) and Farpoint Observatory (Kansas).  
- Visible only briefly from the northern hemisphere; difficult to observe from the southern hemisphere due to rapid motion.  

## Potential impact
- If it struck Earth, the energy release would be comparable to the 2013 Chelyabinsk meteor (≈30 times the Hiroshima bomb).  
- An impact of this size could “ruin a city quite efficiently,” according to astronomer Mark Norris.  

## Context within near‑Earth asteroid monitoring
- Only five known asteroids will pass within the Moon’s orbit in the next year; 2026 JH2 is the closest among them.  
- Larger asteroids (>1 km) are largely catalogued, but objects of this size remain hard to detect because they reflect little light.  
- Ongoing improvements in observation are expanding the database of smaller near‑Earth objects.
