# AI applications have two growth curves

## Overview
- Current AI pitch decks showcase rapid growth benchmarks (e.g., Cursor, Sierra) that set unrealistic expectations for all AI startups.  
- This creates a category error: fast‑growing companies succeed for reasons that don’t apply to the most complex, high‑value AI problems.  
- Infrastructure‑focused markets appear slower, but their “slowness” is intrinsic to the problem space and can be a source of long‑term value.

## Market map revisited
- AI markets can be plotted on a 2×2 grid:  
  - **Technical difficulty** (easy vs. hard)  
  - **Organizational adoption difficulty** (easy vs. hard)  
- Quadrant insights:  
  - **Easy‑easy** (consumer chat, basic search): rapid growth, dominated by model providers.  
  - **Easy‑hard** (Cursor quadrant): fast growth because individual users adopt without committee approval; data flywheels amplify momentum.  
  - **Hard‑easy** (Sierra quadrant): strong growth despite required enterprise buy‑in; problems are tractable enough for quick value demonstration, and large fundraises signal credibility to Fortune 500 buyers.  
  - **Hard‑hard** (SRE, security ops, complex infrastructure): slow growth; founders are unfairly benchmarked against faster quadrants, but slowness is fundamental to these challenges.

## What slowness buys you
- **Deeper problem understanding**: Extended evaluation periods force founders to question obvious solutions (e.g., runbook‑driven RCA agents) and explore more effective approaches such as early‑warning detection.  
- **Avoidance of premature scaling**: Rushing to match fast‑growth curves leads to taking unsuitable POCs, over‑promising, churn, and reputational damage that can poison the entire category.  
- **Higher product relevance**: Time allows iteration toward the true problem rather than the most monetizable shortcut.

## Education as a moat
- In hard‑hard markets, buyers lack a clear mental model of what an AI agent should do.  
- Vendors that provide a coherent, differentiated point of view (e.g., rebuilding the general ledger concept rather than merely layering AI) earn trust and create a defensible moat.  
- Unlike fast markets where the ecosystem educates users (as with coding agents), slow markets require the vendor to lead the education, making the vendor memorable to customers.

## How to read these markets
- Recognize that each quadrant follows its own physics; compare only within the same quadrant.  
- For hard‑hard problems, prioritize patience, rigorous problem validation, and thought‑leading education over chasing headline growth metrics.  
- Success in slow markets often translates into durable value and stronger competitive positioning once the category matures.
