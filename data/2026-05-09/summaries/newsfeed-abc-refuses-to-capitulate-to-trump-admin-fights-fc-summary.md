# ABC refuses to capitulate to Trump admin, fights FCC probe into The View

## Background
- The FCC, led by Chairman Brendan Carr, alleges that ABC’s *The View* violates the equal‑time rule, despite historic exemptions for interview portions of talk shows.  
- The agency opened an unusual review of ABC’s broadcast licenses one day after President Donald Trump and First Lady Melania Trump urged ABC to fire Jimmy Kimmel for a joke.  
- In 2002 the FCC ruled that *The View* qualifies as a bona‑fide news program, exempt from the equal‑time requirement; ABC had sought a declaratory ruling in 2000 to confirm this status.

## ABC’s Legal Challenge
- ABC filed a public petition arguing the FCC exceeded its authority, threatening “decades of settled law” and chilling protected speech.  
- The filing asserts that the Media Bureau lacks power to demand a new declaratory ruling or to force early license‑renewal applications for all ABC stations.  
- ABC maintains that *The View* still meets all criteria for the news‑program exemption and that the 2002 order remains valid.

## Advocacy and Industry Reaction
- Free‑speech groups (FIRE, Freedom of the Press Foundation) praised ABC’s refusal to submit to regulatory pressure, calling the FCC’s legal theories “frivolous and unconstitutional.”  
- Media‑advocacy organization Free Press highlighted the perceived retaliation after the Trump‑Kimmel incident and noted bipartisan condemnation of Carr’s actions.  
- Democratic FCC member Anna Gomez emphasized the importance of “courage over capitulation.”

## FCC’s Position and Actions
- The FCC claims the investigation stems from a year‑long probe into possible violations of anti‑discrimination (DEI) rules, not solely from the *The View* issue.  
- The Media Bureau ordered ABC’s Houston station (KTRK‑TV) to file another petition on *The View* and demanded early filing of all ABC license renewals by May 28, 2026.  
- Carr publicly suggested *The View*’s guest lineup makes it difficult to argue the program is “straight news,” implying a shift in equal‑opportunity policy.

## Allegations of Viewpoint Discrimination
- ABC points out that the FCC has not pursued similar equal‑time scrutiny of conservative talk‑radio programs (e.g., *The Mark Levin Show*, *The Glenn Beck Program*).  
- The network argues this selective enforcement indicates viewpoint discrimination and retaliatory targeting, undermining long‑standing FCC respect for broadcasters’ editorial judgment.  
- ABC warns that uncertainty about editorial discretion could chill political coverage ahead of the 2026 midterm elections.

## Potential Impact
- If the FCC overturns the exemption, broadcasters could face new constraints on interviewing political candidates, reshaping political discourse on television.  
- Legal experts suggest the courts are likely to side with ABC, given precedent protecting the news‑program exemption.  
- The outcome will set a precedent for how aggressively the FCC can use its licensing authority to regulate broadcast content.
