**An American Privacy Emergency: Guest Post from Cynthia Dwork et al.**

The U.S. Secretary of Commerce has issued a directive (DAO 216-26) that restricts confidentiality protection in the Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) and the Census Bureau's publications, effectively banning modern techniques for preserving privacy in large datasets like the 2030 US Census.

This move marks a significant deviation from over half a century of progress in protecting data subjects' confidentiality. The direction is largely driven by political interests rather than scientific merit, bypassing legally required administrative procedures.

The impact will be less informative statistics or weaker protection, potentially leading to a lack of useful data for research and analysis. This decision also raises concerns among experts about the implications on algorithmic fairness, as differential privacy methods are widely used to ensure that sensitive information is protected in complex statistical analyses.

**Key Points:**

* DAO 216-26 restricts confidentiality protection in BEA and Census Bureau publications
* The ban encompasses modern techniques, including those not considered "modern"
* The directive undermines over half a century of progress on data subject privacy protections
* Potential consequences for research, analysis, and algorithmic fairness
