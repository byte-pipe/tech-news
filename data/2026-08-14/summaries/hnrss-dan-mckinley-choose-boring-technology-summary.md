# Dan McKinley :: Choose Boring Technology

# Choose Boring Technology – Summary

## Embrace Boring
- Treat technology choices as limited “innovation tokens”; each novel stack consumes one.
- Favor mature, well‑understood tools (e.g., MySQL, Postgres, PHP, Python, Memcached) that are “boring” but reliable.
- Boring does not equal bad; avoid technology that is both boring and poor.
- Known unknowns (e.g., performance at 100 % CPU) are manageable; unknown unknowns explode with brand‑new tech.

## Optimize Globally
- Technology decisions affect the whole team, organization, and long‑term system health.
- Adding a new language or service introduces operational and cognitive overhead (monitoring, testing, onboarding, scripts).
- The “best tool for the job” view is myopic; the true goal is to keep the company running, so choose the tool that is the “least worst” across all problems.
- Long‑term reliability costs dominate short‑term development convenience.

## Choose New Technology, Sometimes
- New tech should be introduced only after a company‑wide discussion and clear visibility.
- First ask: “Can we solve the problem with our existing stack?” If the answer is “yes, but hard,” explore alternatives.
- Document why the current stack is insufficient before proposing a new solution.
- When adding new tech, set explicit migration plans and timelines to avoid fragmented, locally‑optimal solutions.
- A lightweight review process (few questions + a meeting) can filter viable additions.

## Just Ship
- While polyglot freedom sounds attractive, unrestricted tool choice can hinder delivery.
- The overarching principle is to ship reliable software; freedom should not compromise that goal.
