# PBS Broadcaster Loses Access to 70 Years of Archival Data

## Background
- Nine PBS stored over 50 TB of archival material (spanning 70 years) with Open Source Storage (OSS) under a contract that began in 2019.  
- The contract was set to renew on March 6, 2024, but OSS failed to respond and cut off access despite a 30‑day retrieval window.

## Discovery of OSS Collapse
- OSS’s website disappeared and the company was listed as delinquent with the Colorado Secretary of State.  
- OSS had been using Iron Mountain’s data‑center services for the stored media.

## Legal Actions Against OSS
- Nine PBS sent a demand letter to Iron Mountain to preserve the data and cover reasonable costs.  
- The broadcaster also sued OSS and its leadership.  
- A “managing partner” claiming to have acquired OSS (James Tramel) contacted Nine PBS, later stating he was defrauded and that ownership reverted to the original owners.  
- The court ruled that Nine PBS has the right to access its data and ordered OSS to facilitate transfer to a new vendor.

## Conflict with Iron Mountain
- Iron Mountain initially acknowledged holding the data but later claimed OSS owned it, refusing to release it.  
- Nine PBS is now suing Iron Mountain to obtain a court order compelling the data’s release.

## Risks and Implications
- Without access, the broadcaster risks losing irreplaceable historical content.  
- It is unclear whether Nine PBS maintains any independent backups, which would violate the 3‑2‑1 backup principle.  
- The situation highlights the dangers of relying on a single cloud provider and the importance of robust backup strategies.

## Outlook
- Prompt legal action is required to secure a court order for data release.  
- Once access is restored, Nine PBS is expected to adopt stronger backup and archival practices to prevent future losses.
