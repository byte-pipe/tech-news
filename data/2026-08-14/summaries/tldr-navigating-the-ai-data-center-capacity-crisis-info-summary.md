# Navigating the AI data center capacity crisis

## Context
- In 2023‑2024 hyperscalers announced gigawatt‑scale data center expansions that proved unrealistic.
- By 2026 many of those projects are delayed or cancelled due to power‑grid bottlenecks.
- The mismatch between promised capacity and actual infrastructure is a simple math problem, not a mystery.

## Enterprises left holding the bag
- Companies that based AI roadmaps on vendor‑promised cloud capacity now face shortages and must reassess.
- Vendors have incentives to over‑promise to capture market share; their timelines often do not align with customer needs.
- Clients who ignored early warnings are scrambling; those who heeded advice and adopted hybrid, flexible strategies are better positioned.
- The key recommendation: conduct independent analysis of capacity claims; rely on internal or trusted, non‑selling advisors.

## Three realities to plan for
1. **Assume constraints** – design infrastructure expecting limited power, space, and compute availability within required timelines.
2. **Build for flexibility** – avoid single‑vendor lock‑in; maintain relationships with multiple providers and architect workloads to shift as conditions change.
3. **Do the math yourself** – verify vendor capacity forecasts against power‑grid limits, construction schedules, and historical delivery performance; require written, enforceable commitments.

## Hype never stops
- The AI capacity crisis was predictable; warnings were ignored because they were unwelcome.
- Power grid expansion is slow, creating inevitable bottlenecks for massive AI demand.
- Vendors and media will continue to promote optimistic visions; enterprises must stay skeptical and base strategies on real‑world constraints.
- The choice to adapt or suffer the consequences rests with each organization.
