# University of California math professors demand return of SAT for STEM admissions

## Faculty petition and main arguments
- More than 600 UC faculty, led by mathematicians at UC Berkeley, signed an open letter urging the system to require SAT or ACT scores for STEM applicants beginning fall 2027.  
- Professors say six years of test‑free admissions have left many students mathematically unprepared, forcing instructors to reteach middle‑school math while covering college‑level material.  
- Diagnostic data show at least 20 % of first‑semester calculus students at Berkeley exhibit significant math deficits; a UC San Diego report documents a thirty‑fold increase in incoming students whose math skills fall below high‑school level, with 70 % below middle‑school level.

## Context of UC’s testing policy
- In May 2020 UC Regents voted to suspend SAT/ACT requirements, citing concerns that the tests disadvantage students of color and low‑income backgrounds.  
- The decision overrode the UC Academic Senate’s Standardized Testing Task Force, which argued that test scores improve admission rates for disadvantaged groups and predict college performance better than high‑school GPA.  
- A California state court injunction in 2020 forced UC to stop using scores earlier than originally planned.  
- Elite institutions such as Harvard, Stanford, Caltech, and others reinstated standardized testing requirements in 2024‑2025; USC uses scores optionally in a holistic review.

## UC system response
- UC leadership has not formally endorsed the faculty letter but says it is listening to the concerns.  
- Rachel Zaentz, UC spokesperson, emphasized a focus on strengthening math instruction and collaboration with K‑12 partners.  
- Ahmet Palazoglu, chair of the UC Academic Senate, noted he has heard faculty worries about college readiness and is calling for a roadmap of policy work and partnerships with state and K‑12 education leaders.

## Divergent viewpoints within academia
- Zvezda Stankova, a Berkeley mathematics professor and lead organizer of the letter, argues that reinstating the SAT can actually help diversity by providing an “entrance ticket” for under‑represented students who otherwise fail without it.  
- Saul Geiser, director of the UC Berkeley Center for Studies in Higher Education, contends the SAT is a poor fit for public universities; he cites research showing high‑school GPA outperforms SAT scores in predicting first‑year success when income and race are controlled, and warns that reliance on SAT scores may disadvantage high‑achieving low‑income and first‑generation students.

## Statewide math readiness data
- California students are roughly a quarter‑year behind pre‑COVID math instruction, equivalent to about 45 school days.  
- Overall, 37.3 % of students meet state math learning standards; in 11th grade—the grade most linked to college readiness—only 30.5 % meet or exceed the standards.

## Upcoming decision point
- The faculty letter arrives just before the UC Academic Senate Board of Admissions and Relations with Schools meets to discuss system‑wide admissions changes, a potential first step toward reinstating standardized testing in the nation’s largest public research university system.
