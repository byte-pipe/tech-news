# Modern slavery claim over alleged abuse by Al Fayed associate

# Summary of “‘Lifting the shame that was never mine to carry’ – alleged victim of Al Fayed associate”

## Background
- Joanna Brittan, a woman from Devon, says she was raped and trafficked by Ahmed Obaidly, a UAE diplomat, and by Mohamed Al Fayed, the former Harrods owner, beginning in 1990.  
- She first reported the abuse to police in December 2017, describing “traumatic memories” from almost three decades earlier.  
- Both Obaidly (died 2015) and Al Fayed (died 2023) are now deceased.

## Alleged Abuse
- In 1990 Joanna was recruited to manage a UAE royal’s estate; she was forced to undergo STI testing at the UAE embassy and was threatened with loss of the job.  
- She says Obaidly provided her accommodation and raped her three times, describing the assaults as occurring while she was asleep.  
- After a dispute with her recruiter, she was dismissed and forced to sign a non‑disclosure agreement under threat.  
- Obaidly allegedly introduced her to Al Fayed at Harrods; she was taken in a locked limousine to Al Fayed’s Surrey estate, pressured to become a nanny, and believes she was drugged and abused there.  
- She reports additional assaults on trips to Cannes and the Paris Ritz.

## Police and Legal Response
- Joanna’s 2017 statement was passed from Devon & Cornwall Police to the Metropolitan Police; no action was taken then because the alleged perpetrators were dead and evidence was insufficient.  
- The case is now being reviewed by the National Referral Mechanism (NRM), the UK system for identifying modern‑slavery victims.  
- The Met has reopened a live investigation into anyone who may have facilitated or enabled Al Fayed’s alleged offences and is urging witnesses to come forward.  
- French police have interviewed Joanna as part of a separate trafficking investigation.

## Wider Context and Other Victims
- Other women have made similar accusations: one anonymously alleges assault by Al Fayed’s brother Salah and another man in 1989; at least four women have been referred to the NRM.  
- Dame Jasvinder Sanghera, an advocate for survivors, says more victims are likely to emerge and stresses the need for broader scrutiny.  
- Joanna’s MP, Caroline Voaden, echoes calls for investigation, noting it appears Al Fayed did not act alone.

## Current Status
- Joanna has waived anonymity to encourage other survivors to speak out.  
- She reports feeling “seen and heard” after a recent interview with the Met.  
- The Met emphasizes a victim‑centred approach and continues to seek information from the public.  
- Harrods’ new owners declined comment on individual cases but expressed support for women coming forward.
