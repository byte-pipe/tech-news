# I've applied for more than 400 roles – how young people are facing the job shortage

## Overview
- More than one million people under 24 in the UK are NEET (not in education, employment, or training), described by experts as a “lost generation.”
- The article presents five personal accounts of recent graduates struggling to secure work despite extensive job applications and qualifications.
- Common themes include lack of experience, overskilling for entry‑level roles, repetitive and opaque application processes, and the psychological toll of repeated rejection.

## Individual experiences  

- **Zaynah, 24**  
  - Applied to over 200 jobs in a year, received no responses.  
  - Previously worked in nail art; now seeks makeup roles.  
  - Joined the charity scheme *Spear* to build confidence and improve interview skills.  

- **Luke, 23**  
  - Product design graduate; applied to more than 400 positions.  
  - Describes the online application process as “vile” and repetitive.  
  - Faces a Catch‑22: overqualified for basic jobs, under‑experienced for design roles; has been rejected for cleaning, barista, receptionist, and waiter positions.  

- **Tarun, 18**  
  - Interrupted plumbing apprenticeship to travel to India after his grandmother’s death.  
  - Unable to re‑enter training; job applications rejected for lack of experience.  
  - Turned to rapping and songwriting as a self‑motivating outlet while continuing the job search.  

- **Eloise, 24**  
  – Holds a first‑class undergraduate degree and a master’s in English/creative writing.  
  – Four interviews only; most roles offered are in hospitality.  
  – Worried about ending up in a “minimum‑wage life” and possibly moving back to a remote village.  

- **Clover, 20**  
  – Spent three years following career advice, now works a zero‑hours retail contract secured through a friend’s referral.  
  – Highlights that even with university CV support and extensive volunteering, opportunities remain scarce.  

## Common challenges identified
- **Experience paradox**: employers demand experience that recent graduates lack, while their degrees make them appear overqualified for low‑skill jobs.  
- **Application fatigue**: repetitive online forms and lack of feedback lead to frustration and mental health strain.  
- **Geographic constraints**: candidates sometimes list addresses outside their residence to increase eligibility, indicating limited local opportunities.  
- **Financial pressure**: reliance on Universal Credit and fear of long‑term low‑wage employment.  

## Expert perspective
- Analysts label the situation as a “lost generation,” emphasizing the systemic mismatch between education output and available entry‑level positions.  
- Automation and AI are cited as factors reducing the number of junior roles traditionally filled by graduates.  

## Outlook
- Young people continue to adapt by seeking alternative income (e.g., zero‑hours contracts) and creative outlets (e.g., music) while awaiting structural changes in the labour market.  
- The article underscores the need for more targeted training programmes, streamlined recruitment processes, and realistic pathways from education to sustainable employment.
