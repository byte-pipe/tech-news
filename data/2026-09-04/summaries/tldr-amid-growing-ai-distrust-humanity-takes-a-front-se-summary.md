# Amid growing AI distrust, humanity takes a front seat in advertising

# Summary of “Amid growing AI distrust, humanity takes a front seat in advertising”

## Overview
- Brands are increasingly foregrounding “humanity” in their advertising to address growing public anxiety about AI and data‑center impacts.
- The shift reflects a cultural moment where consumers feel a loss of control over technology and seek reassurance that brands are made by and for people.

## Notable Campaigns
- **Anthropic – “Hope in Hard Questions”** (90 s): poses existential AI questions before ending on optimism.
- **Meta – “The Future is For Everyone”** (60 s): emphasizes “doubling down” on people despite technological change.
- **Garage Beer & Liquid Death**: a 90‑second musical satire urging viewers to send urine to cool AI data centers, framed as “saving humanity.”
- **Etsy, TD Bank, Polaroid, Pinterest, Heineken**: campaigns highlight human touch, outdoor experiences, and a break from screens.

## Industry Insight
- Marketers view humanity as a powerful counter‑cultural message that empowers consumers to feel in charge of their destiny.
- Executives (e.g., Megha Parikh, VML) note that the current “existential crisis” makes explicit human‑focused messaging culturally resonant.
- Academic perspective (Marcus Collins, University of Michigan) stresses that brands must contribute a point of view on humanity to stay relevant.

## Creative Tactics
- **Explicit Messaging**: Direct statements about human benefit and authenticity.
- **Aesthetic Cues**: Use of film, handmade textures, natural colors, and tactile visuals to evoke a human feel.
- **Behind‑the‑Scenes Content**: Videos showing real people creating ads (e.g., Apple, Coinbase) increase audience appreciation.
- **Craft‑Focused Design**: Clients request “imperfect,” “natural,” and “tactile” creative elements.

## Expert Quotes
- Andy Pearson (Liquid Death): “There is a lot of anger and anxiety around AI… we turned it on itself… in the most Liquid Death way possible.”
- Laurie Pressman (Pantone): “The more technology becomes part of our lives, the more we revert back to things that make us human—nature, authenticity, things you can physically touch.”
- Ty Gates (Mother): “I liked it more because I knew there were real humans involved in making it.”

## Takeaways
- Humanity‑centered advertising is becoming a mainstream response to AI distrust, not just a niche trend.
- Brands that successfully integrate genuine human narratives—whether through direct copy or subtle visual cues—can strengthen consumer trust.
- The effectiveness of this approach hinges on authenticity; superficial “humanity” buzzwords risk backlash.
