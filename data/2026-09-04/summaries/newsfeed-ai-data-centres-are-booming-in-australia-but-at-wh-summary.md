# AI data centres are booming in Australia – but at what cost?

## Boom and drivers
- Australia now hosts about 162 data centres with plans for 90 more, backed by over A$155 bn (£80 bn) of investment.  
- Investors are attracted by abundant clean energy, natural‑gas reserves, plentiful land and political stability.  
- OpenAI’s Sam Altman has suggested the country could become the world’s data‑centre capital.  
- The surge follows the 2022 launch of ChatGPT, which sparked a race to build facilities that can power AI workloads and reduce latency.

## Community concerns
- Residents in Sydney’s Lane Cove hear constant humming from a data centre only 350 m from homes, describing it as “jet‑plane” noise.  
- Similar facilities are planned in the local industrial park, raising fears about quality of life.  
- Local deputy mayor Rochelle Flood questions whether the growth can be sustained without harming the environment.

## Economic and strategic arguments
- Data‑centre infrastructure is seen as essential to keep AI development and value chains within Australia rather than importing tools.  
- Belinda Dennett (Data Centres Australia) stresses that without local facilities the country would miss out on AI‑related economic benefits.  
- Jon Whittle (former CSIRO director) argues the boom helps avoid recession and that the government has little choice but to support it.

## Energy and water challenges
- The Australian Energy Market Operator projects data‑centre power demand could triple by 2030.  
- Without new renewable generation, electricity prices in New South Wales could rise 26 % by 2035.  
- Some operators plan gas‑fired power stations; backup diesel generators are also common.  
- Data centres can consume up to 40 million litres of water per day—about 25 % of Sydney’s projected drinking‑water use by 2035—pressuring a drought‑prone nation.

## Government and policy response
- NSW Treasurer Daniel Mookhey links data‑centre growth to renewable‑energy investment, claiming private funding will replace coal plants.  
- Prime Minister Anthony Albanese announced a 2027 legal framework requiring large‑scale data centres to secure power supplies, limit water use and fund extra water infrastructure.  
- The legislation will apply only to new projects; existing and under‑construction sites are exempt.  
- Environmental groups are calling for a pause on all further data‑centre development.

## Outlook
- Australia is positioned to become a major AI and data‑centre hub, but the rapid expansion raises significant environmental, resource‑allocation and community‑impact questions.  
- Balancing economic benefits with sustainable energy and water management will be crucial to determine whether the boom can be maintained without compromising the country’s climate and livability goals.
