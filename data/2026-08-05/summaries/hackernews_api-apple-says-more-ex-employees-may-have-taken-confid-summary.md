# Apple says more ex-employees may have taken confidential data to OpenAI | TechCrunch

# Summary of Apple vs. OpenAI Trade Secrets Case

## Legal Action
- Apple has filed for a preliminary injunction to stop OpenAI from developing AI devices or products that use Apple’s technology.  
- The company seeks expedited discovery of information from OpenAI employees Chang Liu (senior systems engineer) and Tang Yew Tan (Chief Hardware Officer), as well as from OpenAI, its foundation, and the startup “io” co‑founded by former Apple designer Jony Ive.  

## New Allegations
- Apple’s investigation now identifies 11 additional former Apple employees who may have been witnesses or participants in the alleged trade‑secret theft.  
- Specific claims include:  
  - A former Apple employee met with Liu and Yu‑Ting Peng (another former Apple employee now at OpenAI) before Peng’s interview and discussed confidential, unannounced Apple product information.  
  - Another former employee captured screenshots of confidential Apple documents related to an unannounced product before an OpenAI interview.  
  - After Apple’s original complaint, several former Apple staff now at OpenAI contacted Apple about returning Apple‑issued devices they retained after leaving.  

## Apple’s Position
- Apple argues it has “good cause” to suspect further involvement and therefore requests expedited discovery.  
- The motion for a preliminary injunction remains pending.  

## OpenAI’s Response
- OpenAI publicly called Apple’s injunction request “based on false information and completely unnecessary,” stating it does not possess Apple’s trade secrets and has no interest in them.  
- The company emphasized its focus on building innovative products and highlighted past mistakes by Apple, such as:  
  - Mis‑addressed email due to similar surnames.  
  - Alleged false statements about discussions with Apple’s general counsel.  
  - Dispute over Apple’s claim that “residual access” to its systems resulted from poor security practices.
