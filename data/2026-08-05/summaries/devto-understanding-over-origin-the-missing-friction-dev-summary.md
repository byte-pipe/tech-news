## The Friction Concept
- Hand‑writing code introduces “friction”: encountering walls, refactoring, and exploring constraints, which builds understanding.  
- AI assistance reduces friction to prompt crafting, option selection, and validation, which may not foster the same depth of learning.  
- Friction can be introduced deliberately in AI workflows (critique prompts, aggressive refactoring) and can be absent in careless hand‑written code (copy‑paste, autopilot patterns).  
- Therefore, friction—not who typed the code—drives comprehension and ownership.

## Implications for Developers
- Quality assessment should focus on the presence of frictional learning experiences rather than the code’s origin.  
- Developers can create friction in AI‑assisted workflows to achieve the same understanding benefits.  
- Conversely, hand‑written code without critical engagement offers little advantage.  
- Maintaining transparent “maintenance receipts” (tests, incident logs, refactor histories) provides concrete evidence of ownership.

## Conclusion
- The author’s initial stance that “origin doesn’t matter” was too simplistic; the missing element is friction.  
- Hand‑writing often creates friction naturally, but intentional friction can be built into AI‑augmented development.  
- Recognizing and cultivating friction leads to deeper understanding, better maintenance, and genuine trust in software, regardless of how the code was initially produced.
