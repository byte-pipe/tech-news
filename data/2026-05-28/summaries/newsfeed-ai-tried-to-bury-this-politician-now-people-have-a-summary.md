# AI tried to bury this politician — now people have actually heard of him  

## Background  
- The Democratic primary for New York’s 12th congressional district has become a proxy war between AI giants Anthropic and OpenAI over future AI regulation.  
- Alex Bores, a little‑known New York state assemblyman, authored one of the nation’s first AI‑regulatory bills (the RAISE Act).  

## Super PAC “Leading the Future”  
- A super PAC funded by OpenAI, Palantir, a16z executives, and billionaire backers (Joe Lonsdale, Marc Andreessen, Greg Brockman) launched in late 2025 to oppose Bores.  
- By May 2026 the PAC had spent roughly $2.4 million on attack ads, despite New York’s costly media market.  
- The PAC’s strategy mirrors earlier efforts (e.g., 2024 Fairshake campaign) that ousted incumbents by flooding the airwaves.  

## Impact on Bores’s Campaign  
- Bores entered the race in October 2025 with no major ad buys; his first campaign ad appeared only on May 11, 2026.  
- The relentless attacks unintentionally raised Bores’s name recognition, turning the negative ads into free publicity.  
- Polls from Emerson College show Bores neck‑and‑neck with front‑runner Micah Lasher and often ahead of other contenders.  
- Voters exposed to the attacks reported higher willingness to support Bores, perceiving him as a target of Silicon‑Valley billionaires.  
- The campaign leveraged the controversy, mocking the PAC’s claims (e.g., pointing out the irony of a Palantir billionaire attacking a former Palantir employee).  

## Broader Implications  
- The fight highlights how AI companies are willing to spend millions to shape regulation by influencing elections.  
- The “Streisand effect” turned a defensive campaign into a signal booster for AI‑safety advocacy.  
- Anthropic’s allied groups (Jobs and Democracy PAC, Public First Action) publicly backed Bores, bringing national media attention to the OpenAI‑Anthropic rivalry.  
- Ultimately, the battle may benefit the public discourse on AI regulation, even as the corporations continue to pour money into political proxy wars.
