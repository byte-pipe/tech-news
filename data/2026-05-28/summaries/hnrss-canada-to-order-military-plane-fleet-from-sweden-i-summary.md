# Canada to order military plane fleet from Sweden in shift from US suppliers

## Decision and aircraft
- Canada will purchase Saab’s GlobalEye early‑warning aircraft, based on the Bombardier Global 6500 jet.  
- The selection replaces the previously considered Boeing E‑7 Wedgetail, which has suffered delays and cost overruns.  
- Prime Minister Mark Carney announced the decision at a defence conference in Ottawa.

## Rationale
- Reduces reliance on US defence firms and diversifies Canada’s military procurement.  
- Enhances capability to monitor and protect Canada’s Arctic territory (over 4.4 million km²).  
- Supports a broader policy of strengthening ties with Nordic NATO allies.

## Programme details
- Military officials have indicated a need for six aircraft; exact fleet size and contract cost were not disclosed.  
- Saab plans to invest in research and development in Canada and work with the Canadian supply chain, creating local jobs.

## Strategic implications
- Viewed as a test case for the Carney government’s pivot away from American military capability.  
- Reinforces defence cooperation between Canada and Sweden; Swedish Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson highlighted the partnership on social media.  
- Saab is also a contender for future Gripen fighter sales, while Canada continues its existing F‑35 purchase from Lockheed‑Martin.

## Context
- The move follows concerns about US trade measures and reflects Canada’s desire for greater Arctic collaboration with Nordic countries.
