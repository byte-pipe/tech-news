## A crisis, in the new chief's own words

John Ternus,Apple's▲+0.16%— Apple, up 0.16 percent todaychief executive since September 1, used his first cover interview to keep Chinese memory onApple'slist of options12. Memory supply constraints are "clearly a serious crisis," he told TIMEin an interview published October 15↗, andApple's"basic stance is that we must review all options"; he named no supplier1. The Financial Times reported in July thatAppleis trialing DRAM, the workhorse memory in phones and servers, fromChangXin Memory Technologies▲+3.28%— ChangXin Memory Technologies, up 3.28 percent today(CXMT), starting with devices sold in China. The Wall Street Journal reported in August that the tests span iPhones and MacBooks, with no final decision on adoption1. The market has already voted on the odds: on October 16, as the preliminary results circulated,CXMT'sShanghai-listed shares rose 3.28% whileMicron▼−6.00%— Micron, down 6.00 percent todayfell 5.63%, leavingMicrondown more than 10% for the week69.

## The memory bill pushed the door open

Tim Cook, Ternus's predecessor, called memory pricing a "100-year flood" onApple's July 30 earnings call↗, and finance chiefKevan Parekhsaid memory caused more than 100% ofApple'sgross-margin compression3. Samsung andSK Hynix▼−6.61%— SK Hynix, down 6.61 percent todayraised prices on the low-power DRAM they supply for iPhones by 80% and 100% in the first quarter of this year1. A report then saidApple cut October iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max orders↗by about a seventh to a fifth of plan, after memory costs pushed those phones to $1,199 and $1,2994.Applehad walked away fromCXMTtalks earlier this year, finding no price advantage, andCXMT'scost per bit still runs about 30% above the incumbents5. Washington pushes the other way: seven US senators, Chuck Schumer among them, have askedAppleto avoidCXMT, which sits on a Pentagon list of Chinese military-linked firms1.

## Who wins if Apple says yes

IfApplequalifiesCXMTfor China-market devices,CXMTgains a brand buyer at iPhone scale, and Samsung, SK Hynix andMicronlose the three-supplier market Cook complained about3.Micron'srevenue is cushioned:about $150 billion of take-or-pay contracts, paid whether or not customers take delivery, covering more than 35% of its revenue through 20305. What breaks is the pricing power in the shares. That sets up a two-sided trade:CXMT, the breaker, whose market already rewards the news with a 3.28% gain againstMicron's5.63% fall, or patience untilAppleanswers. The one position this setup punishes is the incumbents' pricing power held into their earnings while the door stays open.

## Overblown, say the analysts; the tests decide

Rosenblatt's Kevin Cassidy called the selloff "overblown" andkept a Buy rating on Micron↗9. Fundstrat's Kent Fung arguesCXMT's4F² design, a denser memory-cell layout, attacks conventional server DRAM rather than the high-bandwidth memory AI data centers buy9.CXMThas also yet to prove it can build the new cells at commercial yields5. Its president, Cao Kanyu, has promisedserver-grade DDR5 modules by the end of 20269. No adoption decision exists1. The tell is whetherCXMTDRAM appears in a China-market iPhone or Mac: that is the signalApplewill split its memory bill.
