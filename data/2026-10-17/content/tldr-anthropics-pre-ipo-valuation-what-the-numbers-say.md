## The $965B number: what a primary round actually fixes

A primary round is Anthropic selling newly issued shares to investors to raise cash. The price those investors agree to pay, multiplied across all shares after the raise, is the post-money valuation. The Series H produced $965B post-money on a $65B raise (CNBC, TechCrunch, 2026-05-28).

This is the cleanest pre-IPO valuation figure available for a private company, for a simple reason: real money changed hands at a negotiated, documented price, with sophisticated investors on both sides. Think of it like an appraisal backed by an actual purchase, not a listing estimate. It still has a shelf life. A round mark is a snapshot of one negotiation on one date, and private companies only reprice when they raise again.

## Why the secondary number is higher, and shakier

Between funding rounds, early employees and early investors sometimes sell existing shares to other buyers. These secondary transactions generate their own implied valuations. In September 2026, Anthropic secondaries implied roughly $1T to $1.15T, above the primary mark.

Higher does not mean more authoritative. Secondary prints are thin (few shares, few trades), privately negotiated, and often reflect a specific buyer's willingness to pay for scarce access rather than a broad market consensus. A single eager buyer can set a headline-grabbing mark on a small block. Treat the secondary range as a signal of demand, not as a repriced valuation of the company.

## The token called ANTHROPIC is not Anthropic

Here is where readers get tripped up. There is a token on Solana (a blockchain) named "ANTHROPIC," issued by a company called PreStocks. It is a tokenized wrapper: a crypto token that tracks, or claims to track, exposure to a pre-IPO name. Its total market value is about $8.1 million.

That number is not a valuation of Anthropic, is not comparable to the $965B round mark, and does not discover the company's worth. Holders of the token do not own Anthropic equity, carry no shareholder rights, and have no voting rights. The distinction matters because the token's price can move for reasons that have nothing to do with the company: speculative interest, liquidity on a particular venue, or how many buyers want crypto-native exposure to a famous private name.

What is genuinely striking about the onchain data is the churn. Over a recent 30-day window, the ANTHROPIC wrapper recorded roughly$110.6M in transfer volume against its own ~$8.1M market cap, meaning it turned over about 13.6 times its own size in a month, across 60,582 holders. It is the highest-volume tokenized pre-IPO asset tracked on Solana by a wide margin. High trading velocity on a small asset describes trading behavior, not company valuation.

## What "in registration" does and does not mean

Anthropic confidentially submitted a draft S-1 registration statement to the SEC on 2026-06-01. A confidential S-1 is a private draft filing that lets a company begin the IPO process with the regulator without publicly disclosing financials yet. It is a real, dateable step toward a possible public listing.

It is not a commitment to go public, a price, or a timeline. Companies file confidentially and then wait, revise, or shelve plans depending on markets and their own readiness. The correct reading is procedural: Anthropic has started the clock on the regulatory side, nothing more. Anyone attaching an IPO date or offer price to that filing is speculating.

## How to cite Anthropic's valuation without getting it wrong

If you are writing or deciding based on this, the hierarchy is straightforward. Lead with the$965B primary roundand date it to May 2026. If you cite the secondary range, label it as thin secondary prints from September 2026 and do not present it as a repriced valuation. Never cite the Solana token's market cap as a company valuation. And report the S-1 as a confidential filing dated 2026-06-01, with no inferred timing.
