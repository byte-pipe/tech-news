# Three Additional Responses to Problems (Other than Solving Them)

## Pushing Problems Around
When navigating issues other than solving them, people often engage in "pushing problems around." This approach focuses on making everything better by worsening it. It is a common outcome in medium and large organizations but can be inefficient and wasteful when local optimization is being sought.

## Preserving Problems
Other approaches include "preserving the problem" or "fixating on preserving the very problem you are trying to solve." In institutions, this occurs often when addressing issues as solutions. To address such problems effectively, focus on identifying those who depend on the issue and involve them in your solution plan.

## Promoting New Problems
Promoting new problems refers to questioning whether each problem eliminates the next one after it is addressed. Neil Postman's six questions from 1998 suggest that when you solve one problem, a new problem may emerge as a consequence.

These additional responses highlight key behaviors and strategies people employ in addressing issues beyond traditional solutions.
