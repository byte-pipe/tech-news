# xAI is looking more like a datacentre REIT than a frontier lab - Martin Alderson

# Summary of “xAI is looking more like a datacentre REIT than a frontier lab – Martin Alderson”

## Background
- xAI merged with SpaceX in February, so any revenue from new deals flows directly to the entity that will soon IPO.  
- Recent unexpected partnerships:  
  - Anthropic receives capacity from xAI’s older Colossus 1 datacentre in Memphis.  
  - Google secures a similar arrangement for its own AI workloads.  

## Anthropic’s capacity crunch
- Users of Claude have experienced severe shortages, especially during peak hours (5 am–11 am PT / 1 pm–7 pm GMT).  
- Anthropic responded with “peak‑hour restrictions” that limited usage during high‑demand periods.  
- Demand continues to outpace supply, forcing the company to consider further rationing despite competition from Google and OpenAI.  

## xAI’s rescue deals
- **Anthropic deal**: access to 300 MW (≈220 k GPUs) at Colossus 1, lifting the peak‑hour limits.  
  - Reported fee: about **$1.25 bn per month**.  
- **Google deal**: 110 k GPUs for roughly **$920 mn per month**.  
- Both contracts include a 90‑day cancellation right after an initial lock‑in period.  

## Financial implications
- Assuming the deals run for 18 months, xAI would recover its capital expenditure and retain hundreds of MW of GPU capacity.  
- Operating costs (power, fuel) are a small fraction of revenue (≈1 % of Anthropic’s $15 bn annual spend).  
- The agreements appear highly profitable even before accounting for depreciation.  

## Potential red flags
- Ongoing legal battle between Elon Musk and OpenAI; the Anthropic deal may be intended to pressure OpenAI.  
- Google is a major SpaceX shareholder, giving it incentive to boost the IPO valuation.  
- Nonetheless, the global shortage of GPUs makes such large‑scale capacity valuable regardless of motives.  

## Competitive advantage in datacentre build‑out
- SpaceX/xAI can construct datacentres extremely fast (Colossus 1 built in 122 days).  
- Traditional hyperscalers typically take years for similar projects, leaving them lagging as demand surges.  
- This speed gives xAI a genuine edge in supplying compute to AI firms.  

## Impact on Grok
- Capacity originally earmarked for training and inference of Grok models is now leased to direct competitors.  
- Suggests a strategic retreat from positioning Grok as a “frontier‑lab” effort, possibly due to over‑estimated demand.  
- Monetising spare capacity may be a pragmatic short‑term move while the training landscape remains uncertain.  

## Overall assessment
- Three factors are all at play:  
  1. Financial engineering surrounding the upcoming SpaceX IPO.  
  2. A severe, medium‑term compute shortage.  
  3. SpaceX/xAI’s superior ability to build and operate large datacentres quickly.  
- The relative weight of each factor will determine the success of what could become the largest IPO in North American history.  
- In practice, xAI is beginning to resemble a datacentre REIT with a frontier‑lab component attached, rather than the reverse.
