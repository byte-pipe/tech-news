# How much of Thermo Fisher’s antibody data has been manipulated?

## TL;DR
- By 3 June 2026 more than 450 images in Thermo Fisher’s online primary‑antibody catalog show signs of manipulation (plus one from Abcam).  
- A curated repository of the problematic images is hosted on Zenodo (maintained by the author and Sholto David).  
- Researchers can contribute new findings through a Google form.  

## Investigation Overview
- The inquiry began when Sholto David noticed a Western blot for an anti‑p53 monoclonal antibody labeled “Advanced Verification” that contained duplicated bands after vertical/horizontal flips and 180° rotation.  
- Johan Duchêne reported a similar suspicious blot for another anti‑p53 antibody, prompting a systematic search.  
- Over 100 verification images have been documented as apparently manipulated; many more are suspected but not yet catalogued.  

## Types of Manipulation Observed
- **Identical bands**: multiple lanes showing the same band pattern after flipping or rotating.  
- **Brush‑stroke artifacts**: contrast‑adjusted images reveal painted‑over regions suggestive of Photoshop edits.  
- **Copy‑pasted background**: repetitive blocks of noise or abrupt discontinuities indicating that portions of the image were duplicated.  
- **Recurring background pattern**: at least 50 antibodies share an almost identical background (“pattern A”) with only the target band repositioned.  

## Highlights of Findings
- Image‑search tools (Google Lens, Bing Images, DuckDuckGo) reveal hundreds of additional instances that have not yet been added to the repository.  
- The same problematic image is sometimes used to verify different antibodies, raising concerns about data provenance.  

## Context on Antibody Reliability
- Antibodies are essential but notoriously fickle reagents; YCharOS estimated in 2024 that >50 % of antibodies fail in at least one application.  
- Non‑specific or ineffective antibodies contribute heavily to irreproducibility in biomedical literature and cause costly experimental delays.  
- A single vial of Thermo Fisher antibody typically costs USD 400–500, amplifying the impact of unreliable validation data.  

## Purpose of the Repository
- **Awareness**: alert biomedical scientists that vendor‑provided verification data may be unreliable.  
- **Community reporting**: invite others to submit suspect images via the provided Google form for inclusion in the Zenodo spreadsheet.  
- **Encouragement of independent validation**: stress the importance of testing antibodies in‑house before use.  

## Thermo Fisher’s Response (8 June 2026)
- The company issued a 15‑point FAQ denying data fabrication and stating that any image adjustments were made solely for “presentation clarity.”  
- Thermo Fisher pledged to label images where the original, unadjusted version is unavailable, acknowledging that such adjustments can raise integrity questions.  

## Take‑away Message
- Researchers should always validate antibodies themselves, regardless of vendor claims.  
- Reviewing the Zenodo collection can help assess the extent of manipulation and guide more critical evaluation of vendor‑provided verification data.
