# Are we offloading too much of our thinking to AI?

## Key observations
- The author notices a growing habit of delegating both trivial and complex decisions to AI, from search engines to advanced LLMs.
- AI now performs intermediate steps such as research, synthesis, and reasoning, delivering finished answers in minutes.
- This shift raises concerns about autonomy and who ultimately makes important life decisions.

## Illustrative examples
- **Ken Liu’s “The Perfect Match”**: the AI assistant Tilly predicts the protagonist’s preferences so precisely that he lets it choose breakfast, music, and even a date.
- **“Microphone Man”**: a startup founder records all conversations, feeds them to Claude Fable, and relies on the model for all critical thinking, even turning the service into a business that does the same for others.
- **Personal anecdote in Portugal**: the author and sister first brainstormed hypotheses about Portugal’s view of its explorers, then used ChatGPT to test and expand those ideas, finding AI useful as a supplement rather than a replacement.

## Benefits of offloading
- Saves time on routine tasks (e.g., weather checks, factual look‑ups, translation of long reports).
- Enables professionals to focus on higher‑level work: researchers generate ideas while coding agents handle implementation; students use ChatGPT as a personalized tutor for exam preparation.
- Potentially increases life satisfaction and productivity when mundane cognition is automated.

## Risks and trade‑offs
- Over‑reliance may erode critical thinking skills and personal agency.
- Quick answers can replace slower, reflective thinking that helps retain meaningful knowledge and discern importance.
- The line between assistance and loss of autonomy is thin; decisions about values, relationships, and identity may become outsourced to algorithms.

## Personal reflections
- The author values moments of “slow thinking,” such as walking without a phone and letting questions fade, suggesting that forgetting the trivial can be beneficial.
- Experiencing AI as a tool that extends, rather than replaces, human reasoning feels more rewarding than letting it dominate every decision.

## Conclusion
- Offloading thinking to AI offers clear efficiency gains but threatens the practice of autonomous, reflective thought.
- A balanced approach—using AI to test and augment ideas after initial human reasoning—may preserve autonomy while still leveraging the technology’s strengths.
