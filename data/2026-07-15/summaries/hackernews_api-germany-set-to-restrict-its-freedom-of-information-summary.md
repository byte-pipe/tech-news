# Germany set to restrict its Freedom of Information Act  

## Main developments  
- Lawmakers approved a 34‑point reform package just before the summer recess, promoted by Chancellor Friedrich Merz as a way to “get Germany back on track.”  
- The package includes a controversial overhaul of the Freedom of Information Act (IFG), in force since 2006, which currently allows any individual to request government data free of charge (with limited security exemptions).  

## Proposed changes to the IFG  
- Requests may be limited to “natural persons” only, excluding associations, NGOs and journalists.  
- Fees for information requests could be increased substantially.  
- Names of civil‑service employees could be redacted to protect them from threats.  
- The government plans to examine whether the right to request information should be restricted to German citizens and EU citizens residing in Germany.  
- Information concerning critical infrastructure, counter‑intelligence and counter‑terrorism would receive heightened protection.  

## Reactions and criticism  
- Opposition Green Party MP Konstantin von Notz argues the reforms undermine transparency and civil rights.  
- An open letter signed by 110 civil‑society groups (including Greenpeace, Amnesty International and Transparency International) urges the government to keep the IFG unchanged, warning that higher fees and exclusions would “effectively abolish freedom of information.”  
- SPD members of the Bundestag’s Interior, Digital Affairs and Consumer Protection committees state they will not support any reduction of access rights.  
- Greenpeace climate expert Martin Kaiser warns the reforms would weaken public oversight of infrastructure, land use, species conservation and climate policy, eroding trust.  

## Current use of the IFG  
- Between 2015 and 2022, about 105 000 information requests were filed with German authorities.  
- Roughly 16 200 requests resulted in partial withholding of information, and about 9 000 were denied outright; the majority of requests were fulfilled.  

## Context  
- The CDU/CSU coalition justifies the changes by citing “highly complex domestic and foreign threats” such as cyber‑warfare and hacker attacks, arguing that greater confidentiality is needed for government data.
