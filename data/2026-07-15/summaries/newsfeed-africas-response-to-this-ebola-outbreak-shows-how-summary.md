# Africa’s response to this Ebola outbreak shows how to shape global health

## Overview
- The 2024 Ebola outbreak (Bundibugyo species) in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) and Uganda has exceeded 1,700 confirmed cases, making it one of the largest recorded.
- Dozens of governments and health organizations are involved in the public‑health response, highlighting shifts in global‑health strategy after recent U.S. aid reductions.

## U.S. and other travel restrictions
- Following the 2025 closure of USAID and cuts to overseas medical budgets, the United States imposed travel bans on entrants from the DRC, Uganda, and South Sudan and funded a field hospital in Kenya for exposed Americans.
- Canada and the Bahamas adopted similar bans.
- The Africa CDC and WHO criticize these bans as lacking scientific justification and potentially harming economies by disrupting trade and tourism.

## Africa CDC’s evidence‑based model
- Emphasizes community reporting, case isolation, infection‑control protocols in health facilities, and strong community engagement.
- The author, a pandemic‑preparedness governance expert, argues this cooperative approach is the most effective and ethical for future epidemics and calls for broader international support.

## Rapid continental emergency declaration
- Within days of confirming human Ebola transmission, Africa CDC declared a “public health emergency of continental security.”
- The declaration was used to mobilize resources quickly, coordinating with WHO under a single plan, budget, and team—contrasting with slower WHO declarations in past outbreaks (e.g., 2014 West Africa).

## DRC’s on‑the‑ground actions
- Expanded bed capacity in health facilities.
- Established a decentralized testing network across the affected region.
- Created screening and referral units for case identification and triage.
- Resource allocation decisions are led by the DRC for its own population rather than solely to prevent cross‑border spread.

## International solidarity versus bilateral agreements
- At a May meeting of African health ministers, several countries pledged unconditional cash or in‑kind support to the DRC response, emphasizing solidarity.
- This contrasts with U.S. bilateral agreements that require recipient nations to share disease data without reciprocal access and to co‑fund large portions of preparedness efforts.

## Implications for global health
- The outbreak demonstrates how African‑led, evidence‑based coordination can shape a more ethical and effective global‑health response.
- It underscores the need for the international community to back regional initiatives rather than rely on restrictive travel measures or conditional aid.
