# Free Daytime Electricity Is Coming – How It Works  

## What the Solar Sharer Offer Is  
- From 1 July 2026 retailers in NSW, South‑East Queensland and South Australia must provide at least three hours of free daytime electricity each day.  
- Access requires only a smart meter and opting‑in with the retailer; no rooftop solar or home ownership is needed.  
- The free window aligns with peak solar generation (roughly 11 am–2 pm or noon–3 pm, varying locally).  

## Eligibility and Geographic Coverage  
- Mandatory in New South Wales, South‑East Queensland and South Australia under the federal Default Market Offer.  
- Victoria is under consultation and may join from October 2026; other states are expected to follow by 2027.  
- Retailers such as AGL, Red Energy, GloBird Energy and OVO Energy already offer similar voluntary plans in non‑covered states.  

## Key Change After Public Consultation  
- A “reasonable use” cap of 24 kWh per day was added to keep the scheme financially sustainable for retailers and fair for the grid.  
- The cap reflects the average daily consumption of a five‑person household; most users will stay well below it.  

## Impact of the 24 kWh Cap  
- Households with rooftop solar rarely hit the cap because solar already supplies much of the midday load.  
- The cap may be approached on cloudy winter days, when charging a large home battery and an EV simultaneously, or with very small solar systems.  
- Exceeding the cap simply reverts the excess usage to standard daytime rates—no penalties apply.  

## Expected Savings  
- Shifting 10 % of daily use to the free window: **$100–$190 / year**.  
- Shifting 20 %: **$300–$790 / year**.  
- Shifting 25–30 % (adding pool pump, EV charging, dishwasher, etc.): **$400–$1,100 / year**.  
- Actual savings depend on the household’s tariff, size and consistency of load shifting.  

## Benefits for Solar‑and‑Battery Households  
- Grid‑charged battery during the free window can be used later in the day when rates are highest.  
- EVs can be charged at no cost if plugged in during the free period.  
- Platforms like Sigenergy can automate scheduling of batteries and smart appliances to maximise the free window.  

## Who Benefits Most  
- Residents who are at home during the day (remote workers, retirees, carers).  
- Households with programmable or smart appliances (hot‑water systems, EV chargers, pool pumps).  
- Renters and apartment dwellers, who previously could not tap into rooftop solar benefits, now gain access via the smart‑meter opt‑in.  

## How to Sign Up and Optimise Use  
1. Ensure a smart meter is installed (retailers will provide one at no charge if needed).  
2. Opt in with your energy retailer when the scheme launches on 1 July 2026.  
3. Identify appliances that can be timed to run during the free window (e.g., washing machine, dryer, dishwasher, hot‑water heater, EV charger).  
4. Consider using an energy‑management platform to automate load shifting and battery charging.  

By following these steps, households can capture free midday electricity, reduce bills and make better use of existing solar and storage assets.
