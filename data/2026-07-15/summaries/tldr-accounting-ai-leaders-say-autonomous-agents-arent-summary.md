# Accounting AI leaders say autonomous agents aren’t there yet

## Overview
- The July 8 Earmark webinar “AI Agents for Accountants: What’s Working Right Now” explored which accounting workflows are currently run by AI agents.
- Panelists found it difficult to point to any end‑to‑end processes that are fully autonomous today.

## Current Uses of AI Agents
- **Inbox management** – sorting and prioritizing emails.  
- **Spreadsheet automation** – transforming point‑of‑sale exports, validating formulas, converting raw data into standard templates.  
- **Dashboard creation** – generating client‑facing visual reports.  
- **Reusable AI skills** – standardizing recurring tasks across teams.  
- **Workflow standardization** – embedding AI steps into repeatable processes.

## Adoption Pattern
- Firms begin with **predictable, rule‑based work** such as Excel‑heavy tasks, CSV imports, and template generation.
- Example: Hewlett Packard Enterprise’s CFO started with a single strategic process before extending AI to accounts receivable, accounts payable, and forecasting.
- The shift from experimentation to integration takes time; organizations must embed AI into existing workflows before realizing efficiency gains.

## Challenges
- **Data context**: Gartner notes that AI agents need richer semantic layers; insufficient context limits reliable performance.  
- **Data integration**: Bain reports inadequate integration as the top obstacle to achieving expected cost savings.  
- **Human oversight**: CFOs remain cautious, preferring structured, repeatable workflows before tackling more complex tasks.

## Expert Insights
- **Toni Witt (Sweet)** – highlights Excel‑heavy processes as low‑hanging opportunities.  
- **Twyla Verhelst (Karbon)** – stresses an “adoption curve” and the need to embed AI before it can consistently deliver efficiencies.  
- **Colleen Oates (Gusto)** – feels overwhelmed learning the components of autonomous agents and seeks clear resources.  
- **Audience Q&A** – No accounting close tasks are fully owned by AI agents; roughly 75‑80 % of workload can be automated, while 20‑25 % still requires human review.

## Research Findings
- **Bain & Company**: Only 7 % of surveyed organizations have fully autonomous AI agents in production.  
- **Gartner**: Organizations that build semantic layers around data improve AI accuracy and reduce costs, indicating that data infrastructure, not tool capability, is the primary barrier.

## Takeaway
- Practical AI applications exist (spreadsheet automation, inbox sorting, dashboard generation, administrative approvals), but fully autonomous agents that run entire accounting processes without human review are not yet viable.
- CFOs are adopting a **step‑by‑step approach**, automating repeatable high‑value workflows first and expanding only after data context and integration challenges are addressed.
