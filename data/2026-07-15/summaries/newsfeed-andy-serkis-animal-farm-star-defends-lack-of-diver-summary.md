# Andy Serkis: Animal Farm star defends lack of diversity in Lord of the Rings cast - BBC News

# Summary of Andy Serkis interview – BBC News (14 July 2026)

## Lord of the Rings projects
- Serkis is directing and starring in **The Hunt for Gollum**, set between *The Hobbit* and the original *Lord of the Rings* trilogies, due for a Christmas release next year.  
- The film explores Gollum’s psychology and the origin of Bilbo’s ring, occurring in “two different dimensions.”  
- He will not direct the second new film, **Shadow of the Past**, written by Stephen Colbert, but acknowledges its potential.

## Casting and diversity comments
- Serkis, a long‑time equality campaigner, notes that earlier Tolkien films featured almost entirely white casts.  
- Current casting announcements (Jamie Dornan, Anya Taylor‑Joy, Kate Winslet, Leo Woodall, Elijah Wood, Ian McKellen) are also white.  
- He explains that Tolkien’s world draws heavily on Norse mythology, giving the Shire a “very white” feel, and that the filmmakers are not aiming for “politically correct just‑casting” but will consider diversity where it feels relevant.

## Animal Farm animated film
- Serkis directed a new big‑screen animated version of George Orwell’s *Animal Farm*, released in the UK.  
- Voice cast includes Seth Rogen (Napoleon), Woody Harrelson (Boxer), Kieran Culkin, Jim Parsons, Glenn Close, Kathleen Turner, Steve Buscemi, and Serkis himself as Randolph the rooster.  
- The story adds a new piglet protagonist, Lucky (voiced by Gaten Matarazzo), shifts the allegory from anti‑communist to anti‑modern autocracy, adds a third act with a happy ending, and includes comedic elements such as fart jokes.  
- Serkis recalls reading the book as a child on a bus and being emotionally moved by its depiction of a collapsing utopia.

## Historical context and funding
- The original 1954 British animated *Animal Farm* was secretly funded by the CIA, a fact Serkis was asked about.  
- He states unequivocally that his film had “zero chance” of CIA funding and points to its modest US box‑office take ($5.5 m of a $35 m budget) as evidence.

## Reception and criticism
- US critics were largely negative: *The New York Times* called it “lost in the mud” and ideologically confused; *The Wall Street Journal* described it as “feeble” comedy and heavy‑handed allegory.  
- Despite the criticism, the film performed well with young audiences, scoring highly in tests with children as young as six.

## Serkis’s responses
- He argues that Orwell’s purpose was to provoke thought and discussion, and the new film aims to reach a broad audience.  
- Serkis is frustrated by reviewers who dismissed the film after only seeing the trailer, noting the polarized online reaction.  
- He believes Orwell would not have condemned the adaptation, emphasizing close collaboration with the Orwell estate.
