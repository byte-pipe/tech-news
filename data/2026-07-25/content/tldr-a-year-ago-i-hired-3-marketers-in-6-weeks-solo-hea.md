description: 'A year ago I hired 3 marketers in 6 weeks, solo head of marketing at a company growing 3x YoY. I wrote about my observations for others in this hard job market and it did pretty well: 165k impressions and 4.7k engagements. Yesterday I posted about looking for two roles for a client, and my DMs got destroyed (in a good way). Running it back with what I saw then, and now. 24 hours in, dozens of candidates. The job market is HARD! Here''s some observations to stand out to help: 1. Roles exist before they''re posted → I''ve been in the leadership conversations, they''re talking about a hire months before it''s public. The best time to start a conversation is when there''s no opening → if there''s a company you want to work for, get in the door now. Comment on their stuff, DM someone on the team, ask what they''re building toward 2. Portfolios go a long way → Mine was just a Notion page (nothing fancy), but it made a difference. A top SaaS CMO told me to keep using it. I hired candidates
  who used a Google Slides PDF. Now with AI, you could spin up a simple webpage → Don''t stress on format, but on content → A deeper dive on your roles and impact, visual examples of your work, results you''re proud of → what others say about you (I had 7 mini testimonials) → Your working style, leadership style, and a bit about you as a person 3. Your resume still matters → I reviewed hundreds last time, and I know I missed great people because of formatting → Clean, easy to skim, focused on what''s relevant. Don''t cram everything you''ve ever done. Link to your portfolio for the rest 4. "I''d love to chat" doesn''t cut it → I''m getting a lot of DMs that are just an intro and a request for time → Send a small pitch instead. Two or three sentences on what you''d do in the role 5. Research the company before you reach out → Deeply here, look into their ads library, others who are posting, etc. 6. Timing matters → Applying early helped candidates stand out last time (and this time) 7. Come
  with great questions → Asking thoughtful, advanced questions shows how you think and how you prepare. I can also spot the vanilla, generic ones pretty easily now 8. Multithread → Finding someone on the team, asking what the role actually looks like day to day, what''s hard about it, what would make someone great at it 9. Posting on LinkedIn is becoming a real differentiator → even if you don''t have a large following, it helps highlight how you think, consistently → When I''m evaluating someone and they have a body of work I can read, it shortcuts half the interview → Companies want people who can build distribution. Willingness to post is step one The market is hard. I know the instinct is to apply everywhere. But going slower on fewer roles is what actually gets you one. If you''re in it right now, I hope this helps even a little. Rooting for you! | 10 comments on LinkedIn'
