# ADHD in the Workplace: Consequences and Importance of Reasonable Adjustments

## Introduction

* Ryan Toghill, a deputy store manager at Lidl UK, was wrongly dismissed for gross misconduct after using equipment he wasn't trained on, but successfully appealed and won £45,000 damages.
* The case highlights the need for employers to make reasonable adjustments to accommodate employees with Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD).

## Key Points

* Ryan Toghill's ADHD was not taken into account during the disciplinary process leading to his termination.
* Lidl UK failed to provide adequate accommodations and communication, which contributed to his unfair dismissal.
* The tribunal found that reasonable adjustments, including additional breaks, would have mitigated the impact of his condition on his work.

## Reasonable Adjustments in the Workplace

* The Equality Act 2010 ensures employees with disabilities are protected from discriminatory treatment.
* Employees with ADHD may face difficulties due to their condition, and it's the employer's responsibility to make reasonable adjustments to overcome these challenges.

## Employer Responsibility

* Companies like Lidl UK must take into account employees' conditions, including ADHD, and provide necessary accommodations to prevent unfair dismissal.
* Failure to do so can lead to costly lawsuits and reputational damage.

## Challenges Faced by Employees with ADHD in the Workplace

* Discrimination and lack of communication can occur without intending it, such as when a manager makes non-verbal frustration towards an employee with ADHD.
* A 2025 tribunal case found that a software engineer with ADHD had faced perceived discrimination due to a manager's expressions of "non-verbal frustration".
* Companies need to create a supportive work environment to help employees manage their conditions and perform at their best.
