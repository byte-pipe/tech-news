# AI Firms Seek to Relax Permitting Process for Data Centers and Other Polluting Facilities

The Trump administration is considering rolling back the Environmental Protection Agency's rules on permitting power for new sources of air pollution, including data centers. This move could give polluters more flexibility in deciding what information their facilities need public notification about.

## Key Points:

* The proposed rule would allow states to decide how much participation they require in the permitting process.
* Data centers could use a "minor source" permit system that's currently less stringent than the traditional "major" permit system.
* Current state-by-state enforcement of these permits can vary significantly between regions.
* Changes under consideration could impact local communities' ability to participate in permitting processes and publicize new facilities.

## Background:

Data centers are increasingly being built using minor source permits, which are often used for fossil fuel production. These smaller facilities do not meet the emissions standards as rigorously as larger ones, but the EPA currently requires more public participation before issuing approvals. Trump's administration might use this relaxed process to favor data center builders who may argue that their operations don't pose significant air pollution risks.

## Analysis:

The proposed rule could have far-reaching consequences for community engagement on new development projects and permit issuance. By deviating from the traditional permitting protocol, the EPA aims to give states more control over transparency requirements. However, these changes also expose communities to a lack of consistency and enforcement across different regions.

### **Industry Perspective:**

Companies like xAI and Meta have already implemented minor source permits, utilizing data centers behind-the-meter gas plants. Their approach allows for operational flexibility as they can now focus on maintenance rather than meeting regulatory requirements.

### **Community Response:**

Communities in areas with strong opposition to new polluting facilities often use the permitting process as an opportunity to slow down development.

## Possible Consequences:

If approved, this rule change could have several detrimental effects:
* Greater control for states over transparency and public engagement on environmental issues.
* Increased reliance on minor source permits for data centers and other facilities without rigorous emissions standards.
* Inconsistent application of regulations across different regions due to patchwork state enforcement laws.
* Weakened ability of local communities to voice opposition or concerns about new development projects.

### **Regulatory Response:**

In response, the EPA may advocate for stricter oversight of minor sources by states and require more consistency in their permitting processes nationwide. Regulatory agencies may also work towards harmonizing regulations across different regions for data centers and other polluting facilities.
