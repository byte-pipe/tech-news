# TBM 435: 20 Unfiltered Operating Takes – Summary

## North Star Framework
- Useful mainly as a teaching tool for metrics, causal models, and avoiding vanity metrics.  
- Companies that try to adopt it as a strict framework often hit resistance with edge cases.  
- Most teams adapt parts of it (multiple KPIs, leading/lagging) rather than implement it wholesale.  

## Vision‑to‑Tactics Pyramids (Trees/Pyramids)
- Pyramids that map vision → strategy → tactics tend to distract rather than align.  
- Labels become mistaken for real work; they serve more for “deckware” and a false sense of symmetry.  
- Real work happens at the front‑line team level; strategy is messy, non‑hierarchical, and shouldn’t be forced into neat boxes.  

## Stable Teams?
- “Stable, durable” product teams can create internal kingdoms that become hard to unwind.  
- Over‑building durable structures during periods of abundant funding leads to later layoffs when they’re no longer needed.  
- Stability is valuable only while the underlying need exists; leaders must recognize when to restructure.  

## Shipping Smaller/Faster
- Teaching a small‑working team to think big is easier than teaching a big‑thinking team to work small.  
- Continuous delivery, frequent feedback, and low‑risk iteration are ideal but require the right environment and skill set.  
- Risks include becoming a “feature factory” that doesn’t move key metrics; measuring impact is essential.  

## Planning Cadence (Cycles)
- Quarterly cycles are too long for small work and too short for big initiatives.  
- Two‑week sprints are the opposite; six‑week cycles are a reasonable middle ground but still imperfect.  
- Do not become beholden to any single cadence; focus on the present work and avoid letting the cycle dictate outcomes.  

## Takeaway
- Avoid rigid frameworks and hierarchies that obscure real work.  
- Prioritize front‑line execution, continuous shipping, and adaptable planning over formal structures.
