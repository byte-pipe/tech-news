# A Spoonful of Reality for ‘Bent’ Valuations

## Overview of the Airtable acquisition
- Bending Spoons bought Airtable for $1.285 bn enterprise value ($2.25 bn equity value).  
- The deal forces a re‑examination of software‑company valuations after the ZIRP era.  
- Airtable entered the deal with strong fundamentals: ~ $480 m ARR (‑20 % YoY), ~ 90 % gross margin, 170 % net dollar retention, and about $1 bn cash on hand.  
- Valuation multiple was only 2.7 × ARR, far below the ~7.6 × EV/ARR average for comparable “EM‑Cloud” firms.  
- The company’s AI‑agent platform, Hyperagent, was carved out before the sale, indicating the buyer was interested mainly in the legacy SaaS business.

## Lesson I – The valuation recalibration was inevitable
- Post‑ZIRP market conditions have exposed a gap between inflated private SaaS valuations (e.g., Airtable’s $11.7 bn peak in 2021) and public‑market multiples.  
- Some startups tried “dirty term sheets” to preserve previous round valuations, but this only postponed the correction.  
- By 2026, capital, talent, and customers shifted toward AI‑native solutions, causing multiples across the industry to collapse.  
- The author calls the situation the “Schrödinger’s Startup Paradox”: companies carry a valuation that no longer exists, yet their true market value remains hidden until a transaction forces a price discovery.  
- Airtable is viewed as the first high‑profile “canary” of a broader wave of mis‑priced private unicorns that may face similar exits or write‑downs.

## Lesson II – Burning the boats can create “Phoenix” incumbents
- Not all legacy SaaS firms will die; a few can reinvent themselves by leveraging existing distribution, data, trust, and workflow ownership to launch AI‑driven products.  
- Bessemer labels successful reinventions “Phoenixes.” The key driver is leadership that chooses offense over defense, deliberately disrupting the current business before competitors do.  
- Example: Eoghan McCabe (Fin/Intercom) led an AI rebirth that culminated in a $3.6 bn Salesforce acquisition. His message stresses that survival requires “destroying everything you love” and rebuilding on the brand, cash flow, and workflow assets.  
- Airtable’s decision to spin out Hyperagent reflects a similar bold move: incremental AI tweaks failed to reignite growth, so the leadership opted for a more drastic separation.  
- Whether Hyperagent becomes a Phoenix remains uncertain, but the case illustrates that incumbents may need a full “bend” away from legacy models to thrive in the AI era.

## Take‑away for software founders
- Expect continued pressure on private SaaS multiples; valuations will align more closely with public‑market benchmarks.  
- Defensive postures are unlikely to succeed; proactive, sometimes radical, AI‑focused transformations are essential.  
- Leadership willingness to sacrifice short‑term comfort for long‑term relevance is the differentiator between companies that become Phoenixes and those that are dismantled.
