# Big Tech’s Anti-Labor Playbook Has Come for Wikipedia

## TLDR
- In mid‑May the Wikimedia Foundation fired longtime MediaWiki lead developer Brooke Vibber and dissolved the Community Tech team, whose members were largely union organizers.  
- Wikipedia editors responded with a solidarity petition and threatened an editorial strike, marking the first coordinated action between volunteers and paid staff.  
- The Foundation sits on $296 million in reserves, has a profitable AI‑related revenue stream, and therefore the dispute is not driven by financial scarcity.

## Ten‑day timeline
- **Mid‑May:** Brooke Vibber, the first full‑time employee and CTO of WMF, is terminated.  
- **May 21:** Announcement that the five‑engineer Community Tech team and its manager are disbanded; the team previously built features from the volunteer‑submitted Community Wishlist.  
- **Hours later:** Editors launch a solidarity petition, pledge collective action up to an editorial strike, and administrators offer to step down.  
- **General Counsel’s stance:** Stephen LaPorte says the Foundation will negotiate with the union in good faith, but actions over the next weeks will test that claim.

## Financial picture
- FY 2025 revenue: $208.6 million; reserves: $296.6 million (≈17 months of operating costs).  
- Wikimedia Endowment net assets: $169.4 million, up $25 million YoY.  
- Wikimedia Enterprise (high‑volume API for AI labs) generated $8.3 million, a 148 % increase and is now profitable.  
- The Foundation’s cash position means it can afford to retain or replace six engineers; the conflict is not about money.

## Pattern of governance failures
- **2015‑16 Knowledge Engine:** Secret funding and lack of community disclosure led to project collapse and CEO resignation.  
- **2019 admin ban:** Secret evidence used to ban a long‑time admin, sparking the largest admin revolt; the decision was later returned to the community.  
- Subsequent years saw top‑down product rollouts, redesigns, and strategic plans imposed without meaningful consultation, while reserves grew and fundraising messages implied existential risk.  
- New CEO Bernadette Meehan (appointed Jan 2026) continued this playbook by removing key technical staff and confronting the union.

## Union’s modest demands
- Transparency and accountability from leadership to staff and community movements.  
- Genuine staff input on annual planning before final decisions.  
- Consistent hiring, firing, and promotion practices.  
- Protection for safe dissent.  
- Mental‑health support for workers interacting directly with the community.  
- The organizing principle: “nothing about us without us,” drawn from disability‑rights advocacy.

## Why it matters beyond Wikipedia
- Wikipedia is the world’s largest free reference work and a primary training corpus for AI models.  
- The encyclopedia’s free content relies on a small, paid workforce that supports volunteers; treating these workers as expendable undermines the entire ecosystem.  
- The Foundation’s claim of being “different from other tech companies” is tested by its handling of the union; the outcome will signal whether mission‑driven nonprofits can truly align rhetoric with practice.  
- A break with the union could embolden other nonprofits to prioritize cost‑cutting over stated values of community, transparency, and equity.
