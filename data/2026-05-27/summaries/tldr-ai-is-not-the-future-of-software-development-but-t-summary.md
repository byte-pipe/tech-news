# AI is not the future of software development, but the last dying gasp of the past

## Context
- Google I/O 2026 announced the end of the classic “search‑query‑→‑10 blue links” model, replacing it with an “interactive experience.”
- The change is framed as a response to user preferences, a revenue push, and a defensive move against Google’s shrinking market share.
- The author sees the real driver as a corporate‑level impulse to transform products into interactive experiences, a cliché that dominates ideation workshops.

## The Two Ideologies of Product Value
- **User‑problem focus:** Products should solve existing user problems with minimal effort; this perspective aligns with traditional UX thinking but has never been a strong lever for developers.
- **Task‑creation focus:** Value is generated by getting users to do more tasks within a product, turning the absence of a feature into a manufactured problem that can be sold back as a solution.
- Over the past 30 + years, developers leveraged their indispensable role to push the user‑problem view, but AI now erodes that leverage.

## AI and LLMs as the New Lever
- Large language models embody the task‑creation mindset: they produce abundant, often unnecessary code and features, turning any “problem” into a solvable output.
- The author argues that when problems are invented rather than discovered, the mere existence of compiled code is treated as a solution.
- LLMs also enable the fabrication of “synthetic users,” a discredited practice that pretends to validate invented problems.

## Synthetic Users and Manufactured Legitimacy
- Companies sell the ability to legitimize any imagined problem to the “interactive experience” crowd, rather than delivering genuine user insights.
- Historical criticism (e.g., Weizenbaum) warned that technology is often presented as the sole guardian against its own risks, a narrative that persists today.

## Consequences for the Industry
- Leading AI firms (OpenAI, Google) rose by creating the biggest new problems—environmental impact, energy costs, layoffs, unreliable code, and more.
- The industry now tells customers to buy solutions from the very entities that generated those problems.
- The old tech model, which controlled customers and locked them into ecosystems, is collapsing as megacorporations lose that grip.

## Closing Thoughts
- The shift toward interactive experiences and AI‑generated problem creation signals the end of a decades‑long conflict between user‑centric design and profit‑driven task creation.
- The author predicts that the “old world of tech” is dying, and the promised new paradigm—centered on AI as the future of software development—may never materialize.
