## Expenses

### Mortgage loan fees
- Settlement costs were about 3 % of the home value (early 2011 data).  
- Total loan‑related fees summed to **$12,777.92**, including origination, appraisal, title services, insurance, recording, escrow, and first‑year homeowner’s insurance.

### Mortgage payment
- Monthly payment consists of principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and possibly PMI.  
- Principal reduces debt and is not treated as an expense; interest is an expense that lowers net worth.  
- First payment (Jan 2011):  
  - Principal $482.64  
  - Interest $1,299.81  
  - Tax $389.67  
  - Insurance $44.75  
  - PMI $113.05  
  - **Total $2,329.92** (≈ 21 % toward principal, 79 % expense).  
- After PMI removal, current payment is **$2,440.48** (tax $515.50, insurance $111.17, escrow shortage $31.36).

### Insurance
- Required by lenders; cost rises annually.  
- Current homeowner’s insurance: **$111.17 / month**.  
- May require improvements (e.g., roof replacement) to maintain coverage.

### Taxes
- Property taxes increase each year; new construction triggers reassessment at higher values.  
- Current tax payment: **$515.50 / month**.  
- Look for programs that cap assessment or rate increases (e.g., Homestead Tax Credit).

## Maintenance & Repairs
- Recommended savings: at least **1 % of home value per year**; older or previously rented homes may need more.  
- Notable expenses incurred:  
  - Skylight leaks $6,566  
  - New roof $9,390  
  - New windows $10,530  
  - New siding $21,046  
  - Tree removal $2,800  
  - Pipe replacement $5,050  
  - Gutters $2,714  
  - Minor repairs (faucet, dishwasher pump, HVAC board) ≈ $350 total  
  - Yard upkeep ≈ $500 / year  
- DIY work (e.g., faucet, pump, yard) can significantly reduce costs.

## Improvements
- Separate from maintenance; include aesthetic or functional upgrades.  
- Examples: painting, baseboard replacement, bathroom remodel, new lighting, kitchen upgrades (cabinets, granite counters), deck and front step construction, skylight shades, yard landscaping.  
- DIY can cut costs dramatically; a self‑built deck cost **≈ $15,000**, about half of typical contractor estimates.

## Higher Utility Costs
- Homes are larger than apartments, leading to higher heating/cooling energy use (≈ 50 % of electricity).  
- Electricity rates rose from **17.3 ¢/kWh (Jan 2024)** to **24.7 ¢/kWh (May 2026)**, a **42 % increase** in two years, driven partly by market factors such as data‑center demand.
