# AI is speeding up the quantum threat to crypto, security experts warn

## Main insights
- AI is accelerating quantum‑computing research, shortening the timeline for machines that could break current encryption used by blockchains and the wider internet.  
- The combination of AI and quantum computing creates a continuous cybersecurity arms race: attackers gain faster tools to find vulnerabilities, while developers use AI for code auditing and formal verification of post‑quantum systems.  
- Security can no longer be treated as static infrastructure; continuous upgrades will become necessary to stay safe.

## How AI influences quantum development
- Machine‑learning models are being applied to optimise quantum error correction, one of the biggest bottlenecks in building practical quantum computers.  
- Researchers note that AI has already accelerated scientific discovery in materials science and may soon be used to design the next generation of quantum hardware.  
- This feedback loop (“AI feeds quantum, quantum feeds AI”) could compress the arrival of cryptographically relevant quantum computers from years to a few.

## Emerging threats
- Governments and sophisticated actors may be employing “harvest now, decrypt later” strategies, collecting encrypted traffic today in anticipation of future decryption capabilities.  
- A powerful quantum computer could derive private keys from public elliptic‑curve keys, exposing wallets and blockchain systems that rely on the same cryptography as the broader internet.  
- AI‑driven tools are increasingly capable of locating software bugs and even breaking cryptographic primitives directly.

## Defensive uses of AI
- Developers are deploying AI for automated code audits, vulnerability scanning, and formal verification of post‑quantum algorithms, potentially increasing the security of new cryptographic schemes.  
- Projects such as NEAR are integrating post‑quantum cryptography into account infrastructure, allowing seamless key‑scheme rotation without moving assets.

## Industry response
- Major blockchain ecosystems (Ethereum, Zcash, Solana, Ripple, NEAR) are actively researching or piloting post‑quantum migration paths.  
- Transition challenges include larger key sizes and slower performance of current post‑quantum standards.  
- The broader consensus among security researchers is that encryption can no longer be assumed reliable for long periods; adaptive, continuously evolving security models will become the norm.
