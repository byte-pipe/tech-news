# Summary of “‘We knew somebody would die’: Teenage patients ‘ignored’ before fatal NHS trust failures”

## Background
- The article examines serious shortcomings in mental health care provided by Tees, Esk and Wear Valleys NHS Foundation Trust (TEWV) in North Yorkshire, County Durham and Teesside.  
- Former patients and families describe the trust’s services as “chaotic and unsafe,” with a lack of compassion and inadequate treatment.

## Key incidents
- **Christie Harnett (17)** – patient at West Lane (now Acklam Road) Hospital; died by suicide while under trust care.  
- **Nadia Sharif (17)** and **Emily Moore (18)** – also died by suicide within eight months of Christie’s death.  
- **Nathan Evison (19)** – died by suicide in 2019 after community mental‑health support failed; lived in an isolated rural cottage.  
- **Laurent McNamara** – died in 2023 while under trust care.  
- All cases share reports of excessive restraint, staff ignoring or shouting at self‑harm incidents, and failure to provide meaningful therapy.

## Patient and family testimonies
- Laura Kenny, a former patient, recounts being repeatedly warned that a death was imminent and writing letters to staff, but receiving no response.  
- Staff reactions described as shouting, ignoring, or quickly restraining and sedating patients to “shut them up.”  
- Families (e.g., Michael Harnett, David Moore) claim their pleas for help were ignored and that the trust’s failures extend beyond the three teenage deaths.

## Investigations and legal actions
- An independent NHS England inquiry published in 2023 examined the deaths of Christie, Nadia, and Emily, confirming claims of inappropriate restraint and managerial tolerance of failures.  
- TEWV apologized and said improvements were made, but families doubt lasting change.  
- In 2024 the trust was prosecuted by the Care Quality Commission, pleading guilty to two charges of failing to provide safe care and fined £215,000 for safety failings linked to Christie’s death and another woman’s death.  
- A statutory public inquiry was announced in December 2023; families are frustrated by delays and lack of clarity on leadership, timing, and location.  
- The Department of Health and Social Care says it is working “at pace” to appoint a chair and emphasizes that patient and family voices will be central.

## Current concerns
- Patients and families continue to fear ongoing unsafe practices and inadequate community care.  
- The public inquiry is expected to have legal powers to summon witnesses and documents, aiming to prevent repeat failures and deliver justice for the victims.  
- TEWV has declined comment on individual cases but pledged cooperation with the inquiry, stating it will act with “honesty, openness, humility, grace and kindness.”
