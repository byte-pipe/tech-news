# A few interesting modern pixel fonts – Unsung

## Overview
- The article highlights recent vector‑based fonts that emulate classic pixel aesthetics while addressing historical shortcomings.
- Emphasis is placed on functional design, typographic rigor, and the often‑overlooked technical details such as kerning and metrics.

## Fonts discussed
- **Analog Mono (Andrew Gleeson)**
  - Inspired by the ubiquitous 1990s VCR OSD Mono font.
  - Fixes the low baseline issue that forced descenders upward, improving readability.

- **Coral Pixels (Kumiko Yoshida)**
  - Available on Google Fonts as a color font.
  - Incorporates 1990s/2000s “fringing” originally caused by subpixel rendering, now used deliberately for nostalgic or visual effect, akin to chromatic aberration.

- **Two Slice (Joseph Fatula)**
  - Extremely compact, only 2 pixels tall.
  - Claims to remain “somewhat readable” despite its minimal height.

- **Geist Pixel (Vercel)**
  - Presented as a system‑level extension rather than a novelty.
  - Designed for real‑world usage: scales across viewports, integrates with existing typographic metrics, and provides functional utility beyond decoration.
  * The team highlights careful attention to kerning, metadata, extra glyphs, and vertical metrics.

## Key takeaways
- Modern “pixel” fonts are vector fonts that mimic pixel art while solving legacy issues such as baseline alignment and scaling problems.
- Functional considerations (metrics, kerning, extensibility) are as crucial as the visual style.
- Geist Pixel exemplifies a shift toward treating pixel‑style fonts as robust components within broader typographic systems.
