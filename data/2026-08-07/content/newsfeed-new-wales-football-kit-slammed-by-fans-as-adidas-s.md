Wales football fans have been left divided over the country's new home kit, with some comparing the pattern on the red shirt to that seen on bus seats.

The new shirt was revealed on Thursday by the Football Association of Wales (FAW), which said it was "inspired by the rich tradition of Welsh double cloth weaving".

It is the first Wales shirt produced by sportswear brand Sudu, after the FAW parted ways with Adidas after 13 years.

FAW said the reaction to football kits was "subjective", adding it remained "incredibly proud of the creative process behind the new Cymru home kit".

The association said the kit rejected "reused designs", and that Sudu had worked with the association and fans "to understand the DNA of the nation".

A "pro" version of the adult shirt costs £90, with a matching pair of shorts and socks costing a total of £136.

A replica adult shirt costs £70, while a full kit for a child including shorts and socks costs £89.

Home nations rivals England have their most expensive adult home shirt on sale for £134.99, while Scotland's is £75.

But people who were not pleased with the kit slammed it online, with one describing it as the "worst Welsh shirt in history".

Speaking to a BBC Wales reporter at the Eisteddfod on Thursday, one fan said the new kit reminded her of "a bus seat back in the day".

But not everyone thought the kit was bad, with some arguing the reaction would be different had Adidas released the same design.

Before the shirt was revealed, BBC Wales Sport spoke to Noel Mooney, CEO of the FAW, on 9 June about the kit.

He said: "We've had a brilliant, more than decade-long relationship with Adidas.

"At the same time we have to keep thinking about what is the best for the FAW in terms of design, in terms of the amount of kit that we get."

He added: "We've got to fund the grassroots game here and we'll always look at opportunities for getting more resources for the grassroots game as well.

"We need to grow Welsh football and we will always look for the option that's best for Welsh football."

The FAW said the shirt was designed "with incredible passion and care" and the "bespoke pattern draws on elements of traditional Welsh tapestry".

A spokesperson said: "We understand that change is difficult but we are incredibly excited to start this new journey. It has been heartening to see the views of supporters who connect with the meaning behind the shirt and the sense of Welsh pride that it evokes.

"The shirt has also been on display at the National Eisteddfod this morning, with feedback from supporters in person being overwhelmingly positive."
