# FDA approves Moderna flu shot made with mRNA

## Approval details
- The FDA approved Moderna’s mRNA‑based seasonal influenza vaccine, called **mFLUSIVA**, for adults aged 50 and older.  
- Moderna announced the shot will be available “in a few weeks,” in time for the 2026‑2027 respiratory virus season in the United States.  
- It is the first flu vaccine to use the same mRNA technology that powered COVID‑19 vaccines.

## Clinical findings
- Trials involving more than 40,000 participants showed the vaccine is safe.  
- Early data suggest mFLUSIVA may provide stronger protection than traditional flu vaccines.

## Regulatory process
- The agency initially declined to review the product, citing political pressure and skepticism toward mRNA technology.  
- After industry and public‑health advocacy, the FDA convened an independent advisory panel in June; the panel voted unanimously to recommend approval.  
- The decision came despite criticism from figures such as Robert F. Kennedy Jr., who has long opposed mRNA vaccines.

## Public‑health implications
- mRNA platforms can be updated and manufactured faster than conventional vaccines, allowing quicker responses to emerging flu strains or potential pandemics.  
- Experts highlight the vaccine’s potential to improve preparedness for severe flu seasons and other biological threats.

## Expert opinions
- Michael Osterholm (University of Minnesota) called the approval “a critical step forward in advancing influenza vaccine technology.”  
- Dr. Jeanne Marrazzo (Infectious Disease Society of America) said the FDA’s openness signals progress for a controversial platform.  
- Dr. Brian Hooker (Children’s Health Defense) expressed concern that the technology is “too new” and lacks sufficient long‑term study.
