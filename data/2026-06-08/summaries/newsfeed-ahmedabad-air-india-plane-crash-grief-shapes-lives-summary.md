# Ahmedabad Air India plane crash: Grief shapes lives of people on the ground

# ‘We don’t look at the sky anymore’: The Air India crash victims who were not on the plane  

## Background  
- In June 2022 an Air India flight crashed into the BJ Medical College hostel complex in Ahmedabad, killing 260 people (241 on the plane, 19 on the ground).  
- The damaged hostel remains a visible scar; demolition and rebuilding are approved but not yet carried out.  

## Impact on the family of Prahlod Thakur  
- Thakur’s wife, Sarlaben, and two‑year‑old granddaughter, Aadhya, were among the 19 ground fatalities.  
- Their photographs dominate the walls of Thakur’s modest home, reminding him daily of the loss.  
- Thakur recalls the chaotic moments after the crash: fire, exploding gas cylinders, and his frantic calls for “Sarla”.  
- The family’s tiffin‑service business for hospital staff halted; they spent days searching hospitals and relief camps before finding the bodies in a mortuary.  

## Survivors among the students  
- Arman Khan Pathan was pinned by a collapsed table; he broke a window with his bare fist to escape the smoke.  
- Friend Aditya Dayal helped carry Arman out on a mattress and later recalls the lingering smell of burnt bodies.  
- Many classmates died; survivors speak of the sudden loss of futures and the lasting nausea triggered by memories of the scene.  

## Ongoing injuries and daily reminders  
- Brijesh, a student with severe burn injuries, continues physiotherapy and avoids looking at the ruined building.  
- Vijay, a resident living 200 m from the crash site, joined rescue efforts and still sees “fire” in his mind’s eye whenever he looks around.  

## Institutional response  
- Dean Meenakshi Parikh had to keep the medical college operating while managing collective grief, DNA identification delays, and the emotional toll on staff and families.  
- The campus gradually returned to routine, but the hostel’s wreckage remains an “open wound” for students and faculty.  

## Community and psychological aftermath  
- The crash has altered everyday perceptions: planes overhead now trigger pain, and many locals avoid looking at the sky.  
- The site has become a permanent part of the city’s landscape, unlike typical disaster sites that fade over time.  
- Residents and students have developed coping habits such as looking away or suppressing memories, yet the trauma resurfaces unexpectedly.
