# AI is not a line item – Summary

## Background and backlash
- Companies began tracking AI token usage with internal leaderboards, leading to budget overruns and public criticism.
- Recent headlines cite firms like Uber exceeding AI token budgets and executives freezing AI spend.
- Token counts are compared to measuring engineers by lines of code, which the authors argue is an ineffective metric.

## Why a single AI spend number is misleading
- Different AI agents and experiments deliver varying business impact; aggregating all spend into one figure obscures value.
- Treating AI spend like a single salary line item ignores the nuanced contributions of each tool or project.
- The authors propose evaluating AI tools based on the specific team or functionality they support.

## Setting limits wisely
- Budgets are necessary, but extreme scrutiny on every expense creates “organizational whiplash” and stifles innovation.
- Distinguish between proven value‑add tools (e.g., coding agents) and experimental tools; allocate separate budgets for each.
- Over‑restricting experimentation can cause companies to fall behind competitors.

## Encouraging experimentation
- Rapid AI advances mean many application areas remain undiscovered; limiting spend on new tools hampers learning.
- Teams report being blocked from trying solutions that could solve problems far cheaper than current salary or existing AI costs.
- Reframe software budgets to complement headcount budgets, recognizing that agents can offload tedious work and increase capacity.

## Build vs. buy considerations
- Building in‑house often leads to budget overruns compared with purchasing off‑the‑shelf solutions.
- Coding agents enable quick demos at low token cost, but productionizing those demos incurs significant token and engineering effort.
- Maintenance, bug fixing, and lack of code ownership can cause hidden token consumption and complicate ROI calculations.

## Prioritization of experiments
- Engineers may jump between many low‑value experiments, inflating token usage without delivering business outcomes.
- Each poorly scoped experiment can balloon from a few dollars to a thousand-dollar sinkhole.
- Clear objectives linking token use to business goals are essential for disciplined experimentation.

## Approaches to AI budgeting
- Organizations have tried various models: no limits, all spend routed through engineering, or a dedicated AI budget per organization.
- The authors argue for a nuanced approach that separates experimental spend from production‑grade AI usage, aligning budgets with measurable impact rather than a single aggregated number.
