# A World Cup for them not us: Fans' anger at US travel bans and visa restrictions

## Overview
- The 2026 FIFA World Cup matches in Boston and Philadelphia have triggered widespread frustration among fans from many qualifying nations who face US visa hurdles.
- While the tournament promises a global spectacle, restrictive US immigration policies are preventing supporters from attending in person.

## Individual cases
- **Iraq (Abdulla Adnan)**
  - Bought tickets for Iraq’s games but cannot obtain a visa because routine US consular services in Iraq were suspended after the US‑Israel‑Iran conflict.
  - Traveled to Jordan for an interview; was denied a visa because he is not a Jordanian citizen.
  - Spent about $1,800 on tickets and travel and has abandoned attempts to secure a visa.

- **Ivory Coast (Julien Kouadio Adonis)**
  - Cites Trump‑era travel bans on Haiti, Iran, Senegal and Ivory Coast as “a form of segregation.”
  - His fan association decided not to pursue visas, calling the restrictions “exorbitant” and questioning the US’s right to host.

- **Senegal (Aliou Ngom)**
  - Previously attended two World Cups; now sees little point in applying for a visa after US deposits were lifted only for ticket‑holders from certain countries.
  - Highlights cultural exchange as a key tournament value.

- **Jordan (Abu Kass)**
  - Reports a 57 % visa refusal rate for Jordanians; none of the fans he knows have obtained visas.
  - Describes the World Cup as “not ours” and suggests the US is favoring non‑Arab supporters.

## US travel bans and visa policies
- President Trump’s travel‑ban list still affects four World Cup participants (Haiti, Iran, Senegal, Ivory Coast), blocking the recommended B‑1/B‑2 visitor visas.
- Wealthier nations benefit from the ESTA electronic travel‑authorization waiver (≈ $40); no African country is on this list.
- Recommended fan visa costs $185 and requires an in‑person interview proving intent to depart and ability to pay.

## Visa rejection statistics
- Analysis of State Department data (Oct 2024 – Sep 2025) shows a > 40 % rejection rate for applicants from 11 of the 48 qualified countries:
  - Ecuador, Egypt, Haiti, Algeria, Uzbekistan, Cape Verde, Jordan, Iran, DR Congo, Ghana, Senegal.
- This is higher than the overall 34 % rejection rate for B‑1/B‑2 visas worldwide.

## FIFA Pass and other mitigation efforts
- FIFA Pass allows ticket holders to request priority visa interview slots, but does not improve approval odds.
- Fans can resell FIFA‑purchased tickets on the official site if visas are denied.
- Immigration lawyer Celine Atallah notes that the US government remains the ultimate gatekeeper; even approved visas can be denied entry at the border.

## Reactions and broader implications
- Fan associations argue that restrictive policies undermine the inclusive spirit of the World Cup.
- Critics claim the US is “segregating” supporters from less affluent or non‑Western nations.
- A State Department spokesperson asserts the US is “prepared to welcome visitors from around the globe” for the “largest and greatest FIFA World Cup in history.”

## Conclusion
- Visa obstacles, travel bans, and high rejection rates are preventing a significant portion of the global fan base from attending the 2026 World Cup in the United States.
- While measures like FIFA Pass aim to ease the process, the fundamental barrier remains US immigration policy, raising questions about the tournament’s accessibility and the fairness of its hosting arrangements.
