# Iran school bombing: grounds to believe US was behind atrocity, UN finds

## Key findings of the UN fact‑finding mission
- The mission concluded there are reasonable grounds to believe the United States carried out the February 28 strikes on a school in Minab and a sports complex in Lamerd, actions that qualify as war crimes.
- Both sites were “clearly identifiable” civilian objects; the attacks were deemed indiscriminate and caused large civilian casualties.
- The report also determined that Iranian authorities committed crimes against humanity during the crackdown on anti‑government protests that began in late December.

## Details of the February 28 strikes
- **Minab school attack**
  - Target: Shajareh Tayyebeh primary school, Hormozgan province.
  - Weapon: U.S. Tomahawk missile that collapsed the school roof.
  - Casualties: 156 civilians killed, including 120 children.
  - Intelligence failure: U.S. relied on outdated data suggesting a senior Iranian commander was present and did not verify the building’s civilian status before striking.
- **Lamerd sports complex attack**
  - Target: Sports facility and nearby residential area in Lamerd, Fars province.
  - Weapon: Munition dispersing tungsten pellets.
  - Casualties: 22 civilians killed or injured; damage to nearby homes and a school.
  - U.S. Central Command later stated no strikes were launched in Lamerd on that day, contradicting the UN findings.

## UN mission conclusions on U.S. conduct
- The United States directed attacks knowing there was a substantial risk of hitting civilian objects and acted recklessly.
- The school strike constitutes an indiscriminate attack under international law, meeting the definition of a war crime.
- The mission’s report will be submitted to the UN Human Rights Council in Geneva.

## Findings on Iranian government actions
- Iranian forces carried out a systematic, widespread crackdown on protests, involving unlawful killings, torture, arbitrary detention, enforced disappearances, and severe restrictions on freedom of expression.
- The death toll from the protests is likely higher than official figures (officially 3,038 dead, 25,000 injured).

## Reactions and context
- U.S. officials maintain that operations comply with the law of armed conflict; former President Donald Trump previously claimed “nobody” deliberately attacked the school.
- Iran blames “terrorists and rioters” backed by exiled opponents, the U.S., and Israel for the violence.
- The investigation highlights a marked escalation in both U.S. military actions in Iran and Tehran’s response to dissent.
