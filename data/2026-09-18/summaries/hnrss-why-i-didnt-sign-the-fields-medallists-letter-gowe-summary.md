# Why I didn’t sign the Fields medallists’ letter

## Background story
- As a child I was fascinated by Fermat’s Last Theorem, tried to prove it, and explored successive differences of cubes.
- The experience taught me that thinking about a problem can be valuable even without solving it, echoing the message of the letter signed by 25 Fields medallists.

## Reason for not signing
- I agree with much of the letter but disagree with its implied hierarchy that “conceptual understanding” should be the primary goal over “problem‑solving.”
- I view mathematics as a spectrum:
  - Some are motivated mainly by solving problems, using understanding as a tool.
  - Others are motivated mainly by gaining understanding, using problems as a tool.
- Both attitudes have historically been productive; I do not want to label one as “wrong.”

## My position on AI and mathematics
- I have contacts with the OpenAI mathematics group and early access to their models, but I have never been paid by OpenAI.
- My work on automatic theorem proving aims to understand human proof methods; the recent success of LLMs challenges that motivation.
- I remain interested in the intellectual exercise of understanding proofs, even if LLMs act as black boxes.

## Interpretation of the letter’s argument
- The letter warns that focusing solely on “true/false” statements generated by AI could undermine fertile ground for new ideas.
- I worry that the letter suggests the “right” attitude is to prioritize conceptual insight above problem‑solving, which I think is too narrow.

## Related viewpoints
- I share a view expressed by Jacob Tsimerman (and echoed by Daniel Litt and Noah Smith) that both problem‑solving and conceptual work are valuable.
- The collective understanding of mathematics may benefit from many individuals motivated by problem‑solving, as long as it contributes to the shared body of knowledge.

## Open question
- Should the community prioritize maximizing collective conceptual understanding, or should it also value providing answers to as many problems as possible? This tension underlies my decision not to sign the letter.
