# Did Trump lower Medicaid drug prices, as promised?

## Main points
- In September 2025 the Trump administration announced voluntary “most‑favored‑nation” agreements with Pfizer and later 26 drug makers, promising Medicaid discounts at prices comparable to other wealthy nations.  
- The program, called the **Generous** pilot, is voluntary for both pharmaceutical companies and states; each state must opt‑in and meet specific conditions.  
- Details of the agreements have not been released, and companies have not confirmed that *all* of their products will be discounted for Medicaid.  
- Several firms (e.g., Gilead, Sanofi, Eli Lilly, Genentech) indicated only selected medicines would be offered at reduced prices, raising concerns about “cherry‑picking” low‑discount drugs.  
- The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services declined to disclose how many drugs per company will receive discounts.  

## Implementation challenges
- The pilot’s rollout has been delayed repeatedly, with original deadlines in spring/summer pushed back.  
- States may be reluctant to join because Medicaid costs are a large portion of state budgets, and states must balance their budgets annually, unlike the federal government.  
- Financial risk exists for states if they must cover higher upfront drug costs before long‑term savings materialize.  

## Expert commentary
- Dr. Thomas Hwang (Brigham and Women’s Hospital) warned that limited drug selections could allow companies to claim discounts that have little impact on overall Medicaid spending.  
- Ameet Sarpatwari (Harvard) noted the repeated deadline extensions as a negative signal for the program’s viability.  
- Jack Rollins (National Association of Medicaid Directors) emphasized that Medicaid cost control will still rely on tools such as prior authorizations and step‑therapy requirements.  

## Current status
- One year after the promise, the Medicaid discount program remains a pilot with uncertain scope, limited drug coverage, and uneven state participation, making it unclear whether the promised reduction in Medicaid drug spending has been achieved.
