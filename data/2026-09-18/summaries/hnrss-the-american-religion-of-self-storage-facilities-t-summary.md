# The American Religion of Self‑Storage Facilities | The New Yorker

## Overview
- The author’s 2019 road trip from Connecticut to Kansas City sparked the insight that **church and self‑storage are the two great pillars of American culture**.  
- Self‑storage is defined as a business renting lockable space to people overwhelmed by possessions; it has become a major institutional asset class.

## Scale and Geography
- The United States holds **≈90 % of global self‑storage capacity**, far outpacing any other country.  
- Texas has the most facilities; Hawaii the fewest.  
- City rankings (by total units): Dallas‑Fort Worth, Houston, then New York; per‑capita coverage is strong in many smaller municipalities.  
- Notable locales: Reno (Burning Man gear storage), Wichita (once “Self‑Storage Capital”).  
- Facilities range from tiny closets to spaces large enough for whole houses; most are single‑story metal‑door structures, some resembling office buildings.  
- There are **more self‑storage sites in the U.S. than the combined total of Starbucks, McDonald’s, Walmart, Home Depot, Domino’s, Dunkin’, and Costco**.  
- Annual industry revenue exceeds **$40 billion**.

## Drivers of Demand (“Four D’s” + a Fifth)
1. **Death** – heirs need space for inherited items.  
2. **Displacement** – moving after natural disasters or relocations.  
3. **Divorce** – splitting households creates surplus belongings.  
4. **Downsizing** – moving to smaller homes or apartments.  
5. **Delusion** – belief that children will want or need stored “antique” items.

### Illustrative Stories
- **Paul Roossin** kept a large Queens unit for years after moving from a suburb to Manhattan, eventually donating stored musical instruments and later downsizing further.  
- The author and his wife rented a **Pods** portable unit to ship unwanted furniture to their daughter; she accepted only two pieces, highlighting the “delusion” factor.  
- **Ray Underwood** built a 145‑unit self‑storage complex in the 1990s, now charging $80–$300 per month; a quarter of tenants are contractors storing tools and paperwork.

## Architectural Context
- Modern fulfillment centers (Amazon, etc.) are the newest iconic American structures—massive, low, windowless warehouses in industrial parks.  
- Self‑storage facilities share similar design traits but are smaller and less truck‑dense; they could logically sit within the same industrial zones, allowing direct delivery of purchased goods to storage units.

## Economic Insight
- Early investors (pre‑REIT era) missed the cash‑cow potential; today, platforms like **Neighbor** treat storage like an Airbnb for space, reflecting its mature, institutional status.  
- Zoning approvals can be challenging (as experienced by Underwood), but once granted, facilities generate steady rental income from a diverse customer base.
