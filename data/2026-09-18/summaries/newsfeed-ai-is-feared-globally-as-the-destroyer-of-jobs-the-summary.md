# AI is feared globally as the destroyer of jobs

## Survey overview
- Pew Research surveyed 42,151 people in 37 countries from 8 Feb to 13 May 2026, before recent apocalyptic warnings.
- The study examined attitudes toward AI’s impact on employment, daily life, and income inequality.

## Perceived job threat
- In 34 of the 37 countries, respondents expect AI to cause net job losses over the next 20 years.
- Highest concern levels were recorded in Australia (76 %), South Korea (76 %), and the United States (71 %).
- The fear aligns with Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei’s claim that AI could eliminate half of entry‑level white‑collar jobs.

## Views on inequality
- A majority believe AI will widen the gap between rich and poor.
- This belief is strongest among residents of wealthier, left‑leaning nations.

## Age‑related attitudes
- Older adults are more worried about AI’s effect on everyday life than younger people.
- Younger adults (18‑34) show mixed feelings but growing anxiety, especially in Sweden, Poland, Japan, Australia, Brazil, and the United States, where concern has risen sharply over the past year.

## Overall sentiment
- Global median: 41 % feel both concerned and excited about AI.
- 37 % are primarily concerned, while only 13 % are primarily excited.
