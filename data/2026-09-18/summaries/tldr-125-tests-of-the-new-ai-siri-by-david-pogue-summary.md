# 125 Tests of the New AI Siri - by David Pogue

# 125 Tests of the New AI Siri – Summary

## Introduction
- The article, written by David Pogue on September 17 2026, evaluates the AI‑enhanced Siri released with iOS 27 (also on iPadOS, watchOS, and macOS).  
- Pogue describes the new Siri as a “phone‑changer,” claiming it can replace many Google searches, ChatGPT/Claude interactions, and routine copy‑paste tasks.  
- He emphasizes that the AI is “ethical”: it does not deceive, does not displace workers, and runs entirely on renewable energy.

## Core Capabilities
- **Deep integration with Apple apps**: Siri can read and act on data from Mail, Messages, Notes, Files, Calendar, Contacts, Reminders, Photos, Music, Health (limited), etc., all stored locally on the device.  
- **Contextual personal queries**: Users can ask Siri about past conversations, appointments, travel details, recipes, and more, and Siri returns concise answers with links to the original source.  
- **Actionable commands**: Siri can create reminders, add calendar events, send emails, attach files, and even perform calculations on selected documents.  
- **Privacy‑first design**: All personal data remains on‑device; Apple does not have access to the content Siri processes.

## Testing Methodology
- Pogue used the beta version throughout the summer and performed a final round of 125 tests after the public release.  
- Tests were grouped into five categories; the article details the first category (Personal info).  
- Each test records the spoken or typed query, Siri’s response, and whether the outcome was correct (✅) or a failure (❌).  
- Some tests were suggested by Apple, others sourced from Reddit, and some were original ideas.

## Overall Performance
- **Success rate**: The majority of the 125 tests succeeded; only a handful failed, often due to incomplete integration with the Health app.  
- **Common failure**: Siri could not retrieve step counts or add medication to the Health app’s medication list, redirecting users to open the Health app manually.  
- **User experience**: Pogue notes that the hardest habit to break is opening apps manually; once users trust Siri, they save significant time.

- **Item 25** – Performed on‑the‑fly calculations (total and average) on a highlighted folder of invoice PDFs.  
- **Item 28** – Generated a comprehensive “what‑are‑people‑waiting‑on‑me” list from Mail, Messages, and Reminders, surfacing forgotten tasks.  
- **Item 30** – Provided an apartment number for a party without being told which party, demonstrating contextual inference.  
- **Item 31** – Calculated departure time for a wedding, factoring in distance, traffic norms, and the specific event’s start time.  
- **Item 27** – Suggested a personalized gift by recalling past celebrations and a note in a “Christmas 2026” list.  
- **Item 20** – Executed a compound command (add happy‑hour details to calendar and email a colleague) flawlessly.

## Enabling the AI Siri
- Users must go to **Settings → Siri** and toggle on “Siri AI.”  
- Some may encounter a waiting‑list message because Apple is rolling the feature out gradually to its 2.5 billion devices.

## Conclusion
- The new AI Siri delivers a substantial productivity boost by turning personal data into conversational answers and actionable shortcuts.  
- While a few niche functions (e.g., Health‑app integration) remain unfinished, the overall reliability across a wide range of personal‑information queries is impressive.  
- Pogue recommends giving Siri a few tries, learning to trust its responses, and then “saving incredible amounts of time and fumbling.”
