# How Big Tech and Silicon Valley are Transforming the Military‑Industrial Complex

## Overview
- Anthropologist Roberto González examines the rapid shift of the U.S. military‑industrial complex from the Capital Beltway to Silicon Valley.  
- The Pentagon, while still spending heavily on conventional weapons, is increasingly pursuing AI‑enabled systems.  
- Big‑tech firms, venture‑capital (VC) funds, private‑equity investors, and fast‑moving defense startups are receiving large, often classified, Defense contracts.  

## Funding Figures
- Tech Inquiry reports that three major tech corporations received about **$28 billion** in contracts from 2018‑2022:  
  - Microsoft – $13.5 billion  
  - Amazon – $10.2 billion  
  - Alphabet (Google) – $4.3 billion  
- The five largest contracts to major tech firms between 2019‑2022 total at least **$53 billion**.  
- From 2021‑2023, VC firms invested nearly **$100 billion** in defense‑tech startups, a rise of 40 % compared with the previous seven years combined.  

## Implications
- The lack of transparency in classified contracts makes it difficult to gauge the full extent of spending diverted to Big Tech.  
- González argues that the influx of private‑sector money is driving the creation of costly, high‑tech defense products that may be ineffective, unpredictable, and unsafe, ultimately burdening American taxpayers.
