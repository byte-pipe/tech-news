### 📌 AI for Education Fellowship

Who it's for:Builders, engineers, educators, researchers, and anyone interested in building something at the intersection ofAI and education. No coding experience or other prerequisites are required.

What stands out:TheAI for Education FellowshipfromWorldwide Studiosis a 100-day virtual fellowship where you pick projects to work on, build and test them, and share what you're working on through demos every two weeks.

I wanted to include this because I like that it is focused onactually building something, rather than just learning about AI or education. You can explore an idea, build a first version, test it, and keep improving it throughout the fellowship.

The fellowship is fully virtual and you can join from anywhere in the world. Fellows can also work in teams if they want, and the community includes people from different backgrounds, including engineers, researchers, educators, and school owners.

Another thing worth knowing is thetime commitment. The fellowship expects around8–10 hours a week, with weekly squad syncs and bi-weekly demos. So this is something to think about if you're considering applying alongside work or school.

There are also examples of past fellows building and testing real projects during the fellowship, including AI tutoring and education-focused products.

Format:Virtual |Duration:100 days

Dates:September 15 – December 15, 2026

Time Commitment:8–10 hours per week

Cost:Free for accepted fellows

Eligibility:Anyone interested in building solutions in AI and education | No prerequisites

Application:Applications are reviewed as they come in

### 📌 AWS Student Rewards

Who it's for:University students who want to learn cloud and AI, build projects, and earn AWS learning and certification benefits along the way.

What stands out:AWS Student Rewardson AWS Builder Center gives verified higher education students12 months of AWS Skill Builder Premium, along with opportunities to earn AWS Credits and an AWS Foundational Certification exam voucher.

I wanted to include this because12 months of premium learning access is a pretty significant amount of time. The Skill Builder access includes 900+ courses, hands-on labs, certification preparation, and other learning resources, so students can actually use it throughout the year rather than having access for only a short period.

There is also a reward system built around participating in the AWS Builder Center community. After verifying your student status and completing your profile, you get the Skill Builder reward. You can then earn badges through things like publishing articles, commenting, and maintaining engagement.

At7 badges, you unlock $10 in AWS Credits, at14 badgesyou get another $20, and at21 badgesyou can unlock anAWS Foundational Certification exam voucher worth $100.

Another thing I liked is that AWS also hasStudent Builder Groupsat universities in 60+ countries, so there is a community side to this as well if there is a group at your university.

One thing to check before signing up is the eligibility. You need to be18 or older and enrolled at an accredited higher education institution, and your student status needs to be verified.

Benefits:12 months of Skill Builder Premium + up to $30 in AWS Credits + AWS Foundational Certification exam voucher

Eligibility:Students 18+ enrolled at accredited higher education institutions worldwide

Cost:Free

### 📌 Multi-App AI Agent Hackathon

Who it's for:Developers, AI builders, and anyone who wants to build an AI agent that can actually take actions across different applications. You can participatesolo or in a team of up to four.

What it's about:TheMulti-App AI Agent Hackathon, hosted byLemma and Comma Capital, is a one-day virtual hackathon where you build a multi-step AI agent connected to at leastthree external apps.

I wanted to include this one because the challenge is pretty clear: you're not just building a chatbot or showing what an AI agent could do. The idea is to build something that can actuallytake actions across multiple apps, and then show that it works.

The hackathon takes place onSeptember 13, 2026, from9 AM to 5 PM Pacific Time. You'll have most of the day to build, with judging and awards happening toward the end.

For the submission, you'll need aworking project or repository, a two-minute demo, and a short system and reliability brief. The project is judged on technical execution, reliability and evaluation, usefulness, originality, and how clearly you can explain what you built.

There is also$15,000 in cash prizes, with $10,000 for first place, $4,000 for second, and $1,000 for third.

And probably the part many developers will care about:all three winning teams get a guaranteed interview with either Arga Labs or Lemma AI. Of course, that is an interview opportunity and not a job offer, but I think it makes the hackathon a little more interesting if you're also looking for opportunities to get your work in front of companies.

One thing to keep in mind is that this is asingle-day hackathon, so you'll need to be comfortable building and making decisions fairly quickly. The challenge also has a specific requirement of connecting your agent to at least three external apps, so it's worth thinking about what you could build before the event starts.

Prize Pool:$15,000

Date:September 13, 2026 |Time:9:00 AM – 5:00 PM Pacific Time

Team Size:Solo or 2–4 people

What you need to build:A useful, multi-step AI agent connected to at least 3 external apps

Submission:Working project/repository + 2-minute demo + system and reliability brief

Judging:Technical execution, reliability & evaluation, usefulness, originality, and demo clarity

### Tech Interview Handbook

If you're preparing for a software engineering interview and not sure what you should actually focus on, theTech Interview Handbookis a free resource that puts a lot of the preparation material in one place.

It covers the whole interview process, starting fromresume preparation and applying for jobs, then moving into coding and algorithms, system design, behavioral interviews, and finally offer negotiation.

I think the useful part here is that you don't have to use it only for coding practice. There are sections on things likehow to prepare for coding interviews, common algorithm patterns, system design, behavioral questions, interview best practices, and questions you can ask the interviewer.

It also includes curated coding questions through theBlind 75andGrind 75, along with explanations and tips for different algorithm topics. So if you don't want to randomly pick hundreds of problems to practice, you can use those lists as a starting point.

There are also sections oncompany interview formats, engineering levels, compensation, and choosing between offers, which can be useful once you get further into the process.

I wouldn't say you need to go through every section. Depending on where you are in your interview preparation, you can just pick the parts that are relevant to you. For example, if your coding preparation is already good, you could spend more time on system design or behavioral interviews.

So if you're preparing for interviews and looking for one place to get an idea ofwhat to prepare and in what order, this is a resource I think is worth going through.

Format:Free online resource

Covers:Resume preparation, coding & algorithms, system design, behavioral interviews, interview best practices, offer negotiation, and more.

Also includes:Blind 75, Grind 75, curated practice questions, interview cheatsheets, and company interview information.
