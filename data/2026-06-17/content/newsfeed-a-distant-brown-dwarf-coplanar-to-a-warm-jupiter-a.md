## Abstract

In transiting planetary systems, in which planetary sizes are accurately determined from transit observations, the presence of transit-timing variations1(TTVs), especially when combined with radial velocity (RV) data, provides powerful constraints on masses and orbital eccentricities. Together, these measurements offer crucial insights into system architecture, formation mechanisms and dynamical evolution. We present long-term RV and transit/TTV monitoring of the relatively young star (age approximately 1 Gyr) TOI-201, revealing an exceptional multi-planet system composed of a hot super-Earth (SE) size planet transiting every 5.8 days, a warm Jupiter (WJ) on a 53-day orbit and an eccentric (e= 0.62) low-mass brown dwarf (BD) on an approximately 8-year orbit, with an estimated massMBDof about 16 Jupiter masses. The BD is the longest-period transiting substellar object ever characterized by means of RVs and the only one known to be coplanar with inner planets. The architecture of this system suggests that the SE was formed isolated and in the innermost region of the gaseous disk. On the other hand, the orbital configuration of the outer companions suggests a nearly in situ formation of both objects, with the WJ forming in a dense inner disk. Alternatively, the BD might have formed farther out and migrated inward, while increasing its eccentricity owing to interactions with the disk.

### Extended Data Fig. 2 System present state and stability criterion.

a, Observed secular state of the WJ + BD system.b,c, Stability maps of the WJ + BD system as a function ofaWJandeWJ, with the BD on its present orbit, and as a function ofaBDandeBD, with the WJ on its present orbit (bandc, respectively). ### Extended Data Fig. 3 Observed TTVs.

Top, observed minus computed mid-transit time of TOI-201 d, for all 34 TESS sectors. No substantial TTV signal has been detected. Bottom, the measured TTVs and the TTV model of TOI-201 b, retrieved with a simultaneous fit of the RVs using a two-planet dynamical model, along with its residuals on the bottom.

### Extended Data Fig. 4 Photodynamical model posteriors.

Posterior probability distribution from the MCMC photodynamical fit of TOI-201 b and c. Only the key fitted and derived orbital parameters are shown. The nuisance parameters, such as RV and transit offsets, jitters and limb-darkening coefficients, exhibit well-defined Gaussian behaviour. Their median and 1σuncertainties are tabulated in theSupplementary Information. The lower-left corner plot shows the fitted orbital parameters, whereas the upper-right corner plot shows the derived posterior distributions.

### Extended Data Fig. 5 RV curve.

RV curve of TOI-201 from FEROS, HARPS, CORALIE, CHIRON and PLATOSpec spectrographs jointly fitted with the TESS transit data with anN-body model (solid line). The bottom sub-panel shows the RV residuals.

### Extended Data Fig. 6 Observed transits of TOI-201 b.

All of the TESS transits of TOI-201 b. The data and model are shown ±0.4 days from the fitted mid-transit time. The residuals are plotted in the sub-panels at the bottom of each transit.

### Extended Data Fig. 7 Orbital evolution at different mutual inclination values.

N-body evolution of the eccentricity (left panels) and inclination (right panels) of the SE (blue curves), the WJ (red curve) and the BD (green curve) companions in the TOI-201 system over an interval extent of 100 kyr. The simulation is based on the median posterior configuration of the SE TTV + RV fit test (see text for details).a, Allowing mutual inclinations between the SE and the WJ–BD pair. This solution yieldsΩd= 25.7° and the orbital evolution of this configuration results in substantial mutual inclination oscillations of the SE exoplanet orbit with respect to the orbits of the massive WJ–BD outer pair. The eccentricity of the SE oscillates within large bounds, yet the system remains stable.b,Ωd−b−cfixed effectively to 0°. This configuration is very close to coplanar, with low mutual inclinations. Notable oscillations inedandebandidandibare observed and the system remains well separated and stable.c, FixedΩc= 45°, assuming larger mutual inclinations between the SE–WJ with respect to the BD orbit. Much larger oscillations inedandebandidandibare observed, although the system remains stable.
