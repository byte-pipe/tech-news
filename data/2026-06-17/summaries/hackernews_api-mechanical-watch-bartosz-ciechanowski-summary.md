## Power
- Energy is stored in a torsion (spiral) spring called the **mainspring**, not in a coil spring.
- The mainspring is housed in a barrel; winding is done by turning an arbor that engages a hole in the spring.
- A metal strip attached to the mainspring adds friction, preventing over‑winding and acting as a safety mechanism.
- The spring’s S‑shaped relaxed form balances tension across its coils, improving reliability.
- A lid seals the barrel to keep dust out.
- Directly attaching a hand to the barrel makes the hand spin too fast and for too short a time, showing the need for gear reduction.

## Gears
- Gears translate the fast, low‑torque rotation of the barrel into slower, higher‑torque rotations for the hands.
- The speed change depends on the ratio of teeth (or radii) between the driving and driven gears.
- Adjusting the tooth count alters the driven gear’s rotation speed, enabling the minute and second hands to complete the required number of revolutions over a typical 40‑hour power reserve.
