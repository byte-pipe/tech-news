# The right kind of AI sceptic – by James Stanier

## The two kinds of sceptic
- Scepticism can be a **conclusion** drawn from personal experience or an **identity** adopted from surrounding discourse.  
- Experience‑based sceptics say things like “I’ve used AI for six months and it hasn’t made me measurably faster, here’s why,” grounding their view in concrete work.  
- Identity‑based sceptics repeat memes such as “it’s just autocomplete” without having tried the tools themselves.  
- Real‑world observations show both patterns: some engineers flip from dismissal to enthusiasm after hands‑on trials, while others remain unconvinced because the tools still fall short for large codebases or niche languages.  
- In certain developer circles, rejecting AI has become a badge of belonging; the “crab bucket” metaphor describes how the group pulls down anyone who tries to climb out and examine the claim independently.

## The enthusiasm mirror
- Ungrounded enthusiasm is as damaging as ungrounded scepticism, especially when leaders overcommit resources based on hype.  
- Notable failures include:
  - Klarna’s claim that AI could replace 700 support agents, later reversed when quality collapsed.  
  - CEOs at Microsoft and Google announcing high percentages of AI‑generated code without transparent measurement methods.  
  - Start‑ups promising autonomous AI agents that never materialised.  
- Mandates to use AI without clear guidance or leadership example create cynicism and reinforce sceptical attitudes.  
- Both sides share a common flaw: adopting a position as identity, insulating it from feedback, and prioritising being right over being accurate.

## How to hold your view well about AI
- **Use the tools yourself** – go beyond short demos, forum comments, or quick videos; tackle a real problem with the best available model.
