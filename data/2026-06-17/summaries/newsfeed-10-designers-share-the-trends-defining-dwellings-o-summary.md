# 10 Designers Share the Trends Defining Dwellings of Tomorrow

## Kulapat Yantrasast – “Buddy Block” Concept
- Homes will act as collective hubs where friends share a clubhouse‑like space.  
- The “buddy block” offers shared meals, a physical trainer, life coach, or driver for a small community of about ten people.  

## David Flack – Integrated Wellness & Tactile Touch
- Full‑blown wellness areas (steam showers, saunas, cold plunges, hyper‑baric chambers) become standard.  
- Gyms are dispersed: lightweight‑exercise zones and dedicated yoga spaces.  
- Preference for tactile, analog controls (light switches) over overwhelming smart‑tech interfaces.  

## Harry Nuriev – Quiet Minimalism with Strong Contrast
- Interiors shift toward calmer, less visually busy spaces; empty space gains importance.  
- Minimalism becomes a mental clearing tool, not just an aesthetic.  
- Strong black‑and‑white contrasts replace endless beige palettes.  
- Calls for re‑evaluating the prominence of objects like treadmills versus dining tables.  

## Sara Zewde – Re‑thinking Outdoor Landscapes
- Move away from high‑maintenance lawns toward meadow mixes, seed‑grown, wilder plantings.  
- Shade structures become essential for comfortable outdoor living and dining.  

## Britt Moran – Cinematic, Layered Domestic Layouts
- Homes read in layers rather than a strict sequence; spaces connect visually or perceptually.  
- Primary suites become autonomous worlds with their own light, materiality, and silence.  
- Growth of secondary, independent units that offer emotional distance while remaining proximate.  

## Mark Grattan – Dedicated Coffee/Tea Rooms & Return of Color
- Separate rooms for coffee/tea to keep electronics out of the kitchen.  
- Designers embrace authentic individuality: more color, prints, warmth, and less sterile, plastic finishes.  

## Andre Herrero – Meditation, Retreat, and Guest Apartments
- Creation of rooms for meditation, retreat, and isolation to break from technology.  
- Demand for full‑size guest apartments attached to the main house, enabling socialization and idea exchange.  

## Toshiko Mori – Fluid, Informal Workspaces at Home
- Work areas become informal and adaptable: breakfast tables that convert to desks, bedroom nooks, or small discreet corners.  
- The home’s lifestyle is fluid; rooms are not locked to a single function.  

## Carlos Campos Morera – Hands‑On Gardening and Soundscapes
- Homeowners increasingly participate in garden maintenance; personal connection defines garden quality.  
- Water features and sound elements (wind chimes, rustling plants) gain popularity, with careful water‑use design.  

## Hugo Toro – Invisible Smart Tech & Craftsmanship Revival
- Smart technology should be invisible; screens are undesirable.  
- Emphasis on natural materials, textures, and artisan craftsmanship reminiscent of the 1930s.  
- Acceptance of imperfect, asymmetrical finishes that reveal the hand of the maker.
