# Google set to shutter CBRS SAS management service

## Key announcement
- Google Cloud stopped accepting new customers for its CBRS SAS platform on June 10 2026.  
- The service will stay operational for existing customers until it is fully shut down on June 10 2027.  
- A migration guide is provided, directing customers to alternative SAS providers: Federated Wireless, Red Technologies, Nokia, Sony, and Keybridge.

## Industry response
- Federated Wireless CEO Iyad Tarazi announced the company’s readiness to help migrating customers, citing successful migration of over 180,000 devices and a 100 % nationwide sensor network covering all U.S. coastal areas, Alaska, Hawaii, and territories.

## What is a CBRS SAS?
- In 2015 the FCC allocated 150 MHz of the 3.5 GHz band for shared use (CBRS) to open under‑utilized spectrum.  
- The band uses a three‑tier access model:  
  - Tier 1 – protected incumbents (e.g., DoD Navy radars, satellite stations)  
  - Tier 2 – Priority Access Licenses (PAL) for geographically reserved use  
  - Tier 3 – General Authorized Access for opportunistic use  
- A Spectrum Access System (SAS) acts as a clearinghouse, coordinating spectrum reservations among users. Initial SAS administrators included CommScope, Federated Wireless, Google, and Sony.

## Performance and debate
- Deployments have focused on private networks and municipal smart‑infrastructure projects.  
- A 2022 CTIA‑commissioned Recon Analytics report criticized CBRS for low utilization, limited market demand, and few innovative use cases.  
- Proponents (Amazon, HPE, Charter, Comcast, Federated Wireless, Public Knowledge) countered with a letter to the FCC highlighting:  
  - Over 285,000 CBRS‑capable base stations deployed, including 10,000 from cellular operators  
  - FCC approval of 187 CBRS base stations and 496 end‑user devices  
  - Federated Wireless managing roughly 45 % of the >400,000 CBRS‑enabled devices in the market.

## Future spectrum sharing
- The FCC opened 1,200 MHz in the 6 GHz band for unlicensed Wi‑Fi use (Wi‑Fi 6E/7) in 2020, with incumbent protection mechanisms.  
- New frequency‑coordination platforms enable Wi‑Fi devices to exploit the expanded 6 GHz pool and improve outdoor coverage and capacity.
