# Summary of “Hull adventurer Karl Bushby ‘overwhelmed’ at Humber Bridge crowds – BBC News”

## Key Event
- Karl Bushby crossed the Humber Bridge in thick mist, marking the final leg of his 31,000‑mile, 28‑year walk from South America to Hull.  
- The crossing attracted large crowds, which Bushby described as “absolutely overwhelmed”.

## Background of the Journey
- Began in 1998 from Punta Arenas, Chile, with only a few hundred dollars and two self‑imposed rules: no return to the UK until the trek was finished and no use of transport to advance.  
- Over the years he traversed four continents, a frozen sea, deserts, mountains, jungles, and swam across the English Channel on 5 October.  
- The trek was motivated by personal circumstances, including the breakdown of his marriage and his wife moving to Northern Ireland with their son.

## Personal Reflections
- Bushby sees the Humber Bridge as an iconic, almost symbolic landmark, recalling its “mystic look” and the inability to see the opposite riverbank.  
- He likened his situation to “a homeless guy pushing a shopping trolley,” referencing the cart (“The Beast”) he has been pulling for 28 years.  
- He compared his solitary companion to “Wilson” from the film *Cast Away*, highlighting the emotional weight of the moment.

## Upcoming Arrival in Hull
- On Monday, Bushby will walk from Hessle to Queen Victoria Square in north Hull, where a reception will be held outside the Civic Hall.  
- He will be met by his mother Angela, his son, and his granddaughter at his mother’s home.  
- The event will be covered on BBC Radio Humberside, BBC Sounds, and the programme *Look North*.
