# The world’s best toilets | Norwegian Scenic Routes

## Overview
- Norway’s public restrooms along the Scenic Routes are celebrated for striking design, breathtaking scenery, and their appeal as tourist attractions.
- Many visitors stop to admire the architecture and views even when they don’t need to use the facilities.

## Notable Restrooms
- **Stegastein viewpoint (Sognefjord)** – ranked among the world’s 10 best public toilets by DesignCurial; offers panoramic views of the Aurlandsfjord.
- **Ureddplassen (Helgeland coast)** – wave‑shaped structure featured in several publications; name means “the place of the fearless.”
- **Eggum (Lofoten)** – amphitheatre‑style toilet built beneath the ruins of a German radar station from World War II.
- **Ryfylke route near Flesefossen waterfall** – architecturally impressive with a stunning waterfall backdrop.
- **Golden loo (Senja island)** – gold‑plated seat; its popularity has inspired the construction of additional, lower‑key toilets nearby.
- **Utsikten (Gaularfjellet, Sognefjord area)** – discreet but notable for being solar‑powered.
- **Ostastein (Ryfylke)** – outer walls designed to encourage moss growth, providing natural camouflage.
- **Tungeneset (Senja)** – picturesque location under the northern lights.
- **Tyrvefjøra (Hardanger)** – two pine‑log cabins with a large “floating” roof that appears to hover over the landscape.
- **Selvika (Scenic Route Havøysund)** – designed by Reiulf Ramstad Arkitekter.
- **Allmannajuvet (Ryfylke)** – created by Atelier Peter Zumthor & Partner.
- **Bukkekjerka (Andøya)** – designed by Morfeus Arkitekter.
- **Oscarshaug (Scenic Route Sognefjellet)** – reinforced with steel to withstand harsh weather, resembling a Viking helmet.

## Design and Architecture
- All but one of the restrooms are the work of well‑known or emerging Norwegian architecture firms.
- **Snøhetta** designed the smallest example at Eggum.
- Designs emphasize the connection between architecture and nature: solar power, moss‑camouflage, forest‑inspired structures, and use of local timber.
- Many facilities are only reachable in summer because some Scenic Route roads close in winter.
- Harsh weather conditions have led to robust solutions, such as steel reinforcement at Oscarshaug.

## Future Projects
- A forest‑inspired restroom is under development, using dried wood from 60–70 trees to form an upside‑down tree trunk with roots supporting the roof.

## Visitor Etiquette
- Keep the toilets clean: flush after use, dispose of paper towels in bins, and avoid leaving any mess for the next visitor.
