Astronomers at theEuropean Southern Observatory (ESO) have discovered a debris disk around awhite dwarf, consisting of the remnants of the solar system that once orbited it. For researchers, these structures could offer a clue as to what the end ofour solar systemwill look like once thesun runs out of fuel.

The white dwarf J1328−1450 is located about 1,600 light-years from Earth. Astronomers were already aware of it thanks to star-mapping catalogs, but new observations taken with ESO’s Very Large Telescope (yes, that’s its real name) revealed that it is surrounded by a disk of debris with an unusual structure: Twodistinct rings of gasseparated by a gap.

According tothe observatory, this disk of material is likely the remnant of the planetary system that once orbited J1328−1450. As it passed through a red giant phase and subsequently became a white dwarf, the star may have destroyed some of its planets and leftother rocky bodiesexposed to an extreme environment. Some of the released material ended up forming a disk of gas and dust around the dead star.

An illustration showing what the debris rings may look like around a white dwarf.
Illustration: Maria Cristina Fortuna/ESO

The process that shaped this system could repeat itself in our own. In about 5 billion years, the sun will exhaust its fuel and expand into a red giant. During this phase, it couldengulf nearby bodies, like the closest planets. It will then shed its outer layers, leaving behind a white dwarf. Any rocky bodies that survive this transformationcould break apartand form a debris disk around the dead star, like the one astronomers are now observing in J1328−1450.

But the white dwarf’s two debris rings hint at another possibility. Their structure appears to be shaped by bodies that astronomers have not yet been able toidentify directly. The data suggest that, following the star’s transformation, at least two planetary objectsmay have survivedamong the remnants of that system.

Researchers believe these two bodies could be very different in size. The one closest to the white dwarf would measure just a few kilometers in diameter, while the more distant one could be as large as Pluto oreven the moon. Their interaction with the surrounding material would explain the formation of the two rings, although it has not yet been confirmed that they exist as separate objects. An alternative possibility is that the structure originates from different parts of a single planet-like body, such asits crustand core.
