## Earth’s history

Outcrop shows where a rock’s age falls in Earth’s 4.5
 billion years.

* Cenozoic66–0 Ma
* Mesozoic252–66 Ma
* Paleozoic539–252 Ma
* Precambrian4540–539 Ma

Hover over or tap the line to read a period or an event.

## Geologic maps in augmented reality

Hold your phone up to a canyon wall, a road cut or a far
 ridge. Outcrop lays the geologic map over the land, names the
 rock at the center of the screen and labels the peaks and lakes
 in view. Pick a rock and the land keeps it in color, the rest
 gone grey.

## Know what’s under your feet.

The map opens on the rock where you’re standing: its
 name, its age, what it’s made of and how it formed. Tap
 anywhere else to read that spot instead.

## Read the rock below.

Where Macrostrat has a column for the spot, see the rock
 beneath it from the surface down: each unit with its age, each
 gap with the time it leaves out, and the fossils found in them.
 Tap a unit to read about it.

## See where it formed.

Turn the map into a globe at the rock’s age, drawn from
 Christopher Scotese’sPALEOMAPmaps: where it lay, how far it has come and how fast. Press
 play to ride its plate to today.

## Find any rock.

Search for a unit, an age, a rock or a fault. The map lights
 the places it’s mapped and tells you how far the nearest
 is, even when it’s off the screen.

## From bedrock to topsoil.

Read the bedrock and its faults fromMacrostrat’s
 geologic maps, in as much detail as each place has been mapped.
 Switch to Quaternary geology for the glacial and river deposits
 on top and the faults that have moved recently, or to the soil
 and the plants that grow in it. On the map and through the
 camera.

* Bedrock and faults worldwide
* Quaternary, active faults and soil in the US

## Save it before you go.

Outline an area to save it to your phone. The map and the camera
 view then work with no signal. Fossil finds and active faults in
 the camera view still need a connection.
