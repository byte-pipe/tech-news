# Adidas faces boycott calls over campaign featuring former Israeli soldier

## Main Issue
- Adidas released an advertisement for its in‑store single‑shoe service for amputees, showing former Israeli army soldier Shalev Biton trying on a shoe in Israel and then running outdoors.  
- The ad was labeled “an affront to Palestinians” and sparked calls for a boycott of the brand.

## Background of the Soldier
- Biton lost his leg after being wounded during the 2021 Israel‑Gaza conflict, part of an 11‑day Israeli assault on the enclave.  
- The World Health Organization estimates that between 5,000 and 6,000 people in Gaza had undergone amputations by early October 2025, within a total of about 42,000 Palestinians who sustained life‑changing injuries during the two‑year war.  

## Reactions and Boycott Calls
- Stephanie Westbrook, sports‑boycott campaigns coordinator for the Palestinian Campaign for the Academic and Cultural Boycott of Israel (PACBI), condemned the ad as a “normalisation of Israel’s crimes” and an insult to Palestinians who have lost loved ones and limbs.  
- Spanish actor Javier Bardem, authors Susan Abulhawa and Fatima Bhutto, and many Palestinians on social media publicly called for a boycott of Adidas products.  
- Critics highlighted that more than a thousand Palestinian athletes were killed in the war, while hundreds of amputee football players continue to use sport as a means of survival.

## Humanitarian Context
- Nearly a year after the ceasefire, Gaza remains under a severe blockade that restricts humanitarian aid, medicines, and prosthetics.  
- Children account for about a quarter of all amputations in Gaza, making the territory the world’s highest‑per‑capita location for child amputees, according to the International Rescue Committee.  
- UNICEF reports that 42,000 Palestinians have suffered life‑altering injuries, including 11,000 children, since the conflict began in October 2023.  

## Wider Implications
- The campaign is seen by activists as part of a broader pattern that “normalises Israel’s crimes against Palestinians and allows them to continue with impunity.”  
- The boycott movement ties the Adidas ad to ongoing concerns about Israel’s alleged genocide, illegal occupation, and apartheid‑like policies toward Palestinians.
