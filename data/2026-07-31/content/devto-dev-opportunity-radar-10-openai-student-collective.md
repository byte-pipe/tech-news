## 📌 Develop for Good Winter 2027 Student Volunteer Program

Who it's for:University students, bootcamp graduates, and recent graduates (with less than two years of full-time work experience) who want to gain real-world product, design, or engineering experience while contributing to nonprofit organizations. International students are welcome, and the program is fully remote.

What stands out:Develop for Goodmatches students with nonprofit organizations to work on real technology projects over a 16-week virtual program. Participants join cross-functional teams alongside designers, engineers, and product managers, receive mentorship from industry professionals, and build products that support causes like education, healthcare, sustainability, poverty alleviation, disaster relief, and more.

I wanted to include this because one of the biggest challenges early in your career is answering the question,"How do I get experience if every opportunity asks for experience?"Programs like this help bridge that gap. Unlike many volunteer programs, this one is intentionally structured to feel like working on a real product team. Instead of working on another tutorial or personal project, you get to collaborate with a real client, solve real problems, and build something that can become a meaningful portfolio piece.

Another thing I like is how closely the experience mirrors a real software team. Students work through project planning, stakeholder communication, technical collaboration, documentation, and product delivery over four months. Participants can also receive mentorship, recommendation letters, portfolio case studies, and opportunities to network with professionals during Demo Day. For eligible students from underserved and underrepresented backgrounds, theDevelop for Good Fellowsprogram also provides$1,536 unrestricted cash grantsupon successful completion.

Program Duration:16 weeks (Winter 2027 Batch)

Time Commitment:Approximately 5–10 hours per week

Format:Fully remote

Roles Available:Engineering, Design, Product Management, Technical Management, and Design Management

Who can apply:Applicants aged 18+ who are university students, bootcamp students, or recent graduates with less than two years of full-time work experience. International students are eligible.

Application Deadline:September 15, 2026

## 📌 MLH Global Hack Week: Agents

Who it's for:Students, beginners, aspiring developers, experienced engineers, and anyone interested in learning about AI agents through hands-on challenges. You don't need prior hackathon experience to participate, and the event is open to people around the world.

What stands out:Global Hack Week: Agentsis a free, week-long online event hosted byMajor League Hacking (MLH)that combines workshops, technical challenges, community activities, and project building around the theme of AI agents. Throughout the week, participants can attend live coding sessions, complete challenges at their own pace, earn points, connect with other builders on Discord, and learn directly from the MLH community.

I wanted to include this because learning about AI agents is becoming increasingly valuable, but it can be difficult to know where to start. This event lowers that barrier by combining guided workshops with practical challenges, making it approachable whether you're experimenting with agents for the first time or looking to build on your existing skills.

Another thing I like is that you don't have to commit to building a full hackathon project over a weekend. Instead, you can participate throughout the week, choose the challenges that interest you, attend live sessions, and learn alongside thousands of developers from around the world. It's a great way to build new skills, meet other developers, and stay motivated without the pressure of a traditional hackathon.

Event Dates:August 7–13, 2026

Format:Fully online

Cost:Free

Who can apply:Anyone, anywhere. All experience levels are welcome, including beginners and non-students. Participants aged 13+ may join with parental permission.

Registration:Open

## 📌 OpenAI Student Collective

Who it's for:Undergraduate students who enjoy building communities and are excited to help others learn how to use AI tools like ChatGPT and Codex. Students from all majors are encouraged to apply. The program is open to students studying in theUnited States, Canada, the United Kingdom, France, Germany, India, Japan, and South Korea.

What stands out:TheOpenAI Student Collectiveis a new campus leadership program where students work in pairs to build AI communities at their universities. Campus Leads organize workshops, host weekly Studio Hours where students can build projects together, run campus showcases, and help create an environment where anyone curious about AI can learn and experiment.

I wanted to include this because AI communities on campus are growing quickly, but they often depend on a few students taking the initiative to bring people together. This program gives students the support, resources, and funding to create those spaces while helping others become more confident using AI tools.

Another thing I like is that the focus isn't just on teaching ChatGPT or Codex. It's about helping students actually build projects together. Campus Leads receive training, event funding, a ChatGPT subscription, Codex credits, OpenAI swag, a semester stipend, and access to the OpenAI team and a global network of Campus Leads. There are also opportunities to visit OpenAI HQ and connect with other student leaders from around the world.

Program Duration:August 2026 – June 2027

Time Commitment:Approximately 4–6 hours per week

Who can apply:Undergraduate students aged 18 or older who expect to graduate after December 2027. Applicants should be studying in the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, France, Germany, India, Japan, or South Korea.

Application Deadline:August 10, 2026 (11:59 PM PT)

## Learning How to Learn (Free Coursera Course)

Who it's for:Students, self-taught developers, career changers, and anyone who wants to become better at learning technical subjects like programming, AI, math, or computer science.

What stands out:Learning How to Learn, taught byDr. Barbara OakleyandDr. Terrence Sejnowski, is one of Coursera's most popular courses, withover 4 million learners. Instead of teaching a specific programming language or framework, it focuses on something even more valuable:how to learn effectively. The course covers topics like procrastination, memory, focused and diffuse thinking, chunking, learning strategies, and practical techniques you can apply no matter what you're studying.

I wanted to include this because I genuinely found it helpful. I first came across Barbara Oakley through her TEDx talk, which eventually led me to this course. It completely changed the way I thought about learning difficult subjects. Whether you're learning AI, web development, cybersecurity, or preparing for interviews, I think understandinghow to learnis one of the best investments you can make.

One thing I especially wanted to share is that you don't have to pay to take the full course. Coursera has apartner-supported free versionthat gives you access to all the course content. The only thing you don't receive is the certificate, which I think is a great option if you're primarily there to learn.

Course Length:Approximately 2 weeks (around 10 hours)

Cost:Free (certificate optional)

💡Free Access Tip:You can take the full version of this course for free (without the certificate). On the Coursera course page, scroll all the way down to theFAQsection. UnderMore Questions, clickPartner-supported free access, then selectLearn Morefollowed byContinueto enroll. You'll get access to the complete course content without paying for the certificate.

Format:Self-paced online course
