# IBM: AI-driven attacks drive up data breach costs by 12%

## Key findings from IBM’s 2026 Cost of a Data Breach Report
- Average cost of an AI‑enabled breach: **$6 million**, about $1 million higher than the global average of $4.99 million.  
- Global average breach cost rose 12% year‑over‑year to $4.99 million, a new high.  
- One‑quarter of malicious breaches involved AI tools.  
- AI‑driven attacks increased 56% compared with the previous year, adding roughly $1 million per breach.  

## How AI changes the attack lifecycle
- AI automates reconnaissance, creates persuasive phishing content, adapts malware, and tests exploits at machine speed, reducing the cost and complexity of sophisticated attacks.  
- Faster attack velocity compresses the detection‑to‑containment window, inflating losses from operational disruption, legal fees, remediation, and reputational damage.  
- Detection, escalation, and lost‑business costs together accounted for 63% of total breach expenses.  

## Benefits and gaps in AI‑based defense
- Organizations using AI for threat hunting, response, and containment saved an average of $1.93 million per breach.  
- 50% of breached firms had deployed AI agents for those functions, but only 18% applied AI to vulnerability scanning and management, indicating a reactive rather than proactive use of the technology.  

## Most costly AI‑related incident types
- Model inversion attacks: average loss $6.07 million.  
- Prompt‑injection attacks: average loss $5.89 million.  
- These attacks exploit AI models to reconstruct sensitive training data without direct dataset access.  

## Broader security implications
- Many AI‑related breaches stem from structural weaknesses in enterprise environments, not just model flaws.  
- Over 50% of organizations plan to invest in AI security and governance tools post‑breach, an 88% increase from the prior year.  

## Additional IBM findings
- Malicious or criminal attacks accounted for 55% of all breaches, up ~8% YoY; they outnumber IT‑failure (22%) and human‑error (23%) causes.  
- Phishing remained the top initial attack vector; voice/SMS phishing (17% of attacks) produced the highest average breach cost ($5.29 million), followed by help‑desk impersonation (13%) at $5.23 million.  
- Customer PII was the most stolen data type (52% of breaches), costing $192 per record on average.  
- On‑premises storage was involved in 30% of breaches, a rise from 28% the previous year and 20% in 2024.  
- 53% of breached organizations did not encrypt sensitive data at rest or in motion; another 10% were unsure about encryption status.
