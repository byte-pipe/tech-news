description: 'Have you heard of the concept of ''meta-analysis'' in digital experimentation? It''s a concept borrowed from the world of science where the results of multiple identical experiments, ideally conducted under identical conditions, are combined to provide better sample sizes of data. The way this has been adopted in the digital experimentation space is through the idea of a database of experiments. This might be a database within a company of their own experiments, or there are also various attempts to combine experiments from multiple companies together. The idea is that you would classify or group experiments and then combine the results of those experiments to produce results by category. This would tell you the likelihood of one category being a better overall idea than another. Sounds great? Well, not really, unfortunately. There are some serious issues with this when you pick it apart. If you can do it, then of course, why not, but you need to understand the limitations.
  1. How you classify and therefore group tests is completely and utterly subjective. Just try it with a list of experiments. There are a ridiculous number of possible ways of classifying even a single experiment. At the end of the day, you are therefore introducing a huge dose of subjectivity into a statistical calculation by choosing two tests to put together. 2. The ''conditions'' of the experiments are nowhere near identical. They are not even remotely similar in most cases. You are no longer ''controlling'' for a single variable. 3. You cannot possibly know why the thing made the impact it made. For example, if you add some urgency messaging to a page, you will likely classify and group this as ''urgency''. But how do you know the effect wasn''t simply due to what it did to the layout of the page - for example, pushing something else below the fold? You have therefore categorised the test incorrectly. 4. Especially with a database spanning multiple different companies'' tests, even
  if this did work, what you would be discovering are the lowest common denominators. These are things likely to work on any website but are therefore also totally uninteresting and without differentiation value. This is not to say it isn''t worth doing if you can, but it should only ever be a way of coming up with test ideas, not best practices. It''s therefore an incredibly high maintenance piece of ''research'' #cro #experimentation #ecommerce #digitalmarketing #ux #userexperience'
