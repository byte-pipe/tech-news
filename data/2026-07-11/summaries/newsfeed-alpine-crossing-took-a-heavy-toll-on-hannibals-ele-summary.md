# Alpine crossing took a heavy toll on Hannibal’s elephants and troops

## Main findings
- The Col de la Traversette is identified as the most energy‑efficient Alpine pass for Hannibal’s 218 BC march.  
- Even this “lowest‑energy” route proved extremely demanding for the Carthaginian army.  
- The crossing caused severe loss of men and war elephants, confirming historical reports of high casualties.  
- A biophysical study (Berti & Vollrath, 2026) quantifies the physical strain on both soldiers and elephants.

## Implications
- Confirms that Hannibal’s Alpine crossing was possible but came at a great cost to his forces.  
- Provides a scientific basis for interpreting ancient military logistics and animal endurance in extreme environments.
