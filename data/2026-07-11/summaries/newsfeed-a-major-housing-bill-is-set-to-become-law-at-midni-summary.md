# A major housing bill is set to become law at midnight — Trump says he won’t sign

## Key events
- The 21st Century Road to Housing Act passed both chambers of Congress in June with broad bipartisan support.  
- House Speaker Mike Johnson delivered the bill to the White House on June 29, starting the 10‑day period for the president to act.  
- The deadline expires at 11:59 p.m. ET on July 10; without a signature or veto, the bill will automatically become law.  

## President Trump’s stance
- Trump called the housing legislation “a big yawn” and refused to sign it, saying he will only act if the Senate passes his SAVE America Act, a strict voter‑ID measure.  
- In a Truth Social post, he announced his refusal to sign “in protest” over the Senate’s inability to pass the SAVE America Act.  
- The White House redirected inquiries to Trump’s statement and did not provide additional comment.  

## Main provisions of the housing bill
- Over 40 bipartisan measures aimed at increasing homeownership affordability.  
- Caps corporate landlords owning 350+ single‑family homes from purchasing additional properties, echoing a proposal Trump previously supported.  
- Grants for “pattern books” to streamline local approvals for pre‑approved housing designs.  
- Allows developers to bypass certain environmental reviews when building between already‑reviewed structures.  
- Reduces construction costs for manufactured homes by eliminating the permanent chassis requirement, potentially saving $5,000–$10,000 per unit.  
- Redirects existing federal housing funds toward communities that increase construction, without adding new budgetary funding.  

## Political and electoral context
- Democrats praised the bill as the most significant housing legislation in decades; Republicans framed it as a win for families.  
- Housing affordability is a prominent issue ahead of the 2026 midterm elections, prompting both parties to claim credit.  
- Trump’s refusal ties the housing bill to his broader voter‑ID agenda, leveraging the legislation for political leverage.  

## Limitations and outlook
- Federal actions have limited impact compared with local zoning rules, which remain unchanged.  
- High construction costs, labor shortages, and 30‑year mortgage rates around 6.5% constrain immediate market effects.  
- New homes built under the bill will take years to reach the market, meaning affordability gains may not be felt until after the current election cycle.  
- Experts argue the bill is a necessary first step, but sustained local reforms and broader economic conditions are required for lasting change.
