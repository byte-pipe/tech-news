# Data is your only moat

## Overview
- The article examines how problem complexity and ease of adoption shape AI product success, using a 2×2 framework (easy/hard to adopt vs. easy/hard to solve).
- Central thesis: regardless of quadrant, competitive advantage comes from owning data; data forms the sole sustainable moat.

## Quadrant 1: Easy to adopt, easy to solve
- Typical use cases: consumer search, simple Q&A, health advice.
- Low barriers attract many entrants and large model providers (OpenAI, Google, Anthropic) who collect massive data volumes.
- Result: high competition, low loyalty, and a high risk of being displaced by better‑funded players.
- Prediction: dominant brands will likely be the big foundation‑model providers.

## Quadrant 2: Easy to adopt, hard to solve
- Example: coding assistants (e.g., Cursor, ChatGPT code generation).
- Adoption is quick because developers can switch IDEs with minimal friction.
- Frequent, fine‑grained feedback creates a “data flywheel”: each accepted or rejected suggestion improves the model.
- Other hard‑to‑solve tasks lacking such feedback loops (e.g., slide generation) improve more slowly.
- Expect heavyweight competition from large model labs; smaller players need substantial capital to compete.
- Stickiness remains modest; users often run multiple agents and may switch for better output.

## Quadrant 3: Hard to adopt, easy to solve
- Enterprise AI for well‑defined workflows (e.g., e‑commerce returns, password resets).
- Adoption requires organization‑level decisions and complex integration with legacy systems.
- Data moat arises from deep knowledge of each customer’s processes, making the product hard to replace.
- Large incumbents (e.g., Sierra, Decagon) dominate; smaller startups face steep GTM and integration challenges.
- Unclear how much raised capital is spent on go‑to‑market versus building technical advantages.

## Implications for Builders
- **Data collection is the key moat** in every quadrant; without proprietary data, products are vulnerable to displacement.
- Easy‑to‑adopt products gain data quickly but are also easy for competitors to replicate.
- Hard‑to‑adopt enterprise solutions build sticky, customer‑specific data that is harder to copy.
- Strategic focus should consider both the acquisition speed of useful data and the defensibility of that data.

## Takeaway
- Success in AI‑driven applications hinges less on the intrinsic difficulty of the problem and more on how quickly and uniquely a product can gather and leverage data. Data, not model size or algorithmic novelty, is the enduring competitive advantage.
