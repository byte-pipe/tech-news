# GameStop’s $55.5 bn takeover offer for eBay – Summary  

## Offer details  
- GameStop proposes a cash‑and‑stock deal valued at $55.5 bn (£40.9 bn).  
- The offer prices eBay at $125 per share, about $20 above its closing price on the New York market.  
- Ryan Cohen, GameStop’s chief executive, says eBay could be worth more under his leadership and could rival Amazon.  
- If eBay’s board rejects the proposal, Cohen is prepared to take the bid directly to shareholders.  

## Financing and leadership  
- GameStop’s current market value is around $11.9 bn.  
- A commitment letter from TD Securities provides roughly $20 bn of debt to help fund the acquisition.  
- Under the merged entity, Cohen would become chief executive, receiving no salary or bonuses and being compensated only by the combined company’s performance.  

## Expected operational changes  
- Cohen plans to cut eBay’s costs by $2 bn within the first year, mainly targeting the sales and marketing division.  
- GameStop’s 1,600 U.S. stores would give eBay a national “live commerce” network.  

## Financial performance of the parties  
- GameStop’s net profit rose to $418.4 m in 2025, up from $131.3 m in 2024, despite a decline in sales.  
- eBay, valued at about four times GameStop’s size, has seen its user base fall to 136 million from 175 million in 2018.  

## Market reaction  
- eBay shares gained 5.5 % in New York trading on the morning of the announcement.  
- GameStop shares fell more than 4 % following the news.  

## Analyst perspectives  
- Morgan Stanley notes “fundamentally different” business models between the two companies, casting doubt on a successful merger.  
- Forrester analyst Sucharita Kodali describes the offer as not “terribly good,” highlighting the added debt burden on eBay.  
- Kodali adds that the deal could boost GameStop’s valuation by linking it to a larger brand, but it does not combine two strong companies.  

## Background of the companies  
- GameStop rose to prominence during the COVID‑19 pandemic as a focal point of the meme‑stock phenomenon.  
- eBay, launched in 1995 as a hobbyist marketplace, remains a well‑known tech brand but faces competition from Amazon and other platforms.
