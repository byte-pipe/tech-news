# Google to sell TPU chips to 'select' customers in latest shot at Nvidia

## Key Points
- Alphabet announced it will sell its custom Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) to a select group of customers for installation in their own data centers, shifting from its previous model of renting TPU capacity.  
- The announcement was made during the Q1 earnings call, a week after unveiling two new TPU models: the TPU 8t for AI training and the TPU 8i for inference.  
- CEO Sundar Pichai said the strategy is intended to expand the addressable market as demand grows from AI labs, capital‑markets firms, and high‑performance computing applications.  
- Alphabet has already signed a multiple‑gigawatt agreement with Anthropic for next‑generation TPUs, with chips expected to go live in 2027, and reportedly a multibillion‑dollar chip deal with Meta.  
- The move intensifies competition with Nvidia, which maintains that its own chips offer greater flexibility for AI developers.  
- Amazon is also entering the AI‑chip market, offering Graviton, Trainium, and Nitro processors through AWS; it reported a chip‑related revenue run rate above $20 billion (potentially closer to $50 billion).  
- Amazon has secured 5 GW of AI chip capacity with Anthropic, 2 GW with OpenAI, and will deploy its Graviton chips for Meta’s agentic AI workloads.  
- Both Google and Amazon are targeting the same AI‑chip customers, signaling a broader industry challenge to Nvidia’s dominance.
