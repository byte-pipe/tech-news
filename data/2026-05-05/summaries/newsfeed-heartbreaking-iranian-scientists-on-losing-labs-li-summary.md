# Summary of “‘Heartbreaking’: Iranian scientists on losing labs, libraries and liberty”

## Background
- Since the war began on 28 February 2026, bombs dropped by the United States and Israel have damaged about 30 Iranian universities, according to the Iranian Red Crescent Society.  
- More than 1,400 international scholars signed an open letter to UN officials and the warring parties condemning attacks on civilian academic, health, and research infrastructure.  
- The White House and an Israeli military spokesperson claim they do not target civilian facilities, but offered no explanation for the specific institutions hit.  
- Prior to the conflict, Iranian students and academics were protesting for greater democracy, human rights, and liberty; the government violently suppressed these protests in January.

## Affected Institutions
- **Sharif University of Technology (Tehran)** – Iran’s leading technology university; its library of over 1,000 personal books was destroyed.  
- **Pasteur Institute of Iran (Tehran)** – historic research institute for drugs and vaccines.  
- **Isfahan University of Technology** – attacked twice in late March; internet blackout hindered research.  
- **Shefa Neuroscience Research Center (Tehran)** – damaged on 1 March; nearby hospitals also hit, but a large brain‑tissue bank survived.  

## Researchers’ Experiences
- **Abideh Jafari (CERN physicist, Isfahan University)**  
  - Internet shutdown prevented access to CERN data.  
  - Students are distressed by ongoing bombings.  
  - Requests patience from journal editors and referees, noting delays in publishing are understandable.  

- **Hamed Bikaraan‑Behesht (Research ethicist, Tehran)**  
  - Describes the situation as “heartbreaking.”  
  - Researchers feel unsafe on campus and cannot concentrate.  
  - Lost contact with an international collaborator and a submitted paper due to the internet shutdown.  
  - Anticipates post‑war financial challenges that could further limit research funding.  

- **Ali Gorji (Neuroscientist, Münster University, supervising at Shefa)**  
  - Calls for a louder global scientific response to attacks on research infrastructure.  
  - Emphasizes that while buildings can be rebuilt, normalizing attacks on universities threatens future science.  

- **Ebrahim Azadegan (Philosopher, Sharif University)**  
  - Personal library of over 1,000 books, notes, manuscripts, and students’ drafts was destroyed in the bombing of his office.  
  - Now works from a physics department office, describing the attack on a civilian philosophy department as an act of cruelty and atrocity.  

## Broader Implications
- The combined effect of physical destruction, internet blackouts, and fear for personal safety is severely disrupting Iran’s scientific output.  
- International journals and reviewers are urged to consider these constraints when evaluating Iranian submissions.  
- Concerns that, after the war, the Iranian government may tighten control over universities and settle scores with dissenting scholars.  
- Reconstruction costs and limited funding are expected to pose long‑term obstacles to the revival of Iran’s research ecosystem.
