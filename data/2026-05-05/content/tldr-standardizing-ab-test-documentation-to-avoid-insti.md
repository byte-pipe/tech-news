description: 'We ran this A/B test already. Nobody knew 👀 Let''s talk about institutional memory — bear with me, this is simpler than it sounds You''re about to run an experiment, and you discover that another team ran something nearly identical two years ago 😶 You pull up their documentation: two lines of description and numbers with no context. Completely useless — even though that team once invested significant effort into that test, and you could have built on their work. The stronger the data culture in a company and the more experiments that run, the more we forget. We forget our champion experiments 🏆 We forget the risky ones that didn''t pan out 🧨 We forget the micro-improvements that consistently moved the needle by 0.5% 📈 "But we have Confluence, we have docs!" — yes, and yet. Documentation gets filled in hastily, teams are decentralized, and with thousands of analysts running experiments in parallel, things slip through. The fix is straightforward: a single standardized A/B test
  doc template, filled in carefully every time. When I write up an experiment, I always make sure it serves two audiences: — Business stakeholders: the idea, visuals of each variation, and a summary card with results that anyone can read without context — Analysts: design parameters, MDE, sample size, and the decision-making logic I was recently watching Lenny''s Podcast with Ron Kohavi — he made a similar point about the outsized importance of experiment documentation. So I''m attaching my own A/B test doc template below 💡 I''d love to hear your thoughts, and I hope the template proves useful for running experiments at your company! 💪'
