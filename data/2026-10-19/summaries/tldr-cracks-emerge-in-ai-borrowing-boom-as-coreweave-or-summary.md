# Cracks emerge in AI borrowing boom as CoreWeave, Oracle and SoftBank debt worries grow

## Main developments
- AI‑related borrowing, amounting to trillions of dollars, is facing growing uncertainty about future funding and repayment ability.  
- CoreWeave, a data‑centre operator with $72 bn of liabilities for 51 sites, saw its bond prices collapse and the market assign a >60 % chance of default.  
- Oracle’s AI‑focused data‑centre projects are delayed by permitting and power‑grid approvals; debt tied to these projects is trading at steep discounts (≈$0.80‑$0.90 per $1).  
- SoftBank’s massive $30 bn investment in OpenAI has been financed by record‑high‑cost debt (≈10 % interest) and rising insurance premiums for default risk.  

## Market reaction
- Bond spreads for risky AI debt have risen >50 % in the past six months (Barclays).  
- Investors are selling bonds of CoreWeave, Oracle and, to a lesser extent, other AI giants such as Oracle and hyperscalers; however, large hyperscalers (Meta, Alphabet) have faced only modest interest‑rate increases.  
- Credit‑default‑swap‑like instruments that insure corporate debt are being priced up, reflecting heightened default concerns.  

## Business‑model concerns
- CoreWeave’s model mirrors WeWork: it purchases AI chips, leases long‑term space from data‑centre owners, then sub‑leases short‑term to customers (e.g., Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI).  
- This creates a double‑sided cost exposure—upfront capital outlay before tenants arrive and long‑term lease commitments that may outlast customer contracts.  
- Investors question whether hyperscalers will continue to need CoreWeave’s leased capacity and whether its facilities could become obsolete.  

## Regulatory and permitting hurdles
- A rising number of U.S. data‑centre projects are stalled by permitting delays, power‑grid approvals, or other regulatory obstacles, affecting both CoreWeave and Oracle’s expansions.  

## Potential implications
- Higher borrowing costs could increase the overall expense of AI projects, potentially slowing the AI build‑out.  
- Some analysts view the slowdown as a “silver lining,” making existing AI infrastructure more valuable as new financing becomes scarce.  

## Outlook
- The sector’s “weak links” (CoreWeave, Oracle, SoftBank) may signal broader financing stress for AI infrastructure.  
- Continued monitoring of bond spreads, default probabilities, and permitting progress will be crucial for investors and AI developers alike.
