# AI cloud firm Nscale faces investor pushback over IPO valuation

This comes after its investor Microsoft pulled out of a UK data centre deal with the firm

[NEW YORK] Nscale is facing opposition to the valuation target it is pursuing in its initial public offering, according to people familiar with the matter, after a series of market setbacks for artificial intelligence companies.

The Nvidia-backed data centre developer has been seeking an IPO valuation of about US$30 billion, the people said.

A cohort of investors have lately urged that a valuation in the US$16 billion to US$19 billion range would be more likely to find favour with buyers, in light of factors including theimplosion of Australian AI data centre company Firmus’ IPO plan, some of the people said.

A disconnect over valuation would add to recent challenges facing Nscale’s planned IPO.

Microsoft, an investor in the company as well as a client,pulled out of a UK data centre deal with Nscale, the company said on Sunday (Oct 18), after the site was contending with delays from power constraints.

Sentiment toward AI infrastructure companies including chipmakers and cloud computing firms has turned as borrowing costs have risen and uncertainty over the technology’s long-term payoff has grown.

A discrepancy in expectations between a company seeking an IPO and potential investors can pressure a firm to slow down the process.

The so-called valuation gap may reflect differing outlooks on where funding costs are likely to go and whether challenges to the broader sector may be quickly resolved.

Nscale filed publicly for a US listing on Sep 18, and has yet to begin formal marketing even though the required 15-day waiting period has elapsed.

The company is working through comments from the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) on its filing, and intends to kick off marketing when those are resolved, people familiar with the matter said.

Deliberations are ongoing, details of the IPO plan could change and no final decisions have been made, the people said, asking not to be identified as the information is not public.

A representative for Nscale declined to comment.

The SEC did not immediately respond to a request for comment outside normal office hours.

The London-based firm raised US$3.36 billion in a pre-IPO financing round in September in a deal led by Dan Loeb’s Third Point with investors including Nvidia, Apollo Global Management, Citadel, Hudson Bay Capital and Abu Dhabi Investment Council participating.

Stock prices for several of Nscale’s peers have been battered.

CoreWeavewiped out a quarter of its market value last weekas a measure of its credit risk climbed to an all-time high, and Nebius Group’s stock slumped 32 per cent in the past two weeks.

Nscale, which is developing data centres in locations including Norway and West Virginia, was expected to raise as much as US$3 billion in its IPO, Bloomberg News reported in August.

The Norway facility, just north of the Arctic Circle, is billed as a flagship site for the British data centre developer, but construction is six months behind schedule, set back by leaks in the cooling system, local regulations, construction delays and a fire, Bloomberg News reported.

Bankers have faced a cocktail of risks which is threatening to stall the IPO market for deals outside of Anthropic after smart-ring maker Oura and Bamboo Insurance Services were forced to postpone debuts as interest rates climbed higher and the Iran war dragged on.
