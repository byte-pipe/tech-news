At least one person died and more than a dozen were injuredin a major Ukrainian drone strike yesterday on Saint Petersburg, Russia's former imperial capital. The attack targeted strategic sites, including a key port oil terminal. Over the weekend, Russian missile strikes in Ukraine killed at least 11 people. This conflict continues as President Trump says that relieving pressure on Russia is the best way to end the war.

 
* 🎧The recent attack on Saint Petersburg appears to mark the largestone on the city since Russia's 2022 invasion, NPR's Charles Maynes tellsUp First. While it's unclear how many drones struck the city, Russia's Defense Ministry reported intercepting 1,000 drones across various regions that night. Trump seems to be working to protect Russian oil facilities after cutting a deal with President Vladimir Putin to import Russian diesel. The move was aimed at lowering gas prices for U.S. voters before the midterm elections. Trump has called previous Ukrainian strikes a "disgrace," even as Russia continues to strike Ukraine, Maynes says. He adds that the president blames President Volodymyr Zelenskyy for attacks thatallegedly raise U.S. fuel prices, despite economists attributing the price increase mainly to Trump's war in Iran.
 

French President Emmanuel Macron denounced violence over the weekendthat he says has overshadowed largely peaceful protests across the country. The unrest began as a student protest for better conditions and has expanded to include public-sector workers protesting high energy prices, the rising cost of living and social inequality. The Interior Ministry reported about 2,300 arrests and nearly 1,000 injured police officers.

* 🎧NPR's Eleanor Beardsley, who was in the streets of Paris yesterday, witnessed burned bikes, shattered glass and graffiti on looted stores saying things like "tax the rich." She spoke with 23-year-old Sarah Berlia, who says she believes these actions come from a frustrated generation facing dwindling rights and opportunities. Beardsley says the protest culture in France often attracts "troublemakers," but the scale of current events is massive. The protests havehad political implications, as France is six months away from a presidential election. Far-left candidate Jean-Luc Mélenchon has joined the protesters, while far-right leader Marine Le Pen, who is leading in the polls, has condemned the violence and the government.

Living Better is aspecial seriesabout what it takes to stay healthy in America.

 

Saunas are gaining renewed attention in the wellness world for their potential to improve mental health. This trend reflects the growing interest in the broader wellness movement and social connection, especially post-pandemic. While scientists understand how saunas affect the cardiovascular system, they are just beginning to explore their impact on mental health. Here'swhat they knowso far.

 
* 🥵 When our skin detects heat, it sends signals to a primitive brain area that prompts our body to cool down. This pathway also influences mood and pleasure, which is associated with depression.
* 🥵 Heat triggers an anti-inflammatory response similar to the effects of exercise, which is known to help with depression and other mental health issues.
* 🥵 Heating people up can act like a "thermoregulatory workout," helping their bodies cool down more efficiently over time.
