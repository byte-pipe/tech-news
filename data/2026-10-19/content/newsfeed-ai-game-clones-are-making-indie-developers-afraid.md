Since its earliest days, the video game industryhas been builtondevelopers creating clonesofcompeting games, withvarying levels of originalityandvarying levels of enforcement from game platforms. Now, though, AI coding tools are making that cloning process so efficient that many developers are getting extremely cautious about sharing their early work too widely.

Developer Alliance Arts highlighted this trend over the weekend, delaying the launch of a planned public demo for its demon-themed Russian Roulette drinking gameDrunken Goddess Reflex, which had been set to go live withtoday’s launch of Steam Next Fest.

Ina post on social media Friday evening, the Alliance Arts team wrote that, in addition to concerns about managing streaming restrictions, “we’re concerned that the demo’s content could be used to quickly imitate or replicate the game’s presentation and gameplay with AI or other tools and that such works could be released before our launch.”

Instead, the developer now plans to share the demo only in “closed sessions” with those who agree to rules regarding posting and streaming gameplay.

Alliance Arts isn’t alone in taking a more defensive stance toward sharing ideas.Papers, Pleasedeveloper Lucas Popesaid on an April podcastthat, in general, “you don’t really talk about stuff when you’re working on it because I don’t know it’s going to get slurped up by AI or people are going to copy it or something else like that… I just didn’t feel as comfortable talking about the stuff I was working on again.”

## Whose game is it, anyway?

Concerns about ideas getting “slurped by AI” have been more than theoretical for a number of indie developers in recent months. In March, developer Freya Holmér attracted some viral social media attention aftersharing a video of a prototypeof aTetris-style game played on a central rotating pivot point. Days later, Holmér was confronted witha numberofvibe-coded copiesthat made the basic concept widely playable before Holmér could release her own version.

“Y’know it kinda disincentivizes me from sharing progress when there are slop ghouls around every corner, AI or otherwise,” Holmérwrotein response to the spread of those preemptive clones. “Luckily these people are incapable of original thought and don’t know how to elevate this concept beyond what they’ve already seen.”

McPixeldeveloper Sos Sosowskihas beendocumenting numerous examplesof fellow indie devs complaining of AI-generated clones making wholesale copies of their efforts. Sosowski says the situation puts indie developers in a hopeless bind when trying to promote their work: “Don’t share your game—no sales. Share your game—gets stolen,” he wrote. “How do you even survive as an indie in the current landscape? This is insane.”

Charlie Greenman, who posted one of those AI-powered clones of Holmér’s rotationalTetrisgame earlier this year,told 404 Mediathat he didn’t see anything wrong with what he did. “I feel like I had this brand new creation,” Greenman said. “When it gets to that point, is one song copying another? Is one game copying another? Whoever created Blox, Jenga, is that a copy ofTetris?”

Some think that added complexity and originality is the best true protection from blatant clones in this kind of environment. “If AI can copy your game overnight, it wasn’t a real game. It was just a quick cash grab built on a single mechanic, with no soul or vision,” Howltide Games’ Ivan Lagowrote on social media. “AI isn’t cloning actual games. It’s just making cheap knockoffs of stuff that was already an asset flip to begin with.”

But even if your game idea is cloned by AI slop merchants, that doesn’t mean you can’t build out the better version you always pictured. “In the very beginning, that first week, just every day there was a new AI clone [of the rotationalTetrisprototype]. I was like, OK, well fuck me, I guess,” Holmértold 404 Mediathis summer. “But there’s way less engagement [now], and I feel more in the clear to take my time to actually make something right, something good I can be proud of, and not just get it out there as soon as possible.”
