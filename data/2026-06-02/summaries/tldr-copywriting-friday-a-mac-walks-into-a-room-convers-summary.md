# Copywriting Friday: A Mac walks into a room – Summary  

## Core strategy: Talk about PC, not Mac  
- Apple’s “Get a Mac” campaign flipped the usual focus: instead of highlighting Mac’s virtues, it highlighted PC’s shortcomings.  
- The ads entered the conversation Windows users were already having in their heads, letting the audience compare themselves to the two characters.  
- This audience‑flip made the format simple: a Mac walks in, talks about the PC, and the comparison does the persuasive work.  

## Three techniques you can apply to your own marketing  

### 1. Personify the choice—and make your competitor likable  
- Each spot features two characters: a calm, capable Mac and a well‑meaning but slightly out‑of‑touch PC.  
- The competitor (PC) is portrayed as funny and sympathetic, not a straw man, allowing viewers to see themselves in the losing character without resentment.  
- Application tip: cast your brand and its main rival as distinct personalities (e.g., “the agency that always pitches” vs. “the team that ships”). Ensure the rival is honest and relatable.  

### 2. Let your competitor voice the objection  
- Objections are spoken by the PC, who then undermines his own credibility (e.g., promising Windows 7 will be problem‑free, then recalling past failures).  
- This technique surfaces hidden doubts and answers them from the competitor’s perspective, which feels more genuine than a self‑served testimonial.  
- Application tip: gather real customer concerns and let them be voiced (or quoted) as the “objection” that your solution then resolves.  

### 3. Trade abstractions for specifics  
- The ads avoid vague claims like “faster” or “more reliable.” Instead they dramatize concrete moments (e.g., MagSafe’s magnetic power cord snapping off harmlessly).  
- Specific, visual scenarios convey product benefits without needing technical jargon or price talk.  
- Application tip: illustrate a single, tangible feature or user experience in your copy rather than relying on broad adjectives.  

## Takeaway  
- The “Get a Mac” campaign succeeded by treating the competitor fairly, using the competitor’s voice to surface objections, and grounding the message in vivid, specific moments.  
- These principles can be replicated on landing pages, emails, or sales decks without a TV budget, simply by framing the conversation around the rival’s perspective and concrete experiences.
