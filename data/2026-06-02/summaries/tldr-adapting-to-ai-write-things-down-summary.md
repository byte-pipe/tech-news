# Summary of “Adapting to AI: Write Things Down”

## A Culture of Writing
- AI tools for software engineering depend on text; without written records, language models have nothing to process.  
- Organizations that institutionalize writing (e.g., Amazon’s six‑page memos, Oxide Computer’s Requests for Discussion) gain a competitive edge because clear writing forces rigorous thinking.  
- Jeff Bezos argues that memos are hard to produce but easy to consume, fostering truth‑seeking rather than sales‑oriented persuasion.  
- Jim Gray emphasized that documenting ideas enables them to grow and be shared widely.  
- I find that investing time in well‑crafted documents pays off when I revisit or distribute them, especially now that agents can amplify their reach.

## Not a Culture of Summary
- The writing culture I advocate is about canonical primary‑source documents (design specs, incident reports, executive memos), not AI‑generated summaries.  
- Such documents preserve the reasoning behind decisions, distinguishing intentional choices from accidental ones; non‑canonical writing adds noise.  
- Overreliance on AI‑produced summaries creates detailed but unfocused text that overwhelms readers and obscures strategic insight.  
- What we still need from humans: deciding what to build and evaluating its quality.

## Using AI For Writing
- I use AI as a research assistant: it enumerates pros/cons, spots subtle typos, and helps with fact‑finding.  
- I avoid letting AI write canonical content or summaries because I want to control detail, intent, and voice.  
- When I capture meeting notes and send them back for confirmation, the process multiplies value for both parties.  
- I believe companies that nurture genuine writing practices will outperform those that do not.

## Footnotes (Key Takeaways)
- Transcribing meetings can preserve valuable information, but some conversations may be better left informal.  
- Powerful political actors might avoid written records to evade accountability.  
- Amazon’s “Working Backwards” book provides deeper insight into its writing‑first culture.  
- Executive email memos serve as durable, clear guidance that shapes culture over years.  
- AI can surface patterns (e.g., linking data loss to throttling) but still requires human interpretation to confirm causality.
