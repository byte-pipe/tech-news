# AI Networking Will Ramp As Enterprise Wi-Fi Access Points Move Into the Wi‑Fi 8 Era

## Overview
- AI networking is expected to accelerate as enterprise Wi‑Fi access points adopt Wi‑Fi 8.
- New Wi‑Fi 8 system‑on‑chips will embed inference capabilities for better channel selection, device connectivity, and selective telemetry handling.
- At the Wi‑Fi NOW Conference (April 14‑16, 2026, Mountain View, CA), multiple vendors highlighted the role of AI SOCs in enterprise Wi‑Fi.

## Vendor Insights

### MediaTek – James Chen, Vice President, Wireless Business Unit
- Demonstrated Wi‑Fi 8 platform’s DSO capability, using an older Wi‑Fi 6 smartphone with an early Wi‑Fi 8 phone to achieve superior throughput.
- Emphasized that Wi‑Fi 8 is designed to coexist with legacy standards (Wi‑Fi 5/6) rather than replace them.
- MediaTek is accelerating its Wi‑Fi 8 shipment timeline and is engaging with enterprise Wi‑Fi vendors for collaboration.

### Cisco – Matt McPherson, Chief Technology Officer, Wireless
- AI inference in Wi‑Fi 8 access points will enable intelligent channel updates and client management while controlling telemetry flow.
- Centralized “data lakes” are unsustainable due to storage costs and processing latency at scale.
- Advocates “AI at the Edge”: APs pre‑process data locally (filter noise, detect anomalies) and transmit only relevant information such as security alerts or channel congestion reports to controllers/cloud.

### RUCKUS Networks – Anindya Chakraborty, Senior Vice President, R&D and Products
- The company is moving decisively into the AI era for its enterprise Wi‑Fi portfolio.
- Skeptical of a purely cloud‑based AI model; supports hybrid/distributed approaches where edge devices handle real‑time tasks (channel selection, interference mitigation) and the cloud focuses on long‑term trends and policy.
- Aligns with Cisco’s edge‑enhanced AI perspective, noting industry consensus that cloud‑only AI for Wi‑Fi operations is inefficient.

## 650 Group Perspective
- The 650 Group researches AI networking and asserts that the Wi‑Fi 8 generation will accelerate campus AI networking initiatives.
