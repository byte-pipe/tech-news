# 'Bereft and beleaguered' – Mandelson messages reveal criticism of No 10  

## Overview  
- Government released 1,500 pages of communications about Lord Mandelson’s appointment as UK ambassador to the US, a post he later lost due to links with Jeffrey Epstein.  
- The documents show frank criticism of Prime Minister Keir Starmer, Downing Street (No 10) and Labour MPs.  

## Main criticisms expressed in the messages  
- Mandelson describes No 10 as “beleaguered and bereft” and calls for a “complete revamp”.  
- He says senior advisers “don’t work as a team, they are not led and none of them really know what Keir thinks or wants”.  
- Mandelson and Pat McFadden discuss the government’s welfare legislation, noting a major Labour rebellion that forced the bill to be watered‑down.  
- Both express doubt that Starmer will survive the rebellion and that his authority is being eroded.  
- Mandelson complains that “the government doesn’t do policy… well enough” and repeats the “rubbish in, rubbish out” maxim.  

## Specific exchanges highlighted  
- **Pat McFadden (Cabinet Office → Work and Pensions Secretary)**  
  - Complains that backbenchers focus on “who can we tax to pay benefits to others”.  
  - Later claims his public stance is to ask “how can we change your life?” rather than entitlement questions.  
- **Mandelson ↔ McFadden (June 2025)**  
  - Discuss the £5 bn benefit cuts, the risk of losing the vote, and Starmer’s possible defeat.  
- **Mandelson ↔ Morgan McSweeney (Prime Minister’s chief of staff)**  
  - McSweeney expresses no faith in Starmer’s ability to change No 10; Mandelson notes his own waning confidence.  
- **Mandelson ↔ Torsten Bell (now Treasury minister)**  
  - Bell says policy is seen as “someone else’s job”; Mandelson replies with the “rubbish in, rubbish out” comment.  

## Reactions from political figures  
- **Pat McFadden’s spokesperson**: Emphasises McFadden’s public focus on improving lives and opportunities for young people.  
- **Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch**: Uses the leaks to claim Labour MPs treat taxes as “their money” and criticises the private comments as contradictory to public statements.  

## Publication details and cost  
- The documents were forced into publication after a parliamentary vote.  
- Cabinet Office minister Darren Jones told the Commons it was “one of the largest government publications” and cost over £1 million.  

## Context of Mandelson’s appointment  
- Starmer announced Mandelson’s ambassadorship on 20 December 2024.  
- In a handwritten note to then‑Foreign Secretary David Lammy (a month earlier), Mandelson pledged he would “make sure you never regret it” if appointed.  

## Overall implication  
- The released communications suggest deep internal dissent within Labour’s leadership, questioning Starmer’s authority, the coherence of No 10 staff, and the party’s handling of policy and welfare reforms.
