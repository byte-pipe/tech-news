# AMD extends Socket AM5 support through at least 2029; AM4 refuses to die

## Overview
- At Computex AMD announced new chips for both the current AM5 socket and the aging AM4 socket, emphasizing longer platform lifespans amid rising upgrade costs.
- The announcements reinforce AMD’s historical strategy of supporting sockets longer than Intel.

## AM4 Refresh
- Re‑launch of the 2022 Ryzen 7 5800X3D, labeled a “10th Anniversary Edition.”
- Features 64 MB of 3D V‑Cache, which benefits gaming more than general productivity.
- Slightly slower in non‑gaming tasks compared to standard 8‑core Ryzen 5000 CPUs.
- Release date: 25 June, MSRP $349 (cheaper than most used units).

## AM5 Roadmap Extension
- Officially extended support for AM5 to at least 2029 (originally slated for 2025, later “2027+”).
- Expect one or two additional full‑generation Ryzen releases compatible with existing AM5 boards.
- Upcoming Zen 6 CPUs will be supported, increasing maximum core count from 16 to 24.

## New AM5 CPU
- Ryzen 7 7700X3D announced:
  - 8 cores with 3D V‑Cache (64 MB).
  - Base/boost clock up to 4.5 GHz (lower than 7800X3D’s 5 GHz).
  - Launch: 16 July, MSRP $329 (about $50 less than 7800X3D, $110 less than 9800X3D).

## Implications for Builders
- Longer platform roadmap reduces need for full system replacements, offering more confidence when selecting AMD Ryzen CPUs, AM5 motherboards, and compatible memory.
- AMD’s commitment mirrors its earlier dedication to the AM4 platform.
