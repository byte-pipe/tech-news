# Owed a billion dollars in NVDA stock

## Early Days
- In September 1993 I was invited by Jensen Huang to join NVIDIA’s Technical Advisory Board and was granted 25,000 stock options that were to vest after one year, payable in quarterly installments.  
- The invitation followed a meeting on my houseboat with Jensen, Curtis Priem and Chris Malachowsky, after I had worked with Curtis on fast texture‑mapping techniques for his Sun SPARCstation GX chip.  
- My bi‑quadratic texture‑mapping implementation impressed Curtis; we demoed it on prototype NVIDIA hardware at the Guggenheim SoHo in 1993.  
- When the NV1 shipped in 1995 Microsoft’s DirectX supported only triangles, not quadratic mapping, which hurt NVIDIA financially and led to layoffs.  
- In April 1996, while I was living in Tonga, NVIDIA’s CFO wrote that 15,625 of my options had vested and required me to exercise them. I did so and then forgot about the grant.

## 30 Years Later
- In 2024 I revisited the original option agreement and realized it specified a **four‑quarter** vesting schedule, not a four‑year schedule as NVIDIA’s CFO and counsel had claimed.  
- The math: 15,625 of 25,000 shares = 62.5 %, which matches ten quarters of a four‑year schedule; under the one‑year schedule all 25,000 shares should have vested long before the 1996 letter.  
- After 480‑to‑1 stock splits, the unexercised 9,375 shares represent roughly **4.5 million** NVIDIA shares today.  
- I retained attorneys Allan Steyer and Chris Burke; after a year of letters citing case law, NVIDIA’s counsel (Cooley) acknowledged the agreement’s authenticity but argued the claim was time‑barred.  
- A settlement meeting ended with Cooley essentially saying “sue us.” Given the thirty‑year delay, the statute of limitations makes a successful claim unlikely.

## Lessons
- Monitor vesting schedules and exercise rights promptly; contractual obligations may only be enforceable for a limited period in the United States.  
- Even large, successful companies may rely on procedural defenses to avoid honoring long‑forgotten equity promises.  
- The story serves as a cautionary tale for anyone with dormant stock options; sharing similar experiences could shed light on how such disputes have been resolved.
