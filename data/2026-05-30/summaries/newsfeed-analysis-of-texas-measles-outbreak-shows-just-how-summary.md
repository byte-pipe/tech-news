# Analysis of Texas measles outbreak shows just how dangerous virus is

## Key points
- Anti‑vaccine rhetoric by figures such as Robert F. Kennedy Jr. has contributed to declining vaccination rates and the resurgence of measles in the United States.  
- A Morbidity and Mortality Weekly Report study examined the first three months of the 2025 Texas measles outbreak (Jan 20–Mar 18), focusing on 325 cases, of which 60 (18.5 %) were hospitalized.  
- All hospitalized patients were unvaccinated; 56 % were children under 5, 35 % were ages 5–17, and the remaining five were adults, four of whom were pregnant.  

## Hospitalization details
- 54 hospitalized patients provided detailed data.  
- Only six had underlying conditions; none were immunocompromised.  
- 47 (87 %) developed complications:  
  - Pneumonia: 39 (72 %)  
  - Dehydration: 25 (46 %)  
  - Diarrhea: 21 (39 %)  
  - Co‑infections with other pathogens: 17 (31.5 %)  
- 28 (52 %) received antibiotics.  
- 38 (70.4 %) required supplemental oxygen; 37 (68.5 %) experienced hypoxia.  
- Intensive care was needed for four children; two required intubation and mechanical ventilation.  
- One child died during the study period (a second child death occurred later, outside the study window).  

## Impact on pregnant women and infants
- Four pregnant women were hospitalized; two gave birth during admission.  
- Both newborns tested positive for measles; one later showed signs of acute measles meningoencephalitis and was hospitalized.  

## Conclusions drawn by researchers
- Approximately one in five confirmed measles cases required hospitalization for severe complications, including rare cases of life‑threatening illness or death.  
- Measles vaccination remains essential for preventing infection, severe disease, and hospital burden in both routine and outbreak settings.  

## National context (2025‑2026)
- The U.S. recorded 2,288 measles cases in 2025, the highest number since 1991.  
- By May 28, 2026, 1,983 cases had been reported across 40 jurisdictions, with 30 new outbreaks, putting the country at risk of losing its measles‑elimination status.
