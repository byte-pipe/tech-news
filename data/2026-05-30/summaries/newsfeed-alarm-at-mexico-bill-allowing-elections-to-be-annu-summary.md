# Alarm at Mexico bill allowing elections to be annulled for ‘foreign interference’

## Main development
- Mexico’s Senate approved a constitutional amendment that would allow election results to be annulled on the basis of “foreign interference”.
- The amendment defines foreign interference broadly, including illicit financing, propaganda, systematic dissemination of misinformation, digital manipulation, and intervention by foreign governments or agencies.

## Legislative process
- The proposal was presented by President Claudia Sheinbaum and has already passed the lower house of Congress.
- Ratification now requires a majority of the 32 states; the ruling Morena party controls 24 state governments.

## Criticism and concerns
- Opposition leaders and analysts say the vague wording could let virtually any foreign comment, article, or report be used to cancel an election.
- Former ambassador Arturo Sarukhan called the law “egregious” and a “veto over election outcomes”.
- Political analyst Carlos Bravo Regidor warned that a Morena‑aligned electoral court could readily accept interference claims.
- Opposition senator Ricardo Anaya described the amendment as a trap for Morena to annul any election it dislikes.

## Context
- The amendment comes amid increased U.S. pressure on Mexico’s security, including a recent U.S. indictment of officials from the state of Sinaloa for ties to a drug‑trafficking group.
- Sheinbaum emphasized Mexican sovereignty, stating that only Mexicans should decide who governs them.
- Midterm elections scheduled for next year could threaten Morena’s control of the presidency and both chambers of Congress.

## Implications
- If enacted, the electoral court could overturn election results when it determines foreign interference, but its independence was reduced under the previous administration.
- The amendment could provide the ruling party with a tool to maintain power by challenging unfavorable electoral outcomes.
