## Origins and rise
- Tulips arrived from the Ottoman Empire in the late 16th century; rare striped varieties became status symbols among the wealthy.  
- As Dutch maritime trade enriched the Republic, demand for exotic tulips grew, turning bulbs into speculative assets.  
- Futures contracts were traded in taverns and backrooms, allowing people to bet on bulbs they did not yet own.  
- The legendary *Semper Augustus* bulb was reputedly worth as much as a house.

## Bubble burst
- In February 1637, an auction in Haarlem failed to attract buyers, triggering panic.  
- Prices collapsed within days, ruining many investors who had pledged life savings.  
- Although the crash was dramatic, the broader Dutch economy did not collapse; the episode remained a cultural embarrassment.

## Legacy today
- The Netherlands remains the world’s largest producer and exporter of tulip bulbs, with famous fields in the Bollenstreek and the Keukenhof garden attracting millions of visitors each spring.  
- Tulips continue to be woven into Dutch culture through festivals, parades, and international trade, though now they are appreciated rather than used as speculative assets.
