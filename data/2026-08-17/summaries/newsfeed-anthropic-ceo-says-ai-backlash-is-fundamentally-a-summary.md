# Anthropic CEO says AI backlash is “fundamentally a crisis of trust”

## Main arguments
- Dario Amodei responded to investor Gavin Baker’s claim that his warnings have fueled a negative AI backlash and that he should adopt a more positive public stance.  
- Amodei argues his messaging is balanced—he has written major pieces on both AI risks and benefits, including the essay “Machines of Loving Grace.”  
- He acknowledges the public’s negative view of AI but attributes it to a broader “crisis of trust” in companies, governments, and the tech industry, not solely to leaders’ warnings.  
- The core criticism, according to Amodei, is that AI firms have not yet delivered on promised societal benefits (e.g., curing cancer), and real achievements would improve perception more than rhetoric.  

## Views on regulation
- Amodei rejects the notion of a false choice between unrestricted AI deployment and concentration of power through regulation.  
- He sees regulation as a tool that can curb corporate power and protect ordinary people, though he does not fully endorse every perspective.  
- Anthropic’s policy proposals aim to slow frontier AI companies while advantaging smaller competitors, reflecting the belief that AI inherently concentrates power.  

## Proposed “rules of the road”
- Effective regulation should simultaneously:
  1. Mitigate cyber, bio, and alignment risks of AI.  
  2. Institutionally limit the influence of leading AI firms.  
  3. Allow space for open‑weight models while managing their specific risks.
