# Australian hotel chain leaks guests’ PII after breach at third‑party database operator

## Incident overview
- Quest, an Australian aparthotel chain, announced a data‑security incident on 17 August 2026.  
- Unauthorized access was gained to a database operated by a third‑party service provider.  
- The breach was detected and contained by Quest on the same day.

## Exposed data
- Records date back to before June 2025.  
- Information disclosed includes:
  - Guest full name  
  - Email address and/or other contact details  
  - A small number of entries also contain date of birth  
- The data set enables potential identity‑fraud attempts.

## Company response
- Quest has:
  - Contacted all affected guests.  
  - Contained and fixed the vulnerable systems.  
  - Completed remediation work.  
  - Started forensic investigations.  
  - Engaged external cybersecurity and privacy advisers.  

## Impact and context
- Quest operates over 120 properties across Australia, New Zealand and Fiji.  
- Listings on travel sites such as Expedia, Wotif and Booking.com suggest overseas visitors may also be affected.  
- The breach adds to recent Australian incidents, e.g., Qantas data theft (6 million customers), MediSecure ransomware probe, and Medibank cyber‑attack.

## Open questions
- Identity of the third‑party provider responsible for the breach.  
- Exact cause of the vulnerability.  
- Total number of customers whose data was compromised.  
- How far back the leak extends beyond the “before June 2025” statement.  

The story is still developing, and further details may be provided as investigations continue.
