# I'm writing a history of Visual Basic, Chapter 1 is up

## Overview
- The author has launched a long‑form, book‑style history of Visual Basic on EvilGeniusLabs.ca.
- Chapter 1, titled **Origins (1964–1992)**, is now published and covers the early BASIC era through the launch of Visual Basic 1.0.
- The project aims to highlight the lineage of the language and the people behind it, rather than the usual focus on Gates, Allen, or pure technical analysis.

## Contents of Chapter 1
- **Six sequential articles** covering:
  1. The birth of BASIC at Dartmouth and its commercial spread before Microsoft (1964–1975).
  2. Microsoft’s BASIC dynasty from Altair to QBasic and the branding decision for “Visual Basic” (1975–1991).
  3. Alan Cooper’s Tripod project, the drag‑and‑drop Windows shell that became VB’s form designer (1985–1988).
  4. Project Thunder: internal codename, team members, and the pivot that turned a rejected tool into Visual Basic (1989–1991).
  5. Microsoft’s marketing narrative, including Gates’s 1989 BYTE article, the 1991 launch demo, and the $199 price point (1989–1991).
  6. The dual release of VB for Windows and VB for DOS, their divergent code bases, and why most retrospectives get this wrong (1991–1992).

## What Is Not Covered
- Detailed biographies of Bill Gates, Paul Allen, or Microsoft executives beyond what is needed to explain corporate decisions.
- The author states that existing works (e.g., *Hard Drive*, *Idea Man*, *Showstopper*) already cover those angles.
- Focus is instead on lesser‑known contributors such as Alan Cooper, Mark Merker, Gary Kratkin, Mike Geary, Frank Raab, Scott Ferguson, Greg Whitten, and others whose roles are often reduced to footnotes.
