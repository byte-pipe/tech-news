# Army parachutes onto remote island to help Briton with suspected hantavirus  

## Overview  
- British Army medics parachuted onto Tristan da Cunha, the world’s most remote inhabited overseas territory, to treat a British resident suspected of hantavirus infection.  
- The patient had left the cruise ship MV Hondius in mid‑April, developed symptoms two weeks later, and is now in stable condition while isolating.  

## Medical situation on the island  
- Six confirmed hantavirus cases have been reported from the MV Hondius, including two other Britons being treated abroad; three deaths have occurred.  
- The Andes strain of hantavirus, identified on the ship, can spread person‑to‑person.  
- Tristan da Cunha’s medical team normally consists of two staff; 3.3 tonnes of supplies and additional oxygen were airdropped to support the island’s hospital.  

## Details of the parachute operation  
- Six paratroopers and two clinicians from 16 Air Assault Brigade were deployed from an RAF A400M, supported by an RAF Voyager, after a stop at Ascension Island.  
- The jump was performed from about 5 km over the South Atlantic, with parachutists turning into strong winds (often >25 mph) and landing on the island’s golf course.  
- It was the first UK military parachute deployment of medical personnel for humanitarian aid.  
- The team will be extracted by ship once the medical situation allows.  

## Logistical challenges  
- Tristan da Cunha has a population of 221, no airstrip, and can normally be reached only by boat, making a rapid aerial delivery essential as oxygen supplies were critically low.  
- High winds and the island’s small size made the jump “really challenging, technical.”  

## Official reactions  
- Foreign Secretary Yvette Cooper emphasized the UK’s commitment to overseas territories and British nationals everywhere.  
- Minister for the Armed Forces Al Carns praised the personnel’s professionalism under pressure.  

## Wider context of the cruise‑ship outbreak  
- The MV Hondius arrived in Tenerife after a month at sea; over 100 passengers are being repatriated.  
- Two British passengers are self‑isolating in the UK after disembarking at St Helena; the remaining 22 will fly home from Tenerife and undergo a brief hospital isolation followed by a 42‑day self‑isolation period.  
- The UK Health Security Agency notes the risk to the general public remains very low.
