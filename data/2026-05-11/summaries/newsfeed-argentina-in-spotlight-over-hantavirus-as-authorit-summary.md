# Argentina in spotlight over hantavirus as authorities retrace footsteps of ship’s passengers

## Background
- Patagonia recorded the first documented person‑to‑person hantavirus transmission 30 years ago, previously thought to spread only via rodents.  
- A later outbreak in the same region confirmed inter‑human spread when an infected 68‑year‑old attended a birthday party, leading to 11 deaths.  

## Recent cruise outbreak
- Three passengers died on the Dutch cruise ship MV Hondius, which left Argentina for Cape Verde.  
- Surviving passengers tested positive for the Andes strain, the only hantavirus known to transmit between humans and found mainly in Argentina and Chile.  
- The WHO is investigating whether infection occurred before boarding; it has ruled out an epidemic.  

## Epidemiology in Argentina
- Since July 2023, 101 hantavirus cases and 32 deaths have been recorded; previous seasons showed lower numbers (e.g., 64 cases/14 deaths in 2024‑25).  
- Argentina reports roughly 100 cases annually, the highest in Latin America but far below Asian and European totals.  
- Fatality rates in the Americas can reach 50 %, compared with up to 15 % elsewhere.  

## Climate and global context
- Researchers link recent case increases to rodent behavior changes after a 2023‑24 drought followed by heavy rains, providing more food and habitat.  
- Global climate change may expand hantavirus risk to new regions.  
- The WHO states the overall risk to the general population is “absolutely low” because human‑to‑human transmission is rare.  

## Response measures
- Argentina’s health ministry will capture rodents along the travel route of the Dutch couple who first showed symptoms (multiple car trips in Argentina, Chile and Uruguay before boarding in Ushuaia on 1 April).  
- Authorities note no confirmed hantavirus cases in Tierra del Fuego, the ship’s departure province, in the past 30 years.  

## Political context
- WHO Director‑General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus urged President Javier Milei to reconsider Argentina’s withdrawal from the WHO, a move mirroring former US President Donald Trump’s stance.  
- Critics argue Milei’s broader cuts to science, education and healthcare undermine the country’s capacity to manage hantavirus and other health threats.  

## Outlook
- Argentine scientists emphasize that expertise to combat hantavirus exists; adequate investment and international cooperation are needed to address the rising risk linked to climate change and ecological imbalance.
