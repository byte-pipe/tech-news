# Copywriting Friday: Steve Jobs launches the iPhone

## Main takeaways
- The iPhone launch was a live “sales page” that demonstrates timeless copywriting tactics.
- Jobs used a withheld reveal, problem‑first selling, pre‑emptive objection handling, and benefit‑focused specs to keep the audience engaged and convinced.

## Techniques illustrated

### Withheld reveal & building anticipation
- Opened with a promise (“a revolutionary product…”) instead of the product itself.  
- Repeated a three‑part description (iPod, phone, internet device) to misdirect and heighten impact.  
- Showed a quick glimpse of the prototype, then tucked it away, creating a tease that made the audience lean in.

### Problem‑first selling
- Identified the “enemy”: existing smartphones with plastic keyboards.  
- Highlighted the pain points (unnecessary keyboards, clunky interfaces) to amplify audience frustration.  
- Positioned the iPhone as the inevitable solution to that problem.

### Answering objections before they arise
- Anticipated the stylus objection (“Who wants a stylus?”) and dismissed it with a relatable “Yuck.”  
- Followed with the answer: multi‑touch finger input, turning the objection into a selling point.

### Turning specs into reasons to care
- Paired every technical detail with a clear benefit (e.g., proximity sensor → turns off display during calls).  
- Used precise numbers instead of vague superlatives (160 ppi screen, 11.6 mm thickness, 100 million iPods sold).  
- Demonstrated that specific data acts as proof of a promise, not just a feature list.

## How to apply these lessons
- **Delay the main offer**: start with a compelling promise or problem to build curiosity.  
- **Name the competitor’s flaw**: make the audience feel the pain before presenting your solution.  
- **Pre‑empt objections**: voice the likely concerns and address them directly in your copy.  
- **Link every feature to a benefit**: explain why each spec matters to the reader, using concrete numbers where possible.  

By mirroring Jobs’s stage tactics on landing pages, launch emails, or ads, marketers can create more persuasive, conversion‑focused content.
