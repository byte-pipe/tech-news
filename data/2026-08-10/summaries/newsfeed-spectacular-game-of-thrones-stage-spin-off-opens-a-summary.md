# Spectacular Game of Thrones stage spin‑off opens at home of Shakespeare

## Overview
- The Royal Shakespeare Company launched **Game of Thrones: The Mad King**, the first stage spin‑off of the franchise, at Stratford‑upon‑Avon.
- The play is a prequel set shortly before the events of George R.R. Martin’s first novel and the TV series.
- Runtime: 3 hours 45 minutes; cast of 36 actors.
- Features younger versions of familiar characters (Ned Stark, Jaime Lannister, Robert Baratheon) and key figures such as Lyanna Stark, Prince Rhaegar Targaryen, and the “mad king” Aerys II Targaryen.

## Critical reception
- **The Telegraph** – 4 stars; called it a “spellbinding spectacle” but noted the absence of the TV series’ grit, gore and nudity.
- **The Guardian** – 4 stars; praised spectacular stagecraft and modern resonance; highlighted that devoted fans may rate it higher than newcomers.
- **Metro** – 4 stars; regarded it as a solid extension of the Game of Thrones world.
- **The Times** – 3 stars; criticized slow pacing, lack of poetry in the script, and overly shouted lines.
- **What’s On Stage** – 5 stars; described the production as a “triumph” with superb direction and ensemble performance.
- **The Independent** – 4 stars; highlighted thrilling battles, dragons, and an unexpected amount of emotional heart.

## Production details
- Development took seven years; written by Duncan Macmillan.
- Directed by Dominic Cooke; Michael Shaeffer stars as King Aerys II.
- Intended to match the scale of a Shakespearean tragedy and history play.
- George R.R. Martin attended the opening night and kept a representative in rehearsals, offering collaborative support on the script.

## Shakespeare connection
- Martin’s novels were heavily inspired by Shakespeare’s history plays about the Wars of the Roses.
- RSC co‑artistic director Tamara Harvey emphasized shared themes: power, succession, dynastic struggle, and intergenerational conflict.

## Future prospects
- The RSC hopes the play can achieve runs comparable to “Harry Potter and the Cursed Child” and “Stranger Things: The First Shadow”.
- Plans to expand beyond Stratford to reach broader audiences.
