# RAF Fairford: 'Quantity of petrol' but no explosives found in three vehicles - BBC News

# Summary of RAF Fairford incident

## Key events
- Three vans were spotted near RAF Fairford in Whelford early Sunday; a member of the public reported them to 999.
- Police found a quantity of petrol in the vehicles but no explosive devices.
- Five men (UK nationals, ages 23‑25) were arrested on suspicion of explosives and terrorism offences; they were released on bail the next day as an “investigative decision.”
- A 400 m cordon was set up, bomb disposal team deployed, and 85 nearby homes were evacuated; the cordon was lifted on Wednesday and residents returned.

## Police and investigative actions
- Counter‑terrorism policing head Laurence Taylor said the response was based on “a range of information.”
- Searches of the five suspects’ homes in Westminster and Camden have been completed.
- Bail conditions include strict limits on movement and contact.
- Officers are pursuing multiple lines of inquiry, including possible involvement of proxies or foreign actors.

## Political and diplomatic reactions
- US Secretary of State Marco Rubio suggested a “foreign actor” was involved, without presenting evidence.
- US President Donald Trump expressed surprise that the men were released on bail.
- Prime Minister Andy Burnham chaired a Cobra meeting, urging the public not to speculate while facts are established.
- Iran’s London embassy condemned “unfounded and malicious speculations” linking Iran to the incident; earlier, Iran had warned that any base used for attacks against it could be a legitimate target.

## Context
- RAF Fairford serves as a forward operating location for the US Air Force and has recently been used for defensive strikes on Iran.
- The incident occurred amid heightened tensions after the UK approved US use of the base for operations against Iran.
