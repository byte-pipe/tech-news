# DraftKings Is Using AI to Supercharge the Harms of Online Behavioral Advertising

## Overview
- DraftKings employs AI‑driven online behavioral advertising to target customers who are most likely to lose bets.
- The Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF) argues that all behavioral advertising should be banned because of its harmful effects.

## How DraftKings Uses AI
- Betting records are fed into a machine‑learning model that identifies “losing gamblers.”
- Identified users receive personalized promotions designed to lure them back to place more losing bets.
- The company profits from keeping these problem gamblers engaged, rather than mitigating their risk.

## Why AI Amplifies Harm
- AI requires large, detailed data sets, prompting companies to collect ever more user information.
- The black‑box nature of AI makes it difficult to know which data points are most influential, driving continuous data harvesting.
- Faster processing of massive data sets enables more precise and aggressive targeting.

## Broader Consequences
- Data gathered for ad targeting is sold to insurance firms, banks, and law‑enforcement agencies (e.g., CBP, ICE).
- ICE has issued a Request for Information to understand how commercial big‑data and ad‑tech providers can support investigations.
- DraftKings relies only on “first‑party data,” showing that limiting third‑party data sharing would not stop predatory ads.

## Policy Implications
- Banning third‑party data sharing alone is insufficient; a comprehensive ban on online behavioral advertising is needed.
- Removing the ability to send personalized ads would reduce incentives to collect the underlying behavioral data.

## Recommendations and Resources
- EFF’s Surveillance Self‑Defense project provides tools and guidance for protecting personal data on mobile apps and websites.
- Individuals can use EFF’s tips to limit data collection and mitigate exposure to targeted gambling promotions.
