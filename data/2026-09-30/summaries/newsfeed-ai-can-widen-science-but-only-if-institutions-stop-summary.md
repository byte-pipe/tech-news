# Summary of “AI can widen science — but only if institutions stop rewarding the already measurable”

## Key Findings
- Researchers who use AI tools publish roughly three times more papers and receive about five times more citations than peers who do not.
- AI‑assisted work covers 4.6 % less topical ground than non‑AI work, a pattern seen in over 70 % of examined sub‑fields.
- The narrowing of research topics is driven by institutional incentives, not by the technology itself.
- Over the past six decades, papers and patents have become less disruptive; AI accelerates this trend by making it cheaper and faster to extend familiar lines of inquiry.

## Problems Identified
- Funding systems favor downstream exploitation of existing data rather than the creation of new datasets and measurement capabilities.
- The cost of generating predictions with AI models has fallen dramatically, while building new observational infrastructure remains expensive and slow.
- Universities and funding agencies penalize researchers who pivot to new fields, even though AI reduces the informational cost of such moves.
- Evaluation criteria (continuous publication record in a single domain, prerequisite preliminary data) discourage interdisciplinary exploration.

## Recommendations
- **Make new terrain measurable**: Fund and subsidize data‑infrastructure projects, especially in neglected areas (e.g., under‑studied diseases), to enable AI to work with novel observations.
- **Stop punishing pivots**: Revise hiring and funding evaluation practices to reward interdisciplinary shifts and the use of AI for entering new fields.
- **Support long‑term, unglamorous research**: Recognize and finance longitudinal cohort studies, biodiversity‑monitoring programmes, and other costly but essential data‑generation efforts.
