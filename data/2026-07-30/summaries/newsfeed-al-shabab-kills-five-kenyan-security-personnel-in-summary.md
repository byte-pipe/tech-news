# Al‑Shabab kills five Kenyan security personnel in ambush  

## Main incident  
- Five members of Kenya’s Special Operations Group were killed on patrol near Wantey Dam, about 6 km from Lafey police station in Mandera county, close to the Somalia border.  
- A reinforcement team sent to the scene was hit by a roadside bomb that destroyed an armoured vehicle; no casualties were reported among the reinforcements.  
- Al‑Shabab claimed responsibility through an affiliated media outlet, attributing the attack to its armed wing.  

## Recent security context in Mandera  
- The attack follows a series of violent incidents in the same stretch of Mandera in recent weeks.  
- On 13 July, Kenyan forces raided a suspected camp near the border, killing 11 al‑Shabab fighters and thwarting a planned attack on a nearby village.  
- On 24 July, gunmen struck the villages of Fino and Arabia in the district within an hour of each other.  

## Broader background on al‑Shabab and Kenya  
- Al‑Shabab, linked to al‑Qaeda, controls large parts of south‑central Somalia and has been fighting the Somali government for nearly two decades.  
- Kenya has been involved in the conflict since 2011, when Kenyan troops entered Somalia and began cooperating with Somali authorities to contain the group.  
- The group has repeatedly carried out cross‑border attacks in Kenya and Ethiopia as part of a strategy to pressure Kenya to withdraw its troops from Somalia.  

## Regional security trends  
- A parliamentary report (Nov 2024) identified the Lafey corridor as a primary infiltration route for militants and recorded dozens of security force casualties in the wider region over the previous year.  
- Between Sep 2024 and Aug 2025, Kenya logged 61 terror‑related incidents in Mandera, Wajir, Garissa and Lamu counties; Mandera accounted for 38 of them, making it the hardest‑hit county.  

## Strategic implications  
- The attack occurs ahead of a summit of heads of state from countries contributing troops to the African Union Mission in Somalia (AU SM), where the future of the peacekeeping force will be discussed.  
- The United States announced in early July that it will end logistics support for AU SM, raising concerns about the mission’s viability and potentially affecting the regional balance of power.
