# AI Is Not Replacing Marketing. It’s Crowding the Room.

## Main Argument
- AI is adding layers to the marketing mix rather than eliminating existing channels.  
- The useful question is “What role does each channel play now?” instead of “Which channel will die?”

## Why the “Dead Channel” Story Fails
- Marketing often declares channels dead (TV, radio, SEO, email, etc.) but habits change slowly.  
- Adoption depends on age, income, trust, convenience, and confidence, so older channels persist alongside new ones.  
- New platforms usually add a path rather than remove the old one.

## Media‑Habits Shifts in One Year
- Television: 91 % of Australian adults used online video in 2025, yet 52 % still watched free‑to‑air TV, showing coexistence of streaming and traditional broadcast.  
- Streaming fragmented viewing but did not erase TV.

## Audio Landscape Mirrors the Same Pattern
- In Q4 2025, U.S. ad‑supported audio time was split as follows: radio 61 %, podcasts 21 %, streaming music 15 %, satellite radio 3 %.  
- Each format serves distinct purposes (commuting/local info, depth/expertise, personalization, discovery).

## News Consumption Has No Single Door
- 2025 Australian Digital News Report: TV remained the top news source (37 %), social media overtook online news sites for the first time (26 % vs 23 %).  
- Audiences now blend TV, TikTok, direct site visits, creator content, podcasts, group chats, and informal sources.

## AI Adoption Is Growing but Uneven
- February 2026: 49 % of U.S. adults used AI chatbots (up from 33 % in 2024).  
- Users under 50 are roughly twice as likely to use ChatGPT as older adults.  
- People continue to use Google, websites, email, social media, and AI in parallel when making decisions.

## Websites Are Not Dead – Weak Websites Are
- AI may reduce traffic to generic pages, but businesses still need sites to verify offers, view pricing, read reviews, compare options, and complete purchases.  
- Search remains dominant (Google 86.67 % market share in June 2026).  
- Adobe Analytics (May 2026) shows shoppers arriving via large language models spend more time and generate higher revenue on retail sites.

## The Real Issue: Channel Proliferation
- Marketers are expected to manage an ever‑expanding set of channels (search, paid ads, social platforms, email, video, influencers, PR, podcasts, review sites, AI assistants, events, offline).  
- Trying to test everything is panic, not strategy.  
- Effective work means defining each channel’s job and investing based on audience, business model, and customer journey.

## A Better Model: Layered Marketing
1. **Home Base – Control Hub**  
   - A strong website remains the primary asset a business controls; it houses the message, proof points, demand capture, and conversion pathways.  
2. **Discovery – First Notice**  
   - Brands are found via search, AI, social, video, creators, PR, and partnerships.  
   - One high‑quality piece of content can serve multiple discovery channels (rank in search, be cited by AI, fuel social posts, support video and email).  
3. **Trust – Turning Attention into Belief**  
   - Trust requires evidence, clear explanations, real examples, and a distinct point of view.  
   - As AI makes generic content easy, credible, nuanced content becomes the differentiator.  

By assigning purpose to each channel—control, discovery, trust—marketers can navigate the crowded landscape without assuming any channel will disappear.
