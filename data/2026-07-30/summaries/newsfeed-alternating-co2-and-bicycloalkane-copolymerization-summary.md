# Alternating CO₂ and Bicycloalkane Copolymerization to Circular Polyesters

## Abstract Summary
- Direct alternating copolymerization of CO₂ with bicycloalkane monomers (bicyclic butane, BCB; bicyclic pentane, BCP) is achieved using a simple organic catalyst.  
- The process yields perfectly alternating, high‑molar‑mass polyesters with a maximum of 50 mol % CO₂ incorporation.  
- The in‑chain ring structures impart tunable thermal and mechanical properties, enabling high‑performance, recyclable polyesters.  

## Key Findings
- **Thermodynamic breakthrough**: Overcomes the infeasibility of CO₂/alkene alternating copolymerization by employing strained bicycloalkanes as comonomers.  
- **Polymer architecture**: Architecturally defined backbones with alternating CO₂ and bicycloalkane units; precise control over composition and molecular weight.  
- **Material properties**:  
  - BCB‑CO₂ polyesters exhibit exceptional thermal stability and hydrolytic resistance across the full pH range.  
  - BCP‑CO₂ polyesters can be tailored to different mechanical profiles.  
- **Selective depolymerization**:  
  - BCB‑based polyesters depolymerize in bulk under basic conditions, regenerating pure BCB monomer with >90 % isolated yield.  
  - BCP‑based polyesters depolymerize to bicyclolactones, enabling monomer recovery.  

## Circular Lifecycle
- Demonstrated sequential depolymerization–repolymerization cycles, establishing a closed‑loop process for both BCB‑ and BCP‑derived polyesters.  
- The approach provides a sustainable route from CO₂ to high‑performance materials and back to the original monomers, supporting circular economy goals.
