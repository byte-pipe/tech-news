# Social care reform: The families hoping for change – BBC News

## Government proposals
- Andy Burnham (Mayor of Manchester) pledges to reform adult social care in England, seeking cross‑party agreement with Conservatives and Liberal Democrats.  
- Plans include:
  - Advancing an independent review of care (Baroness Casey) to be published in 2027.  
  - Major reforms to staff pay and conditions.  
  - Creation of a national care service.  
- The prime minister warns that “difficult decisions” on taxation will be needed; in an ideal world no one should have to sell their home for care costs.  

## Emma Riley – caring for her mother with dementia
- Dental nurse from Manchester caring for mother Carol since her father’s death 18 months ago.  
- Pays £34 per hour for three weekly visits from a private care company – a “huge chunk of money”.  
- Has applied to the local council for funding; after six months there is still no decision, blamed on social‑worker caseloads.  
- Calls for clearer communication and guidance through the system.  
- Says Burnham’s announcement leaves her “quietly confident” that needed changes will come.  

## Lisa Greenwood – the cost of respite care
- Lives in Halifax, West Yorkshire; caring for 90‑year‑old mother Mavis who is in local respite care.  
- Weekly cost of £1,305 is unsustainable; family may have to sell the house.  
- Supports Burnham’s proposal to raise care‑sector wages to attract more staff.  
- Highlights the stress of balancing work, bills, and caring responsibilities.  

## Sophie Daniels – lack of support during her father’s illness
- From Swindon, Wiltshire; cared for father with stage‑4 glioblastoma until his death in 2024.  
- Describes social‑care provision as “incredibly limited” and says the family was left to “figure things out” themselves.  
- Charities provided some help, but the family had to manage without a lifting service, leading to dangerous delays when her father fell.  

## Steve West – need for a unified system
- Former caregiver from Wakefield, West Yorkshire; his mother died after years in a care home.  
- Frustrated by repeated issues across successive governments.  
- Believes a national care service could give focus, but its remit must be clear.  
- Main challenges were knowing who to contact and dealing with multiple health bodies; a single, unified system would be a “massive help”.  
- Suggests paying family carers at rates comparable to private providers to reduce the need to cut work hours.  

## Jeanette Barry – financial strain and loss of faith
- From Essex; husband John, a former policeman, lived with dementia from 2018 until his death in 2024.  
- Paid £2,000 per month in care‑home fees from his police pension plus local‑authority funding.  
- Was told she might need to remortgage the house to keep him in the home, though later reassessed for more support.  
- Describes the period as “horrible, terrible” and expresses little confidence in politicians, but slightly more trust in Burnham because of his personal experience with dementia.  

## Common themes across families
- **Financial pressure:** high private or residential care costs threaten home ownership and basic living expenses.  
- **Staffing and pay:** low wages deter recruitment; families welcome proposals to raise pay.  
- **System navigation:** lack of clear communication, long waiting times for assessments, and multiple agencies create confusion and stress.  
- **Need for reform:** families see Burnham’s proposals as a potential step forward but stress that funding mechanisms must be realistic and sustainable.
