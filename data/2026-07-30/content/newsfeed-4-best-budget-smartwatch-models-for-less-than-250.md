Like many consumergadgets and wearables, smartwatches have gotten more expensivein recent months, thanks in part to rising component costs and supply chain pressures. But demand remains high, and I don’t expect that to change anytime soon. Wearables have become extensions of ourselves, much like our phones. We use them as second notification hubs, health and fitness trackers, and, of course, sometimes just to tell the time.

But not everyone can (or should) spend half a month’s rent on a smartwatch. In this economy? That’s why I’ve rounded up the best budget smartwatches, all of which cost $250 or less. My picks, including my top choice—theNothing CMF Watch 3 Pro ($79)—prove that you don’t have to sacrifice performance just because you’re on a budget.

Nothing

Nothing is known for the affordability and retro charm ofits headphonesandphones, so it was unsurprising to discover that the CMF Watch 3 Pro is both fun to use and very affordable. (CMF is a sub-brandof Nothing.)

Why WIRED recommends:This is a feature-packed smartwatch, with wrist-based gesture controls, an onboard microphone for voice calls, a ChatGPT-integrated voice assistant, and a built-in flashlight. Weighing only 51.9 grams (1.8 ounces), it’s super light, but the 1.43-inch AMOLED display is large and bright with more than 120 vibrant watch faces to choose from. The CMF Watch 3 Pro syncs with the Nothing X app on both iOS and Android, and battery life lasts about 13 days.

The suite of health features is pretty comprehensive. Its optical heart rate sensor is accurate; former editorAdrienne Sotallied the live readings on the Watch 3 Pro with herOura Ring 4,and they almost always matched exactly. It also has dual-band GPS tracking for more accurate outdoor tracking, detailed workout summaries, and fun animations that appear when you’re measuring your heart rate or doing breathing exercises.

The trade-offs:It has a more utilitarian look thanmost smartwatches. The case is made from precision-milled metal, but it looks and feels more like plastic. The app can be a little wonky, the running coach is less flexible than those on otherfitness trackers, and the watch occasionally overestimates sleep by as much as an hour compared with othersleep trackers.

If you have an iPhone, theApple Watch SE (3rd Gen)is the best value in Apple’s lineup. It’s adecent upgradeif you’re coming from an older model and also makes a great smartwatchfor your parents or kids.

Why WIRED recommends:It has the latest S10 chip, which enables many of the standout health features the previous Apple Watch SE 2 lacked, such as sleep apnea notifications and skin temperature sensing. It runs watchOS 27, so you can view the dynamic app grid with Siri-suggested apps, converse with the newSiri AIassistant, and use Workout Buddy without your iPhone nearby. It has fast charging, and even though it doesn’t have the Apple Watch Series 11’s 24-hour battery life, former editorAdrienne Sostill managed to get a full day of battery life out of it, even with the always-on display enabled.

The trade-offs:It can’t measure blood oxygen, doesn’t support the ECG app, and lacks the latest hypertension notifications. The screen is only a 1,000-nit display compared with the 2,000-nit display on the Series 11, and it doesn’t have the UWB locator chip. The bezels around the screen are chunkier, and battery life is also restricted to the usual 18 hours.

TheGarmin Forerunner 165is an excellent running watch for beginners and anyone who wants plenty of accurate data without paying for a more advanced model.

Why WIRED recommends:The Forerunner 165 has a bright AMOLED display, with both a touchscreen and physical buttons for two different ways to navigate. It has accurate multi-band GPS and a suite of useful sensors, including a pulse oximeter for measuring blood oxygen, a barometric altimeter, a compass, and an ambient light sensor. Alongside personalized training plans, you can view metrics like running power and cadence, as well as proprietary metrics such asTraining Effect. Battery life is advertised as 11 days, but former editorAdrienne Sogot around a week, which is still pretty decent for a smartwatch.

The trade-offs:The Forerunner 165 launched in February 2024, so it’s not Garmin’s latest or greatest running watch. It’s also only water-resistant up to 50 meters.

If the idea of owning aGoogle Pixel Watch 4is out of your budget, but you still want a Google Fitbit-centric experience, the Versa 4 is the one to go for.

Why WIRED recommends:You get a smartwatch with a gently curved design that’s almost identical to the original Versa but with the welcome return of a physical button. The 1.58-inch AMOLED display touchscreen is large and vibrant, and battery life lasts up to six days.

As for fitness tracking, the Versa 4 is equipped with a built-in GPS to track outdoor workouts and motion sensors to log indoor swims. Google Health Coach offers AI-powered coaching and insights that provide a holistic view of your health and wellness. Lastly, you get access to a suite of Google apps, including Amazon Alexa, Google Maps, and contactless payments with Google Wallet.

The trade-offs:Automatic activity tracking supports only 40 exercise modes, which is fewer than many other fitness trackers. There are no third-party apps or music controls. Voice and texting features are limited to Android users. Finally, it’s water-resistant only up to 50 meters.

## Honorable Mentions

Amazfit Bip 6 for $79:Former editorAdrienne Soliked the Bip 6 for its aesthetic, responsive AMOLED screen, week-long battery life, and 140 sport modes. She did find its fitness-tracking capabilities and the Zepp app to be occasionally inaccurate. If you’re willing tospend $20 more, get theAmazfit Active 2instead.

Google Fitbit Charge 6 for $160:TheFitbit Charge 6remains one of the best entry-level fitness trackers, packed with just enough features to build healthy habits without overwhelming you with data. Its heart rate tracking held up well against more expensive devices in testing, and it can even connect to compatible gym equipment, including Peloton bikes and NordicTrack treadmills. The Charge 6 is now a couple of years old—it launched in 2023—and the Fitbit Air offers most of these features at a lower price point. But if you’re looking for a fitness tracker with a screen, consider the Charge 6. If you don’t care for a built-in GPS, theFitbit Inspire 3costs less than $100 and is just as accurate.

Samsung Galaxy Watch FE for $226:The Galaxy Watch FE is a fine budget Wear OS smartwatch. The 40-mm model has a smaller screen that doesn’t get as bright, and it’s equipped with a slower processor and a smaller battery compared with the Watch7 series. The battery lasts just about a day, if not a little less. The health sensors are almost the same, and Gear editorJulian Chokkattugot accurate results for heart rate and sleep tracking.

Photograph: Julian Chokkattu

Casio Pro Trek PRG-340B for $221:EditorJulian Chokkattuenjoyed wearing this Casio watch. The LCD is highly visible, with an auto-LED function that lights up the screen when you tilt the watch. Despite being designed for the outdoors—it’s a Tough Solar watch—it looks smart with a range of outfits and works well for various occasions. Julian primarily relied on the timekeeping and the stopwatch during workouts, but you can also monitor barometric pressure, altitude, and temperature, plus check the compass to get your bearings. That said, it doesn’t do heart rate and notifications.

Casio CA53W-1CR Calculator Watch for $31:This cheap calculator watch from Casio is a classic—it’s comfy and lightweight—and you can cycle through a few modes, including a stopwatch, an alarm, and a second time zone. There’s a five-year battery life, and the fully automatic calendar goes through the year 2099 (that’s 15 battery changes).

Apple Watch Series 10 for $371:WithwatchOS 27, the 2024Series 10has many of the same features as the new Series 11, like hypertension notifications and Sleep Score. Avoid paying anywhere close to retail price for this watch. If you can find it for under $300, snag it.

Google Pixel Watch 3 for $300:ThePixel Watch 3is seeing some steep discounts now that the Pixel Watch 4 is here. It’s an excellent smartwatch, with the caveat that it’s not repairable. (The newer model addresses that.) Gear editorJulian Chokkatturecommends the 45-mm model, which offers a better battery life—just about 24 hours with the always-on display enabled. Still, avoid paying anywhere close to MSRP.
