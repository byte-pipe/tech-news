description: 'I''ve posted about comparison hubs before. But everyone kept asking the same question: "Okay, but what actually goes on the pages?" So here you go: Most companies get comparison content wrong because they build one random page and hope it works. A real comparison hub is a system - a category-level page that feeds individual decision-level pages underneath it. Page 1: The Hub. This is your category capture. → H1: "Compare [You] to every [category]" → A positioning map showing where you sit → A grid of comparison cards - one per competitor → Filters by segment if you have many → Links to your alternatives pages as a bonus layer → One clear CTA This page tells Google, LLMs, and buyers: "We''re not afraid of the comparison. Here''s the whole picture." Page 2: The Comparison Page. This is your decision capture. → H1 "[You] vs [Competitor]" → TL;DR verdict box (the snippet LLMs pull) → Honest intro that acknowledges where they''re strong → Side-by-side table: features, pricing, integrations,
  support → Where you win, specific, not "everything." → Where they win. Yes, really. Credibility is the whole point. → Real screenshots of both products → A customer quote, ideally someone who switched → FAQ ("Is [X] better?" "Can I migrate?") → Clear CTA - they''re at peak intent → Clean, evergreen URL The golden rule that ties it all together: The hub captures the category, each page captures the decision. Be honest on both, control the narrative without spinning it. And here''s why this is the best asset we build all year: → It''s your best organic BOFU play → It''s an AEO asset LLMs cite constantly → It''s a paid landing page for competitor keyword ads → One build, three channels, direct pipeline. Your to-dos: 1. Save this post. 2. Send it to whoever owns your website. 3. Build the comparison hub by the end of Q3. Your competitors are already being compared to you. The only question is who''s writing the page. | 28 comments on LinkedIn'
