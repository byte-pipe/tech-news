## Abstract

Transforming abundant but inert CO2into useful polymers has been pursued since the 1960s (ref.1) and has typically been achieved by copolymerization with a reactive comonomer, aided by a catalyst, which can address both thermodynamic constraints and high kinetic barriers associated with CO2fixation and incorporation2,3,4,5. However, making polyesters remains a challenge as alternating copolymerization of CO2with alkenes is thermodynamically infeasible6. Here we introduce a closed-loop CO2-based polyester platform for producing high-performance yet recyclable polyesters by direct alternating copolymerization of CO2with bicycloalkanes, bicyclic butane (BCB) and pentane (BCP) monomers. This copolymerization is initiated by a simple organic catalyst and proceeds in a perfectly alternating fashion to high-molar-mass polyesters with maximum (50 mol%) CO2incorporation and architecturally defined backbones, in which the in-chain ring structure enables tailorable thermal and mechanical properties. These polyesters exhibit desired orthogonal performance and end-of-life outcomes. Although the BCB-CO2polyesters exhibit exceptional thermal and hydrolytic stability across the full pH range, they can be selectively depolymerized in bulk and base-catalysed conditions to regenerate pure BCB monomers in >90% isolated yield. The BCP-CO2polyesters can also be selectively depolymerized but to bicyclolactones. Sequential depolymerization–repolymerization cycles establish circular lifecycles for BCB/BCP-CO2high-performance polyesters.

### Extended Data Fig. 2 Proposed depolymerization mechanism and control reactions.

a, Postulated selective depolymerization of P(BCBS-alt-CO2) to selectively regenerate monomer BCBSand comonomer CO2.b, Designed small molecule control reaction to support the proposed depolymerization mechanism.c, Depolymerization reaction control in the absence of the NaOH catalyst, yielding no monomer formation.

### Extended Data Fig. 3 Reaction energy diagram for the two-step unzipping of P(BCBS-alt-CO2) to form CO2and BCBS, computed using DFT.

The enthalpies (ΔH) and free energies (ΔG) of unzipping intermediates (UZ) along the reaction coordinate are estimated at a temperature of 280 °C and plotted in blue and black colours, respectively. Transition states are delineated with the “-TS” suffix. Unless otherwise noted, all thermodynamic quantities (ΔHand ΔG) are calculated at 1 M in the solution-phase. The products, CO2and BCBS, are assumed to be withdrawn in the gas-phase under a vacuum of 100 mTorr; their reference states are noted beside their structures and their thermodynamic quantities are adjusted accordingly. Note that a single resonance structure is shown for the following intermediates: UZ-2, UZ-3, UZ-4, and UZ-5 and associated transition states; however, as per partial charges determined using DFT, the negative charge is delocalized in each of these intermediates.

### Extended Data Fig. 4 Demonstration of chemical circularity for P(BCPR-alt-CO2).

a, Selective depolymerization of cyclic polyesters P(BCPMe-alt-CO2) and P(BCPEt-alt-CO2) using NaOH as the catalyst (5 wt%) at 180 °C to generate bicyclolactone monomers.b, Re-polymerization of the recycled bicyclolactone monomer to reform polyester P(BCPMe-alt-CO2).c, Re-depolymerization of the reformed P(BCPMe-alt-CO2) using catalyst NaOH (5 wt%) at 180 °C to regenerate the bicyclolactone monomer.d, Proposed depolymerization pathway for the NaOH-catalyzed depolymerization of cyclic polyester P(BCPR-alt-CO2). Shown here is the postulated main pathway via random backbiting mechanism, while other possible pathways are not depicted here.
