# Google, OpenAI, Anthropic Plan Frontier AI Standards Body

Proposed Independent Group Would Set Common Benchmarks and Test Advanced AI Models

Emilia David
 •
 
September 24, 2026

The three largest frontier artificial intelligence labs are reportedly close to establishing a common standards organization that would act as a third-party entity to monitor AI models.

The Informationreportedthat Google, OpenAI and Anthropic agreed to establish what would be called the Standards Authority for Frontier AI, which could launch by either the end of the year or in early 2027.

The three AI companies have approached several key figures for positions in the group, including former White House AI policy adviser Sriram Krishnan, former Biden administration technology official Arati Prabhakar, George W. Bush-era diplomat Condoleezza Rice and venture capitalist David Friedberg.

Potential scientific consultants the companies reportedly approached include Metr founder and CEO Beth Barnes and Paul Christiano, who advises the Center for AI Standards and Innovation. Both Barnes and Christiano have had recent dealings with OpenAI. Metr was the independent research group that investigated theHugging Face incident, while OpenAI recently appointed Christiano to its foundation's board.

The idea behind the organization stems from a proposal by Google DeepMind co-founder Demis Hassabis, which activated rare agreement between many leading AI labs in the United States. In a July 14essay, Hassabis floated the idea of building a self-regulating organization in the same vein as the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority. This group would develop common standards to assess frontier AI models and conduct benchmark testing. Hassabis's proposal noted that funding for such an institute "would need to be substantial and likely mostly come from industry" to attract top talent and gain access to the compute it needs.

It's unclear if the AI labs will mainly fund SAFA. As a self-regulating organization, the planned center would not need approval from Congress or the president. But if it wants to wield any legal authority, it may need to register with a government agency. FINRA is registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission, which approves any changes to its rules.

Plans to establish an independent AI standards body come amid the ongoingdebateon AI safety. Recent agent breaches, especially the Hugging Face hack, which saw OpenAI agents access third-party systems, along with fears from researchers that AI could endanger humanity, have put AI safety in the public consciousness. AI executives, including OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and Hugging Face CEO Clem Delangue,spoketo the United Nations Security Council on Sept. 23 to discuss safety.

Many AI leaders haveconvergedon the idea that labs should "pace" AI development, another instance of the top AI companies agreeing with each other, to allow guardrails and other safety systems to catch up to the models. A notable exception is Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, whosaidthat he supports a more accelerationist point of view.

Most AI-specific regulations in the United States occur at the state level. Both chambers of Congress have several bills seeking to curb the technology, though none have made it out of committee. AI labs can voluntarily submit their frontier models to the government's AI safety institute for pre-deployment testing, a mechanism that stems from a Juneexecutive order. The White House was meant toprovideclear guidelines for this process, but the Trump administration refused to make them public.

President Donald Trump has also publicly said he believes no additional regulations are needed for AI.
