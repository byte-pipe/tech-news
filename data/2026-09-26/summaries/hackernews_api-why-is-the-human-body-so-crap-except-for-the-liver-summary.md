# Why is the human body so crap except for the liver?

## Main observation
- Most organs (kidneys, gums, etc.) deteriorate with age or injury and heal poorly.
- The liver is an exception: it regenerates without scarring, can regrow after partial removal, and maintains function throughout life.

## Examples of “poor design” in the body
- **Menopause**: rare among mammals; only a few cetaceans and one chimp population show similar patterns.
- **Limited regeneration**: loss of limbs or severe injuries does not trigger regrowth (unlike salamanders).
- **Telomere shortening**: each cell division shortens chromosome ends, leading to replicative senescence after ~50‑70 divisions.
- **Transplant rejection**: immune system attacks foreign organs, requiring lifelong immunosuppression.
- **Autoimmune diabetes**: immune attack on pancreatic β‑cells destroys insulin production.
- **Neuronal non‑division**: adult neurons do not proliferate; lost neurons are not replaced.
- **Cellular “junk” accumulation**: non‑dividing cells (neurons, eye cells) accumulate lipofuscin and other debris.
- **Impaired angiogenesis**: high levels of inhibitors prevent rapid formation of new blood vessels, slowing recovery from ischemia.

## Proposed unifying explanation
- **Cancer suppression** is the underlying selective pressure shaping many of these traits.
  - Shortened telomeres limit the number of divisions a potentially cancerous cell can undergo.
  - Most cells silence telomerase after development; only stem, germ, and certain immune cells retain it.
  - When a cell acquires oncogenic mutations, telomere erosion eventually halts its proliferation, acting as a tumor‑suppressive barrier.
  - Occasionally, cancer cells reactivate telomerase, enabling unlimited growth; this is the basis of many cancers.

## Trade‑offs
- **Benefits**: Reduced cancer risk through limited proliferative capacity and restrained regenerative programs.
- **Costs**: Age‑related tissue decline, inability to regenerate complex structures, susceptibility to chronic diseases, and reliance on imperfect repair mechanisms.

## Why the liver is different
- Liver cells retain a higher regenerative capacity and can safely re‑enter the cell cycle without markedly increasing cancer risk, likely due to specialized control mechanisms evolved for metabolic resilience.

## Overall conclusion
- The body’s “fragile” characteristics are not random flaws but evolutionary compromises that prioritize cancer avoidance over perfect regeneration or longevity. The liver’s exceptional regenerative ability represents a localized exception within this broader strategy.
