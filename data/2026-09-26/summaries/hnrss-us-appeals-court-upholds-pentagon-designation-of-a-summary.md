# U.S. appeals court upholds Pentagon designation of Anthropic as supply‑chain risk  

## Key points  
- A three‑judge panel of the U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia affirmed the Department of Defense’s blacklisting of Anthropic.  
- The 2‑1 ruling rejected Anthropic’s claim that the ban on its Claude models was arbitrary, unauthorized, and unconstitutional.  
- The designation bars the military and its contractors from using Anthropic’s AI models.  

## Background  
- In March 2025 the DOD labeled Anthropic a “supply‑chain risk,” citing national‑security concerns over the integration of Claude into DoD information systems.  
- Anthropic had previously signed a $200 million contract with the Pentagon (July 2025) but negotiations over model deployment on the GenAI.mil platform collapsed.  
- The DOD demanded unrestricted access for all lawful purposes; Anthropic sought guarantees that its technology would not be used for fully autonomous weapons or mass surveillance.  

## Court decision  
- Majority opinion by Judge Gregory Katsas (joined by Judge Neomi Rao) held that the Secretary of Defense had authority under the Supply Chain Security Act to deem Claude a risk.  
- Judge Karen LeCraft Henderson dissented.  
- The panel delayed the decision’s immediate effect, allowing Anthropic to petition for a rehearing, an en banc rehearing, or to seek Supreme Court review.  

## Anthropic’s response and next steps  
- Anthropic’s spokesperson said the company “remains confident in our position” and is considering further review, noting a separate San Francisco court had already struck down a parallel designation as unlawful.  
- Potential avenues include a rehearing before the same panel, an en banc rehearing, or a petition to the Supreme Court.  

## Wider context  
- The dispute reflects broader tensions between AI firms and the Trump administration, with President Trump publicly criticizing Anthropic’s CEO Dario Amodei.  
- Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth warned that overly constrained AI could fail unexpectedly or be manipulated, reinforcing the administration’s stance on maintaining control over AI used by the military.
