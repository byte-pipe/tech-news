# Friday Facts #447 – Factorio that you can touch

## Journey to the physical world
- The idea began after the Space Age DLC play‑test (FFF‑427) in summer 2024, where the team sought in‑person feedback.  
- Prusa Research helped create programmes and presentations for the event.  
- Fearghall printed a simple Wriggler model on‑site, sparking interest in real‑world Factorio models.  
- In the following months the team prototyped belts, inserters, enemies, chests, the player character, furnaces, etc.  
- Two design variants were produced for each model: a high‑precision, push‑fit version and a high‑clearance version for gluing or printer limitations.  
- Focus was placed on early‑game entities, resulting in 15 model sets (65 individual models) spread across 247 STL files.  
- The development process involved many test prints, iterations, painting, and size/clearance adjustments before the final “glamour shots” were taken.

## Why 3D printing?
- Physical memorabilia for games are now rare; the team wanted a tangible reminder of the game without becoming a toy manufacturer.  
- Offering free STL files serves as a “thank you” gift that anyone can download and print.  
- The approach encourages community creativity, allowing fans to modify, customize, or expand the models.

## Reimagining of Factorio
- Jarosław, a 3D artist and board‑game enthusiast, became deeply involved once the project started, keeping his printers running constantly.  
- Translating in‑game 3D assets to printable models proved more complex than expected, mainly due to:
  - **Support structures**: FDM printers need supports for overhangs; the team aimed to design parts that could be printed without them and later assembled.  
  - **Isometric geometry**: Factorio’s isometric art contains many floating elements that look coherent only from a specific angle, making direct printing difficult.  
- The solution involved breaking models into printable pieces, minimizing supports, and re‑engineering designs while preserving the game’s visual identity.
