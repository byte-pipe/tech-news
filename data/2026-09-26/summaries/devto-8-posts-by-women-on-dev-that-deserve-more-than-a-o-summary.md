# 8 Posts by Women on DEV That Deserve More Than a Once-a-Year Spotlight

## Anna Villarreal — DEV Follower Analysis  
- Started from a Node.js tutorial and decided to scrape her own DEV followers and their public GitHub data via GraphQL.  
- Built a custom scraper, exported JSON, and created a chart.  
- The post shines because it openly discusses the challenges of creating a non‑plug‑and‑play solution and encourages creative problem‑solving.

## Jessica Doering — I Built a Local AI Music Studio  
- Demonstrates a fully local, open‑source AI music studio that avoids subscription‑based cloud services.  
- Shows how a simple script evolved into a functional digital audio workstation.  
- Highlights the value of local‑first tools that receive far less publicity than SaaS equivalents.

## Sylwia Laskowska — What If Your AI Agent Never Had to Leave the Browser? (Demo)  
- Provides a working demo rather than speculative commentary on AI agents.  
- Lets readers explore a browser‑based AI agent directly, grounding the discussion in concrete implementation.  
- Serves as a practical answer to ongoing AI‑agent debates.

## Sneha M K — MCP vs. API Explained: Do We Still Need APIs After MCP?  
- Offers a clear, timely explainer on the relationship between Multi‑Component Packages (MCP) and traditional APIs.  
- Addresses a common, unspoken uncertainty among developers in a concise, no‑fluff style.  
- Helps readers evaluate whether MCP can replace or complement existing API workflows.

## Maame Afua A. P. Fordjour — Disappeared Since March: Taking a Long Break Was My Best Decision Yet  
- A candid personal essay about stepping away from tech for an extended period.  
- Emphasizes the difficulty of writing honestly about a hiatus compared to a project devlog.  
- Resonates with anyone who feels uneasy about gaps in their output.

## Ava Barron — The Story of How Alicia Keys Accidentally Got Me a Career in Tech  
- An origin story that avoids tidy narratives; it traces a path through an Alicia Keys concert and teenage AIM‑based troubleshooting.  
- Won DEV’s 2026 WeCoded “Echoes of Experience” category for its specific, non‑generic details.  
- Shows that unconventional routes into tech are valuable and relatable.

## Margarita — It Wasn’t Brave. It Was Just Tuesday.  
- Another WeCoded winner that tells a multi‑generational family story about moving toward logic and systems.  
- Refuses dramatization; the title alone conveys the central argument.  
- Demonstrates that everyday decisions can shape tech careers without heroic framing.

## Ashley Childress — Forged Between Coal and Code  
- A technically ambitious, narrated web experience about growing up in an Appalachian coal town and becoming an engineer.  
- Backed by 685 unit tests and 220 end‑to‑end tests, blending storytelling with rigorous craftsmanship.  
- Won DEV’s Frontend Art category, illustrating how personal art can meet high engineering standards.
