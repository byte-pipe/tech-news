# Can Muse make us forget the metaverse?

## Background and past bot efforts
- In 2016 Meta (then Facebook) launched a bot platform for Messenger, attracting brands like 1‑800‑FLOWERS and CNN.  
- The bot hype promised messaging as the next internet interface, but the ecosystem collapsed; by 2018 Meta shut down the platform and its bot‑building apps disappeared.  

## Muse: Meta’s new personal AI agent
- At the recent developer conference Zuckerberg positioned Muse as the company’s flagship product, calling it a “personal superintelligence” for billions.  
- Muse functions like an open‑source AI assistant (e.g., OpenClaw), capable of handling tasks such as form‑filling, reservations, and bill payments when linked to Google Workspace, bank accounts, etc.  
- Upcoming features: voice chat, integration with Meta’s VR operating system, and a hardware accessory called the Muse Charm (a strapless smartwatch‑style screen).  
- Early usage numbers: reported “millions” of testers, with week‑one figures around 500 000 users, far below the rapid adoption of Meta’s Threads app.  

## Skepticism about Muse’s consumer appeal
- The author likens managing AI agents to supervising interns: they require constant new tasks, approvals, and review, making them feel more like enterprise tools than consumer products.  
- Investment in Muse appears driven by a need for a compelling narrative amid Meta’s $145 billion annual capital spend and ongoing losses in the Reality Labs division.  
- Zuckerberg admitted the company shifted from a metaverse‑first strategy to AI after AI’s market traction, but the author doubts Muse will replace the metaverse as the primary user focus.  

## Meta VR Glasses: a notable hardware breakthrough
- Meta unveiled lightweight VR glasses (≈100 g) with an external “puck” for computing, solving common VR issues: bulk, overheating, short battery life.  
- The glasses feature a “5K” display (about half the pixel count of Apple Vision Pro) that delivered crisp visuals in demos of concerts, sports, and games.  
- A “surface keyboard” projects a virtual keyboard onto a surface, offering reasonable typing experience for short tasks.  
- While promising, the author notes traditional VR concerns (content, comfort, price) remain unanswered.  

## Overall assessment
- Muse shows early promise but lacks clear product‑market fit and may remain an enterprise‑oriented tool rather than a mass‑consumer staple.  
- The heavy investment in Muse seems partly a story‑selling move to justify Meta’s massive capital expenditures.  
- The VR Glasses represent a genuine hardware advancement, potentially revitalizing Meta’s VR ambitions, yet broader adoption challenges persist.
