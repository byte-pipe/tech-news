# Shantell Sans – Summary

## Why a new font?
- Shantell Martin’s early struggles with spelling tests and later diagnosis of dyslexia made her view words as drawings and a personal means of expression.  
- Inspired by Comic Sans for its playful readability, she wanted a typeface that is fun, professional, and accessible, especially for dyslexic readers.  
- Stephen Nixon provided a template; Shantell hand‑wrote the Latin alphabet, numbers, and symbols, which were digitised into a font.

## Open‑source release
- Shantell Sans is released under an open‑font license, free on Google Fonts, GitHub, and other platforms.  
- The open release aims to give wide access, particularly to children and young adults, and to encourage other artists to create their own typography.  
- Users are invited to share their creations on social media with the hashtag **#shantellsans**.

## Early applications
- Key tags for the Whitney Museum shop in New York City.  
- Cash App physical cash cards, with all text set in Shantell Sans.  
- Primary font for the collaborative drawing app **tldraw**.  
- Web‑building template for the mobile app **univer.se**.  
- Available for use in Google Docs, Google Fonts, and as a downloadable package.

## Design process
- Collaboration between Shantell Martin and type designer Stephen Nixon (ArrowType).  
- The project was framed as “a new Comic Sans” to capture approachability and emotional resonance.  
- Nixon studied Comic Sans’s cultural ubiquity and purpose, then applied its appealing qualities to a fresh design rooted in Shantell’s handwriting.

## Design goals
1. Appeal to everyday computer users, not only typography enthusiasts.  
2. Provide readability and friendliness for dyslexic readers.  
3. Offer variable axes—Weight, Italic, Informality, and Bounce—for versatile styling.  
4. Balance playful character with professional usability.

## Vision
- Shantell hopes the font will be used in both personal projects and larger design initiatives.  
- By releasing it openly, she relinquishes personal control, allowing the typeface to evolve in unexpected and creative ways.
