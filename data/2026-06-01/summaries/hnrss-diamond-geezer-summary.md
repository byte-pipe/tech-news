# Diamond Geezer – Summary (May 22 2026)

## Introduction
- City developers have begun including free public roof terraces in new skyscrapers to improve planning approval chances.  
- The author set out to visit several of these terraces, avoiding the “big three” that require advance booking.

## Skipped Major Terraces
- **Sky Garden** (35‑37 floors, The Walkie‑Talkie) – requires a three‑week booking; was closed for a private event.  
- **Horizon 22** (57‑58 floors, 22 Bishopsgate) – tickets released on Mondays, early slots often available.  
- **The Lookout** (50 floors, 8 Bishopsgate) – tickets released on Mondays, current availability in four days; author preferred Horizon 22.

## The Terrace – 1 Leadenhall (4th floor)
- Opened April 2026; lift ride lasts ~20 seconds, staffed by two people.  
- Located on the 4th floor of a 36‑storey tower, accessed via a modest door near Leadenhall Market.  
- Terrace dimensions: ~40 m long, up to 10 m wide; formed by cutting a two‑storey wedge from the building side.  
- Features: eight benches, thin shrub line, a restaurant entrance (not yet open), and a solitary security guard.  
- Views: limited central view; better panoramas at either end.  
  - South view blocked by construction at 85 Gracechurch Street.  
  - West end shows 8 & 22 Bishopsgate, the Cheesegrater, Lloyds Building, the Scalpel, Minster Court, Plantation Place, the Walkie‑Talkie, and the Shard.  
  - East end offers silhouettes of St Paul’s dome, St Michael’s tower, and St Peter’s spire.  
- Overall impression: an odd, pleasant diversion useful for a quick toilet break or sandwich eat‑in, but fundamentally limited.

## The Garden at 120 – Fen Court (15th floor)
- Opened February 2019; lift ride ~40 seconds, staffed by six people.  
- Long‑established, most popular, and largest roof garden in the City.  
- Entry includes a scanner checkpoint; typical wait ~3 minutes, longer if queues form.  
- Terrace features a pergola, wisteria, rose‑bush shrubbery, and well‑maintained planting.  
- 360° panorama:  
  - Unobstructed views of Tower Bridge, the Tower of London, and the Royal London Hospital helipad.  
  - Docklands, Crystal Palace transmitter, London Eye, and close‑up of the Gherkin.  
- Visitor vibe: younger tourists often pose rather than enjoy the scenery.  
- Recommendation: a must‑visit for first‑timers or anyone who hasn’t explored a City roof garden.

## Roof Terrace – One New Change (6th floor)
- Opened November 2010; lift ride ~20 seconds, staffed by three people.  
- Situated atop a shopping centre beside St Paul’s Cathedral; likely intended to support a rooftop bar.  
- Glass‑sided lift offers striking cathedral views through a reflective notch.  
- Terrace layout: stepped platform with plants and benches; a grey “Beware Sudden Drop” barrier limits sightlines when seated.  
- Main view: Wren’s dome of St Paul’s, framed more perfectly than from Richmond Park.  
- Secondary view: South Bank and the London Eye.  
- Suggested experience: brief visit for the iconic cathedral framing.

## Level 10 – Tate Modern (10th floor)
- Opened June 2016; lift ride ~35 seconds, staffed by one person.  
- Intended as a café with a four‑sided observation terrace overlooking the Thames.  
- Legal dispute with nearby Neo Bankside residents led to the balcony being permanently locked.  
- Exterior doors remain closed; signs prohibit photography, and a security guard enforces the restriction.  
- Current use: café only; the observation level is inaccessible and described as a “ghastly waste of architectural effort.”

## Roof Garden – The Post Building (9th floor)
- Opened September 2022; requires photo ID and full written name for entry.  
- Westernmost City roof terrace, offering unique views of West End low‑rise roofs and a close‑up of the British Museum’s roof.  
- On the day of the visit it was closed for “essential maintenance,” leaving the author unable to test it.  
- Author speculates the closure may be a convenient excuse for a planning‑driven “white‑elephant” terrace.  
- Recommendation: choose The Garden at 120 or plan ahead for one of the higher, ticketed terraces.

## Overall Impressions
- Free public roof terraces provide valuable elevated viewpoints across the City, though quality varies widely.  
- Some spaces (e.g., The Garden at 120) deliver comprehensive panoramas and pleasant amenities, while others (e.g., The Terrace at 1 Leadenhall, Level 10 at Tate Modern) feel underutilised or restricted.  
- Planning‑driven terraces can be “oddities” that serve more as compliance tools than genuine public spaces.  
- Visitors should check booking requirements, opening status, and possible restrictions before heading up.
