# Hero image best practices summary

## What is a hero image
- Large visual placed at the top of a web page, usually full‑width under the navigation.
- Contains a headline, supporting text, and a call‑to‑action (CTA); can be a static image, animation, or short looping video.
- Used not only on homepages but also on landing pages, product pages, and blogs.
- Serves to tell visitors what you offer and guide them to the next step in the user journey.

## Why hero images matter
- **Branding impact** – typography, colors, and overall design shape visitor perception of the brand.
- **Emotional connection & storytelling** – visuals with emotional cues encourage users to explore the brand’s story.
- **Value communication** – a strong visual paired with a clear headline and CTA conveys the value proposition before any scrolling.

## 7 best practices for effective hero images
1. **Set the right mood for your brand**  
   Align the visual style and emotional tone with the brand’s personality (e.g., calm illustration for a telehealth service).

2. **Design for clarity**  
   Use layout, contrast, and negative space to highlight the headline and CTA; place text over open areas of the image.

3. **Use motion and video intentionally**  
   Short background videos can demo products and add depth, but consider performance impact and provide reduced‑motion fallbacks.

4. **Avoid stock imagery**  
   Prefer custom photography or brand‑specific illustrations to appear authentic and professional.

5. **Optimize file size without sacrificing quality**  
   Compress images, use modern formats like WebP or AVIF, and keep load times fast to protect UX and SEO.

6. **Design for responsiveness across devices**  
   Ensure focal points, composition, and text remain clear on mobile; adopt a mobile‑first approach.

7. **Ensure accessibility and effectiveness**  
   Provide sufficient color contrast, captions or transcripts for video, test across devices and assistive technologies, and run A/B tests to refine performance.
