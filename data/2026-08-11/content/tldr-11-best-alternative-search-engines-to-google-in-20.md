# 11 best alternative search engines to Google in 2026

 

Date published

9 August 2026

Author

Radu Tyrsina

Categories

In recent years, Google has made significant changes that have particularly impacted publishers and other content creators. At the same time, we’ve witnessed the rise of alternative search engines. Although Google continues to dominate the market, there are many reasons you might consider exploring other options. We’ve compiled and tested a list of alternatives in our environment.

Our answer after a fixed 12-query desktop test isDuckDuckGo No-AI for most people. It was the strongest fully tested free alternative, scoring 55 out of 60 against Google’s 58. ChooseBrave Searchwhen an independent index matters more than a completely AI-free interface,Startpagefor mainstream results without AI summaries,Kagiif you will pay for control, andEcosiaif environmental funding and Europe’s emerging search infrastructure matter most.

The short version:make one alternative your default, keep a mainstream fallback for maps and shopping, and use specialist or AI tools only when the task calls for them. The best Google replacement is usually a search stack and definitely not another monopoly in miniature.

### First, understand what you are actually replacing

“Search engine” is used for four different things so treating them as interchangeable creates most bad comparisons. Some of these search engines are also “answer engines”.

Layer

What it does

Examples + why it matters

Interface

Accepts the query and displays results

Startpage, Ecosia and Yahoo Search. A different interface may still use Google or Bing underneath.

Index

Crawls, stores and retrieves pages

Google, Bing, Brave, Mojeek and EUSP. This is the infrastructure-level source of search diversity.

Ranking

Orders, filters or blends candidates

Kagi Lenses, Brave Goggles and metasearch. Overlapping sources can still produce very different results.

Answer layer

Synthesizes sources into prose

ChatGPT Search, Perplexity, Yahoo Scout and Copilot. A fluent answer is not a larger or more independent index.

Be also aware that DuckDuckGo can protect a user’s identity while sourcing most traditional links from Bing. Kagi can add its own indexes and ranking while also making anonymized calls to major providers. SearXNG can hide a user from upstream engines, but the operator of the chosen instance becomes part of the trust model. In other words, if it just has a different logo it does not necessarily mean a different “kitchen.”

That distinction matters more after Microsoftretired the public Bing Search APIs on 11 August 2025. Products that once depended on an easy commercial feed have had to renegotiate, rebuild, switch providers or change shape. Any article that maps today’s search market using a 2024 dependency chart is already unreliable. And that’s precisely why we want to ensure we’re surfacing only the most serious options.

### Why the Google-alternative question changed this year

Google remains dominant, according to most recent data –91.31% of worldwide search and 86.6% in the US in July 2026but the alternatives are changing faster than the share chart suggests.

* AI is now a default-layer decision.Google says AI Overviews are a core Search feature thatcannot be turned off; the Web filter removes them only after a query. In our logged-out test, overviews appeared on ordinary deep-link, shopping and forum-intent searches.
* No-AI search became a product, not a workaround.DuckDuckGo No-AIremoves its AI features and hides images identified as AI-generated by default. Startpage now advertisesno AI summaries or chatbotin core search. Ecosia launched a singleAI-free settingin July.
* Europe is building an actual index.Ecosia and Qwant’s European Search Perspective, or EUSP, began serving France in 2025 andstarted its German rollout on 30 July 2026. The European Parliament also made Qwant its default on internal Edge and Firefox installations on 4 June, while retaining user choice,Reuters reported.
* Search data may become less concentrated.On 16 July, the European Commission adopted binding measures requiring Google to share anonymized search data with eligible search engines, including qualifying AI chatbots, on fair terms.Sharing is due to start in January 2027. That could improve challengers, but it does not instantly give them Google’s crawler, local inventory or product graph.
* The business rules are under pressure.One week later, the Commissionfined Google €890 million, including €460 million for self-preferencing its own Search services and €430 million for Google Play anti-steering.
* Yahoo is trying an answer-engine comeback.Yahoo Scout launched in US betain January and has since spread into Yahoo properties. That is an AI discovery layer, not evidence that Yahoo rebuilt a general web index.

Users can now choose between index diversity, cleaner links, a no-AI interface, a subscription-funded ranker, an environmental model and a conversational research layer. So you just have to make your pick.

### How we tested the alternatives

We froze the query set before opening the comparative engines on8 August 2026. Tests ran in a clean, logged-out Chrome session with no deliberate ad blocker, VPN or extension. The interface language was English. IP-based location resolved to France/Paris; precise location permission was not granted. Local and shopping queries named Bucharest or Romania explicitly (I was not in my London office when writing this). We obviously used various tools including AI assisted help to make this test.

Provider defaults stayed on unless the named product was itself a no-AI version. Google was the baseline and was not eligible for an award.

### Scoring

* 5:the intended, high-quality result or answer appeared in the top three without reformulation.
* 4:a useful result appeared on page one below the top three.
* 3:the task worked after one reasonable refinement or extra step.
* 2:partly useful, but noisy, stale or missing important context.
* 1:mostly irrelevant or impractical.
* 0:failed or produced no useful result.

The score measures retrieval usefulness only. It deliberately excludes privacy, mission, index independence and personal interface taste. Clearly labeled ads did not count as organic positions.

## What the test revealed

Google retrieved every known page and fresh story, and its map and Romanian shopping modules were the most complete. Its 58/60 baseline remains the benchmark for a reason. The cost in this particular comparison was interface control: AI Overviews appeared without being requested, and Google’s own help page says there is no permanent off switch.

DuckDuckGo No-AIcame within three points of that baseline. It found the official EU material, put Reuters first for the breaking-news query, led with Microsoft Learn for troubleshooting and produced the best alternative-engine video result. Its local result lacked Google’s hours-and-map depth, and its shopping snippets did not surface prices as clearly. One query briefly returned a 502 and worked after one reload.

Startpagewas exceptionally good at finding the exact page. It put the official result first for both Commission queries and the exact Ecosia phrase, and it made the official Google Search Central crawler video the first video result. It was less convincing when the job depended on a local or commercial graph. Its Maps tab handed the query to MapQuest, Google Maps or Bing rather than solving it in place; its image grid was dominated by Alamy.

Bingwas strongest on local places and troubleshooting. It was also the busiest interface. Copilot answer material occupied substantial space before ordinary links, and the shopping query led with a sponsored module for the older WH-1000XM5 and French offers despite “XM6” and “Romania” in the query. In our session, selecting Videos left a web-answer layout on screen instead of producing a useful video grid.

Brave‘s partial run showed why independent search is hard. It was excellent for navigation and Romanian shopping, and it found the fresh Commission fine. Yet it missed the official Commission data-sharing page on the deep-link query and its local pack did not prioritize the nearest, open places. AI answers appeared automatically on some ordinary queries. Then its anti-bot system ended the test.

## Best free overall: DuckDuckGo No-AI ⭐⭐⭐

The normal DuckDuckGo product is familiar; the decisive 2026 change is the dedicatedNo-AI version. DuckDuckGo says it works like regular Search except that images identified as AI-generated are filtered by default and all other AI features are removed.

That promise matched the interface we saw. There was no generated summary above the links, and Images exposed anAI images: hidefilter next to size, color, type, layout and license controls. You could say we are biased since the advent of AI Overviews hurt our business as a publisher, but this is actually the result of a test, and not of your subjectiv views.

DuckDuckGo No-AI removes generative features and hides images identified as AI-generated by default. Screenshot: Search Engine Watch, August 2026.

The result quality was not mysterious. DuckDuckGo says it maintains its own crawler and several indexes and blends specialist sources for Instant Answers, buttraditional links and images are largely sourced from Bing. Requests to partners are proxied, according to the company, so a Bing-heavy result set does not mean handing Microsoft a user’s direct IP address or identifier.

That makes DuckDuckGo No-AI an excellent product alternative, not an independent-index alternative. Use it when the goal is a clean, free default that still finds mainstream pages. Skip it if your primary objective is to fund a web index outside Google and Microsoft, or if your daily work depends on the richest possible local and shopping modules.

Best for:everyday links, news, troubleshooting, videos and users who do not want generative answers in search.

Main tradeoff:Bing remains the main source for conventional links, and commercial/local depth can trail Google.

## Best free independent index: Brave Search ⭐⭐

Brave controls a layer most privacy front ends do not. The company says Search uses itsown, built-from-scratch index; its help page describes the service asfully independent. It also offers Goggles, community-defined rules that rerank or restrict the searchable result set.

That infrastructure makes Brave the most capable free answer to “Which search engine does not mainly proxy Google or Bing?” It does not make every result better. In our seven completed tasks, Brave’s best moments were the official navigational result, a correct Romanian retail price and timely coverage. Its most important miss was the official EU deep link.

Brave is also not the pick for someone who wants AI entirely out of sight. Its own documentation saysAI is used across the stack, including AI Answers, Ask Brave, generated descriptions and featured snippets. The product cites sources, but in our test an AI-generated answer sometimes preceded the ordinary results without an explicit trip into Ask.

We cannot publish a full relevance score because Brave’s human-verification screen stopped the remaining five tasks. The award rests on its documented index independence and the useful portion we could reproduce—not on pretending the incomplete 4.00 mean is directly comparable with a 12-query score.

Best for:users who want to support a competitive free index and can tolerate AI features.

Main tradeoff:long-tail and local consistency still vary, and there is no clean No-AI edition comparable to DuckDuckGo’s.

## Best mainstream-style no-AI search: Startpage ⭐⭐

Startpage is the easiest move for someone who likes the shape of conventional mainstream results but wants neither a personal search profile nor a generated answer above the links. Its current homepage states:“No AI summaries or AI chatbots”.

Startpage is not a crawler. It says itpartners with Google, Bing and other providers, applies its anonymizing process, and does not collect information used to identify or profile a searcher. Its most distinctive result-page feature isAnonymous View, a proxy route for opening a destination page through Startpage.

The model worked particularly well for known pages. Startpage scored 5 on all four freshness and exact-retrieval tasks. Its shortcomings were not ordinary blue links; they were the missing product graphs around them. Local, maps, prices and image diversity were weaker than Google’s.

Anonymous View also should not be oversold. It can hide a direct visit from the destination site, but interactive features may break and it is not a substitute for Tor when the threat model requires stronger network anonymity. Ourprivate-search guidecovers that distinction in detail.

Best for:official pages, exact retrieval and mainstream-looking search without generative summaries.

Main tradeoff:it depends on upstream providers and is less capable for self-contained maps, shopping and some visual searches.

## Best mainstream fallback: Bing ⭐⭐

Bing is the alternative most likely to cover all the practical verticals in one account-free interface: web, news, images, local places, shopping and troubleshooting. It also matters far beyond bing.com because its index feeds or influences other products.

Our test gave Bing perfect scores for navigation, deep links, local, troubleshooting, long-tail, exact phrase and images. It was the only alternative with a Google-class local module. It also demonstrated why a fallback is not necessarily the best default. Copilot material was difficult to avoid, ads complicated the shopping task, and the Videos view malfunctioned in this session.

For marketers, Bing still deserves direct attention even after the commercial Search API retirement. Verify the site inBing Webmaster Tools, monitor crawl and query data, and send change notifications through IndexNow where appropriate.

Best for:local intent, image search, Windows users and a second opinion from a major index.

Main tradeoff:heavy AI and commercial modules; uneven shopping and video behavior in our run.

## Best paid Google alternative: Kagi ⭐⭐

Kagi changes the incentive before it changes the interface. Search is funded by a subscription rather than ads. The current individual plans are$5 for 300 monthly searches and $10 for unlimited search; a 100-search trial is available.

The common shorthand that Kagi “has its own index” is only partly right. Kagi documents its Teclis web index and TinyGem news index, but also says a typical query makesanonymized calls to about a dozen major, specialist and vertical sources. Its value is the blend plus incentives and reranking: downranking ad-heavy pages, surfacing the small web, blocking or promoting domains, and creating Lenses for a subset of sites.

That is stronger and more interesting than pretending Kagi rebuilt Google alone. It is a premium ranker with proprietary indexes and broad external retrieval. AI summaries and assistants are available, but search AI can be hidden or disabled in settings for users who want links only.

Kagi was not included in the logged-out score because a paid, account-based session is not comparable with the anonymous free-engine test. We recommend its documented product model and controls, consistent with our separate privacy review, while labeling the evidence honestly.

Best for:researchers, developers and heavy searchers who will pay to remove ad incentives and tune rankings.

Main tradeoff:an account and recurring fee; source independence is blended, not absolute.

## Best mission-led and emerging European option: Ecosia

Ecosia’s environmental model is the visible differentiator, but its search infrastructure is now the more consequential story. Current documentation says organic results can come fromBing, Google or EUSP, depending on location, device, cookie permissions and the query. A result’s three-dot menu can reveal the provider.

EUSP is the joint Ecosia-Qwant index based in Paris. Ecosia says the majority of its French traffic now comes from EUSP, Germany began rollout on 30 July, and the goal is to answer most German queries through it by year-end. This is a real index-independence project—not just a European front end for an American feed.

There is a useful freshness lesson in Ecosia’s own documentation. Its provider help page, last updated 22 May, still says EUSP is available only in France; thenewer German rollout announcementsupersedes it. Search sourcing is now too dynamic for a guide that does not record its fact-check date.

Ecosia also offers one of the clearest AI controls. ItsAI-free settingdisables AI Overviews, removes AI Chat from navigation and autocomplete, disables AI-based autorouting, and restores the traditional search box.

We could not score results because the test browser met a robot challenge before the first query. That limitation should not be confused with a consumer verdict.

Best for:users who want searches to support climate projects and Europe’s developing search infrastructure.

Main tradeoff:the provider can vary by market and even between searches, so “using Ecosia” does not identify one fixed index.

### Qwant and EUSP: important, but not yet a blanket recommendation

Qwant matters in 2026 for two reasons: it co-owns EUSP, and the European Parliament selected it as an internal default. That is a meaningful distribution and sovereignty signal. It is not the same as proving that Qwant beats Google, Bing or DuckDuckGo for ordinary users everywhere.

The homepage we inspected separated conventionalSearchfromAI Chat. The first test query then returned HTTP 403 with a device-verification failure, so we did not score it. Users in France and other supported European markets should try it against their own language, local and commercial queries; users elsewhere should not assume the same provider mix or coverage.

Best for:Europeans who want to test the EUSP ecosystem and institutions diversifying their default.

Main tradeoff:region-dependent coverage and insufficient comparable data in our session.

### Mojeek: the pure independent underdog

Mojeek says it built its crawler, index and ranking technology from scratch and runs the system on its own managed infrastructure. Itstechnology historyrecords a nine-billion-page milestone in 2025.

That makes Mojeek strategically valuable even when its index is smaller: it can produce genuinely different candidates instead of a cosmetically different ordering of Bing. The tradeoff is the same one independent crawlers have always faced—coverage, freshness, spam fighting, local data and ranking feedback all require enormous scale.

The homepage returned502 Bad Gatewayin our test environment, so this edition cannot make a fresh relevance claim. Treat Mojeek as an excellent second-opinion engine for finding pages another index missed, not an unqualified daily-driver winner.

Best for:index diversity, UK infrastructure and exploratory second-opinion searches.

Main tradeoff:a smaller system and, in our session, an availability failure.

### MetaGer: nonprofit, configurable metasearch

Germany’s MetaGer is run by the nonprofit SUMA-EV. It combines several source indexes, reranks them and says it also uses small indexes of its own. Itstransparency statementexplains that the source rankings are converted to MetaGer scores and adjusted using URL, snippet and blocking signals.

The current product is not simply “a free privacy engine.” MetaGer has moved to an anonymous prepaid-key model for ad-free search: no account or email is required, and the company says a key can be bought with privacy-preserving payment methods. Its proxy can also open result pages anonymously. Source engines can be enabled or disabled in settings.

Best for:people who value nonprofit governance, transparent metasearch and an anonymous paid model.

Main tradeoff:prepaid use and reliance on external indexes; result quality follows the selected mix.

### SearXNG: maximum source control, if you trust the operator

SearXNGis open-source metasearch software, not one centralized search company. Its current documentation lists integrations for hundreds of services and roughly 70 maintained public instances. An administrator chooses the sources and policies.

That flexibility is the advantage and the risk. A carefully maintained private instance can aggregate general, academic, code, media and specialist engines while keeping JavaScript and cookies optional. A random public instance may be slow, misconfigured, monitored or blocked by its upstream sources. Self-hosting moves trust toward the user but also moves maintenance, abuse controls and operational security there.

Best for:technical users and organizations that want auditable, configurable metasearch.

Main tradeoff:instance quality is part of the product; “SearXNG” does not describe one universal result set.

### Swisscows: family filtering, with a privacy caveat

Swisscows guarantees a family-oriented result environment that excludes pornography and violent material. A frequently repeated claim says it uses Bing. The company’s current homepage instead says its search cooperates withBrave and adds its own country-index and semantic technology.

There is also an important distinction between the free and Pro products. Swisscows says free search carries System1 advertising that processes some usage data; it positions Pro as the fully data-secure, no-sharing option. “Family friendly” and “fully private” should therefore not be collapsed into one unchecked badge.

Best for:households or schools that want strong built-in content filtering.

Main tradeoff:free advertising changes the privacy story, and hard filtering is not suitable for every legitimate research context.

## AI answer engines are a separate category

An answer engine can be the best tool for a question without being the best replacement for a search engine. It may rewrite one prompt into several searches, retrieve from partner indexes, select a handful of sources and synthesize them. The final prose can save time, but it also adds a model-selection and interpretation layer between the reader and the evidence.

### ChatGPT Search

ChatGPT Search is the best fit here when a task benefits from synthesis, follow-up questions and a readable cited answer. OpenAI says Search is available to logged-in and logged-out free users as well as paid plans, can activate automatically when a question would benefit from the web, and provides inline citations plus a Sources panel.

The underlying retrieval is not a single declared OpenAI web index. Thecurrent help documentationsays ChatGPT may rewrite a prompt into one or more targeted queries and send them to search partners. It may share general IP-derived location, but not the IP address or ChatGPT account information, with those partners. For publishers, inclusion depends in part on allowing OAI-Searchbot.

Use ChatGPT Search to compare sources, build a research trail or explain an unfamiliar topic. Use a conventional engine when you need exhaustive candidate discovery, exact result ordering, visual browsing or a known URL with minimal interpretation.

### Perplexity

Perplexity is the most search-shaped of the major AI research tools. It describes itself as anAI-powered search enginethat searches the web, synthesizes an answer and attaches citations. Pro Search searches across web pages, papers, forums and videos and supports follow-up questions; the company still tells users to validate important information against the cited sources.

A useful 2026 addition is domain-level source labeling. Perplexity now marks some cited domains as Government, Academic or Trusted, while warning that a label applies to a domain rather than proving a specific page or claim. That is sensible interface context, not a substitute for opening the evidence.

### Yahoo Scout

Yahoo Scout is the wildcard. It launched in US beta in January as an AI answer engine and has since appeared across Yahoo Finance, Sports and other properties. Yahoo can combine open-web retrieval with decades of first-party vertical data and user products. For now, treat Scout as a discovery and synthesis surface—not as proof that Yahoo has returned as an independent general crawler.

### A simple rule for AI search

Use an answer engine when the job issynthesis. Use a conventional engine when the job isretrieval. For medical, legal, financial, safety or other high-stakes decisions, open the cited primary sources and check whether they support the exact claim. Citation presence is not citation correctness.

## Four search stacks that work better than a single replacement

### 1. The clean no-AI stack

* Default:DuckDuckGo No-AI.
* Exact-page fallback:Startpage.
* Maps and shopping fallback:Google or Bing when the richer module is worth the tradeoff.
* Media:Openverse, with license verification.
* Archive:Wayback Machine.

This is the easiest setup for a reader who wants ordinary links first without giving up practical fallbacks.

### 2. The independent-index stack

* Default:Brave Search.
* Second opinion:Mojeek.
* European experiment:Ecosia with EUSP where available, or Qwant.
* Aggregation:a carefully chosen or self-hosted SearXNG instance.
* Fallback:Bing or Google only when the independent engines miss.

This stack maximizes candidate diversity. It will sometimes require more reformulation.

### 3. The professional research stack

* Default links:Kagi.
* Synthesis:ChatGPT Search or Perplexity.
* Primary evidence:government sites, original research databases and company documentation.
* History:Wayback Machine.
* Verification:a second conventional index using a materially different source path.

The critical habit is to make synthesis the middle of the workflow, not the end.

### 4. The marketer’s coverage stack

* Demand baseline:Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
* Fast change notification:IndexNow where supported.
* Alternative referrals:segmentbing,duckduckgo,startpage,ecosia,brave,kagi,qwantand AI referrers in analytics.
* Citation monitoring:periodically query ChatGPT Search, Perplexity and other answer engines for priority brand and category questions.
* Regional QA:test from the market and language the campaign actually targets.

Do not create ten thin “optimized for Engine X” pages. Build one crawlable, authoritative source and make sure the indexes and answer layers can understand, retrieve and cite it.

## What to know before replacing Google

### Which search engine gives Google-like results without AI summaries?

Startpage is the closest fit. It partners with Google, Bing and other providers, applies an anonymizing layer, and currently advertises no AI summaries or chatbot in core search. It scored 53/60, with particularly strong official-page and exact-phrase retrieval.

### Does DuckDuckGo have its own search index?

Partly. DuckDuckGo maintains its own crawler and several indexes and uses specialist sources for Instant Answers, but the company says traditional links and images are largely sourced from Bing. It is private by design, but it is not fully index-independent in the Brave or Mojeek sense.

### Which search engines use their own index?

Google, Bing, Brave and Mojeek operate general web indexes. Ecosia and Qwant are building EUSP for European results. Kagi operates its own Teclis and TinyGem indexes but also calls major and specialist providers. “Own index” should not be interpreted as “uses no external source for any feature.”

### What is the best no-AI search engine?

DuckDuckGo No-AI is the clearest zero-setup choice. Startpage’s core search has no AI summary layer, and Ecosia offers an AI-free setting that disables overviews, chat and AI-based routing. Google AI Overviews cannot be permanently disabled; the Web filter is a per-search view.

### Can an alternative search engine match Google for maps and shopping?

Not consistently. Google led both tasks in our test. Bing matched it for local places but performed poorly on the Romania-specific product query. DuckDuckGo No-AI and Startpage found relevant retailers or venues but showed less price, hours and map detail.

### Are private search engines anonymous?

Not automatically. A private engine can minimize storage and profiling while your internet provider still sees the destination domain and the sites you open apply their own tracking. Tor or a carefully chosen VPN addresses a different layer. Read ourprivate search-engine rankingfor the full threat-model comparison.

### Is an AI answer engine better than a search engine?

It is better for some tasks. ChatGPT Search and Perplexity can synthesize several sources and handle follow-ups. Conventional engines are better for exhaustive discovery, visual browsing, known-item retrieval and inspecting the candidate list without a model’s narrative layer. Use both and verify high-stakes claims in primary sources.

### How often will this ranking change?

Potentially every few months. Provider agreements, AI defaults, index rollouts and regional experiments change quickly. EUSP’s German rollout and the gap between Ecosia’s May help page and July announcement illustrate why every ranking needs a visible fact-check date and repeatable queries.
