# AI is the New Netflix – On my Om

## Key observations
- In 2008 I asked Reed Hastings if streaming would be the first broadband killer app; it was, driving downstream speed upgrades.  
- Now AI is becoming the next killer app, not because of downloads but because of massive **uploads** generated by AI services.

## What the data shows (OpenVault Q1 2026)
- **Upstream traffic** has surged; average fiber upstream now exceeds 100 GB.  
- **Cloud sync** is the top upstream category on all speed tiers, accounting for 15‑16 % of upload volume.  
- New AI workloads (ChatGPT models, Microsoft 365 Copilot, Apple Intelligence, agentic AI) are creating upload traffic that didn’t exist at scale in 2023.  
- **IoT devices** (cameras, sensors, doorbells) upload 7× more data than they download; they send video to the cloud.  
- **Connected cars** send three times more outbound data (GPS, diagnostics, telemetry).  
- **Web search** now generates twice as much upload as download due to heavier voice and multimodal queries.

## Changing broadband asymmetry
- The traditional “fat downstream, thin upstream” model, built for passive consumption, is ending.  
- Residential users still show a 23:1 download‑to‑upload ratio, with video making up 48 % of downloads.  
- Non‑residential users have a 7.3:1 ratio; cloud services represent 20 % of their uploads and approach a near‑symmetric 1.6:1 ratio for cloud traffic.  
- At 1 Gbps+ tiers, cloud uploads constitute 25.5 % of non‑residential traffic, making these connections behave like small data centers.

## Personal context & future outlook
- My home setup already runs server‑grade workloads (OpenClaw, NAS, etc.) over a residential link; I expect this to become common.  
- The shift mirrors the cord‑cutting transition I wrote about a decade ago—non‑residential behavior on residential networks will follow a similar mainstream path.  
- The “upload economy” is gaining momentum; persistent, agentic AI on consumer devices is the next wave that will further amplify upstream demand.

## Takeaway
- Broadband is evolving from a simple delivery pipe into a core infrastructure layer that must support massive, continuous upstream traffic driven by AI, IoT, connected vehicles, and cloud‑sync workloads.
