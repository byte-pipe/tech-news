This is going to be a more personal post, exposing my entire personal branding strategy, or rather, the complete lack of one. 😅 If you're only here for technical content, feel free to keep scrolling. Maybe I'll write something technical later this week. I have no idea how many people will read this one — maybe five — but that's kind of the point!

I'm writing this post because it's just been a year since I started publishing here. My beautiful badge:

It has been a truly crazy year, during which I've had the enormous pleasure of earning your trust, and somehow ending it with stats like these. Thank you! ❤️

@francistrdevsuggested under one of his posts that I should tell the story of how I ended up on DEV. But I decided that deserved a separate post, preferably an anniversary one, because the story is definitely not the usual one. You see, I started a blog because I wanted... something to put on my"About Me" slide at a conference.😅

But let's start from the beginning.

## It Didn't Exactly Start at the Best Time

In the summer of 2025, I wasn't in a particularly good place mentally. The previous year had brought several very negative events in my personal life, which I don't want to write about publicly (but seriously, objectively speaking, things were pretty bad).

And then, as the final blow, I had to give up my beloved judo because of an injury. Not my first one, either, so this really felt like the final nail in the coffin. I'd only trained for two years, but I went three times a week. I had my routine and friends there.

For most of that summer, I was just somehow drifting through life. I wasn't depressed, but I was constantly stuck thinking about all the bad things that had happened.

After many weeks, I finally decided that I had to somehow get myself out of that loop and start trying new things. A party, a workshop, a local event. Anything.

And it turned out that it worked! Obviously not overnight, but every new activity and every new idea helped me take another small step out of the darkness and toward the light.

## And Then There Was JS Poland

One day, while scrolling through LinkedIn, I came across a post from a friend of mine who travels around Poland giving talks at meetups. And it suddenly reminded me that I'd once dreamed of speaking at JS Poland.

I figured their CFP was probably open. It was, indeed. I submitted exactly one topic.

Now, JS Poland is a serious conference with some world-class speakers, so I estimated my chances as... rather tiny. But I'd submitted something! A few more dopamine points unlocked. I was very proud of myself.

Some time later, conference organizer, Darek, contacted me and asked whether I happened to have recordings of any of my previous talks. As luck would have it, I had exactly one, from six years earlier. I sent it over and assumed I'd probably get a yes/no answer sometime the following week.

He called me back half an hour later. (The talk was 20 minutes long.) He said the presentation was"excellent"and invited me to the conference. OH, WHAT A DOPAMINE HIT THAT WAS!!! I felt amazing.

In the meantime, another tragedy happened very close to me, but because something was happening in my life, because there was movement and something to look forward to, I didn't completely fall apart.

## So... Maybe I Should Start a Blog?

After a few weeks, however, the dopamine started wearing off, and it was still too early to start working seriously on the conference presentation. I have no idea why, but at some point I thought: Maybe I could start a blog? Then I'd have something to link on my"About Me" slide, so it would look like I was doing something in the community. 🤣

I didn't feel like building my own blog, though, so I decided to use an existing blogging platform. I chose DEV because I'd always liked... the layout. 😅 Also, the articles I came across on DEV had always seemed interesting and approachable.

My friends laughed and said I'd probably get bored with it pretty quickly. I told them that was fine. I'd write one post a week, and if I got bored, I'd switch to one a month so it would still look like something was happening. xD

## Wait, There's a Community Here?

Before that, I'd mostly encountered DEV articles through tech newsletters. I knew basically nothing about the platform itself. So little, in fact, that when@benonce commented on one of my articles, I remember wondering why his one-line comment had received so many likes. 🤦‍♀️😅

Eventually, I realized there was a feed here. A whole stream of posts, a community!

And there's something you should know about me.

A long, long time ago, I worked on the side as an editor for the biggest internet portal in Poland. And even by those standards, I have to admit I was pretty good at it — especially at finding interesting topics and coming up with headlines. 😀

Working in media is incredibly addictive, and that addiction came back immediately. You know, like an alcoholic who hasn't had a drink in ten years and decides to have"just one beer."

Okay, that's an exaggeration.

But only slightly. 😅

I started publishing on DEV much more often than once a week because I wanted to see"what gets clicks here". Or, translated into normal-person language,"what are people here actually interested in?"I still remember how excited I was about my first like, my first comment, or when one of my posts finally passed 25 views. 😅

And even better: on DEV, I could use my old editorial skills, but I could do itwell. No impossible traffic targets to hit. No annoying publishers. No need to squeeze every last drop out of one topic until everyone is sick of it. I could rotate topics. Under my own name, in my own words.

## So, Here Are a Few of My Blogging Rules

So what are my publishing "rules"? Maybe some of you will find these useful.

A headline can be controversial. It can even stretch things a tiny bit, as long as the content delivers. If it does, readers usually won't feel cheated. I always spend quite a lot of time thinking about titles. And yes, I almost always choose the most controversial one. 😉

If you went back to my earliest posts, you'd also notice that I published pretty chaotically and wrote about somewhat different things than I do now. I started series that I never finished. I experimented with topics. I didn't really know what I was doing yet. And I think that's completely fine. Maybe that will help someone who's currently wondering whether they should start blogging. You don't need to have some grand content strategy from day one.

I've always said that I writehalf for myself and half for the reader.😉I don't go to either extreme. I write about things that I think might interest you, but they also have to interest me.

Not every post needs to go viral. I'm fully aware that not every topic will be equally popular. But that doesn't stop me from writing more expert-level technical posts, sometimes with a GitHub repository and a working demo. And there's no point getting upset when those posts attract fewer readers. That's natural. For one thing, not everyone is interested in the same technologies. Meanwhile, obviously everyone is going to click on something called: "You're the real software developer only if..." xD

However, I think that if you want to allow yourself to write posts like that, it's worth first proving that you actually have some credibility in the field you're talking about.

If you want to write about the mistakes senior developers make, then for God's sake, prove that you are a senior developer yourself, that you actually know how to build things, and that you bring something to the community besides clickbait.

## And Then There Are the Comments

When it comes to comments, I've often read influencer advice saying that you should only reply selectively. Apparently, you're supposed to create the impression that you're slightly unavailable.

As you probably know, I couldn't care less about that advice. 🤣 I try to respond to every valuable comment. Of course, I don't always manage. Sometimes I miss one, forget about it, don't notice it, and so on.

But generally, I genuinely think that many of the discussions under my articles are much better than the articles themselves. 😀

## From "Something for My Slide" to Conference Invitations

As you probably know, I like speaking at conferences.

Over time, the blog was promoted from"something to put on my slide"to"something I can put in a CFP form to show that I'm active in the community."

But over the last six months, that situation has changed dramatically. More than once, I've actually beenINVITEDto speak at a conference because someone found one of my DEV articles and wanted a talk about that topic. Or, less often, I'll attach one of my articles to a CFP so that the committee knows roughly what the talk would be about.

And let me tell you: this is probably the most comfortable situation possible. The organizer can relax because they know what to expect. I can relax because I know what the organizer has already accepted. Win-win!

## So What's Next?

What am I planning for the next year? I'm not going to say anything like"I'm only getting started!", because that wouldn't be true. I plan to do exactly what I've been doing so far: publish roughly one post a week.

Sometimes I'm tempted by other distribution platforms, but then I quickly realize that I'd have to spend time figuring those out too, and time is something I don't have much of.

I'm also a little tempted by YouTube, mostly as an extension of what I'm already doing on DEV. Will I actually do it? We'll see!

So, for my DEV anniversary, I wish myself exactly the same amount of joy from writing that I have today. And I wish all of you all the best! ❤️
