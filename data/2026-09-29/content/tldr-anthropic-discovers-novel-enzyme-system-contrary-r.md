This week saw two major announcements in epigenetic editing therapies. The first was from nChroma Bio, a Boston-based genetic medicines company. The companyachievedelimination of hepatitis B virus from human liver cells with an epigenetic therapy thatdisables viral DNA without cutting it, an alternative to CRISPR-method DNA slicing, which can silence gene activity in both integrated and free-ranging viral DNA and lead toelevatedcancer risk.

The second consequential announcement came not from a dedicated drug developer but from Anthropic, which reported Wednesday that Claude had autonomouslydiscovereda novel enzyme system “reminiscent of CRISPR” within bacteriophage DNA. Anthropic reports that the result came from a21-hourcampaign across950 agent sessionsand210 milliontokens. Reportedly, the agent that made the discoveryexclaimed: “[The DNA next to the RT] is spectacular: I can see by eye a tandem repeat array … that’s a CRISPR-like … repeat array?!”. Feng Zhang, one of the pioneers of CRISPR genome editing,saidthe discovery was “an exciting example of how AI agents can contribute to biological discovery.”
