# AI is supercharging hacking, and your local hospitals and banks aren’t ready

## Key points
- Recent AI models (e.g., Anthropic’s Claude Code, Mythos, OpenAI’s Astra) can automate and scale cyber‑attacks, allowing individuals with limited expertise to launch sophisticated hacks.  
- Large tech firms receive early access to these defensive AI tools, while smaller nonprofits, clinics, community banks, and local retailers are largely excluded due to cost and restricted availability.  
- The shift from a finite pool of human hackers to AI‑augmented attackers creates a power imbalance that threatens essential services in underserved regions.  

## Impact on small and medium‑size organizations
- **Vivian’s Door**, a nonprofit serving minority‑owned businesses, suffered a breach that forced a three‑day system shutdown and a $3,000 remediation bill; the owners are unsure whether AI assisted the attack.  
- Small retailers such as **The Cool Hardware Company** rely on AI‑enhanced productivity tools but fear that a compromise of those platforms could cripple operations.  
- Community‑level financial institutions and local hospitals lack the resources to maintain round‑the‑clock cybersecurity teams, making them attractive targets for AI‑driven extortion.  
- Existing incidents include “carting attacks” on a Maryland co‑op, where stolen credit‑card numbers generated costly processing fees despite most transactions being declined.  

## AI tools for offense vs. defense
- Offensive AI: models like Claude Code have been used by cybercrime rings to extort data from healthcare providers, emergency services, religious groups, and governments within a single month (August 2025).  
- Defensive AI: Anthropic’s Mythos flags a high volume of vulnerabilities, overwhelming Microsoft’s patching capacity; however, access is limited to a curated list of large enterprises and critical infrastructure operators.  
- The cost and exclusivity of top‑tier defensive models leave most smaller entities without effective AI‑powered protection.  

## Expert insights
- **Jacob Klein** (Anthropic Threat Intelligence) notes that tasks once requiring a sophisticated team can now be performed by a single individual with AI assistance.  
- **Marius Hobbhahn** (Apollo Research) warns that a basement hacker with an open‑source model could ransom a rural hospital, shifting harm away from tech hubs.  
- **Michael Kleinman** (Future of Life Institute) emphasizes that the previous limiting factor—finite malicious hackers—has vanished, expanding the threat landscape for community banks, credit unions, local power grids, and similar services.  

## Outlook
- Without broader, affordable access to defensive AI and increased investment in cybersecurity staffing, small organizations risk repeated, costly disruptions.  
- Stakeholders call for responsible AI deployment and equitable security resources to prevent a widening gap between well‑protected corporations and the essential services that support everyday communities.
