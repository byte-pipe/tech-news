# China extends AI travel curbs to executives’ families, Bloomberg reports

## Key developments
- Beijing now requires spouses and children of certain top AI and chip executives to obtain government approval before traveling abroad, even for short trips.  
- The rule applies to founders and heads of strategically important AI firms whose work is deemed vital to state security; several individuals have already been notified.  
- It is unclear if all families already under existing curbs must comply, and the list of affected executives is expected to expand.

## Context and background
- Earlier this year, China imposed travel restrictions on senior AI staff at private companies such as Alibaba and DeepSeek to prevent key know‑how from reaching the United States.  
- The new measure broadens those curbs to include families, marking a shift from previous restrictions that mainly targeted researchers, nuclear scientists, and state‑owned enterprise executives.  
- Separate exit‑ban rules, effective 15 September, cover criminal investigations and potential breaches of industrial and technological security.

## Related factors
- Concerns intensified after Meta’s $2 billion acquisition of Manus, which Beijing ordered to be unwound and used to justify tighter limits on U.S. investment in sensitive tech firms.  
- A Carnegie study notes that more elite AI researchers now work in China than in the United States.  

*The Ministry of Industry and Information Technology did not respond to a request for comment.*
