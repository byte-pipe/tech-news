# Parents can help with their kids' online safety

## Overview
- Research shows parental monitoring and clear rules can reduce the harms of excessive screen time and social‑media use.
- Open, early conversations about digital habits build trust and help children seek help when needed.

## 5 practical tips for parents

### 1. Set clear boundaries around device use
- Define which apps are allowed and require permission for new downloads.  
- Example: A mother limited her teens to texting and video calls, prohibiting Instagram and Snapchat.  

### 2. Keep phones away at meals and bedtime
- No devices at the dinner table or in bedrooms, following American Academy of Pediatrics recommendations.  
- Benefits: lower social‑media addiction scores, reduced risk of cyberbullying, better sleep, and decreased likelihood of disordered eating.

### 3. Create a family digital media plan
- Establish household screen‑time rules that apply to parents as well.  
- Parents modeling the same habits are linked to lower addictive use in children.

### 4. Be curious, not furious
- Engage supportively by asking open‑ended questions about apps and online content.  
- Share interesting online finds with kids and join them in games when appropriate to strengthen trust.

### 5. Start the conversation early
- Begin dialogue about digital life in elementary school (e.g., third‑fourth grade).  
- Topics: cyberbullying, online scams, privacy, image ownership.  
- Early talks allow parents to relax restrictions later and ensure children feel comfortable reporting problems.

## Key Takeaways
- Consistent rules, device‑free meals/bedtimes, and family media plans are effective safeguards.  
- Supportive, curiosity‑driven communication fosters trust and reduces conflict‑driven social‑media use.  
- Early, ongoing discussions equip children to navigate online risks responsibly.
