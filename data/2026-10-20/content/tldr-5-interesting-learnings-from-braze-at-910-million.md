# 5 Interesting Learnings from Braze at $910 Million in Revenue: A $5.66B Sale to Salesforce, 26% Growth, and Operating Expenses Up Just 6%

byJason Lemkin|5 Interesting Things,Blog Posts,Scale

Salesforce is buying Braze for $5.7B. That’s 6.8x revenue.

🎁 A gift for Braze holders. It traded at 3.7x on Monday.

Salesforce announced this morning it’s buying Braze for roughly $5.66 billion in enterprise value. Braze Class A holders get $47.50 per share in cash, a 58% premium to Monday’s close of $30.04.

Salesforce plans to plug Braze into Customer 360 to strengthen Marketing Cloud’s cross-channel personalization for brands reaching hundreds of millions of consumers. Marketing Cloud has always been strongest in email and weakest in real-time mobile and in-app engagement. Braze is strongest in exactly those two. Its roughly 2,770 customers include Canva, Instacart, Venmo, Equinox, Urban Outfitters, and Dairy Queen, a group of consumer apps Salesforce hasn’t won on its own.

Braze is also the largest of a long run of Salesforce deals in 2026. This year it has announced acquisitions of Contentful, m3ter, Fin, Listen Labs, and Loops, and closed Fin in September.

We run SaaStr AI’s emails on both Salesforce Marketing Cloud and Resend today and are evaluating what to use most going forward, so we’ll be watching how much of Braze ends up inside Marketing Cloud versus staying a separate product. Bill Magnuson says Braze will keep building as Braze.

Braze’s last few quarters have a lot in them for founders.

## Marketing Software Trades Around 3x Revenue, and Salesforce Paid 6.8x

Salesforce paid about 6.8x Braze’s trailing twelve months revenue of $834 million. The day before the deal, Braze traded at 3.7x.

The rest of the category traded in the same pretty … mediocre … range. HubSpot and Klaviyo were both at about 3.1x trailing revenue as of Monday’s close. Adobe was at 3.8x. Sprinklr was at 1.1x. Salesforce itself was at 5.3x. The broader public B2B median is 4.5x forward revenue, so marketing software trades below an already compressed market.

That’s what public investors think of marketing apps in 2026.Many of these companies charge by contact or by message, and investors expect AI agents to cut into both the seat counts and the campaign work those platforms are built around. They’re discounting that risk before it shows up in revenue.

Strategic buyers see the same category differently. A 26% grower with 2,770 enterprise customers and positive free cash flow is worth 6.8x to Salesforce because it can sell Data 360 and Agentforce into those accounts. Public investors don’t count that cross-sell.

For founders in marketing tech, that changes how you weigh your exit options. If your public comps trade at 3x, an IPO prices you at 3x. A strategic buyer who needs your customer base can pay roughly double that. Braze’s board had the public multiple and the strategic multiple side by side, and the strategic one was 3.1 turns higher.

## The 5 Interesting Learnings:

## 31. Braze Beat and Raised in September, Dropped 25%, Then Sold Six Weeks Later at a 58% Premium

On September 8, Braze reported $227.2 million in quarterly revenue, up 26%.It beat consensus revenue by about $6.9 million, beat on EPS, and raised full-year guidance. The stock finished the week down 25.1%.

Investors focused on moderating growth and AI revenue that wasn’t yet material, and discounted the margin and cash flow gains.

Six weeks later, Salesforce paid 58% over the closing price.

Public B2B companies growing 20-30% are being priced as pre-AI apps at risk of disruption. Strategic buyers price the same companies on their distribution, their data, and thousands of enterprise relationships that would take years to build. Braze’s board had a 58% gap between those two prices, and it sold.

## #2. Braze Grew Revenue 26% While Growing Operating Expenses 6%

Operating expenses rose 6% year over year to $170 million against 26% revenue growth. Free cash flow hit a quarterly record of $21.7 million, with $413.9 million in cash. Non-GAAP operating margin improved more than 600 basis points year over year, and management guided to at least another 400.

Braze decided to stop growing headcount at the same rate as revenue. A 20-point gap between revenue growth and opex growth takes deliberate hiring restraint, plus AI taking on work people used to do.

That also made Braze easier to buy. A $5.66B all-cash acquirer wants a target that generates cash, and Braze got there right before the sale.

## #3. Net Retention Settled at 110%, and Braze Pushed Upmarket

Dollar-based net retention was 110% overall and 112% for large customers, which was up 100 basis points from the prior quarter. Existing customers alone weren’t going to carry growth at 110%.

Braze put its effort into the biggest accounts. Customers paying $500,000+ a year grew to 361 from 282, a 28% increase. Total customers grew about 15%, to 2,789 from 2,422.

## #4. Professional Services Revenue More Than Doubled to $19.6 Million in One Year

Professional services and other revenue was $19.6 million in the quarter, up from $8.3 million a year earlier. Subscription revenue grew to $207.7 million from $171.8 million.

Services grew about 136% while subscription grew about 21%. Services went from roughly 5% of revenue to about 9%.

Braze didn’t break out the driver in its headline numbers. It matches what we see across B2B + AI: AI decisioning and agent deployments need hands-on setup to connect customer data and tune the results, and customers are paying for that work.

## #5. One-Third of Large Customers Pay for Braze AI, and Investors Still Called It Early

Paid adoption of Braze’s AI products reached roughly one-third of large customers, up about 900 basis points from Q1. Management said AI monetization was still early. During the quarter Braze expanded AI Operator, which lets marketers build journey steps through conversational prompts and deploy pre-built agents.

A third of your largest customers paying for AI within a few quarters is meaningful adoption. It didn’t help the stock because it didn’t change the overall growth rate.

Investors in 2026 want AI to create new budget, visible as faster growth or larger deals. An attach rate among existing customers didn’t get Braze credit for that.

The related founder decision is how to package and report AI. Bundled into the core product, its impact is hard to prove. As a separate SKU with its own price, you can report attach rate, deal size uplift, and contribution to growth. Braze could show the attach rate but not yet the growth contribution, and the stock reflected that.

## And a Few More Interesting Learnings

* $47.50 is below Braze’s 2021 IPO price of $65.Even with a 58% premium, IPO buyers are underwater five years later. Many 2021 B2B IPOs are ending in acquisitions below their listing price.
* Salesforce stock fell on the news.Shares dropped 2.6% in premarket trading. Investors have been skeptical of the acquisition pace, and Salesforce keeps buying.
* The founders ran it for 15 years.Bill Magnuson and Jon Hyman started the company as Appboy in 2011 and are still running it at a near-$1B revenue run rate.
* About 6.2x current-year revenue.Braze guided fiscal 2027 revenue to $910 to $913 million. $5.66B on that is roughly 6.2x for a 26% grower with positive free cash flow, a useful marker for where strategic M&A prices non-AI-native B2B apps in 2026.
* The close is months away.The deal is targeted to close in Salesforce’s fiscal 2028, which starts in early February. Braze’s competitors will use that window to pitch Braze customers who don’t want to end up on Salesforce.
