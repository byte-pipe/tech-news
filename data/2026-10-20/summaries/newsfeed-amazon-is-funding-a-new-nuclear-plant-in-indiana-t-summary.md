# Amazon is funding a new nuclear plant in Indiana

## Overview
- Amazon has partnered with X‑energy and American Electric Power (AEP) to fund a new nuclear facility in Fairbanks, Sullivan County, Indiana.  
- The plant is intended to replace the former coal‑fired Breed Generating Station and supply up to 960 MW of carbon‑free electricity to the regional grid and Amazon’s data centers.

## Project specifics
- The design calls for 12 Xe‑100 small modular reactors (SMRs), an advanced reactor type still undergoing development and licensing.  
- Xe‑100 reactors can be cooled by air, water, or a hybrid system, allowing deployment in water‑constrained areas without increasing local water demand.  
- Amazon estimates the project could generate about 1,000 construction jobs and 100 permanent positions, though no construction or operation timeline has been announced.

## Historical and broader context
- Indiana previously hosted the Marble Hill Nuclear Power Plant, an expensive project abandoned in 1983 after $2.5 billion was spent; the cost of Amazon’s agreement has not been disclosed, preventing direct comparison.  
- This is the second collaboration between Amazon and X‑energy. Together they aim to help regional utilities add more than 5 GW of new nuclear capacity in the United States by 2039.  
- Earlier, Amazon previewed its first planned nuclear plant, the Cascade Advanced Energy Facility in Washington, also slated for 12 advanced reactors with construction expected toward the end of the decade.  
- Other tech giants—including Meta, Microsoft, and Google—are similarly exploring nuclear projects to power AI‑intensive data centers.  
- Amazon’s investment in grid infrastructure aligns with a recent corporate blog post outlining commitments to address criticism of data‑center energy use, though benefits from the new plant will not be realized for several years.
