# Summary of “Chicago O’Hare Digs Up 50‑Ton Boulder Under Airfield, Asks The Public For Name Ideas”

## Discovery and Context
- A 50‑ton dolostone boulder was found ~30 ft (9 m) below the airfield while excavating the South Detention Basin for a storm‑water management facility.  
- The excavation is part of the ORDNext terminal expansion, the largest in O’Hare’s history, aimed at modernizing infrastructure and increasing capacity.  
- The rock did not disrupt flight operations but highlighted the hidden engineering work required for the project.

## Public Naming Contest
- The City of Chicago launched an online naming campaign in September, receiving nearly 3,000 suggestions.  
- Six finalists were selected:  
  - Sweet Stone Chicago  
  - No ORDinary Rock  
  - Chicago Rocks  
  - O'Rock  
  - Orchard Rock  
  - Da Rock  
- Voting is open until 5 p.m. CDT on 2 Nov 2026; the winning name will be displayed alongside the boulder in a new airport exhibit (the name will not be carved into the stone).

## Geology and Significance
- The boulder is composed of dolostone, a carbonate rock formed when magnesium‑rich groundwater alters limestone.  
- Its presence illustrates the geological conditions construction crews may encounter during large‑scale infrastructure projects.

## Impact on ORDNext Expansion
- The discovery has become a public‑relations opportunity, giving travelers a tangible story about the behind‑the‑scenes work at one of the world’s busiest airports.
