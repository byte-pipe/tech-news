# AGI Economics: Coordination vs. Competence — DeepMind Institute

# Summary of “AGI Economics: Coordination vs. Competence — DeepMind Institute”

## Overview
- The essay builds on the paper *Organizing Intelligence* to formalize how AI capability improvements affect market structure, employment, and wages.  
- It argues that the economy changes only when AI crosses two distinct capability thresholds: **competence** and **coordination**.  
- Most current AI benchmarks measure only individual competence, missing the coordination dimension that drives large‑scale economic transformation.

## Capability Thresholds
- **Competence**  
  - Improvements in individual tasks (e.g., math, coding, writing).  
  - Lead to smooth adjustments in wages, prices, and the mix of humans and AI within firms.  
  - Do **not** alter the overall industry structure.  

- **Coordination**  
  - Ability of agents—human or artificial—to work together effectively inside organizations.  
  - When a critical coordination level is reached, firms can scale dramatically, causing abrupt jumps in market concentration, employment patterns, and wage distribution.  

## Regimes of AI Capability
| Regime | Description | Economic Impact |
|--------|-------------|-----------------|
| Minimal AGI | Basic general‑purpose AI, competent on many tasks but limited coordination. | Small, gradual shifts in labor markets; industry structure remains stable. |
| Supercompetent AI | Highly capable on individual tasks but still poor at team integration. | Continued smooth wage and price adjustments; no major structural change. |
| Supercooperative AI | Strong coordination among AI agents and with humans. | Triggers rapid consolidation, large‑scale employment reallocation, and wage polarization. |
| Artificial Super Intelligence (ASI) | Both maximal competence and coordination; AI can self‑organize at scale. | Fundamental re‑shaping of the economy, potentially superseding human labor in many sectors. |

## Jagged Agents and Mosaic Organizations
- Human abilities are “jagged”: individuals excel in some domains and lag in others.  
- Organizations create **mosaic intelligence** by pairing complementary specialists, filling gaps in the collective capability profile.  
- Empirical quiz of 100 participants showed that matching specialists increased team performance by ~32% over random pairings.  
- Coordinators (managers) may score low on single‑agent benchmarks but generate high economic value by reducing handoff friction and enabling larger, more productive teams.  

## Implications for Machine Intelligence
- AI agents also exhibit jaggedness, but their weaknesses tend to align across models, limiting natural complementarity.  
- Scaling AI alone (adding compute) improves competence but does not automatically yield coordination benefits.  
- True economic disruption will likely arise when AI systems can **cooperate** with each other and with humans as effectively as human managers do today.  

## Conclusions
- The apparent disconnect between rapid AI progress and stagnant macro‑economic indicators stems from focusing on individual competence rather than coordination.  
- Policymakers and researchers should develop benchmarks that measure collaborative ability and design AI architectures that facilitate seamless teamwork.  
- Monitoring the transition between the identified regimes will help anticipate when AI will start reshaping market concentration, job composition, and wage structures.
