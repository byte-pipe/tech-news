# ASML CEO Christophe Fouquet on his company's monopoly: no one is coming for us | TechCrunch

# Summary of ASML CEO Christophe Fouquet Interview

## Company Overview
- ASML, a Dutch company with ~44,000 employees, spends €4.5 billion annually on R&D.  
- It produces the only extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography machines capable of printing the most advanced semiconductor patterns.  
- Each machine costs $200–$400 million, is the size of a school bus, and requires hundreds of suppliers.  
- ASML is Europe’s most valuable company, valued at over $530 billion.

## AI Demand and Chip Shortage
- The AI boom, driven by Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, and Google’s $600 billion AI infrastructure spend, has sharply increased demand for ASML’s machines.  
- Fouquet says the chip market will remain supply‑limited for the next 2–5 years; hyperscalers expect chronic shortages.  
- ASML is working to expand its supply chain and production capacity to meet this demand.

## EUV Technology and Pricing
- Low‑NA EUV machines are expensive but reduce wafer‑level production costs by 20–30 %.  
- High‑NA EUV, the next‑generation system, costs $350 million+ per unit but is designed for the next 10–20 years of chip manufacturing.  
- Fouquet emphasizes that higher upfront cost is offset by lower per‑chip cost and longer‑term relevance.

## Competitors and Startups
- **Substrate** (backed by Peter Thiel) claims it can build a rival lithography machine; Fouquet notes the gap between concept and mass‑production capability.  
- **xLight**, a laser startup partially funded by the U.S. government, is developing an alternative EUV light source; ASML is collaborating to evaluate performance and cost benefits.  
- Fouquet stresses that ASML’s success built on 80 % existing technology and two decades of solving the EUV light generation challenge.

## China Reverse‑Engineering Claims
- No EUV machines have ever been shipped to China; all units are tracked and either in customer use or returned to ASML.  
- ASML maintains strict internal segregation preventing Chinese teams from accessing EUV technology, documents, or training.  
- Fouquet considers reports of Chinese reverse‑engineering “simply wrong” and indicates little to no progress in that direction.

## Export Controls and Global Sales
- Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang argued for global sales to boost corporate revenue and tax contributions while keeping cutting‑edge tech domestic.  
- Fouquet agrees with the principle, noting that maintaining a technological edge while exporting responsibly aligns with ASML’s strategy.
