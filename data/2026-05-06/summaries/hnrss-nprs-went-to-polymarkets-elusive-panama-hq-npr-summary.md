# NPR’s went to Polymarket’s elusive Panama HQ

## Background
- Polymarket is a popular prediction‑market platform valued at about $15 billion.  
- After U.S. regulators shut it down in 2022, the company relocated its corporate base to Panama, citing tax and legal advantages.  
- The site’s terms of service require disputes to be settled through closed‑door arbitration in Panama.

## Investigation of the Panama Headquarters
- NPR visited the address listed in Panamanian government records: the 21st floor of Oceania Business Plaza in Punta Pacifica.  
- The listed address is actually a law firm run by attorney Mario García de Paredes; no Polymarket signage or staff were present.  
- Inside, a generic corporate lobby led to a room with about a dozen empty computer stations.  
- Neither Polymarket nor its Panamanian entity, Adventure One QSS Inc., responded to NPR’s request for comment, and García de Paredes was unavailable.

## Shared Office Space with Other Crypto Firms
- Public records show at least 15 other cryptocurrency companies (e.g., Helix, Drift Protocol, Goldfinch, Parti) also list the same law office as their headquarters.  
- The firm previously handled legal work for the collapsed exchange FTX and is listed as owed $13,889 in a bankruptcy filing.

## Regulatory History and Legal Context
- In 2022, the Biden administration fined Polymarket $1.4 million and forced it to cease U.S. operations for operating without a license.  
- FBI agents later raided CEO Shayne Coplan’s Manhattan apartment as part of a probe into compliance with that settlement.  
- The Justice Department eventually dropped the investigation; Donald Jr. Trump became an adviser, and his firm 1789 Capital invested millions.  
- Despite political support, the U.S. version of Polymarket remains technically banned; U.S. users can only view markets in “view‑only mode.”

## Growth and Trading Volume
- April 2026 saw over $8 billion traded on Polymarket alone, up from under $1 billion in April 2025.  
- Combined with competitor Kalshi, the prediction‑market industry recorded more than $24 billion in trades in the same month.

## Compliance Measures and Controversies
- Polymarket’s terms prohibit the use of VPNs to mask user location.  
- Former CFTC chair Timothy Massad questioned the platform’s geofencing effectiveness and suggested stronger location verification.  
- A recent indictment of a U.S. soldier for using a VPN to bet on Venezuelan political events highlights potential loopholes.

## Expert Commentary
- Washington lawyer Bruce Zagaris noted that using a shell company in Panama is a common strategy to protect assets and limit regulatory exposure.  
- Legal experts stress that while the practice is not illegal, it can be used to shield firms from lawsuits and government actions.
