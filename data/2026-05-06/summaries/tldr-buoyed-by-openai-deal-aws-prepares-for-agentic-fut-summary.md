# Buoyed by OpenAI deal, AWS prepares for agentic future

## Dive Brief
- AWS reported $37.6 billion in revenue for Q1 FY 2026, a 28 % year‑over‑year increase.  
- CEO Andy Jassy reaffirmed confidence in capital‑expenditure plans, committing $200 billion to chips, data centers, land, buildings and networking gear throughout 2026.  
- The partnership with OpenAI is described as “the most consequential infrastructure deal in enterprise technology in years,” highlighting its importance for enterprise AI strategies.  

## Dive Insight
- AWS is betting on AI agents as the primary source of enterprise AI value and is expanding compute infrastructure to meet higher‑demand workloads.  
- A limited preview of **Amazon Bedrock Managed Agents** powered by OpenAI frontier models allows customers to build stateful AI agents at production scale.  
- Jassy emphasized that modern agentic applications will be stateful and that the new “Stateful Runtime Environment” will accelerate adoption.  
- AWS is positioning itself as the “mission control” for autonomous agents, offering a managed environment with existing governance and security controls.  
- Tools such as Transform and Quick illustrate AWS’s focus on agents for coding, software migration and business operations.  

## Market Context & Infrastructure Investments
- Competitors Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud posted Q1 revenue growth of 40 % and 63 % respectively, indicating a broader shift toward AI‑focused cloud services.  
- All three hyperscalers are investing heavily in custom AI chips; AWS saw nearly 40 % quarter‑over‑quarter growth in its chips business.  
- Multigigawatt agreements with Anthropic and OpenAI represent $225 billion in revenue commitments for AWS Trainium chips.  
- A new agreement with Meta will use AWS Graviton processing cores to support agentic AI workloads.  
- Jassy noted that CapEx is deployed well before monetization—typically 6 to 24 months ahead—while assets have long useful lives (30+ years for data centers, 5–6 years for chips and servers).
