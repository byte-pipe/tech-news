# California farmers to destroy 420,000 peach trees after Del Monte bankruptcy  

## Background  
- Del Monte Foods filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in July 2025 and permanently closed its Modesto and Hughson canneries in April 2026.  
- The closures left hundreds of workers jobless and central‑California growers with cancelled 20‑year peach contracts and an estimated $550 million loss in revenue.  

## Federal assistance  
- A delegation of California lawmakers (Sen. Adam Schiff, Reps. Mike Thompson and David Valadao, plus 39 other members of Congress) petitioned the USDA for aid.  
- The USDA approved funding to pay growers to remove approximately 3,000 acres of clingstone peach trees before harvest.  
- The program could help eliminate about 50,000 tons of peaches from production, saving growers an estimated $30 million and providing up to $9 million in direct federal aid.  

## Impact on growers  
- Removing the trees is seen as a necessary step to prevent “long‑term structured damage” to multigenerational family farms in the Central Valley.  
- Quotes from lawmakers emphasize that the sudden loss of processing capacity for 55,000 acres of fruit cannot be absorbed by individual farms.  

## Market outcome  
- Pacific Coast Producers, based in Lodi, purchased Del Monte’s canned‑fruit business after court approval.  
- The company offered contracts for 24,000 tons of peaches, but roughly 50,000 tons will remain unused, prompting the tree‑removal program.
