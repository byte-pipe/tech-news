# Summary of “I’m Scared About Biological Computing”

## Background
- The author has been involved in AI since the release of ChatGPT, experimenting with language models, building projects, and studying the underlying mathematics.
- Personal interest in the game DOOM, including work on mapping WAD files to run in unconventional formats.

## Encounter with Biological Neural Network
- Saw a video from a company that cultured neurons in a lab and trained them to play DOOM, achieving performance better than the author’s own implementations.
- The experience lingered, prompting deeper reflection on the implications.

## Ethical and Philosophical Concerns
- Traditional LLMs are dismissed as non‑conscious because they are token predictors without inner experience.
- The neuron‑based system blurs that line: it processes visual input and reacts, raising the question of whether it “sees” or experiences.
- Raises the possibility that a human‑derived biocomputer could be placed in a perpetual simulated hell (repeating the same game) using reward mechanisms similar to those used for LLMs.

## Questions About Consciousness
- How can we determine if such a neural construct is conscious?
- Who has the authority to make that determination?
- The system contains 200,000 neurons—more than a jellyfish or worm—challenging the notion that it is too small to be a “person.”

## Commercial Incentives and Risks
- Human brain tissue could store far more information with greater efficiency than silicon, creating strong commercial motivation.
- Historical patterns show that technologies deemed “wrong” (e.g., mass surveillance, black markets) persist because of profit; the same could apply to biocomputing.

## Open‑Ended Reflection
- The author has no definitive conclusion and feels uncomfortable with the lack of public discourse on the creation and use of such biological computers.
