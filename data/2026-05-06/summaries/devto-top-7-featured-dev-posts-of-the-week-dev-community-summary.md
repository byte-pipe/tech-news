# Top 7 Featured DEV Posts of the Week – Summary

## 1. Big Tech Is Firing Humans to Buy More GPUs – Syed Ahmer Shah
- Explains that recent layoffs at companies like Meta and Oracle are driven by a strategic shift toward expanding GPU infrastructure rather than financial distress.  
- Highlights the impact on early‑career developers and identifies emerging high‑value skills such as GPU programming and AI model deployment.

## 2. Automation is a Trade‑off – Sue Smith
- Revisits an earlier discussion on AI‑assisted code generation and the abstractions it introduces.  
- Raises concerns about hidden decision‑making, accountability, and the balance between developer empowerment and reliance on automation.

## 3. My Speaker Broke, So I Built a LAN Speaker – Shrijal Acharya
- Describes the creation of **Gophercast**, a Go‑based tool that streams audio over WebSockets to multiple devices on a local network.  
- Details technical choices, notably why WebSocket was preferred over UDP and WebRTC for this use case.

## 4. Why I’m Building SaaS in 2026 – arunkant
- Counters the “SaaS is dead” narrative by arguing that shipping AI agents without solid workflow integration is the real mistake.  
- Draws on experience with the Releasedog project to illustrate when to leverage AI flexibility versus locking functionality into reliable software pipelines.

## 5. Are We Using AI at the Wrong Scale? – Kernel Pryanic
- Challenges the default practice of defaulting to the largest available AI model.  
- Presents benchmark data showing that smaller, specialized models often deliver more efficient and honest results for many code‑related tasks.

## 6. Sleep, Sort, Repeat: Testing Kotlin Coroutines with Virtual Time – Tiberiu Tofan
- Uses the whimsical “Sleep Sort” algorithm to demonstrate Kotlin’s `runTest` and virtual time mechanisms.  
- Provides a practical guide for testing time‑dependent coroutine code without incurring real‑world delays.

## 7. I Don’t Like Tailwind. Sorry Not Sorry – Andy Robinson
- Advocates for hand‑crafted CSS over utility‑class frameworks like Tailwind.  
- Employs a pumpkin‑pie analogy to argue that defaulting to Tailwind can hinder skill development and diminish design character.
