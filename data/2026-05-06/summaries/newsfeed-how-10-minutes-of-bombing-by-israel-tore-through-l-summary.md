# Summary of “‘Nothing will bring back my son’: How 10 minutes of bombing by Israel shattered lives in Lebanon”

## Background
- On 8 April, Israel launched a wave of air strikes lasting about 10 minutes, hitting roughly 100 targets across Lebanon.  
- The operation was presented as targeting Hezbollah command centres and military sites.  
- Lebanese authorities recorded 361 deaths and more than 1,000 injuries that day.  
- A temporary US‑Iran ceasefire had just been announced, raising hopes that Lebanon would be spared, but the strikes began nonetheless.

## Impact on Hay el Sellom (southern Beirut)
- Hay el Sellom, a densely populated neighbourhood, had previously endured evacuation orders and occasional strikes but remained relatively calm.  
- At 14:15 local time, multiple bombs hit the area; at least five strikes hit the neighbourhood in rapid succession.  
- Buildings collapsed, turning streets into rubble‑strewn corridors that hampered rescue efforts.  
- The Lebanese health ministry reported over 80 deaths in the neighbourhood, including at least 15 children.

## Personal testimonies
- **Mohammed** lost his son Abbas when three floors of the building collapsed onto the room where the boy slept. He described losing a second home and said nothing could bring his son back.  
- **Ghassan Jawad** survived a collapse that buried him and his family; he was rescued after about 10 minutes, but his mother, two sisters and their children were killed.  
- **Noha**, a fitness instructor in the central Beirut neighbourhood of Corniche al Mazraa, survived a bomb that hit a nearby warehouse, killing 16 people. She said the target was civilian and questioned why the area was struck.  

## Simultaneous strikes in other locations
- **Corniche al Mazraa (central Beirut)**: bombs hit a gym, a restaurant and a barber shop, killing 16. No evidence of a Hezbollah target was found.  
- **Sidon (south)**: the al‑Zahraa religious complex, affiliated with Hezbollah, was flattened; two young women, Rahma (27) and Rayan (22), were killed while praying.  
- Additional strikes were reported within a one‑mile radius of the gym and across the country—from Hermel in the north to villages in the deep south—occurring within the same 10‑minute window.

## Hezbollah context and Israeli response
- Hezbollah, an Iran‑backed militia, had fired rockets into Israel on 2 March, prompting Israeli retaliation aimed at its leadership.  
- Some Israeli media claimed a senior Hezbollah figure, Ali Mohammed Ghulam Dahini, was killed in Hay el Sellom, but the IDF did not confirm targets or respond to inquiries.  
- Residents expressed sympathy for Hezbollah’s call to defend Lebanon, though many emphasized the civilian cost of the strikes.

## Casualties and aftermath
- Civilian death tolls far exceeded any previous day in the conflict, with significant numbers of children among the victims.  
- Narrow streets and tightly packed buildings slowed rescue operations, leaving many trapped under rubble.  
- Survivors described the sudden shift from ordinary daily life to silence and devastation within minutes.  
- The attacks underscored the disparity between Israel’s stated military objectives and the extensive civilian suffering inflicted during the brief but intense bombing window.
