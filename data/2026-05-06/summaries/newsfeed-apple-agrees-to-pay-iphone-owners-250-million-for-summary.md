# Apple agrees to pay iPhone owners $250 million for not delivering AI Siri

## Key details of the settlement
- Apple will pay a total of $250 million to resolve a U.S. class‑action lawsuit.  
- Eligible claimants are owners of any iPhone 16 model or the iPhone 15 Pro purchased between 10 June 2024 and 29 March 2025.  
- Claimants can receive a base payment of $25 per qualifying device; the amount may vary from $25 up to $95 depending on claim volume and other factors.  

## Background of the lawsuit
- The suit alleged that Apple’s advertising created a “clear and reasonable consumer expectation” that full Apple Intelligence features, including an upgraded Siri, would be available at the launch of the iPhone 16.  
- When the iPhone 16 shipped, Apple labeled it “built for Apple Intelligence” but many promised capabilities were missing or delayed.  
- Apple later rolled out AI features such as Image Playground, Genmoji, and a ChatGPT integration in Siri, while the personalized Siri update was postponed to later in the year.  
- The National Advertising Division (NAD) had previously recommended that Apple stop or modify its “available now” claim on the Apple Intelligence webpage and pulled an ad featuring Bella Ramsey using the AI‑upgraded Siri.  

## Apple’s response
- Apple denied any wrongdoing in its statement to The Verge.  
- The company highlighted that since launching Apple Intelligence it has added dozens of features (Visual Intelligence, Live Translation, Writing Tools, Genmoji, Clean Up, etc.) across its platforms with privacy protections.  
- Apple said the settlement allows it to stay focused on delivering innovative products and services.  

## Implications for consumers and the industry
- Eligible iPhone owners can submit claims to receive a cash payout, providing direct compensation for the perceived shortfall in AI functionality.  
- The case underscores growing regulatory and consumer scrutiny of how tech firms market AI capabilities.  
- Apple’s gradual rollout of AI features and the settlement may influence how future product announcements frame AI readiness.
