# Summary of “Altara secures $7M to bridge the data gap that’s slowing down physical sciences”

## Problem in Physical‑Science Companies
- Batteries, semiconductors, and medical‑device firms generate massive, fragmented data stored in spreadsheets and legacy systems.  
- Engineers spend weeks or months manually gathering sensor logs, temperature, moisture data, and historical failure reports to diagnose product failures.  

## Altara’s Solution
- Provides an AI‑driven “intelligence layer” that aggregates disparate technical data into a single platform.  
- Claims to reduce manual data triage from weeks to minutes, similar to how site‑reliability engineers (SREs) use observability stacks for software outages.  
- Positions itself as the hardware counterpart to AI‑based software‑failure tools like Greylock‑backed Resolve.  

## Funding Round
- Secured $7 million in seed funding.  
- Round led by Greylock with participation from Neo, BoxGroup, Liquid 2 Ventures, and Jeff Dean.  

## Founders
- **Eva Tuecke** – former particle‑physics researcher at Fermilab and SpaceX employee.  
- **Catherine Yeo** – ex‑AI engineer at Warp.  
- Both met while studying computer science at Harvard University.  

## Market Context
- Other AI startups (e.g., Periodic Labs, Radical AI) are also targeting scientific research, but Altara focuses on a low‑capital‑intensive model that plugs into existing data infrastructures rather than replacing legacy R&D operations.  
- Greylock’s partner Corinne Riley views AI for physical sciences as “the next big frontier,” anticipating rapid growth in the sector.
