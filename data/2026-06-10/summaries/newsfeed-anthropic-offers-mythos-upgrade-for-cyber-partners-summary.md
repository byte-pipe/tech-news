# Anthropic Offers Mythos Upgrade for Cyber Partners and a “Safe” Version for the Rest of You

## Main Announcements
- Anthropic launched two new models on Tuesday: **Claude Fable 5** (public) and **Claude Mythos 5** (restricted).
- Both models are built on the same underlying architecture, but Mythos 5 is offered only to a limited set of industry partners and select biology researchers.
- The company is collaborating with the U.S. government on the rollout of Mythos 5 through the **Project Glasswing** consortium.

## Claude Fable 5 – “Safe” Public Release
- Includes **guardrails** that block answers to queries about cybersecurity, biology, and chemistry; such requests are redirected to the older model Claude Opus 4.8.
- If the system detects attempts at model distillation (training a smaller model from its outputs), those queries are also rerouted to Opus 4.8.
- The guardrails are deliberately **over‑cautious**, meaning some benign questions may be sent to the less capable model.
- Anthropic reports over 1,000 hours of red‑team testing with no universal jailbreaks discovered.

## Claude Mythos 5 – Limited Access
- Provides advanced **software‑vulnerability discovery** capabilities, enabling the design of hacking tools that can target both new and legacy software.
- Access is limited to Project Glasswing partners and a small group of biology researchers until a broader “trusted access program” is launched.
- Anthropic acknowledges that robust safeguards are still needed before offering Mythos‑level capabilities to the general public.

## Pricing and Performance
- Both Fable 5 and Mythos 5 are priced at **$10 per million input tokens** and **$50 per million output tokens**, double the cost of Anthropic’s standard public models but cheaper than the earlier Mythos Preview.
- Fable 5 delivers improved performance on software‑engineering tasks and visual‑understanding workloads.

## Business and Competitive Context
- Anthropic, like OpenAI, is preparing for an IPO and is racing to showcase cutting‑edge models to investors.
- Both companies have privately released models with advanced cybersecurity abilities and have formed working groups similar to Project Glasswing.
- Anthropic predicts that competitors, including open‑weight models, will eventually match Mythos‑level capabilities, raising industry‑wide security concerns.

## Future Outlook
- Anthropic aims to refine its classifiers to reduce false positives in query routing, eventually allowing broader, safer access to Mythos‑class models.
- The company emphasizes the need for **highly robust safeguards** to prevent misuse of AI‑driven cyber capabilities before a general release can be considered safe.
