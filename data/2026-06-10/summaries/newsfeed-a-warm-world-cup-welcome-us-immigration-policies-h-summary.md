# A warm World Cup welcome? U.S. immigration policies have chilling effect

## Overview
- As the 2026 FIFA World Cup begins, U.S. immigration policies are restricting entry for officials, staff, and fans.
- The issue highlights a tension between the tournament’s inclusive image and restrictive U.S. visa practices.

## Specific incidents
- **Referee from Somalia**: Omar Abdulkadir Artan was denied entry at Miami International Airport for “vetting concerns.”
- **Iraq team**: A player was questioned for hours at Chicago O’Hare and later allowed in; a team photographer was denied entry.
- **Iranian team**: Players received visas, but more than a dozen support staff, including federation president Mehdi Taj, were denied. The team had to relocate lodging to Mexico.
- **Fans**: Over 40 Moroccan supporter‑association members were denied visas despite holding tickets and hotel reservations.

## Policy background
- 39 countries face a full or partial U.S. travel ban; visas are suspended for 19 of them.
- Four of the banned or restricted countries—Iran, Haiti, Côte d’Ivoire, and Senegal—are participating in the World Cup.
- The Trump administration cites national security and public safety as reasons for these restrictions.

## Official statements
- **Customs and Border Protection (CBP)**: All travelers, including athletes and staff, undergo case‑by‑case inspection; admissibility decisions are based on law‑enforcement, security, and immigration data.
- **White House Task Force (Andrew Giuliani)**: Described the Somali referee’s denial as “for very good reasons” without further detail.
- **FIFA**: Stated it does not control host‑country immigration processes and that visa decisions rest with the United States.
- **White House**: No comment provided to NPR.

## Reactions
- **Jules Boykoff (professor, author)**: Calls the tournament a “World Cup of exclusion” due to immigration policies and worries about tense interactions for fans.
- **David Niven (University of Cincinnati professor)**: Labels the immigration issues a “self‑inflicted wound” that compromises the competition.
- **Iranian Embassy**: Accused the U.S. of violating FIFA regulations and politically biased interference in sport.
- **Gianni Infantino (FIFA president)**: Asserted that fans will be welcomed in Canada, Mexico, and the United States, despite the visa denials.

## Potential impact on the tournament
- Possible shortage of qualified officials and support staff.
- Reduced fan presence and enthusiasm, especially from countries facing visa denials.
- Heightened political criticism of the U.S. as a host nation and of the Trump‑era immigration stance.
