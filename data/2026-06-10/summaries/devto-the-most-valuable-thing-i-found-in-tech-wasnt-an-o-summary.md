# The Most Valuable Thing I Found in Tech Wasn't an Opportunity

## Why I Joined Tech Communities
- Sought internships, mentors, advice, and career growth as an international student in the U.S.  
- Joined groups such as Rewriting the Code, Mentor Me Collective, CodePath, Girls Who Code, Boba Talks, SheFi, local meetups, and online forums.  
- Initially treated these spaces as resources for learning and opportunity hunting.

## The Loneliness Nobody Talks About
- Moving far from home created feelings of isolation despite being surrounded by people.  
- Tech communities unintentionally provided a sense of belonging and understanding.  
- Shared uncertainty and the struggle to find one’s place resonated across members.

## More Than Networking
- The lasting memories are the people: mentors who answered questions, members who celebrated small wins, and friends who remembered names months later.  
- Opportunities (internships, jobs, referrals) were valuable, but the relationships behind them were the true lasting benefit.  

## The Hidden Value of Community
- Communities offer perspective: seeing that others also face imposter syndrome, career doubts, and setbacks.  
- Realizing you are not alone turns a professional network into emotional support.  
- The most valuable “thing” is the reassurance that your challenges are shared.

## Leaving Didn't End It
- After returning to India, connections persisted across cities, states, and countries.  
- Community is defined by people, not geography; relationships survived time‑zone changes and flights.  

## From Reading to Participating
- Initially a silent observer on DEV, reading articles and discussions without contributing.  
- Gradually began commenting, sharing thoughts, and eventually writing posts.  
- Participation shifted the platform from a place to visit to a place where the author felt they belonged.  

## The Best Decision I Made
- Recognized that missed opportunities often stemmed from lack of timely information, not scarcity of opportunities.  
- Created “Dev Opportunity Radar” to broadcast internships, fellowships, scholarships, and program deadlines to help others avoid missing chances.  
- The initiative reflects the author’s desire to give back the informational access that helped their own career.
