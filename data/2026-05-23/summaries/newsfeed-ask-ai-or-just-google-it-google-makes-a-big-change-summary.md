# Ask AI or just Google it? Google makes a big change to a little search box

## Business
- Google is redesigning its homepage search box, keeping the single‑line look but making it dynamic: it expands for longer queries and accepts videos, images, and files for multimodal search.  
- The change signals a deeper integration of artificial intelligence with traditional web search, described by Liz Reid as “the best of web and the best of AI together.”  
- Critics warn that AI‑generated answers may obscure source provenance and reduce user agency, offering fewer links than classic search results.  
- The move aligns with the broader industry shift toward AI, after a year of testing “AI Overviews” that place short summaries atop search results.

## Technology
- Users are increasingly phrasing queries in natural language rather than keyword fragments, giving Google richer signals about intent, which can improve ad targeting.  
- Google is adding “agentic” capabilities: the search engine can perform ongoing tasks such as monitoring ticket availability, sending sale notifications, or scanning for local events.  
- Analysts note that while the AI integration could make search more personalized and boost e‑commerce, it may also limit the diversity of choices presented to users.  
- Experts highlight the growing opacity of Google’s algorithms; adding AI may make it harder for users to understand how results are generated, especially after past AI missteps (e.g., incorrect advice about glue on pizza).

## Consider This from NPR
- Shifting users from traditional link‑clicking to AI‑driven answers could threaten the “click economy,” reducing traffic for online retailers, advertisers, and news outlets—a scenario dubbed “Google Zero.”  
- Although the new search box will be uniform for all users, there are workarounds for those who wish to limit or disable certain AI features.  
- The article underscores the tension between innovation in search experience and the potential impact on the broader internet ecosystem.
