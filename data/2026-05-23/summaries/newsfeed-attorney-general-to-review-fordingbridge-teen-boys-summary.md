# Attorney general to review teen boys' rape sentences  

## Key facts of the case  
- Three teenage boys (two aged 15, one aged 14) raped two girls in Fordingbridge, Hampshire, in 2024‑2025.  
- The assaults were filmed on the boys’ phones and portions of the footage were shared online.  
- The court handed down youth rehabilitation orders (YRO) rather than custodial sentences, resulting in 11 rape convictions overall.  
- The victims were 15‑year‑old and 14‑year‑old girls; one attack occurred in an under‑pass beside the River Avon, the other in a field near Fordingbridge Recreation Ground.  

## Government and political response  
- The Attorney General’s office has received multiple requests to review the sentences under the Unduly Lenient Sentence (ULS) scheme.  
- Law officers (Attorney General and Solicitor General) will review the case within 28 days and may refer it to the Court of Appeal.  
- Former Home Office minister Jess Phillips called the sentences “unduly lenient” and said they send a bad message.  
- Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch described the outcomes as “a disgrace”; Shadow Justice Minister Dr Kieran Mullan said the sentences cannot be right and appealed them to the Attorney General.  

## Judicial reasoning  
- Judge Nicholas Rowland explained his decision to avoid “criminalising” very young offenders, opting for rehabilitation rather than imprisonment.  
- He acknowledged the seriousness of the crimes and noted that filming the assaults increased their gravity.  

## Impact on the victims  
- Victim impact statements described severe trauma, nightmares, shame, and a desire to die.  
- One victim read a poem in court expressing hopelessness; the other described feeling insecure in her own body.  

## Public and media reaction  
- The case sparked public outrage over the perceived leniency and the role of social media in encouraging misogynistic behaviour.  
- Jess Phillips linked the incidents to a decade of insufficient regulation of social‑media platforms and violent pornography affecting young people.  

## How a sentence is reviewed  
- Under the ULS scheme, any member of the public can request the Attorney General to examine a sentence they consider too short.  
- If the Attorney General and expert lawyers agree the sentence is out of line, the case is sent to the Court of Appeal, where three senior judges assess whether the original sentence should be increased.
