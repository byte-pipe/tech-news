# After Automation – Summary

## Key Takeaways
- **Paradox of AI**: At Every, extensive automation has not reduced human workload; instead, it has created more expert‑level tasks for people.
- **Human expertise remains essential**: As AI commoditizes repeatable knowledge, demand shifts toward work that cannot be fully expressed in training data, i.e., the “different” that only humans can provide.
- **Two emerging modes of AI‑augmented work**:
  1. **Agent employees** – autonomous agents that receive delegated tasks and act independently.
  2. **Human‑agent collaboration** – shared workspaces (e.g., Codex, Claude Code) where humans and multiple agents co‑create complex output in real time.
- **Benchmarks show rapid progress** but do not signal an imminent tipping point where all jobs disappear; rather, they reinforce the need for human oversight and expertise.
- **Every’s internal experience** serves as an early‑adopter lab, revealing patterns that later spread across the broader market.

## How Every Uses AI
- Deploys Codex, Claude Code, and other models across coding, writing, design, and customer service.
- Alpha‑tests new models from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google before public release.
- Automates routine tasks (e.g., 95 % of the CEO’s email replies) while humans review and refine outputs.
- Maintains a team of ~30 people who focus on higher‑order work such as editing, engineering, and strategic decision‑making.
- Examples of agent‑driven tools:
  - **Claudie** – drafts sales proposals, training decks, and tracks project todos.
  - **Andy** – gathers story ideas from Slack and creates first‑pass newsletter content.
  - **Viktor** – aggregates growth metrics, survey analysis, and turns discussions into research memos.
  - **Fin** – embedded customer‑service agent handling ~65 % of support conversations and closing 40 % of actionable tickets without human intervention.

## Two Modes of Working with Agents
### 1. Agent Employees
- **Coworker agents**: Tagged in communication platforms (e.g., Slack) to perform tasks on demand.
- **Embedded agents**: Integrated into product workflows for repetitive, high‑volume tasks (e.g., support chatbots).
- Operate asynchronously; humans set goals and later review results.

### 2. Human‑Agent Collaboration
- Agents act as co‑pilots within development environments (Codex, Claude Code, Claude Cowork).
- Multiple agents and a human share the same computer session, enabling real‑time, complex problem solving.
- This mode transforms AI from a delegable worker into an operating system for knowledge work.

## Implications and Outlook
- **No imminent “tipping point”** where AI fully replaces human jobs; automation expands the scope of expert work instead.
- **Economic impact**: Benchmarks (e.g., Humanity’s Last Exam, GDPval, METR) show AI achieving high performance on tasks traditionally done by humans, but they also highlight the growing need for human validation and creative input.
- **Future of work**: As AI continues to automate explicit knowledge, organizations will increasingly rely on human specialists to handle ambiguous, novel, or value‑adding aspects of work.
- **Strategic recommendation**: Treat AI as a collaborative partner that amplifies human capability rather than a substitute; invest in training humans to work effectively alongside agents.
