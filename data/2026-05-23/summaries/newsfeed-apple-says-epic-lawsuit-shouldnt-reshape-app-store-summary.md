# Apple vs Epic lawsuit: latest developments

## Apple’s petition to the Supreme Court
- Apple asks the U.S. Supreme Court to review a lower‑court ruling that enjoined its conduct for all developers on the U.S. App Store, not just Epic Games.  
- The petition argues that Epic never filed a class action and did not demonstrate a need for a blanket injunction affecting companies such as Microsoft or Spotify.

## Challenge to the Ninth Circuit contempt order
- The Ninth Circuit ordered Apple to allow apps to include links to alternative payment options and warned that charging a 27 % fee on those external purchases defeats the injunction’s purpose.  
- Apple complied with the link requirement but continued to charge fees on outside purchases, prompting the contempt finding.  
- Apple’s new argument contends that a federal court cannot hold a party in civil contempt for violating the “spirit” of an injunction when the injunction’s language left room for interpretation and did not explicitly prohibit fees on external payments.

## Epic’s response and recent court actions
- Epic Games criticized Apple’s move as “one last Hail Mary to delay a conclusion to this case and avoid opening up the gates to payment competition for the benefit of consumers.”  
- The Supreme Court recently rejected Apple’s request to pause additional proceedings while it determines whether the sanctions were justified.  
- This week Epic announced that Fortnite is back in the global App Store (except in Australia), believing the court will not allow Apple’s fee structure to remain unchanged.

## Ongoing litigation context
- The dispute began with Epic’s 2020 lawsuit over Apple’s App Store commission and has now lasted over five years.  
- Apple’s substantial financial resources enable it to fund prolonged legal battles.
