# Abortion issue evolves with Democrats in midterm elections

## Overview
- Voters rank cost‑of‑living concerns as their top issue for the 2026 midterms.  
- Democrats, who previously centered reproductive‑rights messaging, are adjusting their approach.  
- Campaign spending on abortion ads has fallen to roughly one‑quarter of 2024 levels.

## Changing campaign focus
- In the 2022 and 2024 cycles, Democrats spent more on abortion‑related ads than on any other issue.  
- Since January 2026, ad expenditures on abortion are almost four times lower than the same period in 2024.  
- Advocates say the crowded news cycle makes it harder for abortion messaging to break through, but they stress tying reproductive freedom to economic concerns.

## Linking reproductive rights to affordability
- Mini Timmaraju (Reproductive Freedom for All) argues that reproductive freedom resonates when framed alongside the broader economic crisis.  
- Voters who care about abortion also notice connections to rising healthcare costs, childcare expenses, and limited maternal care.  
- The message emphasized: “If you have the right to do something but can’t afford it, you don’t actually have access to it.”

## Candidate examples
- **Graham Platner (D‑ME, U.S. Senate)**  
  - Oyster farmer and veteran who highlights personal experience with costly fertility treatments.  
  - Calls for universal healthcare and childcare, stating that unaffordable services are not truly accessible.  
  - Positions himself against incumbent Susan Collins, whose past votes on Supreme Court nominees conflict with her current pro‑abortion‑rights stance.  

- **Rep. Angie Craig (D‑MN, Senate candidate)**  
  - Frames the right to decide when and with whom to start a family as an economic issue.  
  - Promises to codify federal abortion protections and oppose Republican judicial and cabinet nominees perceived as anti‑abortion.  

## Ongoing legal battles
- The Supreme Court recently ordered that the rule allowing mifepristone to be mailed remain in place, after a Fifth Circuit decision threatened nationwide bans.  
- Kelly Baden (Guttmacher Institute) describes the current situation as an “accidental detente” that is precarious and unsatisfactory.  
- Thirteen states maintain total abortion bans; access varies widely in other states.

## Outlook
- While Democratic candidates are scaling back explicit abortion advertising, they continue to integrate reproductive‑rights arguments into broader economic narratives.  
- The interplay between affordability and reproductive freedom is expected to shape campaign messaging throughout the 2026 midterm elections.
