# A Marc Benioff-backed startup thinks AI can solve the AI deployment problem | TechCrunch

# Summary of “A Marc Benioff-backed startup thinks AI can solve the AI deployment problem”

## Background
- Large enterprises struggle to get AI tools to work reliably on legacy systems.  
- Forward‑deployed engineers (FDEs) and professional services have proliferated to fill the gap.  

## Funding & Founders
- June, founded by former Salesforce exec Efrat Rapoport and three co‑founders (Ohad Hen, Barak Goldstein, Idan Tsitiat), emerged from stealth on Monday.  
- Raised $20 million pre‑seed, led by Marc Benioff’s Time Ventures, with investors including Michael Dell, Aaron Levie, and George Kurtz.  
- The founders previously built Bonobo AI, sold to Salesforce in 2019, and later worked on Salesforce AI projects.  

## Problem Addressed
- AI models must integrate with existing platforms such as Salesforce, ServiceNow, Databricks, Workday, etc.  
- Companies face fragmented data, duplicate fields, complex workflows, and years of technical debt.  
- Building an AI agent template is easy; making it operate correctly within messy legacy environments is hard.  

## June’s Solution
- Scans a company’s current systems to map business processes, identify bottlenecks, and design optimized, agent‑powered workflows.  
- Generates an automatic, step‑by‑step implementation roadmap (e.g., remove duplicates, connect data sources).  
- Users click “build” on each task; June constructs the necessary integrations within the organization.  

## Customer Example
- Paul Akinmade, chief strategy officer at CMG (U.S. mortgage lender), moved engineering to Claude Code but hit integration roadblocks with Salesforce.  
- After weeks of stalled progress, June provided a clear deployment map, allowing the team to launch agents safely before the official kickoff.  
- Akinmade emphasized that he would reject any product requiring additional FDEs; June met that requirement.  

## Market Implications
- June positions itself as a complement to FDEs and consultants, yet may enable enterprises to bypass those services entirely.  
- The approach challenges the notion that AI adoption inevitably drives more professional‑services hiring.
