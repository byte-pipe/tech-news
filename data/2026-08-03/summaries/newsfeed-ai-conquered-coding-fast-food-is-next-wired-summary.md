# AI Conquered Coding. Fast Food Is Next | WIRED – Summary

## Overview
- AI‑powered voice assistants are increasingly used in U.S. fast‑food drive‑thrus.
- In 2024 about 6 % of locations employ AI, up from 4 % in 2022.
- The technology promises faster service, higher upsell rates, and lower labor costs, but early deployments faced accuracy and customer‑experience problems.

## Current Adoption
- **Taco Bell:** AI in 890 drive‑thru lanes (≈10 % of U.S. sites).  
- **Dairy Queen:** Rolling out AI in 25 states, aiming for nationwide coverage.  
- **White Castle:** “Julia” AI in 12 % of locations; all new stores launch with it.  
- **McDonald’s:** After an initial 2019 rollout and later withdrawal, testing a new Google‑partnered system at five sites (English + Spanish).  
- **Other partners:** Presto (Dairy Queen, Del Taco), SoundHound (White Castle, Panda Express), Nvidia/Omilia (Taco Bell), Arc (Jack’s, Pollo Campero).

## Early Challenges
- Social‑media videos highlighted ordering errors (e.g., excess nuggets, bacon in ice cream, massive water orders).  
- Studies by Intouch Insight found AI drive‑thrus were **less friendly** and had **lower order accuracy** than human operators.  
- Accent and dialect recognition remained problematic, often requiring a human on a headset to intervene.  
- Companies such as Del Taco and McDonald’s pulled back or sold their AI assets after costly fixes.

## Improvements & Benefits
- **Speed:** AI drive‑thrus average 21 seconds faster than the industry norm.  
- **Employee workflow:** AI can smooth shift transitions and reduce repetitive tasks.  
- **Incremental gains:** Chains like White Castle focus on gradual enhancements rather than perfect AI from day one.  
- **Employee satisfaction:** White Castle’s AI‑assisted system contributed to its ranking among Fortune’s top 20 retail workplaces.

## Ongoing Concerns
- **Human connection:** Customers like Schereé Reed (Wendy’s) feel uneasy with fully robotic interactions, fearing loss of community.  
- **Brand image:** Chains known for hospitality (e.g., Chick‑fil‑A, Dutch Bros, 7Brew) avoid AI to preserve perceived service quality.  
- **Job impact:** Executives claim AI does not replace jobs; most drive‑thru staff now multitask rather than being displaced.

## Outlook
- The pendulum is swinging back toward broader AI adoption as technology matures and early‑stage bugs are resolved.  
- Continued testing (e.g., McDonald’s‑Google partnership) suggests the industry views AI as a long‑term component of the drive‑thru experience, albeit with options for customers to request human assistance when desired.
