# Report: Apple Sends Legal Letters to Dozens of OpenAI Employees

## Main Story
- Apple has sent legal preservation letters to roughly 40 former Apple employees now working at OpenAI, requesting that they keep any potentially relevant documents and communications.
- The letters broaden Apple’s focus beyond the individuals originally named in its trade‑secret lawsuit, reflecting a belief that the alleged misappropriation may involve more people.
- Apple’s lawsuit, filed last week, claims OpenAI coordinated an effort to obtain confidential Apple hardware‑engineering information, including proprietary designs, manufacturing processes, and other trade secrets.
- Key former Apple executives cited:
  - Tang Tan – now OpenAI’s Chief Hardware Officer, a 24‑year Apple veteran who led product design.
  - Chang Liu – former senior system electrical engineer at Apple, now on OpenAI’s hardware team.
- Apple asserts that more than 400 former Apple employees now work at OpenAI, suggesting the misconduct is broader than isolated incidents.
- OpenAI has denied the allegations, stating it is “not aware of any evidence that this complaint has merit.”
- Apple is seeking an injunction to stop OpenAI from using any Apple information in its AI‑hardware development, as well as damages and breach‑of‑contract claims against Tan and Liu.
- Apple describes the evidence uncovered so far as possibly only the “tip of the iceberg.”
