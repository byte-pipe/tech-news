# After shocking quarter, IBM insists that AI isn't killing the mainframe

## Key financial results
- Q2 revenue: $17.2 billion; gross profit: $9.9 billion; margin: ~58%; net earnings: $2.2 billion.  
- Results fell far short of Wall Street expectations, prompting an unprecedented pre‑emptive warning from CEO Arvind Krishna.  
- Stock plunged 25% in a single day, the largest one‑day decline in IBM’s history.

## Mainframe decline and its ripple effect
- Mainframe hardware sales dropped 42% in the quarter.  
- IBM’s business model generates $3 of software revenue for every $1 of mainframe hardware sold, so the hardware slump threatens software earnings.  
- CFO Jim Kavanaugh highlighted the dependency of software revenue on mainframe sales.

## Reason given: AI‑driven cost pressures
- Customers slated to buy new mainframes redirected budgets to other hardware (data‑center gear, PCs) facing 15‑30% price hikes due to the AI build‑out.  
- Enterprise vendors such as Dell, HP, and Apple have reported similar component‑cost inflation, especially for memory.

## Management’s response
- Krishna described the shortfall as a “temporary blip,” noting that some customers have already placed orders this quarter.  
- He asserted there is “no evidence of clients moving off the mainframe” and expects future purchases of both hardware and accompanying software contracts.  
- IBM lowered its full‑year growth forecasts, acknowledging the quarter’s impact on the remainder of the year.

## Outlook
- The tech industry has long predicted the demise of mainframes; IBM’s leadership argues that even the AI boom will not eradicate the platform.  
- Future performance will depend on whether postponed mainframe purchases resume once price pressures ease.
