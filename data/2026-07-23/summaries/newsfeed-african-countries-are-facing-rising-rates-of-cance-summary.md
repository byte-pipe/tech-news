# African countries are facing rising rates of cancer. What's being done about it?

- Cancer diagnoses are increasing sharply in sub‑Saharan Africa and the Eastern Mediterranean region.  
- Despite the rise in cases, access to diagnosis, treatment, and follow‑up care remains limited compared with global standards.

- **Healthcare Gaps**: Many countries lack sufficient oncology specialists, diagnostic equipment, and affordable treatment options, leading to delayed diagnoses and poorer outcomes.  
- **Regional Initiatives**: Efforts are underway to improve cancer care, including:
  - Partnerships with international health organizations to train medical staff and expand pathology services.  
  - Investment in regional cancer registries to better track incidence and outcomes.  
  - Development of low‑cost screening programs for common cancers such as cervical and breast cancer.  
- **Policy Focus**: Governments are beginning to integrate cancer control into national health strategies, emphasizing prevention, early detection, and access to essential medicines.  
- **Challenges Ahead**: Sustainable funding, infrastructure development, and overcoming stigma remain major obstacles to closing the care gap.
