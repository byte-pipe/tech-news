# Agentic orchestration: Enterprise AI organizations have a deployment problem, not a platform problem — and most are calling chatbots agents

## Overview
- Across 101 enterprises, orchestration is consolidating onto model‑provider platforms; Anthropic’s Claude is the clear leader.  
- The ambition to run sophisticated, multi‑step agents far outpaces reality: most deployed “agents” are still single‑prompt chatbot wrappers.  
- Enterprises seek hybrid control planes to avoid lock‑in, yet real‑time fiscal control over token usage remains rare.

## Methodology
- Survey of 101 organizations with ≥ 100 employees, conducted in June 2026 (single‑wave, cross‑sectional).  
- Size distribution: 100–499, 2,500–9,999, and 50,000+ (each 21%); 10,000–49,999 and 500–2,499 (each 19%).  
- Respondent roles: product/program managers (15%), CIO/CTO/CISO (13%), consultants/advisors (13%), various data/AI/engineering directors and VPs, plus “Other” (18%).  
- 81% are recommenders, influencers, or final decision‑makers for AI solutions.  
- Industry breakdown: Technology/Software (44%), Financial Services (17%), Healthcare/Life Sciences (8%).  
- Findings are descriptive; the sample is self‑selected and not a probability sample.

## Key Findings

### Finding 1 – Orchestration runs on model‑provider platforms
- 40% use Anthropic’s Claude platform & Agent Skills (more than double any rival).  
- 18% use Microsoft AI Foundry / Copilot Studio.  
- 13% use OpenAI’s Agents SDK / Responses API.  
- 8% use Google’s Enterprise Agent Platform; 2% use Amazon Bedrock Agents.  
- 6% use LangChain / LangGraph; 5% build custom in‑house; 3% are not orchestrating.  
- Model‑provider platforms account for roughly 80% of deployments.  
- Overall platform rating: 3.94 / 5; “ease of implementation” lowest at 3.85.  
- 96% of respondents plan to change their orchestration approach within the next year.

### Finding 2 – Model gravity drives platform selection
- 21% choose a platform because of “model gravity” – native alignment with a state‑of‑the‑art base model.  
- 17% value flexibility across models and tools.  
- 17% prioritize ease of development.  
- 14% cite security and permissions.  
- 11% consider total cost of ownership (including control over agent execution).  
- Only 4% list performance (latency/memory) as a primary factor.

### Finding 3 – Success measured by reliable multi‑step execution
- Primary success metrics: task‑completion reliability (32%) and multi‑step workflow management (28%).  
- Reliability and execution consistency dominate optimization goals.

### Finding 4 – Orchestration ambition exceeds current reality
- 71% report that a quarter or fewer of their deployed agents are true multi‑step orchestrated workflows.  
- Only 10% have crossed the halfway mark (>50% orchestrated agents).  
- The orchestration layer is being built ahead of the orchestrated portfolio it is meant to run.

### Finding 5 – Hybrid control plane is the preferred architecture
- By the end of 2026, 51% expect a hybrid control plane (provider‑native + external orchestration).  
- Only 6% anticipate handing control entirely to a provider‑managed service.  
- Vendor lock‑in is the top risk (35%) driving the hybrid preference.

### Finding 6 – Investment focus and fiscal‑control gaps
- Spending priorities: agent workflow tooling (34%) followed by security and permissions enforcement (25%).  
- Fiscal control lagging: 27% have no real‑time mechanism to stop a runaway agent before the bill arrives.

## Implications
- Enterprises are investing heavily in orchestration infrastructure while the majority of agents remain simple chatbot wrappers.  
- There is a clear market need for tools that enable true multi‑step orchestration and provide granular, real‑time cost controls.  
- Hybrid control‑plane solutions and open‑framework integrations (e.g., LangChain) may gain traction as organizations seek to mitigate lock‑in while preserving flexibility.  
- Vendors that improve ease of implementation and deliver robust fiscal safeguards are likely to capture shifting enterprise spend.

## Conclusion
The current deployment problem is not a shortage of orchestration platforms but a mismatch between enterprise expectations for sophisticated, multi‑step agents and the reality that most implementations are still basic chatbot wrappers. Bridging this gap will require better orchestration capabilities, hybrid control architectures, and stronger real‑time cost‑management tools.
