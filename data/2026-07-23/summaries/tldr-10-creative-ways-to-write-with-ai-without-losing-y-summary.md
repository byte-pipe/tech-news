# 10 Creative Ways to Write with AI (Without Losing Your Soul)

## 1. Vibewriting – steer a draft by feel  
- Use rough inputs and iterative feedback instead of a perfect prompt.  
- Let AI generate a first draft, then edit it step‑by‑step (make punchier, cut intro, expand sections, tighten paragraphs, rewrite weak transitions).  
- Example: combined research data and personal notes to produce an article on Moltbook; the content lead called it “the most fun I’ve had writing for Ahrefs in ages.”  
- Works for blogs, newsletters, opinion pieces, essays, short research, and even presentation decks.  
- Starter prompt: describe the topic, share gathered materials, link a sample article, and ask for an abstract and outline.

## 2. Living Draft – keep a topic open and feed it continuously  
- Maintain a permanent draft that never feels “started” or “finished.”  
- Add any relevant material (links, screenshots, thoughts) whenever it appears; AI integrates it, removes repetition, improves structure, and flags gaps.  
- The final shape emerges over time, allowing the same draft to become an article, a talk, or multiple posts.  
- Example: custom app built with Letaido to document ongoing AI perception optimization work, automatically weaving new inputs into a coherent narrative.  
- Suitable for research‑heavy articles, long‑term projects, and exploratory topics.  
- Starter prompt: treat the chat as a living draft, integrate new material, improve flow, and highlight contradictions.

## 3. AI‑led Interview – let the model interview you  
- Instead of asking AI to write, ask it to act as a journalist and interview you on the subject.  
- The interview surface gaps in your knowledge, forces clearer explanations, and produces material that can be polished into an article while preserving your voice.  
- Example: used to document an SEO experiment on structured FAQs for AI assistants, resulting in a clearer, more engaging piece.  
- Ideal for thought leadership, founder stories, case studies, opinion pieces, and lessons‑learned content.  
- Starter prompt: “Interview me for an article about [topic]. Ask one question at a time, challenge vague answers, request examples, and then turn the conversation into a polished article.”

## 4. Recycle Existing Knowledge Base  
- Many new questions can be answered by repurposing already‑published content.  
- Point AI to your source‑of‑truth documents; it extracts relevant passages, removes duplicates, and assembles a draft grounded in existing material.  
- Example: 70 % of this article was assembled from scattered blog posts about AI chatbot traffic, resulting in a coherent, rank‑worthy piece that also captured a new search intent.  
- This approach saves time, ensures consistency, and expands the reach of existing expertise.  

## 5. (Additional ideas mentioned in the article)  
- **Prompt libraries** – keep reusable prompt templates for different content types.  
- **AI‑generated outlines** – start with a high‑level structure that you can flesh out later.  
- **Hybrid drafting** – combine human‑written sections with AI‑expanded paragraphs.  
- **Batch idea generation** – use AI to brainstorm multiple angles or headlines in one session.  
- **Content audit assistance** – let AI scan your archive, tag topics, and suggest updates.  

### Overall Takeaway  
- AI is presented as a creative partner, not a content factory.  
- The methods focus on making difficult, expensive, or impossible tasks feasible while keeping human judgment and voice central.  
- When used intentionally, AI can actually increase the enjoyment of writing.
