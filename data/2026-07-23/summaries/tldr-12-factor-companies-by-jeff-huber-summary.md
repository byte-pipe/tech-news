# 12 factor companies - by Jeff Huber

# 12 factor companies – summary

## Overview
- Inspired by the 12‑factor app manifesto, the article proposes a set of 12 (plus a bonus) principles for building modern, AI‑enabled organizations.  
- The author argues that the bottleneck in future firms will shift from human execution to human taste, judgment, and context.  
- Companies that adopt these principles will be smaller, faster, and create outsized value; those that do not risk obsolescence.

## Core Themes
- **Context ownership**: Keep full control over all explicit, tacit, and institutional knowledge.  
- **Intelligence as a service**: Treat AI capabilities as interchangeable resources that can be rented rather than built in‑house.  
- **Tooling vs infrastructure**: Build custom tools to protect unique context, but buy generic infrastructure components.  
- **Agent‑first hiring**: Automate tasks with AI agents before adding human staff.  
- **High‑taste, small teams**: Prioritize quality and shared judgment over size.  
- **Open internal information**: Reduce silos to give agents and teams the context they need.  
- **Continuous learning**: Design the organization as a compounding learning machine with short feedback loops.  
- **Rubrics and proactive agents**: Provide clear evaluation criteria and encourage agents to act proactively.  
- **Traceability and expertise encoding**: Capture production traces for improvement and codify expert knowledge for reuse.

## The 12 (plus bonus) Factors

1. **Own your context** – Ensure unlimited access to all data and derived insights; avoid tools that lock your knowledge.  
2. **Rent your intelligence** – Use interchangeable AI services to maintain flexibility and pricing power.  
3. **Build your tools** – Develop bespoke software that safeguards your unique context.  
4. **Buy infra** – Leverage existing serverless, database, and OS platforms to accelerate scaling.  
5. **Hire agents before people** – Automate repeatable tasks (e.g., resume screening) with AI before hiring staff.  
6. **Build small, high‑taste teams** – Small, trusted teams make faster decisions when taste is the bottleneck.  
7. **Maximize internal information openness** – Default to open sharing within high‑quality teams to enrich context.  
8. **Design a compounding learning machine** – Structure the firm to learn continuously and reduce cycle time.  
9. **Develop rubrics** – Provide agents with detailed instructions and scorecards that can be iterated over time.  
10. **Reactive + proactive** – Encourage agents to not only execute tasks but also propose improvements.  
11. **Store and learn from production traces** – Use real execution data to refine agents, not just generic telemetry.  
12. **Encode your expertise** – Capture patterns of issues and solutions so agents can learn from expert input.  
13. **Bonus – Think before you AI** – Human judgment and taste remain the ultimate competitive advantage.
