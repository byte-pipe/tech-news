# What was their crime? – BBC visit to Minab school where strike killed 120 children  

## Overview  
- The BBC team visited Shajareh Tayyebeh Primary School in Minab, southern Iran, to document the aftermath of a missile strike that collapsed the building and killed 156 people, including 120 children.  
- The strike occurred on 28 February, the first day of the US‑Israeli war with Iran, and is described as the deadliest single civilian incident of the conflict.  

## The strike and its immediate impact  
- The school, situated next to an Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) compound, was hit by a missile—experts say likely a US‑launched Tomahawk guided by outdated intelligence that misidentified the site as a military target.  
- The upper floor (girls’ classrooms) collapsed onto the lower floor (boys’ classrooms).  
- Survivors reported thick black smoke, a powerful explosion that sent teachers and pupils flying, and the building being reduced to “walls that smell of death.”  

## Testimony of teacher Amaineh Nademi  
- Amaineh, a 33‑year‑old first‑grade teacher, described the normal school day during Ramadan, the sudden announcement to close the school, and the moment the missile struck while she was holding two pupils.  
- She recounted the chaos: smoke, inability to see the children she was holding, blood, and the collapse of the structure.  
- She listed the names of students and staff killed, including a girl on the stairs with her mother and several teachers.  

## Rescue and recovery  
- Red Crescent worker Reza Besharati arrived within minutes, finding roads clogged with desperate families.  
- Rescue teams uncovered severely disfigured bodies; many families identified victims by shoes or clothing.  
- The emotional toll on rescuers was profound, with Besharati saying he could not sleep and was haunted by the images.  

## Political and military response  
- President Donald Trump initially blamed Iran without evidence.  
- Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth announced a high‑level US investigation, stating US forces “never target civilian targets.”  
- Preliminary US media reports suggest the strike resulted from outdated DIA data that marked the school as part of the IRGC base.  
- Satellite imagery and video analysis confirm the IRGC complex was struck by a precision‑guided Tomahawk, a weapon not possessed by Israel or Iran.  
- Historical images show the school had been separated from the IRGC complex since 2016 and was publicly listed online as a civilian school.  

## Iranian perspective  
- Iranian authorities invited the BBC to the site to demonstrate that the US campaign, presented as targeting Iran’s military infrastructure, caused massive civilian casualties.  
- Iran’s foreign ministry rejected US claims that the school was on an “active IRGC cruise missile base,” calling them “baseless fabrication.”  

## Ongoing impact  
- Teachers and families continue to pray at the ruined school daily.  
- Rescue workers are still searching for remains, and the community grapples with the trauma of losing a large portion of its children in a single strike.
