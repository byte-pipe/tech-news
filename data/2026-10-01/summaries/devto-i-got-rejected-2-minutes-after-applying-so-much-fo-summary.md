# I Got Rejected 2 Minutes After Applying. So Much for 'Skills-Based Hiring.' - DEV Community

ATS resume filters spark fierce debate among devs. A young candidate applied for a Junior Solutions Engineer role at a startup, highlighting experience in solutions engineering, automation-focused work, and a degree in STEM or Finance. The job description required Python, SQL, data tooling, automation, AI-driven products, and B2B software skills. However, the candidate doesn't have a relevant degree.

The candidate applied anyway, and was approved with a human-stamped job listing. They didn't receive constructive feedback on their application, as the hiring manager only stated the basic requirements for the role. The applicant felt that they were unfairly judged due to the lack of a degree in a relevant field.

The candidate notes that this is not an unusual situation and that they had, in fact, built a custom solution for an existing business problem, not a tutorial project. The hiring manager's decision to use an ATS and filter the application based on minimum qualifications and basic skills suggests that the hiring manager may not have taken the time to thoroughly review the candidate's application.

The candidate concedes that, in this case, a human-stamped job listing is a clear indicator that the hiring manager did not consider the candidate's qualifications. The experience of seeing their application rejected is not the point; rather, it is a reflection of a screening process that did not accurately assess the candidate's skills and experience.

The candidate concludes that this highlights the limitations of ATS filters and the importance of tailoring applications to the specific job requirements, as well as providing clear and concise language to highlight relevant experience and skills. The candidate's experience may not be representative, but it is an observation of a problem that tech hiring processes seem to be facing.
