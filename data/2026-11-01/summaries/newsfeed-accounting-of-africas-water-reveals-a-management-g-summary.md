# Accounting of Africa’s water reveals a management gap

## Main points
- The water cycle operates globally, but its accounting is done on a country‑by‑country basis.  
- Rainfall, rivers and groundwater freely cross national borders, yet water use statistics are reported only by individual states.  
- The reliability of the global water‑use budget depends on the weakest national reporting system; when a country cannot measure its consumption, that water is omitted from the worldwide accounting.  
- Missing data means that the unaccounted water cannot be allocated fairly among nations, creating a management gap for Africa’s transboundary water resources.  

## Implications
- Incomplete accounting hampers equitable water sharing and can exacerbate conflicts over shared basins.  
- Improving measurement and reporting standards across African countries is essential for a more accurate global water budget and for ensuring fair distribution.  
- Better transboundary water data would support sustainable water management, climate‑adaptation planning, and policy decisions at both regional and international levels.
