# Africa’s ‘unprecedented’ Ebola outbreak could reach UK, says top health official

## Overview
- Dr Jean Kaseya, director‑general of the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention (Africa CDC), warns that the current Ebola outbreak could spread beyond Africa, potentially reaching the UK and other continents.  
- He stresses that all countries must raise preparedness and treatment capacity now.

## Scale and Spread
- Confirmed cases: 10,091 (10,060 in the Democratic Republic of Congo).  
- Deaths: 4,891 (4,887 in DR Congo, 2 in Uganda, 2 in Kenya).  
- The outbreak began in Ituri province (DR Congo) in February, officially declared on 15 May, and has spread to Kenya, Uganda, Nigeria (Abuja) and France (a returning doctor).

## Comparison to COVID‑19
- Kaseya likens the Ebola spread to the early COVID‑19 pandemic, noting that initial “zero‑risk” assessments proved wrong.  
- He warns that without action, other nations could become new hotspots.

## Risks to Other Countries
- The virus could reach the UK and other non‑African nations if screening and containment measures are not implemented at points of entry and exit.  
- France is currently the only non‑African country with a confirmed case.

## Virus Details
- Caused by the Bundibugyo strain of Ebola, for which no approved treatment exists.  
- Vaccine trials are ongoing, with results expected in 3–6 months.

## Impact and Challenges
- Response hampered by:  
  - Shortage of personal protective equipment.  
  - Unpaid health‑worker wages leading to strikes.  
  - Ongoing conflict and insecurity in DR Congo’s Ituri region.  
  - Mistrust, misinformation, and recent cuts to global aid.

## Response and Funding
- Kaseya will convene African health ministers to promote cross‑border cooperation, screening, and testing.  
- He will request a “corridor of peace” in conflict zones to allow health workers safe access.  
- Funding status:  
  - $3 billion pledged overall; >$2 billion still outstanding.  
  - African nations have contributed $120 million; the remainder expected from the US, UK, EU and other donors.  
  - Kaseya warns that delayed funding will increase future costs dramatically.

## Calls to Action
- Immediate global financial support to African response efforts.  
- Strengthened surveillance and border controls worldwide.  
- International collaboration to accelerate vaccine development and ensure equitable access.
