# A simple explainer of the massive $6.3 billion crypto heist at Binance

## 1. What happened  
- On Thursday at 4:05 p.m. ET hackers accessed investor trading accounts on Binance.  
- In about 20 minutes they moved more than $6.3 billion worth of cryptocurrencies.  
- Binance, together with other exchanges and partners, has frozen roughly $3 billion of the stolen assets.

## 2. Who is blamed  
- U.S. authorities, including the FBI, attribute the attack to a North Korean military hacking unit known as **TraderTraitor**.  
- The same unit carried out the previous record‑breaking theft of $1.5 billion from the Bybit exchange.  
- FBI, USSS, and IRS‑CI say the hackers are converting the loot primarily into Ethereum and will launder it through DeFi and instant‑swap services.

## 3. Binance’s response  
- Binance says it will reimburse any customers who lost funds, though the exact reimbursement mechanism has not been detailed.  
- The exchange is working with law‑enforcement and other crypto platforms to trace and recover the assets.  
- Security analysts note that it is still unclear whether a specific flaw in Binance’s systems enabled the breach.

## 4. What the heist reveals about exchange safety  
- The speed and scale of the attack underscore the sophistication of state‑backed cyber actors.  
- Crypto exchanges hold both custody and trading functions, making them attractive high‑value targets.  
- Unlike traditional brokers, many crypto platforms operate under a weaker regulatory framework and lack mandatory insurance protections.

## 5. Wider implications for the crypto sector  
- Critics argue the incident reinforces the “Wild West” perception of crypto finance.  
- The lack of uniform regulation could allow exchanges to misuse client funds, as seen in the 2022 collapse of FTX.  
- Smaller exchanges may struggle to compensate users after a breach, raising concerns for investor protection.
