# Summary of “A Gulf non‑aggression pact could be the path forward | US‑Israel war on Iran | Al Jazeera”

## Main Argument
- A non‑aggression pact among the eight Gulf littoral states (the six GCC members plus Iraq and Iran) could provide a diplomatic route to reduce regional hostilities, especially amid the escalating US‑Israel‑Iran conflict.  
- The pact would aim to make disagreements less destructive by establishing clear rules and mutual restraints.

## Historical Context
- The idea traces back to a 1976 meeting in Muscat where all eight Gulf littoral foreign ministers discussed collective security after Britain’s withdrawal.  
- Subsequent events (Iranian Revolution, Soviet invasion of Afghanistan, Iran‑Iraq war) hardened exclusionary security arrangements, marginalising Iran.  
- In 2019, Iran’s foreign minister Javad Zarif proposed a non‑aggression pact, later endorsed by Russia; President Rouhani launched the Hormuz Peace Endeavour (HOPE) at the UN, but Gulf states dismissed it due to Iran’s support for armed groups.

## Recent Developments
- The US‑Israeli war on Iran has revived interest in a regional pact.  
- Saudi Arabia is reportedly discussing a non‑aggression framework with Iran, modeled on the Cold‑War Helsinki process.  
- Qatar’s defence minister, Turkey, and Pakistan have joined calls for GCC‑Iran dialogue.  
- Oman, leveraging its mediating role and recent hosting of US‑Iran talks (2025, Feb 2026), has put forward the latest proposal.

## Proposed Framework
- **Core commitments:** No use or threat of force, non‑interference in internal affairs, protection of civilian infrastructure, prohibition of support for cross‑border armed groups, and safeguarding navigation.  
- **Gradual approach:**  
  - Start with shared interests (territorial security, functional ports, energy exports, civilian protection, uninterrupted trade).  
  - Implement confidence‑building measures: military hotlines, maritime encounter rules, exercise notifications, rapid post‑attack communication.  
  - Begin with Hormuz navigation arrangements, contingent on US‑Iran de‑escalation.  
- **Verification:** Procedures for evidence collection, corrective actions, and diplomatic consequences for violations, while preserving the right of self‑defence.

## Challenges and Recommendations
- Trust deficit: Gulf states view Iran as a security threat; recent drone attacks on Omani territories underscore concerns.  
- Inclusion must translate into binding obligations, not merely reassurance.  
- Armed groups remain the toughest issue; signatories must commit to cease directing or materially supporting them.  
- External actors (US, Israel) and the Yemen conflict lie outside the pact’s scope, but the framework should still limit escalation among signatories.

## Implications
- Success would be measured by reduced attacks, functional crisis channels, and respect for sovereignty rather than signatures alone.  
- Even a modest agreement would establish the principle that disputes do not justify endangering another state’s security.  
- The pact would not end all regional wars but could make wars among the eight littoral states significantly harder to initiate.
