# Apple reveals ‘shocking evidence’ from ex‑employee’s MacBook in OpenAI suit

## Background
- Apple sued OpenAI in July 2026, alleging that former Apple engineers stole trade secrets to benefit OpenAI.  
- The lawsuit names former senior system electrical engineer Chang Liu, who left Apple for OpenAI in January 2026.  
- Apple has repeatedly asked the court to expedite discovery, while OpenAI seeks dismissal of the case.

## New Evidence from Liu’s MacBook
- Liu’s lawyers handed over the MacBook Liu used after leaving Apple.  
- An initial forensic analysis identified four key findings:
  - Liu downloaded a confidential Apple circuit schematic and used it in his work at OpenAI.  
  - Liu and OpenAI staff were aware of his unauthorized access to Apple’s third‑party cloud storage.  
  - After learning of Apple’s internal investigation, Liu instructed an OpenAI colleague to destroy evidence, and the colleague agreed.  
  - Liu employed a tool at OpenAI that shares the name of an internal Apple engineering application.

## Specific Allegations
- Liu ran a simulation in March using the circuit schematic in LTspice, an electrical‑engineering tool.  
- Messages from that period show Liu’s AI “agent” learned to operate LTspice and interpret results.  
- Apple argues that feeding trade‑secret data to an AI model can create “irreversible and continually propagating uses” of the secret.  
- Apple also discovered Liu accessed the schematic on a Mac mini that later synced via iCloud to the MacBook, prompting a request for access to the Mac mini as well.

## Legal Position
- Apple contends the defendants ignored the available evidence and are using “theories” to claim the data disproves the allegations.  
- Apple asserts the MacBook evidence demonstrates why expedited discovery is necessary and refutes claims of “fishing expeditions.”  
- OpenAI continues to push for dismissal and has not inspected the laptop.
