# Resistance to police surveillance tech like Flock cameras grows

## Overview
- Vandalism, blocking, and removal of Flock cameras reported in at least 36 states.  
- Individuals such as Brady O'Rourke use roof rakes with signs to physically obstruct cameras.  
- Social‑media videos provide “how‑to” instructions for disabling the devices.

## What Flock cameras do
- Automated license‑plate readers (ALPR) produced by Atlanta‑based Flock Safety.  
- Deployed by more than 5,000 law‑enforcement agencies; over 130,000 AI‑powered ALPRs on U.S. streets (according to DeFlock).  
- Capture billions of license plates monthly, recording make, model, color, and other vehicle details.  
- Police claim they help locate stolen vehicles, find missing persons, and deter car theft.

## Concerns and criticism
- Privacy advocates argue the technology violates the Fourth Amendment and enables “fishing expeditions.”  
- Documented misuse includes stalking ex‑partners, immigration enforcement without local consent, and tracking women seeking abortions.  
- Accuracy issues: a 2024 California police analysis found a 71 % false‑alert rate for Flock readings.  
- Centralized AI data storage raises the risk of abuse and mass surveillance.

## Corporate and governmental response
- Flock CEO Garrett Langley states the company does not own or sell data; cities control storage duration and sharing.  
- Recent product changes: shorter recommended data‑retention periods and restricted officer access to prevent misuse.  
- More than 100 municipalities have deactivated cameras or cancelled contracts with Flock or similar vendors.  
- NYU’s Policing Project and the ACLU call for stronger legislative oversight and governance frameworks.

## Grassroots opposition
- Vandalism tactics include stealing, sawing down, painting, crashing into, and shooting at cameras.  
- Legal activism: DeFlock’s National Week of Action registers over 25,000 participants for protests, education sessions, and other events.  
- Opposition spans the political spectrum—left‑wing groups, MAGA supporters, mainstream Republicans, and centrists—all expressing nonpartisan resistance.

## Outlook
- The movement against automated license‑plate readers is described as a “remarkable” grassroots uprising, unprecedented in the privacy‑advocacy community’s experience.
