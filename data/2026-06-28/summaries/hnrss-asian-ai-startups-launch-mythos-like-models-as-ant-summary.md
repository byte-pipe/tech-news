# Asian AI startups launch Mythos‑like models as Anthropic's export ban drags on

## Key developments
- **360 (China)** unveiled **Tulongfeng**, an AI security tool claimed to rival Anthropic’s Mythos, and **Yitianzhen** for automated cyber‑defence.  
- **Sakana AI (Tokyo)** launched **Fugu**, a frontier model positioned “shoulder‑to‑shoulder” with Anthropic’s Fable 5 and Mythos Preview, and built for agent orchestration via external APIs.

## Context of the U.S. export ban
- The U.S. government ordered Anthropic to stop exporting **Mythos** and its restricted version **Fable 5** to non‑U.S. entities two weeks ago.  
- The ban created a short‑term vacuum in the Asian market that the two Asian firms are now filling.

## Sakana AI’s positioning
- Launch timing was “coincidental,” but the company markets Fugu as a way to obtain frontier capability **without export‑control risk**.  
- Co‑founders (Ren Ito, Llion Jones, David Ha) emphasize:
  - Focus on affordable generative AI tuned for Japanese language and culture.  
  - Targeting Japanese businesses and government agencies to reduce reliance on U.S. models.  
  - No permanent shift away from U.S. AI; U.S. models remain important to Asia.  
  - Advocacy for preserving AI access for allies (op‑ed in Project Syndicate).  
  - Vision of “Orchestration Models” as the next frontier, providing a hedge against concentration of power.

## 360’s approach
- Tulongfeng automatically discovers software vulnerabilities; Yitianzhen automates incident response.  
- Founder Zhou Hongyi framed vulnerability‑finding AI as a **national strategic asset** and warned of “one‑way transparency” where only some actors possess advanced detection capabilities.

## Market implications
- Anthropic’s run‑rate revenue hit **$47 billion** in May 2026; the impact of the ban on Asian enterprise revenue is unclear.  
- The emergence of local alternatives (Sakana’s Fugu, 360’s tools) suggests a growing capacity to serve regional language and regulatory needs, even if U.S. firms later regain trust.
