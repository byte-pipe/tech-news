# Apple wants permission to buy memory from a blacklisted Chinese supplier

## Key points
- Apple is asking the U.S. government for an exception that would let it purchase RAM chips from CXMT, a Chinese chipmaker on the Pentagon’s blacklist for ties to the People’s Liberation Army.  
- The request is driven by soaring RAM and storage prices, which have forced Apple to raise prices on most of its products.  
- Legally, Apple is not prohibited from buying CXMT chips, but doing so would expose the company to serious reputational damage and could trigger future export‑control actions.  
- CXMT was slated for addition to the Commerce Department’s “Entity List,” but the move was paused while the White House negotiated trade issues with China.  
- It is uncertain whether the administration will grant the exemption; any approval would likely face strong political criticism.  
- Republican House China Committee chair John Moolenaar warned that partnering with a Chinese military‑linked firm would “help the Chinese Communist Party succeed” and increase U.S. dependence on China’s tech supply chain.  
- Apple CEO Tim Cook has previously tried to cultivate goodwill with the Trump administration, but the current request could still generate significant backlash.
