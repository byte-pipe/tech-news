# Republican candidates say they are healthcare champions despite cuts

## Elections
- Republicans in swing districts are airing ads that stress Medicaid fraud, rural‑hospital support, and affordability, targeting voters over age 35.  
- NPR’s analysis of YouTube video ads (mirrored on TV and other digital platforms) focuses on races classified as toss‑ups or leaning by the Cook Political Report.  
- The party’s traditional priority of repealing the Affordable Care Act has faded; the 2018 repeal effort is cited as a major factor in the GOP’s loss of the House and remains “extraordinarily unpopular.”

## Healthcare impact of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act
- The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) introduced stricter insurance requirements and cut funding for state programs.  
- The Congressional Budget Office projects about 10 million fewer insured Americans by 2034.  
- Early 2026 data show roughly 3 million fewer people enrolled through health‑insurance exchanges compared with enrollment levels when the bill passed.  
- A KFF poll from April indicates only about one‑third of voters approve of the Trump administration’s handling of healthcare costs.

## Republican response to criticism
- Political scientists note that congressional Republicans are pre‑emptively defending the reforms to avoid attacks from challengers.  
- Rep. Mike Lawler (NY) ran ads highlighting Medicaid reform that reduced enrollment of able‑bodied individuals and falsely claimed undocumented immigrants can no longer receive benefits.  
- KFF surveys show Republicans are trusted more than Democrats on “addressing fraud and waste” in government health programs; Republican voters want Medicaid to stay but be tightened to curb fraud.  
- Approximately one‑third of independent voters express distrust of both parties on issues such as healthcare costs, prescription‑drug pricing, corporate influence, and fraud.

## Funding for rural hospitals
- Rural hospitals, which rely heavily on Medicaid expansion funds, face heightened risk after the OBBBA cuts.  
- The Rural Healthcare Transformation Act (RHTA), co‑sponsored by Lawler, adds $50 billion for rural hospitals, far short of the $137 billion Medicaid cuts that affect those areas.  
- Implementation concerns include “clawbacks” if states miss outcome targets, leading to cautious rollout and waiting periods for patients displaced from Medicaid.  
- Maine Sen. Susan Collins promoted ads stating she directed $190 million to Maine’s rural hospitals, which comprise more than 60 % of the state’s facilities and are critical for local safety and economies.  
- KFF notes that many rural hospitals are in debt, and Medicaid remains essential for services such as births and discharges.

## Emphasizing affordability
- Over 50 % of Americans cite healthcare costs as a top voting concern, giving Republicans an opening to claim economic competence on health issues.  
- Candidates highlighting affordability include Rep. Ashley Hinson (IA), Rep. Mariannette Miller‑Meeks (IA), and Rep. David Valado (CA).  
  - Hinson blames insurers and pharmaceutical companies for high costs and points to work with Trump to lower them.  
  - Miller‑Meeks, a physician, attacks pharmaceutical companies and promotes her medical expertise.  
  - Valadoo promises to lower costs and protect Medicare and Medicaid while acknowledging his vote for the OBBBA.  
- An Urban Institute report shows ACA premiums rose 21.7 % between 2025 and 2026; health‑policy expert Jonathan Oberlander links the increase to actions of the Trump administration.
