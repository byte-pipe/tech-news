# AI hedge fund Situational Awareness may have sold its public portfolio, but it still has its Anthropic shares | TechCrunch

# Summary of TechCrunch article: “AI hedge fund Situational Awareness may have sold its public portfolio, but it still has its Anthropic shares”

## Background
- Situational Awareness is a hedge fund founded in 2024 by 25‑year‑old former OpenAI researcher Leopold Aschenbrenner.  
- Aschenbrenner, a Columbia valedictorian who joined OpenAI’s “superalignment” team in 2023, was dismissed in 2024 for improper disclosure of internal information.  
- The fund’s investment thesis centers on the belief that scaling AI will require massive expansion of semiconductors, compute, memory, and energy infrastructure.

## Recent Performance and Losses
- The fund posted a 439 % return for the year through June, with assets under management (AUM) peaking around $45 billion.  
- A sharp decline in AI‑infrastructure stocks and leveraged positions caused a steep drop in value; public holdings fell more than 30 % for several key names (SK Hynix, Sandisk, Bloom Energy, Nebius Group).  
- A July 24 investor letter framed the sell‑off as a buying opportunity, but follow‑up capital commitments fell short.

## Citadel’s Purchase
- Ken Griffin’s Citadel bought the bulk of Situational Awareness’s public stock portfolio, reducing the fund’s AUM to roughly $10 billion (down from about $20 billion a few months earlier).  
- Citadel’s strategy mirrors past moves of acquiring assets from leveraged funds; it already held similar AI‑infrastructure bets, indicating confidence in a sector recovery.

## Private‑Market Holdings Retained
- The fund did **not** sell its private‑company stakes, most notably a $5 billion Anthropic position (Anthropic valued at $965 billion in a May Series H round, with a potential IPO as early as October).  
- Additional private investments include chipmaker MatX and AI data‑center startup Fluidstack, which was in talks for an $18 billion valuation round in April.

## Early Backers and Outlook
- Initial capital came from Jane Street, Stripe co‑founders Patrick and John Collison, and Meta executives Daniel Gross and Nat Friedman.  
- While public‑market losses are significant, the retained private stakes could generate a windfall that offsets some of the downturn, depending on future valuations and IPO outcomes.
