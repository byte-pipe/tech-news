# GCC steering committee announces AI policy

## Policy overview
- The GCC steering committee has adopted an AI contributions policy recommended by the GCC AI policy working group.  
- The policy **declines** any *legally significant* contributions that contain LLM‑generated content or are derived from it.  
  - “Legally significant” follows the GNU Project maintainer guidelines, roughly **15 lines of code or text** for copyright relevance.  
- Maintainers may still **accept legally significant test cases** that were generated by an LLM.  
- Use of LLMs is permitted for **research, analysis, bug discovery, reporting, patch review, etc.**, as long as the output is **not included** in the contribution itself.  
- The committee states the policy will **evolve** and be **re‑examined periodically**.

## Community reaction (selected comments)
- **Enforceability concerns**: Some argue the rules are unenforceable and may encourage covert AI use, citing human nature and the difficulty of detecting well‑crafted AI output.  
- **Impact on contributors**: Critics warn the policy could deter contributors, especially newcomers, labeling it a “witch hunt” that may push people away from the project.  
- **Economic perspective**: Comments highlight industry motives to replace workers with cheaper AI tools, questioning the broader implications of anti‑LLM stances.  
- **Detection observations**: A few maintainers claim they can often spot “AI slop” in contributions, noting that reputation risk discourages regular contributors from using AI covertly.  
- **Alternative solutions**: Some suggest systemic actions like unionizing workplaces rather than relying on restrictive policies.  

Overall, the policy aims to protect copyright and maintain code quality, while the community debates its practicality, fairness, and potential effects on participation.
