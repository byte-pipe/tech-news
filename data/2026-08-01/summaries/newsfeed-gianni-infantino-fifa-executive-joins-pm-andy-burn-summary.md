# Gianni Infantino: Fifa executive joins PM Andy Burnham in questioning Infantino

## Main developments
- Fifa chief operating officer Kevin Lamour publicly condemned Infantino’s plan to sell stakes in competitions to private investors, calling it “the project of one person” and urging football leaders to ask the right questions.  
- The Asian Football Confederation (AFC) issued a statement supporting European, North, Central American and Caribbean confederations, saying the proposal lacks broad consensus and threatens the unity of the World Cup.  
- Senior adviser Carlos Cordeiro resigned, labeling the deal “a bad deal for football” that would “mortgage football’s future.”  
- Greater Manchester’s Prime Minister Andy Burnham described Infantino as “the wrong man” to lead Fifa and called the proposal “outrageous.”  
- US President Donald Trump, who has a close relationship with Infantino since his 2025 re‑election, said he has not discussed the plan with the Fifa president.

## Details of the proposal
- Fifa intends to create a commercial subsidiary, **Fifa Forward Enterprise (FFE)**, to run major events, including World Cups.  
- The subsidiary would invite “minority, non‑controlling” investments from external investors.  
- A JP Morgan‑prepared 25‑page document projects an increased payout of €24 million per member association for the 2035‑2039 cycle and mentions new business initiatives and incentive‑driven compensation.  
- The document does not reference women’s football.  
- If approved, the investor group would be led by **Thrive Eternal**, a US venture‑capital firm founded by Joshua Kushner (brother of President Trump’s son‑in‑law Jared Kushner).

## Internal reactions
- Lamour, who joined Fifa in November 2024 after serving as UEFA deputy general secretary, said working under the current conditions “defies belief and imagination” and expressed a duty to values over loyalty to the employer.  
- Cordeiro, a former Goldman Sachs banker and senior adviser for almost five years, highlighted unanswered questions about the deal’s purpose, timing, oversight and beneficiaries, and warned that member associations face a rushed vote in under 50 days.  

## Governance implications
- Approval would require a simple majority of 106 out of 211 Fifa members.  
- UEFA holds 55 votes, CONCACAF 35, and AFC 46; if each confederation’s members follow their governing bodies’ stance, 136 nations would oppose the plan.  
- The AFC criticized Fifa for setting the initiative’s direction without consulting stakeholders, exposing weaknesses in consultation and decision‑making processes, and sidelining confederations, member associations, and the Fifa Council.  

## Overall assessment
- The proposal has triggered coordinated opposition from senior Fifa officials, multiple confederations, and political leaders, making its passage through a member vote unlikely.  
- The controversy highlights tensions between commercial ambitions and the perceived duty to protect the sport’s universal character and future generations.
