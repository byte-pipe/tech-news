# Cisco cuts nearly 4,000 jobs to spend more on AI, reports ‘record quarterly revenue’

## Key Points
- Cisco is cutting nearly 4,000 jobs, about 5 % of its workforce, despite reporting better‑than‑expected profit and revenue for its fiscal third quarter.  
- The layoffs are intended to reshape the company’s cost structure and fund increased investment in artificial intelligence and cybersecurity.  
- The move follows a broader trend of tech firms (e.g., Cloudflare, General Motors) citing AI spending as a reason for recent staff reductions.  
- Cisco continues to grapple with security vulnerabilities in its routers and firewalls, which have exposed corporate and U.S. government networks, and it suffered a data breach last year affecting customer information.  
- In a blog post, CEO Chuck Robbins highlighted “record revenue” and “double‑digit growth,” while emphasizing strategic investments in AI across the organization.  
- Public filings show Robbins is slated to earn more than $52 million in executive compensation for 2025; Cisco declined to comment on whether his pay will be adjusted.  
- This is the latest round of cuts: thousands of jobs were eliminated in two separate layoffs in 2024, and over 150 positions were cut in 2025.  

## Context
- AI spending is increasingly being used by technology companies as a justification for workforce reductions.  
- Ongoing security issues remain a critical concern for Cisco, especially given the impact on high‑profile customers, including government agencies.
