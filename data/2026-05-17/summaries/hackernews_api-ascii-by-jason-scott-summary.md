# ASCII by Jason Scott

# Summary of “ASCII by Jason Scott”

## Overview
- The author rescued a warehouse of roughly 13,000 technical manuals slated for disposal.
- After eleven years of effort, the collection is now digitized and hosted on the Internet Archive.
- The project involved fundraising, logistics, volunteer labor, and collaboration with the Digital Library of Amateur Radio Communications (DLARC).

## Timeline & Major Milestones
- **Initial discovery:** Received tip about a warehouse of manuals about a decade ago.
- **Rescue phase:** Negotiated a hold, gathered volunteers, raised thousands of dollars, and moved the manuals to a temporary storage site and later to California.
- **Sorting & preparation:** Volunteers sorted the collection into dozens of pallets, deciding which items to scan.
- **Scanning:** DLARC funded the bulk of the scanning; remaining manuals were scanned by volunteers where possible.
- **Completion:** By early 2024 the physical rescue was finished; digital upload concluded later that year, resulting in 13,000 manuals online.

## Funding & Scanning
- Scanning thousands of pages is costly; a personal scanner could not handle the volume.
- General public donations covered part of the expense.
- DLARC provided the largest financial boost, covering most of the scanning costs.
- Some manuals were left unscanned due to ownership by HP/Agilent/Keysight and Tektronix, who prefer to handle their own digitization.

## Unscanned Manuals
- Manuals owned by HP (now Agilent/Keysight) and Tektronix remain only in physical form.
- Scanning them would have doubled the project’s cost without guarantee of better quality.
- The physical copies are retained; they could be scanned later if the owners release them.

## Challenges & Reflections
- **Money:** The primary bottleneck; without funding the project would have taken months instead of years.
- **Logistics:** Moving pallets, sorting massive volumes, and coordinating volunteers required extensive effort.
- **Emotional weight:** The manuals represent historical engineering knowledge, design, and typography, adding cultural value beyond paper.
- **Volunteer debate:** Some professionals argued the manuals should be discarded rather than scanned by volunteers, highlighting tension over resource allocation.

## Current Status & Community Involvement
- All scanned manuals have basic metadata; users can improve descriptions via item reviews.
- Full‑text search is enabled through the “search text contents” option on the Internet Archive collection page.
- The author encourages contributions to metadata and acknowledges the Bitsavers Project for its independent scanning work.

## Future Outlook
- The author would repeat the rescue even if it took a century, emphasizing the lasting importance of preserving technical documentation.
