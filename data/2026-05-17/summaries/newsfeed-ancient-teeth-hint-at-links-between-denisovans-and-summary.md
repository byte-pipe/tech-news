# Ancient teeth hint at links between Denisovans and Homo erectus

## Background
- Homo erectus appeared ~2 million years ago in Africa and spread to Eurasia, persisting in some regions until ~108 ka.
- In 2020, proteins were recovered from a Dmanisi (Georgia) H. erectus tooth, proving that enamel proteins can survive, but the data gave little taxonomic insight.

## New Findings
- A team led by Qiaomei Fu (Institute of Vertebrate Paleontology and Paleoanthropology, Chinese Academy of Sciences) extracted proteins from six teeth dated to ~400 ka.
- The teeth come from three Chinese sites: Zhoukoudian (1 tooth), Hexian (2 teeth), Sunjiadong (3 teeth).
- Protein analysis identified sex: five male (via amelogenin Y) and one female (Sunjiadong).

## Protein Variants and Their Implications
- All six teeth share two specific protein variants:
  1. A variant never observed in any other human group, suggesting it may be unique to H. erectus.
  2. A variant previously found in Denisovan specimens from East Asia.
- The presence of the Denisovan‑type variant leads the authors to propose that Denisovans interbred with H. erectus, introducing the H. erectus protein signature into the Denisovan gene pool.

## Alternative Interpretations
- John Hawks outlines three possible explanations:
  1. The teeth are genuinely H. erectus, and the shared variant reflects Denisovan‑H. erectus admixture.
  2. The teeth belong to Denisovans, and the variants simply represent Denisovan diversity.
   3. The specimens are a Denisovan population that already carries H. erectus genetic input.
- Distinguishing among these scenarios requires additional fossils and molecular data.

## Significance and Future Work
- The study demonstrates that substantial enamel proteins can be recovered from H. erectus‑age fossils, opening a new line of evidence for human evolution.
- Paleoproteomics is still in its early stages, analogous to the early days of ancient DNA research; methodological advances and more specimens will be needed to resolve current uncertainties.
