# Summary of “The main thing about P2P meth is that there's so much of it”

## Overview
- The article investigates claims that “new” P2P‑based methamphetamine differs chemically from the older ephedrine‑based product and may be linked to increased psychosis and homelessness.
- It reviews DEA data, synthesis pathways, isomer composition, contaminants, and usage trends to assess these claims.

## Shift from Ephedrine to P2P Synthesis
- **Regulatory changes**: U.S. restrictions on pseudoephedrine (2006) and Mexican bans (2008) prompted producers to adopt phenyl‑2‑propanone (P2P) routes.
- **DEA seizure data (2009‑present)** shows rapid displacement of ephedrine‑based meth by P2P‑based meth.

## Chemical Differences

### Isomer Composition
- **d‑methamphetamine** (the active, dopamine‑releasing isomer) produces the typical “meth” effects.
- **l‑methamphetamine** has little dopaminergic activity and is far less euphoric.
- Naïve P2P synthesis yields a racemic mixture (≈50 % d‑meth, 50 % l‑meth).  
- Ephedrine‑based methods generate primarily d‑meth.
- DEA “potency” data (percentage of d‑meth) shows an initial dip after P2P adoption, with l‑meth largely disappearing by 2019.

### Purity and Potency
- **Purity**: percentage of total meth (d + l) in a sample. Modern street meth averages ~95 % total meth.
- **Potency**: percentage of d‑meth among the meth present.
- Consumer‑relevant “effective potency” = purity × potency, currently around 95 % d‑meth on average.

### Contaminants
- Lead acetate, once a concern, is only used in the phenylacetic acid (PAA) route and was less common during the 2014‑2018 NTS‑dominant period, weakening the hypothesis that lead causes schizophrenia.
- DEA reports attribute most impurities to the P2P production step, not to downstream conversion to meth.

## Evolution of P2P Production Methods

- Transitions are gradual; a notable fraction of seized samples remain “unknown”.

## Usage Trends and Quantity

### Seizure Data
- Border seizures of meth have surged, reflecting increased production and Mexican‑origin supply (≈80 % of U.S. meth by 2012).

### Wastewater Monitoring
- Seattle’s sewage measurements show a doubling of meth biomarkers in 2017, maintaining high levels thereafter; Seattle ranked highest among participating global cities in 2016‑2020.

### Survey Data
- SAMHSA’s NSDUH data (2002‑present) indicates:
  - Rising percentage of adults reporting any meth use in the past 30 days.
  - A sharper increase in the proportion reporting daily use (available since 2015), suggesting growth in heavy‑use population.

## Conclusions
- The shift to P2P synthesis introduced a temporary increase in l‑meth content, but modern street meth is now highly pure and predominantly d‑meth.
- Chemical contaminants such as lead acetate do not align temporally with reported spikes in psychosis.
- The primary driver of the “new” meth phenomenon appears to be the massive increase in overall supply and consumption, especially among heavy users, rather than a fundamentally different pharmacological profile.
