# Anthropic’s $1.5 B Copyright Settlement – Judge Delays Approval

## Background
- Anthropic reached a $1.5 billion settlement over alleged book piracy used to train its AI models.  
- The settlement is considered the largest copyright class‑action settlement in U.S. history.  
- Over 92 % of the more than 480,000 works in the case have been claimed by authors and other copyright holders.

## Judicial Action
- U.S. District Judge Araceli Martinez‑Olguin refused to immediately approve the settlement.  
- She asked authors to address objections and explain why some class members are opting out.  
- A response deadline is set for May 21, when Anthropic must also file a brief on why late opt‑outs should be denied.

## Main Objections from Class Members
- **Attorney fees:** Plaintiffs argue that $320 million+ in legal fees is excessive compared with the roughly $3,000 payout each author expects.  
- **Pittance payouts:** Authors claim the individual awards are “paltry” and do not reflect the value of the unauthorized use.  
- **Fee structure:** Objectors say counsel’s compensation should be tied to the number of claimants, not the total settlement fund.  
- **Future use of works:** Several authors demand that Anthropic destroy all copies of the pirated works and agree to restrict future commercial use of models trained on them.  

## Specific Points Raised by Objectors
- **Pierce Story:** Estimates lawyer hourly rates at $10‑12 k, cites a T‑Mobile case to argue fees are unreasonable; proposes a $70 million counsel payout to increase plaintiff awards by ~25 %.  
- **Ruben Lee:** Calls the offered amount “paltry” and not reflective of the full value of the infringement.  
- **James R. Sills:** Demands Anthropic delete all digital and physical copies of his works before settlement proceeds.  
- **Robert C. Jacobson, Victoria Pinder, and others:** Report that their objections were improperly marked as untimely or invalid, and note clerical errors (e.g., misspelled names) by counsel.  

## Concerns About Attorney Conduct
- Judges William Alsup (retired) and Martinez‑Olguin have questioned whether the fee request (nearly a third of a billion dollars) is appropriate.  
- Alsup recommended an independent investigation into attorney fees, but the recommendation was not fully disclosed to the incoming judge.  
- Plaintiffs allege that counsel tried to exclude or invalidate objections and that communication with class members has been “messy.”

## Potential Consequences
- If the court finds the fee structure or settlement terms unreasonable, the approval could be delayed further or the settlement could be vulnerable on appeal.  
- A separate lawsuit filed by 25 class members who opted out indicates ongoing litigation against Anthropic.  

## Next Steps
- Authors must submit written responses to the objections by May 21.  
- Anthropic must file a brief defending the settlement and arguing against honoring late opt‑outs.  
- The court will consider whether to adjust attorney fees, modify payout calculations, and require restrictions on future use of the disputed works before granting final approval.
