# The Strongest El Niño Ever - by Zeke Hausfather

# The Strongest El Niño Ever – Summary

## Key Findings
- July 2026 runs from 667 ensemble members across 14 seasonal forecast models give a ~90 % chance that the 2026‑27 El Niño will set a new record.
- The multi‑model median peak Niño 3.4 SST anomaly is 3.6 °C, about 0.8 °C higher than the previous record (2.75 °C in 2015‑16).
- The middle 80 % of the ensemble lies at or above the 2015‑16 record; roughly 91 % of members exceed it.
- Using the relative ONI (RONI) index, 11 of 14 models still forecast a record event, giving a ~77 % chance of a new RONI peak.

## Historical Context
- The gap between the strongest and fifth‑strongest El Niños in the past 150 years is only ~0.5 °C.
- The 1877‑78 event (2.73 °C) and 2015‑16 (2.75 °C) are the closest past peaks; none have clearly surpassed 2.75 °C.
- In RONI terms, the 1982‑83 event (2.69 °C) holds the record, not 2015‑16.

## Model Forecasts
- Every model’s median peak falls in the “super” El Niño range; all but JAMSTEC’s SINTEX‑F (2.2 °C) exceed the 2015‑16 record.
- Model agreement is unusually strong, though the article notes agreement ≠ skill.
- The CMCC model predicts an outlier peak of 5.3 °C, but the multi‑model median remains robust even if CMCC is excluded.

## Forecast Evolution
- Successive monthly initializations (March → July) have consistently warmed, indicating a real intensifying event rather than random model drift.
- Median peak forecast rose from ~2.8 °C (March) to 3.6 °C (July), with the rate of increase slowing in the last three cycles, suggesting convergence.
- Observed SSTs have already outpaced early forecasts, reinforcing confidence in the intensifying trend.

## Trajectory and Timing
- The projected development is faster than the 1997‑98 El Niño, previously the benchmark for rapid onset.
- Unlike 2015, which began from a warm precursor, the 2026 event is emerging from La Niña‑like conditions in January.
- Individual models peak at different months (e.g., CFSv2 in November, ECMWF in December); the median of model peaks is slightly higher than the peak of the median trajectory.

## Spatial Patterns
- All models show the classic east‑Pacific “El Niño tongue” of warm SST anomalies.
- The spatial SST anomaly fields at each model’s own peak month are broadly consistent, with CMCC as the sole outlier.

## Current Observations
- Daily Niño 3.4 SST anomalies are already around 2 °C above era‑adjusted averages in mid‑July, crossing the threshold for a very strong (“super”) El Niño if sustained.
- The daily anomaly record, referenced to each year’s 30‑year climatology, places 2026 among the highest values since satellite observations began in 1982.
