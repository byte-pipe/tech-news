# Chick-fil‑A discloses data breach after credential stuffing attacks

## Overview
- Chick‑fil‑A detected a credential‑stuffing attack on its website and mobile app from June 17‑19, 2026.
- Unauthorized parties used email/password combos obtained from a third‑party source to access Chick‑fil‑A One accounts.
- The breach was discovered on July 13, 2026 and notification letters were sent to affected customers.

## Exposed Information
- Customer name, email address, Chick‑fil‑A One membership number, mobile‑pay number, QR code, credit balance, and last four digits of credit/debit cards.  
- Potentially also birth dates, phone numbers, and mailing addresses if stored in the accounts.

## Affected Individuals
- Texas Attorney General: 2,182 Texans.  
- Massachusetts Attorney General: 39 residents.  
- Additional notifications sent to residents of Iowa, District of Columbia, Maryland, New Mexico, New York, North Carolina, Oregon, Vermont, and Rhode Island.  
- Total number of compromised accounts not disclosed.

## Attack Method
- Credential stuffing: automated use of stolen username/password pairs, effective when users reuse credentials across services.  
- Goal: steal personal and financial data for resale or identity theft.

## Company Response
- Logged out all impacted accounts and removed stored payment methods.  
- Restored account balances and added extra rewards as an apology.  
- Advised users to change passwords immediately.  
- No comment from a Chick‑fil‑A spokesperson at the time of reporting.

## Historical Context
- In March 2023, Chick‑fil‑A confirmed a similar credential‑stuffing breach (Dec 2022‑Feb 2023) that exposed personal data and rewards balances of over 71,000 customers.
