# 5 Storytelling Frameworks to Attract B2B Buyers

## Purpose of the edition  
- Explain how a brand narrative shapes content and marketing strategy.  
- Offer five practical storytelling frameworks for B2B companies or personal brands.  
- Promote a live workshop (Thursday August 30) on writing and deploying a brand narrative across ten channels.

## What is storytelling in B2B?  
- Using a narrative to convey a brand’s message, values, and offerings instead of merely listing facts or features.  
- The story creates a logical thread that links origin, solution, and the customer’s problem, making the brand memorable.

## What is a narrative?  
- **Not** an overly emotional experience; it is a logical, coherent thread.  
- **Is** the overarching story that explains why the brand exists, what it fights against, and how it helps customers win.  

## Why a narrative matters  
- Provides context for product features, IP, and processes.  
- Helps the audience:  
  - Understand why the product matters.  
  - Remember the problem solved and the solution offered.  
  - Position the brand distinctively among competitors.  
  - Experience an “aha!” moment that generates enthusiasm.

## The 5 storytelling frameworks  

### 1. Origin story with Freytag’s pyramid  
- **Exposition:** Describe the market’s status quo.  
- **Rising action:** Highlight the worsening problem and failing existing solutions.  
- **Climax:** Introduce the product and the insight that makes it the answer.  
- **Falling action:** Show proof—results, customers, adoption.  
- **Denouement:** Define the new standard the brand now defends.  

### 2. Pixar’s “because of that” framework (causality)  
- Every event follows a previous one, creating a chain of cause and effect.  
- Benefits of causality:  
  - Generates momentum, pulling the reader forward.  
  - Establishes stakes, making choices matter.  
  - Imparts meaning, teaching a lesson about how the world works.  
  - Delivers satisfying endings that feel inevitable.

### 3. Identify your enemy  
- The antagonist is the practice, belief, or system that blocks the hero’s goals.  
- A good enemy in B2B:  
  - Is clear, relatable, and creates conflict.  
  - Appears after the inciting incident, driving the narrative forward.  
  - Provides a focal point for what the brand is fighting against.

### 4. Define your big idea or point of view  
- A “big idea” is the central, creative message that powers the brand.  
- Three components:  
  - **Intellectual hook:** A fresh insight that reframes the buyer’s problem.  
  - **Primary promise:** The business outcome expressed in the buyer’s language.  
  - **Unique method:** The proprietary mechanism that makes the promise credible.  
- Four steps to craft it:  
  1. Know your audience.  
  2. Find the tension (biggest problem/frustration).  
  3. Name the problem or idea with a memorable label.  
  4. Connect your solution uniquely.

### 5. Use sub‑stories to anchor the narrative  
- Sub‑stories are small anecdotes, analogies, or case examples that make the larger narrative concrete.  
- Types of sub‑stories:  
  - Analogy that simplifies a concept.  
  - Credibility story that proves the solution works in the real world.  
  - Counter‑intuitive lesson that challenges common assumptions.
