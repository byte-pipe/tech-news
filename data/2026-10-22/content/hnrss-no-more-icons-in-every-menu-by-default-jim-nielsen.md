Have you heard the good news?I bemoaned the approach of putting an icon in every menu item by defaultwhich shipped with macOS Tahoe — but now it’s gone in Golden Gate!

John Gruber wrote about this reversion back in June, including the fact that Apple’s new Human Interface Guidelines specifically call out Tahoe’s approach to menus as an example of whatnotto do on macOS.

As the old saying goes, it takes a bigmancorporation to admit when they’re wrong.

As I mentioned inmy original post, one of the arguments I had in my quiver against this practice was “Apple doesn’t do it”. But once Apple started putting icons in all their menus in Tahoe, I lost a persuasive point in my argument.

But now, I think I have an argument that’s even stronger than before!

It’s no longer: “Apple doesn’t put icons in their menus.”

It’s: “Apple tried putting icons in all their menus, and eventheywent back on it!”

They designed it, shipped it, lived with it for a year, then publicly changed their guidance on it. To me, that’s a lot more persuasive than simply saying, “Apple doesn’t do this.”

Choosing not to do something you’ve never done is one thing.

Choosing not to do something you’ve done isexperience.

That’s the essence of life:learning by doing.
