# Agility’s Digit 5 humanoid robot will run on Nvidia as it heads for Nasdaq

## Agility Robotics to Use Nvidia's IGX Thor Modules in Upcoming Digit 5 Version

Agility Robotics, known for its humanoid robot Digit 5, has announced a partnership with Nvidia to use its IGX Thor modules as the only onboard computer in the upcoming version. The decision comes as Agility prepares to list on the Nasdaq exchange, a rare move for the company. This partnership is the fourth for Agility in four days, ahead of its planned merger with Churchill Capital Corp XI.

## Technology Components

Nvidia has integrated its Halos safety platform into Digit's system, which uses AI components to detect people and provide a secure environment. Additionally, Nvidia's Deepu Talla, vice president of robotics and edge AI, emphasized the importance of AI and safety in engineering Digit 5's AI computing and safety capabilities. Pras Velagapudi, Agility's chief technology officer, stated that Nvidia modules will provide a common foundation for Digit 5's deployment, enabling it to be deployed at commercial scale.

## New Infrastructure

Agility has also announced plans to build a new 50,000-square-foot site in Pittsburgh, expanding its space and establishing software and AI development teams. The move adds around 35,000 square feet to its existing space, and staff will gain access by the end of 2023. Agility has also opened a new factory in Pittsburgh to house software and AI development teams and a new service centre. Mark Vale, former president of UPS, has been appointed as Agility's future chair of the audit committee, replacing existing directors Bruce Leak and Matt Ocko.

## Partnership with AGCO

Agility has also signed a commercial agreement with farm machinery manufacturer AGCO, which will test Digit in its factory in Hesston, Kansas. AGCO will sort parts and place storage totes using Digit's technology. This news marks the company's fourth partnership with Nvidia in four days, leading up to its planned merger with Churchill Capital Corp XI.

## Risks and Caution

The companies have warned about potential risks, including Agility's history of net losses. However, they also noted that it may not achieve commercial success, given challenges in integrating the AI components seamlessly. Unitree, a Chinese humanoid maker that launched in Shanghai in August, has also listed on the Nasdaq exchange.
