# The End of AI Frontiers – Machine Learning (Theory) Summary

## Background
- After the 2023 ChatGPT release, I saw a need for more organized research within Microsoft Research (MSR).
- Individual efforts often arrived late and lacked strong baselines, limiting impact.
- This motivated the creation of the AI Frontiers (AIF) sub‑organization within MSR.

## AI Frontiers Organization
- AIF aimed to coordinate coherent research, operating in a “messy middle” between top‑down and bottom‑up approaches.
- Faced disagreements and frequent compute bottlenecks, but achieved pioneering work with a small team (~50 people).
- Key projects included:
  - **Autogen orchestration framework** and **Faracomputer** agent.
  - Fundamental capabilities such as **Next Latent objective**, **Dion3** (optimized Muon), and **Full Bandwidth Transformer** (recursive efficiency).

## Collaboration with MSR
- Developed a shared monorepo containing an edge‑scale pretraining codebase, later highlighted in a COLM keynote.
- The monorepo streamlined model‑creation pipelines, a task otherwise duplicated across multiple internal repos.

## Reorganization into Microsoft AI (MAI)
- Early September, AIF merged into the larger Microsoft AI organization.
- MAI’s leadership emphasized scaling over research, focusing on data acquisition and model size.
- This scaling‑only philosophy conflicted with AIF’s research‑driven goals and led to a lack of support for our work.
- Evidence (e.g., Bytedance experiments) suggested research innovations like the Full Bandwidth Transformer could halve training and inference costs, offering significant economic benefits.

## Decision to Depart
- I positioned my team as best as possible but ultimately left, as other AIF leaders did the same.
- Two main obstacles prevented a return to MSR:
  1. Lack of a clear path from research to real‑world use within the scaling‑focused environment.
  2. Concern that the implicit promise “do good work → good outcomes” no longer held after the non‑consensual reorganization.

## Outlook
- AI Frontiers is now dissolved; no internships will be offered in the upcoming year.
- I remain hopeful that a future Microsoft could become research‑friendly again if leadership gains more wisdom and balances innovation with scale.
