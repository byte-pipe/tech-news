# Almost 600,000 Nvidia H200s stranded in Hong Kong as Chinese gov’t stalls on import approvals – report

## Key points
- About 600,000 Nvidia H200 GPUs intended for mainland China are held in Hong Kong awaiting Chinese import approval.  
- Small shipments have reached “several major Chinese Internet companies,” each reportedly receiving around 10,000 H200 GPUs via intermediaries.  
- The export ban imposed by former President Joe Biden was lifted by the Trump administration in December 2025 and formally approved in January 2026, but with new conditions:  
  - A 25 % export tariff on the H200 chips.  
  - Prohibition on exporting GPUs more powerful than the H200; the RTX Pro 5500 workstation card is allowed.  
- Bloomberg says Chinese AI developers expect to receive only about 1 % of the chips they have ordered in the coming months due to Nvidia’s production constraints.  
- If export controls remain unchanged, Chinese hyperscalers project receiving roughly 700,000 RTX Pro 5500 units by the end of 2027.  
- In March, Supermicro co‑founder Wally Liaw was arrested for alleged violations of U.S. export‑control laws related to smuggling $2.5 billion of Nvidia GPU servers into China; he has pleaded not guilty.
