# ANC faces anger over poor services as South Africans vote in local elections

## Election context
- Local elections held on Wednesday across South Africa’s 257 municipalities.  
- The African National Congress (ANC) is projected to receive **under one‑third of the vote**, down from 45.6% in 2021 municipal elections and 40.2% in the 2024 national election.  
- Voter turnout determines council seats; mayors are chosen later by elected councillors.

## Voter sentiment on service delivery
- Residents cite chronic water outages, electricity cuts, and deteriorating infrastructure as primary grievances.  
- In Johannesburg’s Kensington suburb, voters describe services as “horrible” and infrastructure as “shot.”  
- Many voters split their ballots, supporting opposition parties for change while still backing smaller, ideologically aligned groups.

## Party positions and coalition dynamics
- **ANC**: Still likely the largest party but may need to join coalitions to govern; internal factionalism evident in candidate selection.  
- **Democratic Alliance (DA)**: Campaigning strongly in Johannesburg with high‑profile stunts by Helen Zille; faces skepticism from black voters and criticism over housing costs in Cape Town.  
- **ActionSA**: Led by former Johannesburg mayor Herman Mashaba, polling around 10%; Mashaba refuses any partnership with Zille.  
- **uMkhonto weSizwe (MK)**: Jacob Zuma’s party gaining over 40% in eThekwini (Durban) and pulling votes from the ANC.  
- **Economic Freedom Fighters (EFF)** and **Patriotic Alliance (PA)**: Part of existing ANC‑led coalitions in some metros.

## Notable campaign moments
- Helen Zille (DA) performed viral stunts such as snorkeling in a pothole and kayaking on a flooded street to highlight service failures.  
- Jacob Zuma addressed rallies in KwaMashu, positioning MK as a major contender in Durban.  
- Local ANC leaders reportedly resist a formal partnership with the DA, despite national leadership openness to cooperation.

## Expected outcomes
- The ANC may retain leadership of several municipal coalitions but lose control of others due to fragmented vote shares.  
- Unstable coalition governments, a common feature in South African municipalities, are cited as a key factor behind poor service delivery.  
- Electoral commission will announce provisional results within a week; councils will have two weeks thereafter to form governing coalitions and elect mayors.
