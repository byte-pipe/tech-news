# ANC faces bruising defeat in South African local elections with 80% of ballots counted | ANC (African National Congress) | The Guardian

# Summary of “ANC faces bruising defeat in South African local elections with 80% of ballots counted”

## Key national results
- The African National Congress (ANC) is projected to receive **31.8 %** of the national vote, about 14 percentage points lower than in the 2021 local elections.  
- The centre‑right Democratic Alliance (DA) trails at **24.1 %**, while former president Jacob Zuma’s uMkhonto weSizwe (MK) party holds **9.3 %**.  
- Voter turnout rose to **54.5 %** of registered voters, up from 46 % in the previous cycle.

## Decline of ANC dominance
- The ANC is on track to lose its status as the largest party in Gauteng and KwaZulu‑Natal, the two most populous provinces.  
- Coalition talks are already underway behind closed doors as parties seek governing alliances.

## City‑level highlights
- **Johannesburg** (population 4.8 million): with just over half the votes counted, the DA leads at **40 %**, the ANC is second at **17.5 %**, and ActionSA holds **15.5 %**. The city faces chronic water and power cuts, uncollected rubbish, potholes, and a R220 billion infrastructure backlog.  
- Projections from the Council for Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR) suggest final shares of **34 %** for the DA, **20 %** for the ANC, and **16.8 %** for ActionSA in Johannesburg.  
- **eThekwini/Durban**: MK leads with **41 %** of counted votes, but other parties could form a coalition to keep it out of power.  
- **Cape Town**: the DA is expected to retain its majority despite growing middle‑class discontent over housing costs.

## Electoral mechanics
- Voters elect local councillors and a party list; seats are allocated by proportional representation.  
- Elected councillors have two weeks to choose mayors and establish municipal governments.

## Underlying factors
- High unemployment, crime, corruption, and poor local service delivery have eroded support for the ANC.  
- The MK party combines socially conservative Zulu nationalism with promises of land and wealth redistribution.  
- ActionSA leader Herman Mashaba has ruled out any coalition with DA figure Helen Zille, whom he labels “racist,” a claim she denies.
