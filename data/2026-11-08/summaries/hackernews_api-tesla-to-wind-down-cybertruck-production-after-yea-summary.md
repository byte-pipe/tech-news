# Tesla to wind down Cybertruck production

## Announcement
- CEO Elon Musk said on X that Cybertruck production at the Texas gigafactory will be wound down around mid‑2027.  
- The slowdown is intended to free capacity for the newly launched Cybercab, central to Tesla’s robotaxi plans.  
- It is unclear whether production will shift to another plant or cease entirely; the Austin facility is currently the only one equipped for the truck’s unique parts.

## Sales performance
- Deliveries began in late 2023.  
- U.S. sales fell to 20,237 units in 2025, a 48 % drop from 38,965 units in 2024.  
- Initial forecasts predicted up to 250,000 units annually, far above actual numbers.  

## Factors behind the wind‑down
- Persistent weak demand in the few markets where the truck was sold.  
- Controversial angular, stainless‑steel design that limited appeal, especially in Europe where pedestrian‑impact regulations block certification.  
- Safety‑testing mishap at the 2019 unveiling (window cracks after metal‑ball test).  
- Competition from other electric pickups, which have also struggled in the U.S. market.  

## Shift to Cybercab
- The Cybercab features butterfly doors, no steering wheel, pedals, or mirrors, and is positioned as Tesla’s primary autonomous vehicle for a global robotaxi network.  
- Small‑scale paid rides began in Austin in September 2025, but the U.S. auto safety agency is still reviewing its self‑certification compliance.  
- Lead engineer Wes Morrill described the Cybertruck as a technology platform that lives on through the Cybercab.

## Industry and analyst reactions
- Former Aston Martin CEO Andy Palmer called the decision “the end of an era” and noted that continuing production makes no sense when targets aren’t met.  
- Analyst Felipe Munoz pointed out that electric pickups in general have underperformed in the U.S., not just the Cybertruck.  
- Musk reiterated on X that the Cybertruck is “the best vehicle Tesla has ever made,” despite its commercial shortcomings.
