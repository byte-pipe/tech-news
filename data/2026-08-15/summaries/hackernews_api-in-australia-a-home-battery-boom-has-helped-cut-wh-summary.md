# In Australia, a Home Battery Boom Has Helped Cut Wholesale Power Prices in Half  

## Key Findings  
- Over 500,000 residential batteries have been installed under the government’s subsidy program launched in July 2025.  
- Wholesale electricity prices have fallen by about 47 % in the past year, roughly halving the cost to consumers.  
- Australia now has more home batteries than the United States, despite a population that is one‑twelfth the size.  

## Program Details  
- The scheme offers a 30 % discount on battery systems that are coupled to rooftop solar panels.  
- It is part of a broader effort that also provides free electricity in the early afternoon to households in Queensland, New South Wales and South Australia, encouraging appliance use and EV charging during periods of high solar output.  
- Installations are projected to more than double the nation’s home‑battery capacity within the current year, according to Bloomberg.  

## Impact on the Grid and Prices  
- Rooftop solar now powers more than one‑third of Australian households, creating surplus generation that can depress wholesale prices and force conventional plants offline.  
- By storing excess solar energy and discharging it in the early evening peak, home batteries reduce the need for utilities to fire additional power plants, lowering system costs for all ratepayers.  
- Officials credit the subsidy program as the “major factor” behind the 47 % drop in wholesale energy prices, a rare achievement amid a global energy crisis.  

## Broader Context  
- Australia is the world leader in rooftop‑solar adoption, but the rapid uptake created grid‑stability challenges due to intermittent supply and waste of solar energy.  
- The home‑battery boom demonstrates how targeted policy incentives can turn distributed storage into a grid‑supporting asset, offering a model that other high‑solar‑penetration countries may consider.
