## Roget’s Thesaurus (4th edition)
- Most frequently consulted; acquired in 1989, 4th edition.
- Compared with the 8th edition, found the newer version bulkier and containing material not needed; retired it to a farther shelf.
- Clarifies a common misunderstanding: the thesaurus is not merely a list of synonyms but a hierarchical “storehouse” of concepts.
- Top‑level categories include Abstract concepts, Space, Physics, Matter, Sensation, etc.; deeper subdivisions organize ideas such as Time → Relative time → sections on priority, posteriority, simultaneity, past, present, future.
- Demonstrates how to use sections to refine expression (e.g., choosing “ensue,” “posteriority,” or “simultaneity” instead of a single synonym).
- Index distinguishes multiple senses of words like “immediately,” helping pinpoint the intended nuance.
- Useful for recalling forgotten words by navigating related concepts (e.g., finding “contemporaneous” under “Simultaneity” when searching for a word like “coexisting”).
- Effective use requires knowledge of word meanings; otherwise it can lead to random substitution.
- Prefers the physical book; an online version lacks the same organization and personal attachment.

- Favorite Browne work: *Pseudodoxia Epidemica* (1646), a collection of common misconceptions.

  - Snails’ eyes in their horns—Browne initially denied four‑eyed snails, later revised his view.
