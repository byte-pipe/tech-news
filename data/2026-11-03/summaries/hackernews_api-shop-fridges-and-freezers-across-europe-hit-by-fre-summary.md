# Shop fridges and freezers across Europe hit by fresh suspected cyber‑attack  

## Overview of the incidents  
- Refrigeration units in supermarkets across **Belgium, Estonia, Ireland, Finland, Luxembourg, the Netherlands and Sweden** recorded temperature spikes up to **50 °C (122 °F)** overnight.  
- The pattern mirrors a large‑scale cyber‑attack on French supermarkets that occurred the previous month.  
- Several retailers confirmed that their systems had been **hacked** rather than suffering a technical fault.  

## Official reactions  
- EU food and agriculture commissioner **Christophe Hansen** described the repeated attacks as “a deliberate attempt to disrupt our food supply chain and undermine consumers’ confidence.”  
- National cybersecurity agencies, such as **Sweden’s National Cybersecurity Centre** (head John Billow), are investigating and noting many exposed operational‑technology devices that need updating.

## Characteristics of the attacks  
- **No extortion demands** were reported; the motive appears to be sabotage.  
- Attackers exploit **internet‑exposed operational‑technology (OT) controllers** that often have unpatched vulnerabilities.  
- Known flaws in controllers (e.g., those supplied by **Danfoss**) were identified before the French incident but many devices remained reachable.  
- The method is **cheap, fast and causes immediate, visible damage** (stock loss, store closures).  

## Background: French cyber‑attack (April 2024)  
- Hackers seized control of refrigeration controllers in up to **700 French supermarkets**, raising temperatures to 50 °C, causing fires and food losses estimated in the **tens of millions of euros**.  
- Controllers primarily from **Danfoss**; security flaws had been flagged the previous year.  
- Individual store losses ranged from **€10 000 to over €130 000**.  
- French authorities (Paris prosecutor’s office, ANSSI) opened a formal investigation; no group has claimed responsibility.  

## Outlook and concerns  
- The attacks raise fears of **government‑sponsored hybrid operations**, especially after Russia’s 2022 invasion of Ukraine.  
- Experts stress the need for **prompt patching, network segmentation and removal of unnecessary internet exposure** for refrigeration and other building‑management systems.  
- Ongoing monitoring across Europe is expected as more retailers assess vulnerabilities and reinforce defenses.
