# AI apps with hidden sexual and violent deepfake features made it into the App Store

## Summary
- An investigation uncovered more than 400 AI‑powered apps, apparently operated by a single Chinese organization, that can generate sexual and violent deepfakes after users upload photos.  
- The apps passed Apple’s App Store review, Google Play review, and Meta’s ad policies, then switched to abusive functionality through server‑side changes triggered by ad clicks.  
- Many apps masquerade as ordinary services (e.g., language tutoring, AI workout coaches, pet‑photo clothing) but can load a hidden web interface offering explicit scenes such as “bedroom rape” and “threatening knife.”  
- Apple removed 19 of the offending apps after the investigation was disclosed. Meta reported actions against more than 10,000 ad accounts linked to the network.  

## Key Findings
- **Scale and origin**: Over 400 connected domains and apps, likely created by one Chinese entity, were found in both the Apple App Store and Google Play Store.  
- **Hidden abusive functionality**: Apps normally display benign content; a server check activated after a user taps a deep‑fake‑related ad on Meta platforms unlocks pornographic and violent deep‑fake generators.  
- **Bait‑and‑switch tactics**: Developers passed review with harmless content, then changed the server‑side website to deliver prohibited material, violating Apple’s and Google’s policies.  
- **Promotion**: Thousands of video ads for the apps ran on Facebook and Instagram, exposing the network to a wide audience.  
- **Policy breaches**: Both Apple and Google have explicit rules against harmful deepfake content; Meta also bans pornographic ads. The apps evaded detection by altering content after approval.  
- **Response**: Apple removed 19 apps after the report; Meta disabled over 10,000 ad accounts associated with the domains. Apple says it has strengthened protections against this approach.  

## Implications
- The case highlights vulnerabilities in app‑store review processes that can be exploited through dynamic server‑side changes.  
- It underscores the need for continuous monitoring of app behavior post‑approval, especially for AI‑generated content.  
- The widespread promotion of such apps on major social platforms shows the challenge of policing malicious advertising networks.
