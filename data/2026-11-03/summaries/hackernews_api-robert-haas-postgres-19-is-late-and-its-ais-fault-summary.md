# Postgres 19 is late, and it's AI's fault

## Overview
- PostgreSQL 19, usually released between mid‑September and mid‑October, is now expected in November and may be postponed further.  
- Several major features (UPDATE/DELETE FOR PORTION OF, SQL Property Graph Queries, ALTER TABLE … MERGE PARTITION/SPLIT PARTITION) were reverted late in the beta period, an unusual occurrence.

## Role of LLMs
- Large language models (LLMs) have dramatically increased the detection of bugs in PostgreSQL’s extensive, decades‑old code base.  
- While LLMs can draft fixes, human review is still required, and reviewers have not become faster or more knowledgeable.

## Commit Activity
- 2025 (April 8 – Sept 25): 1,037 master commits, 724 to REL_18_STABLE.  
- 2026 (same period): 1,443 master commits, 1,169 to REL_19_STABLE.  
- Master‑branch commits fell slightly, but back‑ported commits rose >60 %.  
- Back‑ports to older branches also jumped: REL_17_STABLE received 248 commits in 2025 vs. 433 to REL_18_STABLE in 2026 (+74 %).  
- The surge reflects many bug reports generated by users employing LLMs.

## Impact on Release Schedule
- More bugs discovered → more time needed for fixing and reviewing.  
- Human reviewers’ effort was diverted to security and back‑branch issues, delaying decisions on what to revert for v19.  
- The delay is modest in the grand scheme and stems from a focus on quality.

## Long‑Term Concerns
- With 40 years of hidden bugs now exposed by AI, catching up may be difficult and could affect future innovation.  
- Every new feature will be scrutinized by AI, turning previously unnoticed defects into quickly discovered issues—a challenge and an opportunity.

## Personal Perspective
- I became a PostgreSQL developer because of the code’s cleanliness and quality; AI‑driven bug discovery does not diminish my high regard for the project.  
- The rise of LLMs will force all PostgreSQL (and broader software) developers to raise their standards.
