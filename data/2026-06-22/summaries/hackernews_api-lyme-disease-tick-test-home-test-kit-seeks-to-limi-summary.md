# LymeAlert: At‑home 15‑minute test for Lyme‑carrying ticks  

## Overview  
- LymeAlert is a consumer‑grade kit that lets users test ticks they find on their bodies for the presence of Lyme disease bacteria.  
- The test takes about 15 minutes, costs $40 per kit, and is slated for retail launch in August.  
- It aims to reduce unnecessary ER visits, antibiotic use, and the waiting time (often a week) and cost ($50‑$450) of laboratory testing.  

## Origin and Motivation  
- Founder Erin Dawicki, a pediatric orthopedic PA, conceived the idea while pursuing an MBA at MIT’s Sloan School.  
- Frustrated by health‑care inequities tied to insurance status, she sought a technology‑driven solution.  
- A class assignment on Lyme‑disease treatment sparked the concept: test the tick first rather than automatically prescribing antibiotics.  

## How the Test Works  
- The kit includes a plastic container with a built‑in grinder.  
- Users place up to five ticks in the container, close it, and twist to grind the ticks into a pulp.  
- A chemically treated paper strip is then inserted; it changes color if Lyme‑causing bacteria are detected.  
- The process is painless for the person but destroys the tick.  

## Expert Commentary  
- Armin Alaedini (Global Lyme Alliance) says rapid testing could enable timely antibiotic treatment, but warns that false positives could cause panic.  
- The current test detects only Lyme disease and does not identify other tick‑borne pathogens (e.g., those causing Alpha‑gal syndrome).  

## Future Developments  
- A next‑generation version is planned to detect additional pathogens, with a target release next year.  
- An accompanying smartphone app will let users anonymously report locations of positive ticks.  
- Data will be combined with NASA satellite imagery and animal migration patterns to feed an AI model predicting tick and pathogen spread at the neighborhood level.  

## Contextual Notes  
- Early antibiotic treatment (within 72 hours of a positive tick test) is crucial, as Lyme disease is the only major tick‑borne infection treatable with antibiotics before severe illness.
