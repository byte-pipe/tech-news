# Aura's impressive e-ink photo frame doesn't even look digital

## Overview
- The article reviews Aura’s new AuraInk frame, a color e‑ink digital photo frame that aims to feel like a printed photograph rather than a screen.
- Digital photo frames are often criticized for being clunky, requiring power cords, and looking too much like bright displays.
- Aura’s founders set out to solve these issues by leveraging recent advances in color e‑ink technology.

## Technology
- Uses the same e‑ink technology found in e‑readers, offering low eye strain compared to LED screens.
- Current e‑ink panels can produce only six colors (red, blue, green, yellow, white, black).
- Aura developed a dithering algorithm that blends these six colors into smooth‑looking gradients, making photos appear close to the original.
- The algorithm was tuned through extensive testing with users in various lighting conditions to achieve acceptable visual quality.

## User Experience
- All frames connect to the Aura app, allowing photo uploads from phone, web, email, iCloud, or Google Photos.
- The app is described as user‑friendly, even for less tech‑savvy relatives.
- Social sharing features let family members add photos to a shared library that appears on the frame.
- By default, the Ink frame rotates images once per day, usually at night; manual changes trigger a brief “glitch” while the dithering process runs.

## Comparison with LED Frame
- Aura also provided a 12‑inch LED Aspen frame for comparison.
- The Aspen frame is praised for its premium look, anti‑glare surface, and paper‑like matting that mimics printed photos.
- Color fidelity on the Ink frame is not perfect, but many users may view the limited palette as an artistic effect.
- A professional analog photographer noted that the Ink frame’s white balance could be improved.

## Design & Installation
- The frame comes with sturdy mounting hardware; the reviewer used Command strips for installation.
- It can be easily taken on and off the wall and is charged via USB‑C roughly once a month.
- When not in use, the display sleeps to conserve battery.
- The black frame blends into gallery walls but may look out of place depending on surrounding artwork.

## Pricing & Verdict
- Ink frame price: $499; Aspen LED frame price: $229.
- Despite color inconsistencies, the reviewer enjoys having the Ink frame on the wall and finds it hard to imagine a better product given current e‑ink limitations.
- The frame is positioned as a premium, niche product rather than a cheap gift.
