# Summary of “An Anthropic researcher just gave us a peek at self‑improving AI | TechCrunch”

## Main Findings
- Anthropic published a paper titled **“Automated Researchers Can Reliably Mitigate Alignment Failures.”**  
- An Automated Alignment Researcher (AAR) was able to improve performance on all 10 tested alignment benchmarks without degrading overall model performance.  
- The AAR follows a research loop: it searches existing literature, proposes a method, trains the model for about 30 minutes, iterates with gradually harder benchmarks, keeps successful methods, and discards ineffective ones.  
- The authors claim the results provide early evidence that automated post‑training alignment could become practical in the near term.

## Implications for AI Progress
- The work is presented as a step toward recursive self‑improvement, suggesting that models might eventually improve their own alignment training and broader training practices.  
- If successful, such systems could make human AI researchers less essential or even obsolete.

## Comparison with Human Researchers
- The paper states the best AAR method outperforms what experienced humans propose, on average within six hours of work.  
- Human‑guided research directions did not lead to stronger performance in the experiments.  
- Cost comparison: AAR costs roughly **$4 per hour** in API inference versus **$150 per hour** for human researchers.

## Limitations Highlighted
- The approach works only insofar as the benchmarks accurately reflect the true alignment goals.  
- Significant effort is still required to define, maintain, and expand those benchmarks and the literature corpus the AAR draws from.
