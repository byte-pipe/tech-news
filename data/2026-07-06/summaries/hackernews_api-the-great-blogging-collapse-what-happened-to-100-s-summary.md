# The Great Blogging Collapse: What Happened to 100 Successful Blogs? – Summary

## Overview
- I examined 100 blogs that were celebrated in 2022 as six‑figure earners, tracking their organic Google traffic from April 2022 to April 2026.  
- The study is a cohort analysis of these specific “success stories,” not a representative sample of all blogs.  
- Traffic estimates come from Semrush; the changes are large enough that estimation error does not affect the conclusions.

## Key Takeaways
- The median blog lost **85 %** of its Google search traffic.  
- More than half of the blogs experienced catastrophic declines; 55 of 100 are now gutted, wiped out, or dead.  
- Only **21 %** of the blogs continued to grow; I identify the common traits they share.  
- First‑hand experience content (recipes, DIY projects, travel stories) became the strongest competitive advantage because it cannot be easily summarized by AI.  
- Search should be treated as one acquisition channel within a broader marketing mix, not as the sole business foundation.  
- The era of building sites solely for Google traffic and monetizing them with ads and affiliate links has ended.

## How the 100‑Blog List Was Built
- Selected from 2022 “bloggers who make six figures” round‑ups that circulated in the creator economy.  
- Each blog’s name, URL, niche, and latest income report were recorded.  
- The list is available in a public Google Sheet for further research.

## What Was Measured
- **Niche Category** – grouped into 10 main topics (e.g., Food & Recipes, Finance, DIY & Crafts).  
- **Total Traffic** – monthly visits (all sources) for April 2022 and April 2026.  
- **Total Traffic Delta** – change between the two dates.  
- **SEO Traffic** – monthly organic search visits for the same periods.  
- **SEO Traffic Delta** – change in organic visits.  
- **SEO Share** – proportion of total traffic that came from search in 2022 and 2026.  
- **Traffic & SEO Trajectory** – classified as Collapsed, Growing, Declining, or Stable.  
- **Monetization Methods** – primary revenue streams identified from the site and income reports.  
- **Status (April 2026)** – Alive, Dead, Rebranded, Sold, or Unverified.  
- **HCU Impact** – effect of Google Helpful Content Updates (Severely Hit, Hit, Unaffected).  
- **New Brand** – any rebranding that occurred.

## Main Findings
- **Traffic Collapse:** 55 blogs are gutted or dead; 20 lost 99 %+ of organic visits; 12 have zero organic traffic.  
- **Aggregate Traffic:** Overall monthly visits fell from ~17.8 M to ~12 M (≈ 33 % drop). Removing three giant food sites (Kitchen Sanctuary, Pinch of Yum, Jessica Gavin) shows the remaining 97 blogs dropped 63 % (≈ 11 M → 4 M).  
- **Ownership Changes:** 9 blogs were sold; 8 of those were already down > 80 % before the sale, so the buyer inherited the decline.  
- **Median Outcome:** A randomly chosen blog from the list is most likely operating at about one‑seventh of its 2022 traffic.

## Possible Reasons for the Abrupt Change
- **Google’s “Experience” addition to E‑E‑A‑T (Dec 2022):** First‑hand experience became a ranking factor, favoring content that demonstrates personal expertise.  
- **Helpful Content Update (Sep 2023):** Targeted low‑value, mass‑produced content; many independent publishers lost 60‑90 % of traffic with little recovery.  
- **Core Update (Mar 2024):** Further emphasized quality and experience, accelerating the decline for sites that relied on thin, SEO‑only content.  

## Traits of the 21 Growing Blogs
- Produce content rooted in personal experience (e.g., original recipes, DIY tutorials, travel narratives).  
- Diversify acquisition channels beyond Google (newsletter, direct traffic, YouTube, social media).  
- Treat search as one part of a broader marketing mix rather than the sole driver.  
- Adapt monetization to include memberships, courses, or brand partnerships that are less dependent on ad/affiliate revenue.  

## Takeaway
The traditional “blog‑for‑Google‑traffic‑and‑affiliate‑ads” blueprint has largely collapsed. Surviving blogs are those that leverage authentic experience, diversify traffic sources, and view SEO as just one element of a holistic audience‑building strategy.
