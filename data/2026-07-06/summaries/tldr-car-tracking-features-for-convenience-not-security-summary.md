# Car tracking features for “convenience not security” warns Kia

## Overview  
- Experts caution that connected‑car apps should not be relied upon to recover a stolen vehicle.  
- Thatcham Research highlights a growing gap between consumer expectations and the technical reality of these features.  

## Case study: Ian Fogg’s stolen Kia  
- In March, Fogg’s car was stolen from outside his home despite Kia Connect showing live location.  
- Thieves broke into the vehicle, disabled the phone connection via the infotainment system, and drove off.  
- Fogg captured the theft on a video‑doorbell, placed an Apple AirTag inside the car, and attempted to use Kia Connect for tracking.  
- The AirTag was discarded after its audible alert was triggered; Kia Connect provided location data only after 24–48 hours and required a form for each request.  
- The last known location was in Lithuania, and the car has not been recovered.  

## Kia Connect’s stated purpose  
- Described by Kia as a “customer convenience feature, not a certified security vehicle tracker.”  
- Live‑tracking for stolen vehicles is not provided; location release must comply with GDPR and law‑enforcement requests.  
- In the UK and Europe, Kia does not offer a premium security tracking service that is available in the US.  

## Legal and regulatory context  
- GDPR (and the UK’s equivalent) requires organisations to respond to data‑access requests within one month.  
- Police lack formal authority to demand vehicle location data without Home Office consent, which is rarely obtained.  
- Data sharing with law enforcement depends on each manufacturer’s policy.  

## Recommendations from Thatcham Research  
- Use independently certified stolen‑vehicle tracking devices that:  
  - Have their own power source.  
  - Provide real‑time location monitoring.  
  - Are monitored by a professional centre.  
- Recognize that most connected‑car apps are not engineered for security functions despite sophisticated technology.  

## Broader theft statistics (UK)  
- Approximately 55,000 cars were stolen in 2025, an 11 % drop from the previous year.  
- Only about 13 % of stolen vehicles are recovered, according to seller Cinch.  

## Takeaway  
- Connected‑car services like Kia Connect are intended for convenience, not as reliable anti‑theft tools.  
- Owners seeking genuine security should invest in certified tracking solutions rather than relying on manufacturer apps.
