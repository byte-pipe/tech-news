# Summary of “Cannabis Users Face Substantially Higher Risk of Heart Attack – American College of Cardiology”

## Key Findings
- Two new studies indicate a markedly higher risk of myocardial infarction among cannabis users compared with non‑users.  
- In a retrospective cohort of > 4.6 million people, users younger than 50 years had > 6‑fold increased odds of heart attack.  
- A meta‑analysis of 12 studies (≈ 75 million participants) showed a 50 % higher risk of heart attack for current cannabis users.  
- Additional cardiovascular risks observed in the retrospective study: 4‑fold higher ischemic stroke, 2‑fold higher heart‑failure, and 3‑fold higher combined cardiovascular death, heart attack, or stroke.

## Study Details
- **Retrospective Study**
  - Data source: TriNetX electronic‑medical‑record network.  
  - Population: > 4.6 million adults < 50 years, free of major cardiovascular risk factors at baseline.  
  - Follow‑up: average > 3 years.  
- **Meta‑analysis**
  - Included 12 published studies (10 US, 1 Canada, 1 India).  
  - Average age of participants with reported ages: 41 years.  
  - Study quality: moderate to good.  
  - Individual study results: 7 positive, 4 null, 1 slightly negative association with heart attack.  

## Clinical Implications
- Clinicians should ask patients about cannabis use when assessing cardiovascular risk, similar to tobacco questioning.  
- Public‑health messaging should include warnings about potential cardiac hazards of cannabis consumption.  
- Patients are encouraged to disclose full substance‑use histories to enable accurate risk assessment.

## Limitations
- Both investigations are retrospective; causality cannot be definitively established.  
- Meta‑analysis could not adjust for dose, duration, or co‑use of tobacco/other illicit drugs due to inconsistent reporting.  
- Potential confounding from concurrent substance use may influence observed associations.  

## Future Directions
- Prospective studies are needed to confirm findings and identify sub‑populations at greatest risk.  
- Ongoing research will explore mechanisms such as rhythm disturbances, increased myocardial oxygen demand, endothelial dysfunction, and acute risk spikes within one hour of use.
