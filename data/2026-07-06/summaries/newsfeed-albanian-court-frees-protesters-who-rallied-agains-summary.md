# Albanian court frees protesters who rallied against Kushner‑linked resort

## Key developments
- A Tirana court released 19 protesters detained after a violent demonstration against a luxury coastal resort tied to former U.S. President Donald Trump’s son‑in‑law, Jared Kushner.  
- The detainees were held on charges of refusing police orders, organising illegal demonstrations and disturbing public order.  
- According to lawyer Dorian Matlija:  
  - 2 individuals will be placed under house arrest.  
  - 12 must report to the judicial police once or twice a month.  
  - The remaining 5 will face no charges.  

## Public reaction
- Supporters gathered outside the court chanting “Free the boys” and welcomed the release with cheers.  
- Tens of thousands again rallied in Tirana on Saturday, the largest protest since the movement began, also chanting “Free the boys”.

## Background of the protests
- Demonstrations started in May over a luxury resort project in a protected coastal area, revealed in 2024 and linked to Jared Kushner.  
- The protests have broadened into anti‑government and anti‑corruption rallies, with slogans such as “New Albania” and “Edi Rama, resign”.  
- On the preceding Thursday, hundreds of demonstrators stormed parliament; police responded with tear gas, pepper spray and water cannon.  
  - 15 police officers were injured and 25 demonstrators were detained.  
- Earlier clashes this year forced the resignation of Prime Minister Edi Rama’s deputy, Belinda Balluku, over alleged corruption, but public mistrust persists.  

## Significance
- The release of the protesters underscores ongoing tensions between the Albanian government and citizens opposed to perceived foreign‑linked corruption and environmental degradation.  
- The protests continue to serve as a focal point for broader demands for political accountability and the resignation of Prime Minister Edi Rama.
