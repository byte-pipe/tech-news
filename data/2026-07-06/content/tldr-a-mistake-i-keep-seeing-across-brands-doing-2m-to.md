description: 'A mistake I keep seeing across brands doing $2M to $15M: they invest in a loyalty program and then let it live in a silo. It sits on a dedicated page on the site. Maybe there''s a small widget in the corner. Points accumulate quietly in the background. And nobody engages with it because nobody remembers it exists. Then the founder says "loyalty programs don''t work for our brand." No. Your loyalty program isn''t working because it''s invisible everywhere that matters. The brands I see actually getting ROI from loyalty have one thing in common: integration. The program shows up everywhere the customer already is. → In your emails. Your welcome flow should mention loyalty from day one. Your post purchase emails should show points earned. Your campaigns should remind people how close they are to a reward. If your loyalty program never appears in your email strategy, you''re relying on people to randomly visit a page they''ve seen once. → In your subscription flows. Pre-charge
  reminders should show accumulated points. Milestone emails should celebrate loyalty status. Cancellation flows should remind people what they''re walking away from. "You have 2,400 points, that''s $24 in rewards you''ll lose if you cancel." That''s a real retention lever. → On your landing pages. If someone lands on your site from an ad, they should see that joining comes with perks. Not buried in a footer link. Featured in the value prop. → In your retail experience if you have one. Points earned online should work in store. Purchases made in store should count toward online rewards. The problem is almost never the loyalty program itself. The points structure is fine. The rewards are fine. The problem is that it exists on an island disconnected from every other touchpoint in the customer journey. Loyalty is a layer that needs to sit on top of your entire customer experience. If the only place a customer interacts with your loyalty program is one page on your website, you''re leaving most
  of its value untapped.'
