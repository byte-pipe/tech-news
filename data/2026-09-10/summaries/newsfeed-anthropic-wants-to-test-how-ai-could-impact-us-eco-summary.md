# A new Anthropic model seeks to test how AI could impact the U.S. economy

## Key points
- Anthropic released an interactive tool that lets users explore how artificial intelligence might affect the U.S. economy over the next few years.  
- The tool presents two contrasting scenarios:  
  - **Modest change** – AI provides a marginal boost to growth with little impact on employment.  
  - **Extreme transformation** – AI drives GDP growth at more than seven times the current rate, but unemployment rises to about 14 % and many displaced workers struggle to find new jobs.  
- Outcomes depend on AI’s capability, speed of adoption, whether it augments or replaces workers, and the ability of displaced workers to transition to new roles.  
- Anthropic’s blog notes that AI experts generally forecast rapid diffusion, while economists are more cautious; co‑founder Jack Clark expects fast technical progress but slower economic integration.  
- A survey of roughly 11,000 respondents showed mixed public expectations: strong productivity gains paired with concerns about disruption in AI‑sensitive occupations.  
- Anton Korinek, head of transformative AI economic studies at Anthropic, emphasizes that the technology’s economic impact hinges on its adoption rate.  
- In the extreme scenario, the surge in GDP could generate substantial tax revenue, giving policymakers new tools to support affected workers; Clark advises governments to prepare for increased spending.
