# It’s been a nightmare: Passengers face airport chaos after UK air‑traffic issue

## Overview
- A technical failure at NATS, the UK air‑traffic control provider, caused an hours‑long outage on Tuesday‑Wednesday.
- Hundreds of thousands of travellers experienced flight cancellations, long delays and chaotic airport conditions.
- Major airports affected included Heathrow, Gatwick, Luton and Glasgow.

## Passenger experiences
- **Newlyweds Sasha and James Graham** spent three hours in a Gatwick Wetherspoons after their honeymoon flight to Madeira was cancelled; their luggage could not be reclaimed.
- **Phil White** described the baggage‑reclaim hall as “carnage,” with all cancelled‑flight bags dumped onto a single carousel, leading to frayed tempers.
- Several travellers were forced to sleep on airport floors or in hotel conference rooms:
  - Sukie Perera (Cardiff) stranded in Turkey, unable to return until Thursday.
  - Paul Willows (Rhodes, Greece) and about 80 other passengers slept on the floor of a hotel conference room.
  - Alan Jones and Kelly Fewery spent a night on the floor of Dalaman Airport without blankets or pillows.
- Passengers reported poor communication, long waits and exhausted conditions.

## Operational response
- NATS announced that flight operations have stabilised, but airlines warned of continued knock‑on disruption.
- NATS chief Martin Rolfe apologized and met Transport Secretary Heidi Alexander, who called for a full investigation.
- Airlines are required to re‑book passengers on alternative flights or provide refunds; they may also offer meals or hotel accommodation depending on delay length, but no extra compensation is due because the issue was not airline‑caused.

## Impact on flights
- More than 340 flights were cancelled on Wednesday, according to aviation analytics firm Cirium.
- Passengers are advised to check flight status before heading to the airport.
