# A Mod's Plea for Human Messiness in Accessibility Communities

## Introduction
A two-year gap has passed since a similar experience with writing technical posts was shared. As a moderator, it is delightful to review the volume of posts generated by AI tools within the accessibility community. However, the current trend poses an issue.

## Enjoying Human Writing
Personally, I appreciate reading and engaging with Posts written by humans as it brings a unique tone and style that allows me to discover their individual voice and experience without relying on predetermined knowledge (i.e., "it is not A, it is B").

## Desire for Quality Over Quantity
I yearn for posts to be made in messy form. I do not want automated tools to dictate the content; instead, I need human input. Not only does this encourage me to read and engage with the community without filters, but it also facilitates growth from incorrect knowledge through learning.

## Frustration with Accessibility Websites
A concern arises regarding the websites (such as dev.to, smashing magazine, reddit, etc.) that claim to offer "accessible" libraries or software. The situation warrants scrutiny because a sea of AI-generated articles has replaced more traditional human content.

## Unappealing Current State
Where are humans investing their time and effort in creating accessible content? I want to engage with them because it promotes my learning from their mistakes. By not engaging, I avoid unnecessary issues generated by bots or poorly constructed AI-driven responses.

## Templates as a Solution
To tackle this challenge, templates can be used as an efficient way to answer frequently asked questions, store reusable snipped snippets for further use, and offer a simple review feature while reducing manual effort required from humans.
