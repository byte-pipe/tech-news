# Water system controllers don't belong on the internet, says ex‑NSA chief after suspected Iran attacks

## Key points
- Retired General Paul Nakasone, former NSA director, warned at DEF CON that programmable logic controllers (PLCs) used in U.S. water systems should not be internet‑connected.
- The FBI is investigating “malicious cyber actors” targeting operational‑technology devices, and many security researchers suspect Iranian groups are behind recent attacks on water and wastewater facilities.
- Nakasone noted that while the U.S. government has not officially blamed Iran, the pattern of attacks matches known Iranian capabilities and intent, and the U.S. is in conflict with Iran.
- U.S. water infrastructure is highly fragmented: about 50,000 municipalities supply 90 % of the nation’s water, many of which are under‑funded and lack dedicated cybersecurity staff.
- Defense of this sector requires a collaborative approach, exemplified by the DEF CON Franklin project, which pairs hackers with water utilities to improve security.
- Nakasone is involved with Vanderbilt University’s Institute of National Security, the Wicked Problems Lab, and Project Chimera—a cybersecurity platform built on open‑source tools to enhance critical‑infrastructure resilience.
