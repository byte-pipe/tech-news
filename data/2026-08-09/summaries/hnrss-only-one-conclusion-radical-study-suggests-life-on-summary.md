# Only One Conclusion: Radical Study Suggests Life on Earth Arose Twice  

## Main Findings  
- The study published in *Science Advances* argues that the two primary domains of life—bacteria and archaea—originated independently from non‑living chemistry.  
- Enzymes that drive core metabolism are not conserved between the two domains, indicating separate evolutionary pathways.  
- Researchers propose “one origin of the genetic code, but two origins of life.”  

## Proposed Evolutionary Scenario  
- **Phase 1:** Metabolism was driven entirely by inorganic metal catalysts present in hydrothermal‑vent environments.  
- **Phase 2:** Proto‑cells accumulated enough metabolites to begin synthesizing primitive enzymes that supplemented metal catalysis.  
- **Phase 3:** Successive generations evolved more enzymes, gradually reducing reliance on external metals.  
- **Phase 4:** Fully “free‑living” cells emerged, no longer dependent on environmental metals; this stage occurred after bacteria and archaea had already diverged.  

## Evidence and Methodology  
- Protein structures of core metabolic enzymes from bacterial and archaeal genomes were analyzed.  
- A new algorithm ordered these enzymes by structural complexity, allowing reconstruction of a rough evolutionary timeline.  
- Findings show the last universal common ancestor (LUCA) possessed enzymes for only about half of the ~400‑reaction metabolic network; the remainder was likely catalyzed by environmental metals.  
- Instances were identified where bacterial and archaeal ancestors independently evolved distinct enzymes for the same essential reactions.  

## Alternative Pre‑biotic Chemistry  
- The team demonstrated that phosphite, a phosphorus form common in hydrothermal vents, can drive phosphorylation reactions with organic compounds in water, offering a plausible substitute for ATP and enzyme‑mediated phosphorylation in early metabolism.  

## Implications for the Tree of Life  
- Traditional models view LUCA as a single trunk from which all life branches.  
- This study suggests there may have been two separate “trunks” that diverged before true cellular life existed, requiring a revision of the conventional phylogenetic tree.  

## Context and Caveats  
- Defining life remains complex; metabolism is a universal feature absent in viruses, highlighting its importance in the argument.  
- The surrounding medium (water vs. other matrices) for early reactions is still uncertain.  
- Further research is needed to confirm and expand upon these conclusions.
