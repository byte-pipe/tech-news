# AI has entered the workforce: tax tech profits, not people

**Artificial Intelligence in the Workforce: Key Takeaways**

The emergence of AI in various industries can have a significant impact on employment, and governments must prepare for potential economic challenges.

**Taxation and Inequality**

To address job displacement caused by automation and technological change, governments may need to reassess their taxation system. A shift towards taxing capital more heavily could be an effective way to raise revenue, especially when profits are driven by market dominance or windfall gains.

* **Current Tax Status**: Labour is currently taxed, but technology has a relatively light tax profile.
* **Proposed Solutions**:
	+ Targeting the ownership or operation of robotic equipment as potential sources for AI taxation
	+ Possibly introducing measures to cap technological capital gains

**Preparation and Adjustment**

Goverments must prepare for an adjustment period after the introduction of AI-driven economic activities. This requires investment in:

* **Retraining and Upskilling**: To equip workers with skills relevant to AI-induced change

* **Funding Support**: For retraining, unemployment support, and other social welfare measures

**Key Economic Considerations**

As machines generate an increasing share of economic value, governments should confront the following key economic issues:

* Who pays for the costs associated with job displacement?
* To what extent can governments mitigate the impact on employment?

**Conclusion**

While AI has already begun to transform industries and jobs, potential economic challenges are still being mitigated. Governments will need to implement policies that address these issues, such as increased taxation of capital or investment in retraining programs.
