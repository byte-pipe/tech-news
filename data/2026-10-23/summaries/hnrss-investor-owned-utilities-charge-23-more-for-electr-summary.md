# Investor-owned utilities charge 23% more for electricity than public ones: Electricity Prices by Utility Owner | US Energy Price Monitor

# Key Points and Summary

• Investor-owned utilities charge 23% more for electricity than public utilities in 2025, according to data showing a 34% rise in investor-owned prices from 2020 to 2025, and a 19% increase from 2020 to 2025.
• The difference in prices is due to a 24% increase in utilities' operating expenses and a 20% rise in costs tied to inflation.
• The difference varies by utility, with 67% of homes paid 12-20 cents per kilowatt-hour and 11% paid 30 cents or more.
• The gap in prices is widest among the top 5 states, with Maine, Washington, California, New Jersey, New York, and Ohio experiencing significant increases in investor-owned prices.
• Investor-owned utilities charge higher prices in states with higher home prices, with co-ops experiencing even higher costs compared to public and nonprofit providers.
