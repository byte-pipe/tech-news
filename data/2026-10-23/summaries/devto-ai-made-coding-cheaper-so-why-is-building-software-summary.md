# AI Made Coding Cheaper. So Why Is Building Software Still So Expensive? - DEV Community

**The Expense of Complexity: The Trade-Off Between Speed and Understanding**

Code review is now the new bottleneck, requiring an enormous amount of time and effort. However, the reason why your last "simple" feature took three weeks to reach production may not be the code itself, but the complex decisions made during the development process.

**The Cognitive Load of Context**

The article highlights the Jevons Paradox in software engineering, where technological progress increases the efficiency of resource usage, but this also leads to increased total consumption of that resource, including human cognitive labor. In this case, the bottleneck of software engineering is not typing speed, but the cognitive load of context.

*   **Review and Trust Tax**: Humans spend a significant amount of time reviewing code to understand its logic and ensure it meets requirements. This review process is time-consuming and can be tedious.
*   **Context Debt**: AI code has no context debt, which is the time and effort spent by human developers to understand the underlying logic and maintenance context. This lack of understanding can lead to broken code or unexpected behavior.
*   **Integration Cost**: Integrating AI code into the existing repository is a complex task that requires a deep understanding of the architecture, dependencies, and edge cases. This integration process is often overlooked or underestimated, leading to higher costs down the line.

**The Consequences of a High-Cognitive Load**

When the cognitive load of context outweighs the efficiency gains from AI-driven coding, the result is increased costs. In this scenario:

1.  **Cost Overruns**: The review and trust tax can lead to project delays and cost overruns, as the team spends more time addressing issues than delivering the desired outcome.
2.  **Contextual Debt**: The integration cost associated with AI code can be high, leading to a significant increase in maintenance and support costs over time.
3.  **Maintenance and Upgrades**: To maintain and upgrade AI-driven code, teams must invest significant time and resources, which can lead to long-term cost savings.

**The Bottom Line**

The issue of AI making code cheaper is not a problem requiring a simple fix. Instead, it's a complex issue that requires a deeper understanding of human cognition and contextual dependencies. By acknowledging this cognitive load, teams can take steps to mitigate the costs associated with AI-driven coding, leading to more efficient development and a better return on investment.
