### 📌 Basis Fellowship: $50,000 for Selected Fellows

Who it's for:People working on interesting or unconventional projects, including engineers, scientists, founders, operators, and others pursuing work across different fields.

What it's about:

The Basis Fellowship offers$50,000 to selected fellows, along with access to a network of people working across different areas.

Rather than focusing on one particular industry or type of project, the fellowship is interested in people doing a wide range of work. The examples on its website include an electrical setup, a thermoelectric cloud chamber, and a neurostimulation machine. The idea is to bring together people who might be working on very different things but could benefit from finding one another.

The fellowship doesn't require equity, reporting, or restrictions on how the money is spent. Applicants can submit a portfolio and other useful information, and the application asks them to answer the questions honestly.

People from different backgrounds can apply, whether they're engineers, scientists, operators, or working in another field. Applications are accepted on a rolling basis, and an application doesn't expire if someone isn't selected immediately. Applicants can send updates when something new happens.

There's also a nomination option. If someone you nominate is selected, you receive $1,000. The website also mentions a discretionary $3,000 award for a strong submission that isn't selected.

### 📌 More Than a Hack 2027: Football Analytics Hackathon

Who it's for:Football analysts, data analysts and scientists, software developers, and tech enthusiasts interested in using data and technology to understand football.

What it's about:

More Than a Hack 2027 is a football analytics hackathon organized by Mobile World Capital Barcelona, Barça Innovation Hub, and Metrica Sports. Participants work with real FC Barcelona match data to develop analytical solutions related to the game.

The hackathon has two online challenge stages, followed by an in-person final pitch at Talent Arena 2027 in Barcelona.

TheInitial Challengeasks teams to estimate team possession for every frame of a football match using provided automatic tracking data and homography. Participants don't need to build player detection, identification, appearance, or calibration systems from scratch. Submissions are automatically evaluated and ranked.

Thetop 20 teamsadvance to the Innovation Challenge. In this stage, teams can explore analytical or tactical questions using a dataset of 10 professional matches. They can experiment with different approaches to analysis, modeling, interpretation, and generating insights, with specialized technical mentoring available.

Thetop five teamsfrom this stage will be invited to present their projects at Talent Arena 2027 in Barcelona. Travel and accommodation expenses for the final pitch are covered. The listed prizes also include official FC Barcelona shirts, Barça Innovation Hub merchandise, and an exclusive FC Barcelona experience.

Teams must have3 to 5 members. Teams with fewer than three members won't be eligible for the Innovation Challenge or the Final Pitch.

Registration is open until November 10, 2026. The Initial Challenge is already underway, so anyone interested should review the schedule and registration requirements before joining.

### 📌 Perplexity Research Fellowship: AI Research Program

Who it's for:Early-career researchers, engineers, and analysts from technical or quantitative disciplines, including fields such as physics, mathematics, cognitive science, and quantitative finance.

What it's about:

The Perplexity Research Fellowship is a full-time research program for early-career talent interested in advancing AI research. Fellows join Perplexity's R&D teams and work on research areas such as multi-agent planning, continual learning, pre-training, and human-AI interaction.

The program has two tracks:

* Predoctoral Research Fellow:For exceptional early-career talent from technical or quantitative disciplines.
* Postdoctoral Research Fellow:For researchers who have recently completed a PhD in a technical or quantitative discipline.

The fellowship runs forthree months, with opportunities to extend the program or transition into a full-time, research-focused role. Fellows work in person alongside researchers and engineers at one of Perplexity's offices and may contribute to research publications and other research outputs.

I came across this opportunity after its priority consideration deadline had passed, but applications are still being accepted on a rolling basis until positions are filled. The priority deadline was September 30, 2026, so applications submitted now may not receive priority consideration. The November 2026 and January 2027 cohorts are listed as priority start dates, but it's worth checking the application page for current availability.

### Harness Engineering 101: A Practical Guide to Building AI Agents

Who it's for:Developers who want to understand how AI agents work, particularly those interested in LLM APIs, tool use, context management, and building coding agents.

What it's about:

Harness Engineering 101 is a free online book by Isuru Wijesiri that explores how to build AI agents from first principles.

It starts with a simple idea: an LLM provides the reasoning, while the harness is the program that manages the conversation, tools, and execution around it.

The book develops this idea across 16 chapters, using a small Python agent of approximately 300 lines as its foundation. Rather than treating agent frameworks as black boxes, it explains the problems that arise when building an agent and the changes needed to address them.

The chapters cover topics such as the agent loop, tool calling, caching, context-window management, subagents, background work, debugging, guardrails, and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). There are also sections on coding agents, MCP, skills, hooks, model routing, worktree isolation, retries, rate limits, and streaming.

Each chapter follows a practical structure: the problem being addressed, the minimal change needed to solve it, and the trade-offs that come with that change. The book also includes runnable Python code and diagrams to help explain the concepts.

If you're trying to understand what happens behind an AI agent framework, or want to build a basic agent yourself before working with more complex abstractions, this could be a useful resource to explore.
