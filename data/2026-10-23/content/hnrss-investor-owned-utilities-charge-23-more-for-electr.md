# Investor-owned utilities charge 23% more for electricity than public ones

In2025, homes paid17.8¢a kilowatt-hour atinvestor-ownedutilities,14.5¢atpublicutilities and14.1¢atco-ops. Since2020, investor-owned prices rose34%and public ones19%, against24%inflation.

## About the data

* Public power is common in cities and near federal dams, where power is cheap, and public utilities pay no income tax and borrow tax-free. That explains some of the gap, not why it grew.
* Most of the gap opened from2020to2023, during the energy price spike:25%at investor-owned utilities,11%at public utilities and co-ops.Before and since, both rose at about the same pace.
* After inflation, investor-owned pricesrose7.9%. Public and co-op pricesfell4.3%.
* Co-op homes have the highest bills,$159a month in2025, because they use the most power.
* Homes that buy power from a retail supplier aren't in the investor-owned figures. Counting them, prices on investor-owned lines rose36%.
* Price is what homes paid divided by the kilowatt-hours they used, from EIA's yearly survey of utilities. Federal utilities and the smallest ones are left out.
* The data shows what homes paid, not why. Investor-owned utilities earn a return for shareholders on what they build. Public utilities and co-ops have no shareholders. See therate casesfor increases utilities are asking for now.
