# AI/ML is becoming a performance factor in motorsport – Ars Technica

## Changing sponsorship landscape
- Traditional motorsport funding (tobacco, alcohol) has largely disappeared.
- Current sponsor logos increasingly represent crypto, cyber‑security, and AI companies.
- Tech CEOs now act as privateer backers at the amateur level (e.g., GitHub, CrowdStrike, Shopify, creator of Ruby on Rails).

## OpenAI partnership with Chip Ganassi Racing
- Chip Ganassi Racing (CGR) is the most successful IndyCar team of the past two decades, with drivers such as Dario Franchitti, Scott Dixon, and currently Álex Palou.
- Palou secured his fifth championship in six seasons; in the 2023 season he won 3 of 18 races and claimed 8 poles, outpacing all rivals.
- OpenAI and a researcher collaborated with CGR to optimize car setups; the work is featured in a YouTube documentary series (episode 2 released).
- The partnership yielded strong qualifying performance in Washington, DC, though the race result was disappointing.

## Other AI/ML applications in motorsport
- General Motors employed AI to predict caution periods from radio communications and built a tool that analyzes crash footage within seconds; the operation now runs from Charlotte, NC, alongside Cadillac’s new F1 team and many IndyCar competitors.
- IBM and chassis manufacturer Dallara demonstrated that AI trained on computational fluid dynamics (CFD) data can accelerate aerodynamic development, a technique also used by several F1 teams through Neural Concept.
- The article references Mark Donohue’s 1975 biography *The Unfair Advantage*, highlighting the long‑standing value of systematic engineering approaches—now amplified by modern AI/ML tools.
