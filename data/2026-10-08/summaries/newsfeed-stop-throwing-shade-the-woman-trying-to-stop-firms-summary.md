# Stop throwing shade – the woman trying to stop firms leaving the UK

## Main points
- Dame Julia Hoggett, chief executive of the London Stock Exchange (LSE), told the BBC that the UK must become more attractive for investment or companies will continue to list abroad.  
- She urged the government to “take the handbrake off” and create structural incentives for British firms to stay and grow in the UK market.  
- Over recent years, several high‑profile companies have delisted or moved from the LSE to exchanges in Amsterdam, Frankfurt and New York, reducing tax revenue and depressing valuations.  

## Market data
- The LSE’s main market hosts about 930 companies with a total value of roughly £4.9 trillion; around 40 % are foreign‑owned.  
- In 2023 there were only 23 IPOs in London raising £2.1 bn, compared with 354 IPOs in the United States raising $44 bn (£33 bn).  
- UK investors are increasingly buying US stocks, seeking higher returns, while domestic listings have stalled.  

## Proposed policy changes
- Remove the 0.5 % stamp‑duty on purchases of UK shares, which does not apply to foreign stock purchases.  
- Re‑introduce tax credits for UK residents investing in domestic equities, a scheme that existed until 2016.  
- Provide broader incentives and lighter regulation to encourage both companies and investors to choose the UK market.  

## Reactions and context

- The government declined to comment on whether stock‑market reforms will appear in the upcoming Budget, stating that tax decisions are set by the Chancellor at fiscal events.
