NASA’s Crew-12 returns to Earth after 8 months in space
 
Four people — two NASA astronauts, one from the European Space Agency and a Russian cosmonaut — spent nearly eight months at the space station, conducting spacewalks, science experiments and research.

Three astronauts and a cosmonaut of NASA'sCrew-12mission have safely returned to Earth after an almost eight-month trip to the International Space Station.

 

 

### Space

### New space station crew launches from Florida on SpaceX's Crew Dragon capsule

The crew, commanded by NASA's Jessica Meir and pilot Jack Hathaway with mission specialists Sophie Adenot of the European Space Agency and Andrey Fedyaev of Russia's Roscosmos, splashed down into the Pacific Ocean off the coast of Los Angeles, Calif., on Thursday, aboard a SpaceX Crew Dragon capsule.

 

Their trip home started about 45 minutes before, at an orbital speed of 17,500 mph.

 

The spacecraft lit its thrusters to nudge it out of orbit, causing the capsule to plunge into the atmosphere, its ablative heat shield battling extreme temperatures (up to 3,500 degrees Fahrenheit) during the fiery reentry. Over the course of about half an hour, the capsule slowed to a few hundred miles per hour, and then fourorange-and-white parachutestook over, easing the spacecraft to just 16 mph above the cool, blue ocean and a gentle plop into the water.

The Crew-12 mission was the 20th time SpaceX had sent humans into orbit,lifting offin February on a Falcon 9 rocket from Cape Canaveral in Florida. It docked with the space station thenext day. During the eight months in space, NASA said the crew completeddozensof science experiments and research, including studies of stem cells, how crystals grow in space and bone loss in microgravity.

 

Not all of their work happened inside the space station. Meir conducted four spacewalks totaling just over 27 hours to perform maintenance and upgrade the aging orbital outpost. Adenot performed three spacewalks.

 

Before departing, members of the Crew-12 mission were relieved by the three astronauts and cosmonaut ofCrew-13, who arrived at the space stationlast weekto start a six-month rotation.

 

 

### Space

### 'A glorious dawn launch': NASA's newest space telescope has left the Earth

During a pre-departurenews conferenceon the station last month, Meir said she couldn't wait to be reunited with her 3 1/2-year-old daughter.

 

But there was something else she missed after all this time in orbit. "So looking forward to seeing nature again, feeling the breeze on my face, the wind rustling through the leaves. Experiencing all those things that we don't have up here," she said.

 

The two-time space veteran said there were two things she'd miss about space: weightlessness and the wonderful views of Earth.
