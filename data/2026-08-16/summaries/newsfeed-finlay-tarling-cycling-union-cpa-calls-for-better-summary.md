# Finlay Tarling: Cycling union CPA calls for better helmets for racers

## Incident overview
- 19‑year‑old British rider Finlay Tarling (NSN Cycling) died during Friday’s stage of the Volta a Portugal.  
- The crash involved a collision with a light‑goods vehicle that was not part of the race.  
- Portuguese National Republican Guard opened an investigation; media report the driver is unlikely to face charges.

## Union reaction
- Cyclistes Professionnels Associés (CPA) expressed “fury” and said they were “speechless and filled with anger.”  
- CPA president Adam Hansen demanded a major upgrade to helmet standards, noting the current EU CE EN 1078 test is limited to 19.5 km/h and is insufficient for road racing speeds.  
- Hansen criticised the existing “hole in the system” of road closures and rolling roadblocks that left riders exposed.

## Personal background
- Finlay was the younger brother of Josh Tarling, a time‑trial specialist for Ineos Grenadiers who has won a Giro d’Italia stage and a European time‑trial title.  
- He grew up in Ceredigion, west Wales; his parents Michael and Dawn frequently attended races and even set up a family team.  
- Part of a new generation of British male and female WorldTour riders inspired by the successes of Mark Cavendish, Chris Froome and Lizzie Deignan.

## Safety context in professional cycling
- Descents on public roads can reach up to 60 mph, with riders protected only by helmets.  
- Average speeds are increasing; the Tour de France stage 11 set a record average of 50.91 km/h (31.6 mph).  
- Six riders have died in road races over the past three years, including Muriel Furrer (2024 Road World Championships junior race) and Gino Mader (2023 Tour de Suisse).  
- Tarling’s death is the first road‑race fatality recorded in 2026.

## Road‑closure practices
- Major events often use a “rolling roadblock” where police motorbikes and organiser cars temporarily close sections of the route.  
- This method is costly (typically >30 vehicles) and can leave gaps, especially for riders who fall back from the peloton.  
- Past incidents highlight the risk: Max Schachmann was hit by a car in Il Lombardia 2020; the 2023 Tour Féminin des Pyrénées was cancelled after multiple vehicle close‑calls.

## Planned tributes
- Organisers are considering a tribute to Finlay Tarling during the men’s Tour of Britain, which begins on 2 September.
