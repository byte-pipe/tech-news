# Mark Ritson: Brands are all in on creators. Meanwhile in reality, the economics crash  

## Overview  
- The creator economy is portrayed as the biggest marketing trend, with 86 % of U.S. marketers using influencer marketing and projected spend of over $12 bn this year.  
- Estimates of the sector’s total size vary wildly ($34 bn‑$47.8 bn), showing a lack of reliable data.  

## The hype vs. the hard facts  
- Influencer Marketing Hub touts a $5.78 return for every dollar spent, but this figure is based on “earned media value” (EMV), a non‑cash metric that inflates impressions with invented rate cards.  
- Audits reveal that 37 % of influencer followers are fake or purchased.  
- A new report from creator agency New Engen shows:  
  - Over 50 % of creators earn less than $15 000 a year, up from 48 % two years earlier.  
  - Nano‑creators (very small followings) now capture almost half of U.S. creator spend.  
  - Mid‑tier creators, once the “middle class” of the economy, are increasingly struggling; some are leaving the field for corporate jobs.  

## Emerging survival tactics  
- Creators are monetising paid direct messages ($20‑$30 per reply) for personalized content, a practice sometimes outsourced, signalling desperation rather than genuine engagement.  
- Audience fatigue is growing: 55 % of Americans post less on social media than five years ago, and 51 % view maintaining a social presence as “work.”  

## What this means for marketers  
- Influencer marketing remains a useful reach channel, not a trust‑building panacea; its value is lower than the hype suggests.  
- Brands should treat creators as one channel among many, integrating them with TV, search, brand, and activation tactics for a balanced, multichannel strategy.  
- Investment should be disciplined: barter for measurable outcomes, understand the economics, and avoid over‑reliance on a single “shiny new” tool.  

## Bottom line  
- Creators work when used with the right strategy and realistic expectations.  
- The sector suffers from inflated metrics, talent‑economics collapse, and measurement challenges, so marketers must adopt a calm, strategic approach rather than succumbing to hype.
