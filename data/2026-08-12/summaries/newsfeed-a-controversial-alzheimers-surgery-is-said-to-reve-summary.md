# A controversial Alzheimer’s surgery is said to reverse symptoms — here’s what scientists know | Nature

**The Potential of Deep Cervical Lymphatic-Venous Anastomosis (dcLVA) in Treating Alzheimer's Disease**

* A recent surgery in China, known as deep cervical lymphatic-venous anastomosis (dcLVA), has shown promising results in reversing some symptoms of Alzheimer's disease.
* The procedure involves connecting tiny lymphatic vessels in the neck to nearby veins, potentially improving fluid flow and waste protein removal from the brain.
* This treatment was initially reported by microsurgeon Qingping Xie in 2022 in a Chinese-language journal, but gained widespread attention after being showcased at surgical meetings worldwide.
* Researchers have been working to replicate this technique using other hospitals in China and setting off an international frenzy.

**Key Findings and Implications**

* Almost all surgeries for dcLVA were performed in China, with hundreds of hospitals offering the procedure.
* Restrictions on the procedure's use by Chinese regulators led to its adoption globally, sparking concerns about ethics and access to medical treatments.
* Control studies are underway around the world to assess the efficacy of the treatment.
* Researchers face challenges in explaining how quickly some individuals show improvement despite minimal evidence supporting the claim.

**Criticisms and Concerns**

* Studies have raised questions about the mechanism behind the rapid recovery, with many researchers concerned it may not be a reliable or sustainable outcome.
* Potential risks associated with the procedure, such as infection, bleeding, and nerve damage, were cited by clinicians.
* Multiple researchers remain hesitant to endorse the treatment due to these uncertainties.
