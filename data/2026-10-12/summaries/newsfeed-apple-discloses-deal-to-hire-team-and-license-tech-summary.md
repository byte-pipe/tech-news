# Apple discloses deal to hire team and license tech from personalized podcast startup Huxe

## Deal overview
- Apple filed a regulatory notice with the European Commission stating it will:
  - Extend employment offers to “certain employees of Huxe AI.”
  - Obtain a non‑exclusive license to Huxe’s intellectual property.
- The arrangement is described as a reverse acqui‑hire, allowing Apple to acquire talent and technology without a full acquisition.

## Background on Huxe
- Huxe was founded by developers who previously built AI‑generated podcast features for Notebook LM (now Gemini Notebook).
- On May 21, Huxe announced it would shut down, remove its app from Apple and Google stores, halt service, and delete user data.
- The company thanked its users and indicated the team would move on to new projects.

## Timeline
- May 21: Huxe announces shutdown.
- June 9: Apple notifies the European Commission of its employment and licensing agreement with Huxe.
- The filing does not reveal which employees received offers or whether they accepted, nor does it detail Apple’s intended use of the technology.

## Potential implications
- The timing follows Spotify’s launch of its own AI‑powered podcast generation features, suggesting Apple may aim to add similar capabilities to its Podcasts app.
- Reverse acqui‑hires help large firms acquire AI talent and assets while potentially reducing antitrust scrutiny compared with full acquisitions.
- The broader industry interest in personalized audio hints at a shift toward AI‑driven podcast experiences, though consumer reception remains uncertain.
