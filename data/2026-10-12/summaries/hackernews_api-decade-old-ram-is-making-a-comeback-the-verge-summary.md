# Decade‑old RAM is making a comeback

## Background
- A prolonged global RAM shortage has driven up DDR5 prices, making upgrades difficult for many PC users.  
- DDR4, introduced in 2014 and succeeded by DDR5, is now seen as a more affordable alternative.

## Industry response
- **Intel**: Gigabyte announced that upcoming LGA 1700 motherboards will again support both DDR4 and DDR5, with new CPUs expected in early 2027. The socket’s dual‑compatibility offers a cheaper upgrade path.
- **AMD**: Gigabyte also revealed new AM4 motherboards for the DDR4‑compatible platform, timed with the 10‑year anniversary of the Ryzen 7 5800X3D CPU.
- Both companies are effectively reviving older sockets and chipsets to give consumers a choice that avoids the costly switch to DDR5.

## Price comparison
- Current market example: a 32 GB DDR5 Corsair Vengeance kit sells for about **$620**, while a comparable 32 GB DDR4 kit is around **$260**.  
- DDR5 still provides higher speeds and better power efficiency, but the price premium may not be justified for many users right now.

## Implications for consumers
- The price gap suggests DDR4 will remain a viable option for the foreseeable future, especially as DDR5 prices are unlikely to drop soon.  
- Users are encouraged to retain existing DDR4 modules and consider new CPUs that maintain DDR4 support rather than forcing a full platform overhaul.  

## Related context
- The RAM shortage is part of broader memory constraints affecting SSD prices and overall PC component markets.  
- Recent reports note a ~20 % drop in PC shipments in Q3, highlighting the broader impact of component shortages on the industry.
