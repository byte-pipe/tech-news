# Microsoft offers mass buyouts for roughly a quarter of its workforce

## Key points
- Microsoft announced a voluntary exit offer for about 60,000 employees (≈25% of its workforce) in the U.S., Canada, India, and the United Kingdom, covering staff at the vice‑president level and below.  
- Eligible workers have until early December to accept a standard severance package that varies by location.  
- The program builds on a earlier voluntary retirement initiative that attracted over 8,700 U.S. employees, with roughly one‑third participating.  
- Full participation comes from the gaming, Windows, Copilot, human resources, and finance divisions; AI, legal, and cloud teams are partially involved, while marketing, commercial business, and security are excluded.  
- Microsoft has been reducing headcount since May 2025 through layoffs (≈15,000 jobs last year, including 3,200 in Washington) and now through buyouts, aiming for a flatter, more agile organization and to curb rising AI‑related capital expenditures.  
- Leadership cites cultural concerns after the 2024 layoffs and emphasizes reskilling, internal mobility, and continued hiring to keep overall headcount relatively flat (a net loss of about 5,000 employees between 2025‑2026).  
- Chief People Officer Amy Coleman notes the offer provides a new transition option and will be evaluated as a new tool for workforce management.  

## Context
- The buyout follows a period of cost‑cutting driven by massive investment in AI infrastructure ($190 billion planned for custom chips and data centers in 2025).  
- Microsoft’s Redmond workforce remains around 52,000, with stability attributed to reskilling programs and hiring for emerging AI‑focused teams.
