# Anthropic S-1 shows it is at the hyperscalers’ mercy – 5 key takeaways

## 1. Big losses > accounting artifacts  
- Net loss of $81 billion for H1 2026, but $72 billion stem from non‑cash accounting charges.  
- Underlying operating loss was $693 million, down from $2.1 billion in H1 2025.  
- Revenue for the period was $16 billion; compute and infrastructure OpEx was $8.9 billion.  
- Cash and cash equivalents total $112.4 billion plus $19.3 billion in short‑term investments, giving roughly a 12‑month runway.

## 2. Colossal spending commitments  
- Non‑cancelable contracts for GPU compute:  
  - SpaceXAI – $120.5 billion by 2029  
  - Google – $114 billion by 2033  
  - AWS – $110 billion by 2036 (cancellable only on Amazon’s material breach)  
  - Microsoft – $44.9 billion by May 2033  
- Data‑center hardware commitment with Broadcom: $161.2 billion (excluding interest).

## 3. At the mercy of the hyperscalers?  
- Anthropic acknowledges heavy reliance on compute and competition with well‑capitalized tech giants that own cloud and chip assets.  
- Rivals can outbid Anthropic for scarce compute, secure priority from chip makers, and make long‑term capital commitments Anthropic cannot match.

## 4. Compute efficiency is improving sharply  
- Revenue grew 1,368 % in the three months to June 2026, while compute and infrastructure expenses rose 300 % YoY.  
- Claude Opus 5.5 reduced customer cost per task by ~40 % versus Claude Opus 5, indicating progress in lowering per‑task costs.

## 5. Going public with founders retaining control  
- Seven co‑founders will hold 50.1 % of voting power through a “Founder LLC” that issues a single Class F common share.  
- Anthropic will be classified as a “controlled company” on Nasdaq, allowing it to opt out of certain governance requirements (e.g., independent‑director nomination committee).  
- The governance structure is presented as unprecedented and intended to deter unsolicited merger offers or proxy contests.
