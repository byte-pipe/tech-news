# ‘The banks will not accept it’: Dimon escalates battle over stablecoin rewards in CLARITY Act debate

## Main points
- JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon criticized Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong and warned that the current draft of the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act (CLARITY Act) could fail if it allows stablecoin issuers to pay interest‑like rewards without bank‑style protections.  
- Dimon told Fox Business that “the banks will not accept it” and predicted the system would “eventually blow up” if adopted as is.  
- The dispute centers on whether stablecoin reward programs, which resemble high‑yield deposit accounts, should be regulated like traditional bank interest, a key obstacle to advancing the bill.  
- Lawmakers are merging separate Senate Banking and Agriculture Committee versions of the bill before a full Senate vote; the legislation still requires House approval and the President’s signature.  
- Tensions between crypto firms and major banks have escalated: Dimon called Armstrong “full of s---” at Davos, Bank of America’s Brian Moynihan told Armstrong “If you want to be a bank, just be a bank,” while Wells Fargo and Citigroup CEOs largely avoided engagement.  
- Coinbase and JPMorgan did not comment for this article.  

## Context
- The CLARITY Act aims to create a federal framework for how securities and commodities regulators oversee crypto assets.  
- Stablecoin issuers seek to offer yield‑bearing products; banks argue such products should face the same consumer‑protection, reserve‑requirement, and oversight rules that apply to deposits.  
- The disagreement has stalled the bill’s progress despite bipartisan interest in regulating digital assets.  

## Legislative outlook
- The merged bill must clear both chambers of Congress and be signed by President Donald Trump to become law.  
- Ongoing negotiations will focus on stablecoin rewards, consumer protections, reserve requirements, and the permissibility of crypto‑based yield products.
