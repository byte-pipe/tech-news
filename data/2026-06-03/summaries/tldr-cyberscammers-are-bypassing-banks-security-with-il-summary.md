# Cyberscammers are bypassing banks’ security with illicit tools sold on Telegram | MIT Technology Review

# Summary of “Cyberscammers are bypassing banks’ security with illicit tools sold on Telegram”

## Executive summary
- A Cambodian money‑laundering employee demonstrated how a virtual‑camera tool can defeat a Vietnamese banking app’s KYC “liveness” check by feeding a static image instead of a live video.
- The exploit is sold on public Telegram channels as part of a growing market of illicit hacking services that bypass facial‑recognition KYC checks for banks and crypto exchanges.
- These tools enable scammers to open mule accounts, launder proceeds from “pig‑butchering” scams, and move money quickly into stablecoins such as Tether.

## Scale of the problem
- MIT Technology Review identified 22 Telegram channels (Chinese, Vietnamese, English) advertising KYC‑bypass kits and stolen biometric data.
- Channels have thousands of subscribers; many list services like “All kinds of KYC verification services” and post videos of successful hacks.
- Chainalysis estimates crypto scams stole ≈ $17 billion in 2025, up from $13 billion in 2024; UNODC notes rapid expansion of Asian scam syndicates into Africa and the Pacific.
- iProov reports virtual‑camera attacks were >25 × more common in 2024 than in 2023; Sumsub says “sophisticated” fraud attempts, including virtual‑camera bypasses, nearly tripled last year.

## Methods used by criminals
- **Virtual camera (VCam) attacks**: replace the device’s live camera feed with pre‑recorded videos, photos, deepfakes, or stolen biometrics.
- **Phone jailbreaks**: modify the operating system to allow VCam activation at will.
- **Hooking frameworks**: inject code into banking or crypto‑exchange apps to trigger VCam automatically.
- **Combined approach**: compromise both the phone and the target app, then feed a mix of stolen biometric data and deepfakes to the KYC system.

## Impact on financial institutions
- Targets named on Telegram include Binance, Spain’s BBVA, and UK‑based Revolut; all acknowledge the issue as an industry‑wide challenge.
- Binance claims to have prevented such attacks; BBVA and Revolut declined to comment on specific breaches.
- Sumsub’s head of fraud prevention warns that many attacks remain undetected, “the part of the story we don’t see.”
- Talsec’s CEO reports a rise from <10 to ~30 VCam‑based hack requests from banks and exchanges over the past year.

## Response and outlook
- Governments in Vietnam, Thailand, and other jurisdictions are tightening KYC and AML regulations for banks and crypto platforms.
- Telegram removed some identified channels after review, but new marketplaces emerge quickly, keeping the supply of bypass tools active.
- Researchers and law‑enforcement partners (e.g., Hieu Minh Ngo) are documenting demos and assisting investigations, but the cat‑and‑mouse dynamic persists.
- As scrutiny intensifies and scam profits grow, KYC bypass kits are becoming “essential” for money‑laundering networks, suggesting continued pressure on financial‑service security and ongoing adaptation by both attackers and defenders.
