# Being an AI-native designer isn’t what you think it is

## Core insight
- “AI-native designer” is now a common job requirement, but it does **not** refer to designing AI interfaces or mastering new tools.
- The term actually describes a designer’s ability to handle ambiguity and delegate repetitive work to AI.

## The most important skill: critical thinking
- 9 out of 28 design leaders identified critical thinking as the top skill for designers today.
- Designers who pause to ask “why,” “what are we trying to achieve,” and “who is the user” are far more valuable than those who jump straight to wireframes.
- As AI takes over well‑defined, pattern‑based tasks, human judgment is needed for messy, uncertain problems.

## How AI fits into the workflow
- After clarifying a problem, designers can use AI for low‑creativity, high‑effort tasks such as:
  - Summarizing meeting notes
  - Extracting insights from interview transcripts
  - Writing documentation
  - Generating basic design variations
- AI does not define the problem, set constraints, or choose which solutions to explore; the designer still performs those strategic steps.

## What being AI‑native actually means
- It is a mindset of **delegating** repetitive tasks to AI once the task is clearly defined.
- Example: Using Copilot to pull user quotes and Figma Make to sketch menu variations reduces a two‑hour effort to 30 minutes, while the designer still decides the problem definition and evaluation criteria.

## Three questions to ask before delegating to AI
1. **Is the task clearly defined?**  
   - Replace vague prompts (“summarize my research”) with specific instructions (“identify the top three recurring pain points in these five interview transcripts”).
2. **Do I know what a good output looks like?**  
   - Establish a mental standard to evaluate AI results (e.g., a nuanced jacket‑fit recommendation versus a generic size statement).
3. **Can I spot a bad output?**  
   - Only delegate tasks where you have enough domain knowledge to catch errors; otherwise, the AI’s confident mistakes may go unnoticed.

## Delegation mindset
- Job postings for AI‑native designers are essentially asking: **Can you delegate?**
- Delegation here means breaking tasks down so AI can handle them without downstream issues.
- The goal is to let AI handle “dishes and laundry” (tedious work) so designers can focus on judgment, taste, and creative instinct.

## Takeaway
- Being an AI‑native designer is less about tool mastery and more about critical problem‑solving and effective delegation to AI.
- Mastery of craft remains essential; AI is a productivity aid, not a substitute for the designer’s strategic contribution.
