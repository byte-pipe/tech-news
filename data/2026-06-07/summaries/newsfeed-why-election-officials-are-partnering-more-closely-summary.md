# Why election officials are partnering more closely with police

## Background and emerging trend
- Since the 2020 election, local law‑enforcement agencies have taken a larger, year‑round role in securing polls.  
- Threats, harassment, and a surge in bomb threats after former President Trump’s false fraud claims have driven this shift.  
- Survey data: 32 % of local election officials reported personal threats; 89 % say they will coordinate with at least one other agency for the 2026 midterms.

## Reasons for increased collaboration
- Election officials face a “heightened and longer‑term” security burden that cannot be addressed only on Election Day.  
- Ongoing, cross‑functional communication between police and election staff is needed well before the voting calendar opens.  
- Police expertise helps address specific legal issues such as electioneering rules, voter‑challenge procedures, and rapid response to bomb threats.

## Implementation examples
- **Green Bay, Wisconsin** – Police Chief Chris Davis moved from a reactive stance to a proactive, year‑round planning model after recognizing staff anxiety and gaps in election‑law knowledge.  
- **Training and tabletop exercises** – Officials credit extensive rehearsals and joint training for the smooth handling of a record number of bomb threats during the 2024 election.  
- **Committee for Safe and Secure Elections** – Co‑chair Tina Barton stresses that election security planning is a “never‑ending conversation” that begins as soon as an election cycle is contemplated.

## Concerns and safeguards
- Voting‑rights advocates warn that visible police presence can unintentionally intimidate voters and contribute to suppression.  
- Federal law prohibits federal troops or law‑enforcement officers from interfering with voting; recent mixed messages about immigration agents at polls have heightened sensitivity.  
- Incidents such as the Riverside County, California sheriff’s seizure of ballots prompted state legislation explicitly banning such interference.  
- Experts recommend a “light touch” – police should stay largely behind the scenes while remaining ready to intervene when safety is at risk.

## Outlook for future elections
- Election administrators expect continued, deeper partnerships with law‑enforcement agencies to protect the integrity and safety of voting.  
- Finding the right balance between visibility and restraint will remain a key focus for police departments as they refine election‑security protocols.  
- Ongoing training, early coordination, and clear guidelines are viewed as essential to maintaining voter confidence and preventing intimidation.
