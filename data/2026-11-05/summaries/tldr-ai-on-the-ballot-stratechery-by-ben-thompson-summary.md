# AI on the Ballot – Summary

## Election backdrop and data‑center politics
- In the November 2026 midterms Democrats captured both chambers of Congress, a result attributed largely to voter dissatisfaction with the economy and the war in Iran.  
- In Wisconsin the most common campaign theme was data‑center construction, with both parties accusing the other of being pro‑data‑center.  
- In Texas Democrat Gina Hinojosa won the governorship and pledged a moratorium on new data‑center permits, a state that currently accounts for over 25 % of planned U.S. capacity by 2030.  
- Republican incumbent Greg Abbott had already imposed a moratorium in September, underscoring how data‑center siting has become a hot‑button political issue.

## The “Data Center Veto” – why opposition has surged
- Historically local governments competed for data centers because of construction jobs and long‑term tax revenue.  
- The current wave of opposition is tied to AI: the technology promises massive productivity gains but also threatens jobs, prompting communities to push back.  
- Unlike past globalization shocks—where towns lost factories without a voice—AI’s infrastructure requires local permits, giving affected communities a new lever of influence.  
- This grassroots resistance may ultimately benefit the very firms that stand to profit most from a restricted data‑center landscape.

## Core thesis
- Early AI constraints were technical (compute, memory, power). Those bottlenecks are being alleviated.  
- The emerging, and potentially longest‑lasting, constraint is political: local and state opposition to data‑center construction can limit AI deployment more than any hardware shortage.  
- Because political barriers are harder to solve and can impede solutions to other constraints, AI may remain a scarce commodity longer than many analysts expect.

## Ten perspectives on a politically constrained AI future (first four outlined)

1. **The AI trade got it right**  
   - Recent market volatility stems from tightening credit markets.  
   - Short‑term pain for AI companies is evident, but much of the downside may already be priced in.

2. **Neoclouds on the edge**  
   - Neocloud operators face dual challenges: scarce capital and now scarce permitting.  
   - Existing, already‑permitted sites become valuable assets, but acquiring new locations is increasingly difficult, especially compared with hyperscalers.

3. **Hyperscalers are winners**  
   - Large cloud providers (e.g., Amazon, Microsoft, Google) can leverage their existing footprint and repurpose sites, gaining a larger share of AI compute.  
   - Political opposition may actually temper overspending, which investors could view positively.

4. **Nvidia’s mixed bag**  
   - Short‑term: fewer data‑center builds reduce demand for Nvidia’s full‑rack GPUs, hurting revenue.  
   - Long‑term: hyperscalers’ ability to design their own chips poses a strategic threat to Nvidia’s market position.
