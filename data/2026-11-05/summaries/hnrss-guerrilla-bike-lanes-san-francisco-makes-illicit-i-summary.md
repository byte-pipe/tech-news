## Background
- Two cyclists were killed in a hit‑and‑run last summer, prompting the San Francisco Transformation Agency (SFMTrA) to install protected bike lanes with orange traffic cones.
- Guerrilla (tactical urbanism) interventions are usually temporary and removed by authorities, but a recent installation of soft‑hit posts along Golden Gate Park was made permanent by the city.

## The San Francisco Transformation Agency (SFMTrA)
- A collective of volunteers modeled after the city’s Municipal Transportation Agency (SFMTA).
- Goals:
  - Increase safety for cyclists and pedestrians.
  - Highlight unsafe street segments.
  - Pressure officials to implement rapid, substantive safety improvements.
- Primary tactics involve orange traffic cones; the recent use of more durable soft‑hit posts helped persuade the city to keep the installation.

## City Response
- The city’s transportation agency noted that placing objects in the roadway without approval violates code and can create traffic conflicts.
- In this case, officials plan to install official safe‑hit posts while keeping the temporary ones until the review is finished.
