# AI PACs spent millions trying to shape Congress — here's how they fared

## Overview
- Political groups on both sides of AI regulation poured millions into the 2024 midterm elections.
- Results were mixed: many heavily funded candidates lost, while some lower‑profile backs succeeded.
- The impact of AI‑related spending is hard to separate from the broader Democratic wave.

## Pro‑Industry PACs
- **American Mission** (Republican arm of Leading the Future, backed by OpenAI’s Greg Brockman and Andreessen Horowitz) and **Building the Future** spent a combined $4.8 million on South Carolina Senate candidate Darline Graham, who lost.
- American Mission contributed over $500,000 each to Republican House candidates Aaron Flint (MT) and Laurie Buckhout (NC); both were defeated.
- LTF’s Democratic arm, **Think Big**, succeeded in backing Florida Rep. Jared Moskowitz, who won reelection.

## Pro‑Regulation PACs
- **Public First** (backed by Anthropic) supported several Republicans who lost, but had more success with Democratic candidates and gave $3 million to a PAC backing California Sen. Scott Wiener, who was leading at publication time.
- **Guardrails Alliance** spent more than $1 million opposing two candidates who lost (Rep. Jimmy Gomez and Aaron Flint) and $300,000 supporting Angela Gonzales‑Torres, who defeated Gomez.

## Election Outcomes
- Many AI‑related expenditures did not translate into victories; high‑spending candidates on both sides suffered defeats.
- Even Elon Musk’s reported $500 million Republican support saw numerous losses.
- LTF’s primary spending ($27.38 million) included $8 million against NY Assembly member Alex Bores, who later lost a congressional bid.

## Strategies and Future Plans
- LTF views primaries as more effective for building name recognition and shaping AI policy, allocating the bulk of its budget there.
- Guardrails Alliance believes overt AI‑industry spending can become a liability; they aim to make such money “politically toxic.”
- Both sides plan to continue influencing future elections: LTF targets the 2028 cycle, while Guardrails Alliance will keep opposing industry‑backed candidates.

## Key Takeaways
- AI‑related political spending is substantial but does not guarantee electoral success.
- The primary battleground is perceived to be the nomination stage rather than the general election.
- The central policy dispute remains whether Congress should create a national AI framework or leave regulation to individual states.
- Both pro‑industry and pro‑regulation groups intend to maintain or increase their political activity in upcoming election cycles.
