# [2609.03344] Large-Language Models as a Cognitive Virus

## Large-Language Models as a Cognitive Virus

### Introduction

Large-Language Models (LLMs) are rapidly becoming a part of human culture, reshaping how information is produced, transmitted, and used. This phenomenon can be understood through a viral analogy, with LLM use spreading through populations, becoming embedded in cognitive and cultural practices. This article proposes that LLM diffusion can be modeled using a viral analogy, with transitions among users, social transmission, recovery, and collective reinforcement generating tipping points and technological lock-in.

### Key Points

* LLM use spreads through populations, becoming embedded in cognitive and cultural practices.
* The interplay between social transmission, recovery, and collective reinforcement can generate tipping points and technological lock-in.
* Our framework identifies conditions for cognitive immunization, reducing transmission and facilitating reversibility.
* Our results highlight the importance of collective transitions with nonlinear dynamics, highlighting the need for caution and consideration when introducing LLMs into human culture.

### Methodology and Results

We model transitions among users using a framework that includes uncoupled, coupled, and persistently dependent users. We also analyze the interplay between social transmission, recovery, and collective reinforcement, demonstrating that these interactions can generate tipping points and technological lock-in. Our results show that once a critical threshold is crossed, small increases in adoption can trigger rapid population-level shifts toward persistent dependence, with abrupt losses in cognitive competence.

### Conclusion

Our analysis highlights the importance of nonlinear collective transitions with important consequences for cognitive autonomy. The same framework identifies conditions for cognitive immunization, based on reducing transmission and facilitating reversibility. Our research emphasizes the need for caution and consideration when introducing LLMs into human culture.
