# Summary of “ABC sues the FCC over Trump and Carr’s campaign of threats”

## Background
- ABC and its parent company Disney filed a federal lawsuit to stop the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) from reviewing their broadcast‑license renewals.  
- The lawsuit alleges the FCC is conducting a “retaliatory campaign” because of ABC’s political coverage, especially programs such as *The View* and late‑night host Jimmy Kimmeland.  
- In February the FCC opened an investigation into *The View*’s airtime of political candidates and, in April, ordered ABC stations owned by Disney to file for an early license renewal.  

## Claims in the Lawsuit
- ABC and Disney contend the Trump administration has “steadily increased the pressure” on the network, using the license review to punish the network for its speech.  
- The suit argues the FCC’s actions aim to coerce the network into broadcasting only administration‑favored content, thereby chilling free‑speech rights.  
- ABC points to its decision not to air President Donald Trump’s July 16 speech on linear channels, opting instead to stream it on ABC News Live, as evidence of targeted retaliation.  
- The plaintiffs seek a court order that halts the FCC’s broadcast‑license renewal proceedings.  

## FCC’s Response
- An FCC spokesperson told CNBC that Disney is “concerned” and accuses the company of a “campaign of disinformation” while trying to block the agency’s actions.  
- The spokesperson added that the FCC will “continue to follow the facts and law wherever they lead.”  

## Potential Impact
- The lawsuit claims the administration’s approach could set a precedent forcing all media companies to self‑censor to avoid federal coercion.  
- Bloomberg reported that the FCC could take action against ABC and Disney as early as the month of filing.
