# A Good Move is Constructive

## Core argument
- Bad product work often hides behind grand strategy; true strategy emerges from consistently good play.
- A single good move creates strategic possibilities, but a great plan alone does not guarantee good moves.
- Focus on reliable gameplay first; repeated good moves build a foothold for strategy.

## First‑pass criteria for a good move
1. **Bounded** – defined steps with a clear end point.  
2. **Grounded** – targets concrete relationships and carries a testable hypothesis.  
3. **Constructive** – repairs a weakness or adds strength; valuable on its own.  
4. **Enabling** – widens the field of play, anticipates adjacent games and opens new possibilities.

## What makes a move constructive
- Strengthens the coupling between internal and external activities.
- Improves the targeted relationship by:
  1. Repairing an existing weakness.  
  2. Amplifying an existing strength.  
  3. Introducing a new structure when the relationship is latent.
- Worthwhile in isolation; each constructive move changes field conditions and creates new strategic traction.
- Non‑constructive moves rely on imagined future value, creating strategic debt and limiting future options.

## “V2” and “Beta” as masks for bad moves
- Teams often label unfinished work as a beta or promise a V2 to justify a weak current move.
- Such tags hide the gap between the inadequate present move and a hypothetical future.
- Unless a beta is planned with clear participation, learning, and closure criteria, it leads to mess and wasted effort.

## Diagnose and repair
- Ask: *If you couldn’t ship a follow‑up, would this still be a good idea?*
- A clear “yes” indicates a constructive move; a “no” signals a need for repair before execution.
- Grounded moves are more likely to be constructive; reshaping a move early avoids larger waste later.

## Other observations
- Perspective matters: close‑up view of a volcano shows turbulence; distant view shows a seemingly static cloud.
- Similarly, product work can appear chaotic up close and stable from afar; leaders need both the texture of turbulence and the shape of the motionless cloud.

## Closing note
- This is Dave’s Monday newsletter for product leaders: make good moves, play well with others, and change how the game is played.
