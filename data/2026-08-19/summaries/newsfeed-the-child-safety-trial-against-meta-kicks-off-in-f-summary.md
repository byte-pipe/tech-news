# The child safety trial against Meta kicks off in federal court

## Overview
- Opening arguments began in Oakland, California, where four state attorneys general sued Meta Platforms, Inc. over alleged harms to children’s mental health.  
- The states claim Meta knowingly designed Instagram and Facebook to be addictive for minors and misled the public about the risks.

## Legal Claims
- **Addictive Design**: Algorithms, infinite scroll, photo filters, and the “like” button are alleged to encourage compulsive use among youths.  
- **COPPA Violations**: Accusations that Meta collected personal data from users under 13 without parental consent.  
- **Internal Evidence**: Emails and studies cited by the states show a corporate focus on “teen time spent” and the long‑term revenue value of early users.  
- **Misleading Statements**: The states argue Meta prioritized profits over safety while publicly claiming the opposite.

## Meta’s Response
- Denies the allegations as “unsubstantiated.”  
- Highlights existing safeguards such as enhanced privacy settings and a one‑hour timer on Instagram.  
- Plans to present its own opening arguments and defend its practices.

## Potential Penalties
- Initial estimates of damages could reach **$1.4 trillion**, roughly the total market value of Meta’s equity.  
- The states have not specified a target amount, suggesting any figure should relate to Meta’s $200 billion annual revenue.

## Related Legal Context
- **Recent Verdicts**:  
  - March: Los Angeles jury awarded $6 million to a plaintiff for depression and anxiety linked to social‑media use.  
  - New Mexico: Judge ordered Meta to pay $567 million and implement safety measures for child sexual‑exploitation failures.  
- **Future Appeals**: Legal experts expect Meta to appeal any adverse ruling, possibly up to the Supreme Court.

## Trial Details
- Presiding Judge: **Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers**.  
- Duration: Expected to last about six weeks.  
- Key Witness: Former senior engineer **Arturo Bejar**, whose testimony was allowed after the judge rejected Meta’s attempt to block him.  
- Anticipated Testimony: Meta CEO **Mark Zuckerberg** is expected to testify.

## Statements from Officials
- **California Deputy Attorney General Megan O’Neill**: Emphasized Meta’s “hook, hold, harvest, hide” business model, especially harmful to kids.  
- **California Attorney General Rob Bonta**: Stressed the need for Meta to stop harming children and noted the company could remain successful without addictive features.
