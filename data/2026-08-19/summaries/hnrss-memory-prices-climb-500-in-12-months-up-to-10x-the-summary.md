# Summary of “Memory prices climb 500% in 12 months, up to 10x the lowest ever tracked prices — 128 GB of DDR5 now $3,399”

## Overview
- RAM prices have surged dramatically, a situation the author dubs “RAMageddon.”
- The spike affects both DDR5 and DDR4, making memory a major budget obstacle for PC builders.
- Prices are up to ten times the historic lows for high‑capacity DDR5 kits.

## DDR5 price explosion (2025 → 2026)
- Average DDR5 kit prices have risen 350‑500 % year‑over‑year.
- Example price changes:
  - 2 × 16 GB 4800 MT/s: $90 → $425 (+$335, +372 %)
  - 2 × 16 GB 5600 MT/s: $116 → $528 (+$412, +355 %)
  - 2 × 32 GB 5600 MT/s: $191 → $1,118 (+$927, +485 %)
  - 2 × 32 GB 6000 MT/s: $222 → $1,272 (+$1,050, +473 %)
- A 128 GB DDR5‑6400 kit now costs $3,399, roughly ten times its lowest recorded price.

## DDR4 price rise
- DDR4 is not immune; prices have climbed 120‑180 % across capacities.
- Sample changes:
  - 2 × 8 GB 3200 MT/s: $63 → $163 (+$100, +159 %)
  - 2 × 16 GB 3600 MT/s: $120 → $307 (+$187, +156 %)
  - 2 × 32 GB 3600 MT/s: $300 → $789 (+$489, +163 %)

## Global and market context
- The price surge is worldwide; European RAM prices are up ~345 % since September 2025.
- Hard‑drive and SSD prices have also risen >125 % in the same period.
- Hyperscale AI datacenters have locked almost all DRAM capacity for 2027, paying large deposits.
- DRAM has become a high‑value commodity, worth over half as much per kilogram as solid gold.
- All four major memory vendors (SK hynix, Samsung, Micron, CXMT) have seen revenues double or triple in one year.

## Industry outlook
- SK hynix predicts 2027 will be the worst memory‑supply year ever, with shortages extending to 2030.
- ADATA’s chairman warns the DRAM crunch could last another decade, dismissing any imminent “AI bubble” burst.
- A reduction in AI demand would be required for consumer RAM prices to fall, which appears unlikely in the near term.

## Practical advice for consumers
- Buyers must either pay premium prices or reduce RAM requirements.
