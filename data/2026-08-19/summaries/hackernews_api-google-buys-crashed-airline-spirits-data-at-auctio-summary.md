# Google buys crashed airline Spirit’s data at auction, because AI

## Background
- Spirit Airlines entered liquidation in May 2026 after prolonged losses triggered by the COVID‑19 pandemic.  
- To raise cash for creditors, the airline’s assets, including a large trove of de‑identified customer and operational data, were put up for auction.

## Data Acquired by Google
- Purchase price: **$10 million**.  
- Email and collaboration data:  
  - ~100 million emails.  
  - 500 million items from Microsoft Teams.  
  - 17 million OneDrive files.  
  - 20.5 million SharePoint items.  
- Customer‑service records:  
  - Over 30 million recorded phone calls.  
  - More than 15 million chat transcripts.  
  - 600 000 ServiceNow tickets.  
- Marketing and sales data:  
  - 13.7 million active email addresses from Oracle Responsys.  
  - Details of 11 million in‑flight Wi‑Fi purchases.  
- Operational data:  
  - Information on 763 000 flights, 5 million crew pairings, 1.2 million fuel slips, and 787 452 aircraft‑part purchases.

## Intended Use
- Google states the acquisition is to “improve its AI services.”  
- The data could support the development of specialized AI models, such as an aviation‑operations model, or provide financial‑record training sets for broader AI applications.  
- The underbidder, Mercor, a data‑provider for AI training, was outbid, indicating the perceived value of airline data for AI development.

## Privacy and De‑identification
- All data were de‑identified before the auction, as required by the court filing.  
- Google has pledged to scrub any personally identifiable information (PII) that may still be present.  
- The Register notes the risk of inadvertent PII exposure through future AI prompts, but no concrete incidents have been reported yet.

## Industry Context
- The acquisition reflects a growing trend where large language models benefit from domain‑specific datasets rather than only massive generic corpora.  
- Similar moves are seen across the tech sector as companies seek niche data to enhance model performance in particular industries.
