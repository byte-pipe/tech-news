description: 'If you ever run out of campaign ideas, save this post. These 5 email formats work every single month regardless of industry, seasonality, or what''s happening with your product calendar. We use these as the backbone of our campaign calendars across our brands and they consistently perform because they tap into things your audience always cares about. ↳ The "best of" roundup. Your top 3 to 5 best sellers with a one-line description of why each one is popular. Simple, scannable, and it works every month because your best sellers are your best sellers for a reason. New subscribers haven''t seen them. Existing customers need the reminder. Rotate the angle monthly: "most reviewed," "staff picks," "what our subscribers order most." ↳ The founder check-in. Plain text email from the founder sharing something real. A behind-the-scenes update, a lesson learned, a product development story, a personal anecdote related to the brand. No design needed, takes 15 minutes to write, and consistently
  drives strong engagement because it feels human in an inbox full of marketing. ↳ The single product deep dive. Instead of showcasing your whole catalog, pick one product and go deep. Why it exists, who it''s for, what makes it different, how to use it, and social proof from customers who love it. Works especially well with an annotated product callout or a comparison table. One focused email sells better than a catalog dump. ↳ The customer story. Feature a real customer and their experience with your product. Not a generic 5-star review. A real story with context: what they were dealing with before, why they tried your product, and what changed. This format builds trust, provides social proof, and gives your audience someone to relate to. You''ll never run out of these if you''re actively collecting reviews with the right questions. ↳ The educational tip. Teach your audience something useful related to your product category. Skincare routine tips, supplement timing advice, recipe ideas,
  styling suggestions, usage hacks. Position your product naturally within the content without making it the entire focus. This builds authority, keeps people engaged between purchases, and gives subscribers a reason to stay on your list beyond waiting for the next discount. That''s 5 emails you can repeat monthly with a different angle each time. If you''re sending 3 to 4 campaigns per week, these 5 formats fill about half your calendar and the other half can be promos, launches, and seasonal content.'
