# Algae turns reflecting pool green after repainting – summary

## Background
- The Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool was refilled after a renovation that repainted its bottom from neutral gray to “American flag blue.”
- Shortly after the reopening, the water turned a vivid green, drawing media attention.

## Causes of the bloom
- **Environmental conditions**: shallow depth, stagnant water, strong sunlight, and lack of shade create ideal settings for algae growth.
- **Renovation impact**: disturbance of the water body likely altered nutrient balances, accelerating algae proliferation.
- **Darker surface**: the new blue paint absorbs more heat, raising water temperature and further encouraging algae.
- Experts label the rapid onset as “New Pond Syndrome,” a common phenomenon when a cleared, open‑air water body is restarted.

## Expert observations
- Steve Goodale (pool specialist) notes the quick greening is typical for large, newly started ponds.
- Rosalina Stancheva Christova (aquatic ecology professor) identified the algae as the genus *Desmodesmus*—a common, non‑toxic green algae abundant in the region during summer.
- Both agree the renovation likely sped up the bloom but that similar events could occur each summer.

## Historical context
- Algae has resurfaced after previous renovations (2012, 2019) and has been a recurring issue since the pool’s opening in 1922.
- Past responses have included draining, refilling, and adjusting ozone levels.

## Mitigation strategies employed
- **Hydrogen peroxide**: applied as a milder alternative to chlorine, claimed to have no harmful side effects.
- **Nanobubble ozone technology**: tiny, neutrally buoyant ozone bubbles introduced to oxidize algae and pathogens; validated by universities and NOAA.
- **Physical removal**: high‑tech vacuums (“trash pumps”) used to suction dead algae from the pool bottom.
- Interior Department reports indicate the nanobubble treatment has been effective, though visible green water remained in the pool’s center as of the latest update.

## Current status and outlook
- Workers continue vacuuming and operating nanobubble equipment; visible green patches persist, especially centrally.
- Civil engineer Loay Hidmi, monitoring the effort, estimates the pool is about 80 % cleared and hopes the improvement will hold.
- Ongoing concern: the pool’s sunny, shallow nature may allow algae to reappear, posing an aesthetic issue and prompting questions about the long‑term efficacy of the renovation and treatment approach.
