# Summary of “Almost half of US singles feel negatively about AI in dating, Match says”

## Survey Findings
- Match Group surveyed 1,000 U.S. singles aged 18‑39.
- 47 % expressed a negative view of AI use in romantic contexts.
- 40 % would refuse to date someone who uses an AI companion app; the figure rises to 51 % among women 18‑24.
- Only 12 % of 18‑24‑year‑olds reported using a companion app in the past three months, and about one‑third of those users sought genuine connections with the chatbots.

## Current AI Adoption in Dating Apps
- Bumble launched an AI dating assistant called **Bee**.
- Tinder is heavily investing in AI tools, slowing its hiring process as a result.
- Hinge’s former CEO left to create a new, AI‑focused dating app.
- All major apps already employ matching algorithms; the new wave adds features for profile enhancement, photo selection, and conversation assistance.

## User Attitudes Toward AI Features
- 64 % of respondents see potential benefits of AI in their dating journey.
- Users are comfortable with AI helping “hard parts” (e.g., polishing profiles, suggesting messages) but prefer a “hands‑off” approach for the human connection itself.
- Near‑universal disapproval exists for actually dating an AI, as depicted in the film *Her*.

## Implications for Dating App Developers
- Emphasize AI tools that assist rather than replace human interaction.
- Avoid over‑technologizing the experience to prevent feelings of inauthenticity.
- Consider user demand for AI support in profile creation and conversation flow while keeping the core connection human‑driven.
