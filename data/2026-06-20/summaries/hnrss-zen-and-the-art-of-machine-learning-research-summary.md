# Zen and the Art of AI Research

## I. The Path to Becoming a Researcher
- Success comes from a blend of reading and building; neither alone is enough.  
- Insight appears randomly, so consistent effort and discipline are essential.  
- Over‑reading can be counterproductive; try solving a problem first, then consult literature when stuck.

## II. Choosing What to Work On
- The exact topic matters less than the depth of understanding.  
- Avoid chasing trends younger than six months; focus on enduring fundamentals (e.g., cross‑entropy, SVD, policy gradients).  
- Aim beyond benchmark scores; seek datasets that truly test new methods.  
- The right project will emerge from personal curiosity and deep engagement with basics.

## III. Maintaining a Beginner’s Mind
- Experience can sometimes blind intuition; methods that work at small scale may fail when scaled.  
- AI is a very young field—most leading contributors are under 35—so no one holds a lasting advantage.  
- Keep ideas fresh, stay open‑minded, and prevent ego from limiting judgment.

## IV. Sources of Inspiration
- Breakthroughs often arise outside the lab (e.g., benzene’s ring from a dream, GLP‑1 hormone from a lizard).  
- Regular walks and non‑research activities foster “aha” moments, as noted by historic thinkers like Darwin and Feynman.

## V. Experimental Equanimity
- Treat both positive and negative results as valuable learning signals.  
- Negative outcomes can reveal more about a hypothesis than a single success.  
- Maintain healthy skepticism; many seemingly great results stem from measurement bugs.

## VI. Bloom Without Competition
- Research outcomes are heavily influenced by luck and the uneven academic review system.  
- When admiring others’ work, assess whether you’re operating at a depth that could have produced the insight yourself.  
- If not, use the gap as motivation to deepen your understanding.

## VII. The Value of Grunt Work
- Major advances often rest on extensive, low‑glamour effort (e.g., manual labeling for ImageNet, meticulous data filtering for SWEBench).  
- Great researchers typically spend long periods in obscurity before breakthroughs; this toil is a feature of ambitious ideas.
