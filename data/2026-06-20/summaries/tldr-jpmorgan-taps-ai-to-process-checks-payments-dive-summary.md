# JPMorgan taps AI to process checks

## Dive Brief
- JPMorgan Chase introduced a mail‑automation robot at a lockbox site, capable of handling about 4,000 envelope and document types.  
- The robot, built with AI‑robotics firm Ripcord, opens envelopes, removes staples, unfolds and scans documents.  
- Large language models provide an AI assistant that shows real‑time processing data, allowing staff to focus on tasks that need human judgment.

## Dive Insight
- Before automation, lockbox operators manually processed roughly 480 million checks and documents per year, using about 13 billion keystrokes.  
- Automation now achieves over 99.9 % accuracy, dramatically reducing manual effort.  
- Michelle Conklin, head of receivables and public sector for JPMorgan’s payments division, said the technology frees teams for higher‑value decision‑making and delivers a faster, more secure receivables process.  
- JPMorgan invests $19.8 billion annually in technology modernization, of which this AI/robotics initiative is a part.

## Industry Context
- Checks remain popular: an AFP survey showed usage rose from 75 % in 2023 to 91 % in 2024.  
- Check fraud is widespread, with 63 % of respondents reporting fraud or attempted fraud in 2024.  
- Other firms continue offering lockbox services; Deluxe acquired First American Payments in 2022 for $960 million to expand its lockbox and receivables capabilities.
