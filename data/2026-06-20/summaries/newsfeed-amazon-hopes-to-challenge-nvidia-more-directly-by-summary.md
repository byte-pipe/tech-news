# Amazon hopes to challenge Nvidia more directly by selling its AI chips

## Main announcement
- AWS AI chief Peter DeSantis told Bloomberg that Amazon is in early‑stage talks to sell its Trainium AI chips to other companies for data‑center use, without naming potential buyers.  

## Context from Andy Jassy’s shareholder letter
- Jassy said that if Amazon’s chip business were a standalone entity, it could generate roughly **$50 billion** in annual revenue.  
- Demand for Trainium is extremely high; current capacity sold out instantly, and the next generation, Trainium 4, will not be available for more than a year.  

## Potential impact on Nvidia
- Nvidia’s current revenue run‑rate is about **$326 billion**; a $50 billion competitor would be sizable but not enough to topple Nvidia outright.  
- Amazon’s move would place it directly in competition with Nvidia’s dominance in AI chips.  

## Business considerations for AWS
- AWS’s profit from its chips is largely indirect: it charges customers for AI token processing and for related services such as storage, security, networking, and monitoring.  
- Selling chips could create waiting‑list issues unless AWS can produce a surplus through partners like TSMC, which is already heavily booked as a foundry.  

## Official confirmation
- AWS spokesperson Doron Aronson confirmed that, while historically AWS declined direct chip sales, it is “quite possible we’ll sell racks of them to third parties in the future,” echoing Jassy’s remarks.  

## Broader industry landscape
- Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang announced a new **$200 billion** market for Nvidia CPUs aimed at AI workloads, targeting competitors like Intel and AMD.  
- Amazon’s ambition for a **$50 billion** chip market positions it as a direct challenger to Nvidia’s AI‑chip ecosystem.
