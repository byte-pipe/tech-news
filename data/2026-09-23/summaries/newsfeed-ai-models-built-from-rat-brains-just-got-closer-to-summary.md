# AI Models Built From Rat Brains Just Got Closer to Reality | WIRED

# Summary of “AI Models Built From Rat Brains Just Got Closer to Reality | WIRED”

## Overview
- The Biological Computing Company (TBC) uses patterns from rat brain cells to create AI models aimed at improving video‑generation efficiency.  
- Amazon Web Services (AWS) will offer a limited preview of TBC’s “rat brain” model to select customers, with plans to expand to all enterprise users.  

## Core Technology
- TBC records neural activity from rat brain cells (and human stem cells) placed on multi‑electrode silicon arrays supplied by 3Brain.  
- Electrical stimulation patterns are sent to the neurons; the resulting activity is analyzed to extract computational patterns.  
- These patterns are translated into software tools that mimic the biological processing of visual information, targeting existing generative‑AI video models.  

## Business Background & Funding
- Founded in Baltimore four years ago by neuroscientists Alexander Ksendzovsky (CEO) and Jon Pomeraniec (President/COO).  
- Raised $25 million in a Series A round led by Primary Venture Partners, followed by an additional $25 million, bringing total funding to over $50 million.  
- Operates a research lab in San Francisco with ~35 employees working directly with live neurons.  

## AWS Partnership & Deployment
- Starting Tuesday, select AWS customers can access TBC’s model through a limited preview.  
- AWS sees TBC’s “pragmatic” approach—enhancing existing visual models rather than reinventing transformer architecture—as a good fit for its marketplace.  
- The collaboration follows AWS’s earlier “wet‑ware as a service” offerings, such as those from Cortical Labs.  

## Performance Claims
- TBC states its model runs up to five times faster than the comparable open‑source video‑generation model it builds upon.  
- Inference costs are reported to be significantly lower, though the exact baseline model is not disclosed.  

## Challenges & Future Outlook
- Biological computing requires maintaining live cells, complex lab infrastructure, and reliable translation of neural signals into digital data.  
- Scaling concerns include maintaining fidelity and consistency for longer‑form video generation (e.g., ten‑minute or hour‑long clips).  
- AWS’s global director of technology for startups, Deap Ubhi, notes that the technology’s performance at extreme scales remains an open question.  

## Key Personnel & Influences
- Jeff Dean (Google AI researcher and TBC investor) suggested focusing first on fine‑tuning video models to provide measurable benchmarks before broader applications.
