# Mathematicians learn little from AI completing unsolved problem

## Overview
- OpenAI announced that its AI model produced a solution to the Navier‑Stokes Millennium Prize problem.  
- Mathematicians acknowledge the proof appears technically correct but find it difficult to extract human‑readable insight.  
- The 166‑page manuscript, though formally verified in Lean, is considered “not written for humans.”

## AI Solution and Reception
- James Maynard (Oxford) and Javier Gómez‑Serrano (Brown) say the paper is hard to understand and offers little immediate knowledge.  
- Both suggest that extensive rewriting would be needed before the proof could benefit the field.  
- A statement signed by 25 Fields Medalists criticized “misaligned goals” between AI firms and the mathematics community.

## The Navier‑Stokes Problem
- One of the seven Clay Millennium Problems; it concerns the existence and smoothness of solutions to the fluid‑flow equations.  
- Solving it could provide new tools for turbulence, aircraft lift, and other complex fluid phenomena.  
- Mathematicians such as Tristan Buckmaster (NYU) and Martin Hairer (EPFL) had been approaching a breakthrough, using AI tools themselves.

## OpenAI’s Process and Controversy
- After hearing rumors of imminent human breakthroughs, OpenAI mobilized ~10,000 AI agents for 88 hours, generating ~130 billion tokens on the Navier‑Stokes problem.  
- Estimated computational cost: $6–10 million (or up to $15–20 million for the broader search).  
- Buckmaster alleges OpenAI hinted at co‑authorship only if he dropped his Anthropic collaborator, Levent Alpöge, suggesting the company may have had insider knowledge of their approach.  
- OpenAI denies using Buckmaster’s prompts or proofs.

## Community Response
- Mathematicians describe the AI‑generated paper as “terribly written,” lacking clear exposition of key ideas versus routine steps.  
- The rushed release forced Buckmaster to publish his own preliminary, AI‑influenced results, which he also feels are under‑developed.  
- In reaction, OpenAI created an “independent mathematics advisory group,” but the group will not influence the pace of OpenAI’s internal research.

## Technical Validation
- Alongside the manuscript, OpenAI released a Lean formalization of the proof.  
- The Lean code compiled successfully, providing a machine‑checked verification of logical correctness.  
- Despite formal correctness, the mathematical community still struggles to interpret the underlying concepts.

## Outlook
- AI is rapidly generating mathematically significant outputs, yet effective collaboration between humans and machines remains limited.  
- Mathematicians call for slower, more transparent development and better integration of AI‑generated proofs into human‑readable research.  
- The episode highlights the tension between speed of discovery and the need for clear, pedagogical communication in advanced mathematics.
