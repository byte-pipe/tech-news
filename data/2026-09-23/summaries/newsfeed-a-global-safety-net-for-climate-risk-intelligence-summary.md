# A global safety net for climate‑risk intelligence is urgently needed

## Key points
- The August glacier collapse and flash flood on the Nepal–China border highlighted a persistent gap in climate‑adaptation capacity.  
- Communities are increasingly exposed to fast‑evolving hazards when national monitoring systems are limited or when risks cross political boundaries.  
- The event underscores the need for a coordinated, trans‑national framework that can collect, share, and analyse climate‑risk data in real time.  

## Implications
- Sparse monitoring at the national level leaves vulnerable populations without timely warnings, amplifying loss of life and economic damage.  
- Climate‑related hazards do not respect borders; isolated monitoring efforts create blind spots that can trigger regional disasters.  
- Existing early‑warning systems are insufficient without a global safety net that integrates satellite observations, ground sensors, and local knowledge.  

## Recommendations
- Establish an international climate‑risk intelligence network that standardises data collection, ensures open access, and facilitates rapid dissemination of warnings.  
- Promote collective action among governments, scientific institutions, and technology providers to fill monitoring gaps, especially in remote or politically sensitive regions.  
- Leverage emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence to enhance detection, modelling, and communication of climate hazards.  
- Secure sustainable funding and governance structures to maintain the network and guarantee its resilience against political or financial disruptions.  

## Conclusion
The Nepal–China glacier disaster demonstrates that without a global safety net for climate‑risk intelligence, communities worldwide remain exposed to sudden, high‑impact events. Building a coordinated, border‑spanning monitoring and warning system is essential to improve adaptation and protect lives.
