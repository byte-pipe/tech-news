# Jim Acosta on Trump's White House media ban

## Background
- Jim Acosta, former CNN White House correspondent, had his press pass revoked by President Trump in 2018 after a contentious news conference.  
- A federal judge temporarily restored his access and the White House eventually backed down.

## Current Ban
- The Trump administration has barred three news outlets: CNN, MS NOW, and Politico.  
- The three organizations have filed lawsuits claiming violations of their First and Fifth Amendment rights.  
- Major television networks have refused to replace CNN in the White House video pool.

## Legal Action & Press Solidarity
- Acosta notes that the response to this broader ban is larger than the 2018 incident.  
- He praises the press for showing solidarity, though he says it should have happened sooner.  
- NPR announced it will stop using audio from White House events in solidarity with the barred outlets.  
- Earlier, the Associated Press faced restrictions after refusing to use Trump’s preferred term “Gulf of America” for the Gulf of Mexico.

## Trump Administration’s Justification
- The White House claims the outlets are barred because of coverage it deems unfair and overly negative.  
- Citing a Media Research Center study, the administration notes that 92 % of coverage of Trump in the first 100 days of his second term was negative.  
- The administration argues that while the First Amendment protects publication, it does not guarantee White House access.

## Acosta’s Perspective
- He rejects the notion that journalists should aim for “positive” coverage, emphasizing the duty to report news that matters to the public.  
- Acosta suggests Trump’s objections are largely about fact‑checking.  
- He asks whether the press should allow truth to become lies or serve as a “safety valve” to provide accurate information without fear or favor.
