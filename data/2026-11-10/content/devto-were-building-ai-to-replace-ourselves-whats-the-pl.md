The hidden trap for new developers

Weren't we supposed to be replaced by AI by now? Or at least work less? AI agents were supposed to take over the world. So when exactly is that going to happen, and why are we still working so much?

Because we can't pretend that nothing has changed. Even at my workplace, where data security is valued above pretty much everything else, we're already using AI coding agents. Which, in my opinion, means they're basically everywhere by now.

Simple features practically write themselves, and even debugging, including in legacy applications, has become significantly easier.

And we're only at the beginning of this revolution!

## What Happens When AI Can Do Everything?

A friend and colleague of mine recently encouraged me to write an article about how to get into AI before it's too late. After all, someone will have to build this revolution, right?

Soon, applications will practically write themselves. AI will even be sitting in your toilet, politely suggesting that it's time to get up before you give yourself hemorrhoids. xD So what should we do to avoid being pushed out of the job market?

Since I know a thing or two about AI agents, and I've been in this industry for quite a while, I started researching what the next few years might look like. And the longer I researched, the more pessimistic I became. Because sure, right now we have huge conferences dedicated to AI agents. We're trying to build them properly, make them secure, reliable, and so on.

But what happens in a few years, when we've solved those problems too, and an agent can spin up ten sub-agents of its own from a single prompt? Where exactly will developers fit into that picture?

(Okay, for now I still get the impression that hardly anyone even knows what tool calling or WebMCP is. 😅 But maybe that's just a matter of time?)

## There's One Problem With Replacing Everyone...

Okay, but there's a catch in this whole theory. Sometimes, when I hear people talking about how "AI is going to replace all of us," I feel like we're forgetting something pretty important. If a massive company can be run by just a handful of people thanks to AI, and most of the population ends up unemployed,who exactly are those companies going to sell their products to?People need to earn money to buy things, after all!

Now, let's throw all restraint out the window and go full science fiction. Imagine it's 2031. Autonomous AI agents do practically everything. Humans are left with manual jobs, like plumbers or hairdressers.

But even those people have hardly any customers, because everyone else is unemployed and desperately cutting back on unnecessary expenses.

The economy starts collapsing. People start protesting. So what do governments do? To prevent a full-blown revolt and keep themselves in power, they start introducing laws designed to bring humans back into the workforce.

## Or Maybe We Could Finally Work Less?

Maybe in five or ten years, software engineering won't be a job for people who actually know how to program or understand the technology. Maybe it'll be something literally anyone can do.

But perhaps we should take this idea even further. Instead of throwing people out of their jobs, why not take advantage of how productive AI agents are and let people work part-time for the same salary Maybe we could work three days instead of five, and spend the rest of our time doing whatever we want: relaxing, learning new things, developing ourselves?

Oh, what a beautiful vision! Just imagine how many articles I could write. I'd finally get enough sleep. I might even find time to deal with that enormous pile of clothes sitting in my wardrobe, which currently has approximately zero priority in my life! 🤯

But there's just one tiny problem with this wonderful vision.Where is all that free time we've been promised since the invention of the steam engine?Because I'm certainly not working less!

## AI Makes Us More Productive. So Why Are We Working More?

Quite the opposite, actually. I feel like I'm working more and more, and expectations keep growing, because "AI will help you with that." And yes, some of the actual coding, the part so many of us genuinely enjoyed, has already been partially automated.

But what about everything else? The coordination, the endless meetings, the constant effort to make sure the entire project doesn't fall apart?

Don't you get the impression that because itseemslike we should be able to deliver more, that's exactly what we keep trying to do? And somehow, it doesn't always work out the way we expected?

I also feel like AI adoption in companies is still in its infancy, even if everyone already has a paid subscription to a coding agent. Someone still needs to think about the architecture. Someone needs to figure out how to automate the agent's work in the first place.

Yes, I keep hearing about entire workflows handled by "armies of agents." But aren't those mostly greenfield projects? Because I have a feeling that legacy applications still play by their own rules.

And the good old biological human is still the bottleneck... Or maybe we're not the bottleneck at all? 😉

## And What About Junior Developers?

And yes, it's hard to disagree that junior developers have it tough right now. Partly because of AI, absolutely. But notonlybecause of AI.

Ten years ago, the demand for developers was so enormous that companies would hire practically anyone who could writeexport class. Some companies even organized their own bootcamps to train future employees.

But those days were already coming to an end before AI arrived. People realized that IT offered a relatively easy and comfortable way to make good money, so more and more people entered the industry, and the market simply became saturated.

AI just hammered the final nail into the coffin.

## So... What's the Plan?

And now I'm genuinely curious what you think. I really don't want this article to be one of those "look how brilliant Sylwia is, she has figured out the entire future of software engineering" pieces. Because I haven't! I genuinely don't know.

How do you see the future of our industry? Are we all going to end up unemployed? Will entirely new engineering professions emerge, jobs we can't even imagine today?

Or maybe, and this is my personal dream, civilization will finally manage to do something that actually benefits human beings, and we'll end up with a three-day workweek?

I'd really love to hear your thoughts!
