# We're Building AI to Replace Ourselves. What's the Plan? - DEV Community

# Summary of “The hidden trap for new developers”

## Current AI Impact
- AI coding agents are already used even in highly security‑focused workplaces.  
- Simple features and debugging, even for legacy code, are becoming easier thanks to these agents.  
- Adoption is still early; many developers are unfamiliar with concepts like tool calling or WebMCP.

## What Might Happen When AI Can Do Everything?
- The author envisions a future where applications write themselves and AI agents spawn sub‑agents from a single prompt.  
- This raises the question of where developers will fit when most tasks are automated.  

## Economic Concerns
- If AI enables a handful of people to run massive companies, mass unemployment could reduce consumer demand, threatening the economy.  
- A speculative 2031 scenario describes widespread job loss, economic collapse, and government intervention to force human employment.  

## Possibility of Working Less
- The author imagines a world where AI productivity allows part‑time work for the same salary, leading to more leisure and personal development.  
- However, the promised “free time” has not materialized; the author feels they are working more, not less.

## AI Increases Productivity but Raises Expectations
- Expectations grow because AI promises faster delivery, leading to higher workload and more coordination, meetings, and architectural planning.  
- Legacy systems still require human oversight; “armies of agents” are mostly applied to new (greenfield) projects.  

## Impact on Junior Developers
- Junior developers already face a saturated market; AI accelerates this trend, making entry even harder.  
- The demand for developers was already declining before AI’s rise, and AI further intensifies competition.

## Open Questions and Call for Discussion
- The author asks readers to share their views on the future of software engineering: unemployment, new professions, or a shorter workweek.
