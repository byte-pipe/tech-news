## Introduction  
- The “AI bubble” idea claims the AI sector is vastly over‑valued and could collapse, harming the economy.  
- I argue this assumption is unreasonable; the rapid growth of AI firms may be justified and still have room to expand.

## Expenses Are Sky‑High, But Constrained  
- AI companies have committed to roughly **$3 trillion** in spending, with about **$1.9 trillion** already used, bringing total industry expense to around **$5 trillion**.  
- Most of this outlay is for one‑off data‑center construction, not ongoing operating costs.  
- After infrastructure is in place, spending should drop sharply.  
- Operating profitability looks strong: Anthropic’s S‑1 filing shows **$2.08 revenue per $1 of compute**.  
- Companies can quickly cut costs by ending free‑tier services or pausing expensive research.  
- Long‑term debt (≈$5 trillion over 20 years ≈ $250 billion/yr) is large but manageable; comparable to Alphabet’s annual revenue.  
- Convertible notes allow some debt to be repaid with equity, reducing cash strain (e.g., Anthropic’s $72 billion loss in shares in early 2026).

## Profits Can Grow, By A Lot  
- Anthropic’s projected revenue: $41 M (2023) → $386 M (2024) → $4.585 B (2025) → $16.3 B (first half of 2026).  
- Similar growth is expected at other AI firms like OpenAI.  
- Vast untapped demand exists: AI can outperform humans in many tasks (coding, math, paperwork for lawyers/doctors) and enable new applications (self‑driving cars).  
- Even without further breakthroughs, AI adoption is likely to increase tenfold.  
- Skeptics who fear AI’s societal impact yet claim AI will never produce valuable products are contradictory; job displacement will still generate monetary transactions.

## Google Has No Moat, But Neither Does McDonald’s  
- A 2023 Google memo argued that no AI company has a durable competitive moat because open‑source research erodes advantages.  
- The industry will see continuous competition rather than a single dominant player, similar to the fast‑food “chicken sandwich war.”  
- Lack of a moat does not preclude profitability; many firms can earn well despite easy copyability.  
- In practice, most users prefer hosted services (e.g., ChatGPT) over locally run models due to convenience and brand loyalty, limiting the threat of switching to open‑source alternatives.
