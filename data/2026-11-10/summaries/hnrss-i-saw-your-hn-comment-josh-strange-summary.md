# Summary of “I Saw Your HN Comment” by Josh Strange

## Background
- Historically, receiving email replies to blog posts or Hacker News comments was a source of genuine connection, hiring opportunities, and learning.
- Over the past two years, the signal‑to‑noise ratio has shifted dramatically toward unsolicited sales pitches.

## How LLMs Changed the Landscape
- Large language models can generate personalized, convincing emails that reference specific comments.
- These messages often disguise their true intent, posing as genuine curiosity rather than a pitch.

## Common Tactics in Deceptive Emails
- Opening with a compliment and an open‑ended question (“I’m curious, …?”).
- Subtle pivots after the initial question, such as:
  - “That’s close to what I’m building…”
  - “The tool I mentioned, [product], works from the other end…”
  - “Worth saying this is what we build at [company]…”
  - “Would you have 15 min next week?”

## Personal Response Strategy
- Initially responded to every question, but now treats each email as a potential pitch.
- Sends short replies first; only continues the conversation if the responder does not pivot to a sales pitch.
- Feels conflicted: does not want to dismiss legitimate inquiries, yet is weary of deceptive outreach.

## Reflections on the Situation
- Acknowledges that posting an email address publicly invites contact, both good and bad.
- Misses the serendipitous connections that once arose from honest exchanges.
- Remains committed to keeping the email address public, hoping to separate “wheat from chaff,” but feels increasingly grumpy about the trend.
