# AI R&D Is Accelerating Towards Potential RSI in 2027

## Top Conclusions
- As of October 2026 Anthropic’s model capabilities are improving at roughly twice the rate observed between March 2024 and October 2025 (confidence interval ≈ 1.3×‑3×).  
- The majority of this acceleration is attributed to Anthropic’s extensive use of AI for its own R&D, though other factors may also contribute.  
- No clear bottlenecks or strong diminishing‑return effects have been observed, raising the risk of even faster future progress.  

## Empirical Findings
- Benchmark scores measured by Anthropic’s Epoch Capabilities Index (AECI) have grown at an estimated 1.9× the pre‑October 2025 rate (95 % CI 1.5–2.8×), equivalent to about two years of historical progress in one year.  
- The speedup is not explained by increased training‑compute scaling; instead, AI‑driven R&D appears to be the primary driver.  
- Interviews and surveys with Anthropic staff support the view that AI‑assisted research has doubled the pace of capability gains.  

## Timeline Implications
- A naive extrapolation of the current 2× acceleration suggests that full recursive self‑improvement (RSI) could be less than a year away, potentially by early‑mid 2027.  
- This timeline is several months ahead of earlier “AI 2027” forecasts.  

## Uncertainties and Redactions
- Many detailed analyses of potential bottlenecks and risk factors have been redacted from the METR report.  
- The public report relies heavily on AECI and other high‑level metrics; internal data that could alter the picture are not fully disclosed.  

## Community Reactions
- Some experts (e.g., Ryan Greenblatt) had expected only a 1.15×‑1.3× speedup for the year; the reported 1.5×‑1.9× acceleration exceeds those expectations.  
- Others note that public models alone may not show a step change, but internal models and compute usage (often 10,000× larger) reveal a faster underlying progress rate.  

## Risk Assessment
- If the acceleration continues unchecked, the rapid arrival of RSI could lead to catastrophic outcomes, including existential risk.  
- The report’s authors consider the situation grim but not surprising given internal signals.  

## Suggested Actions
- Greater transparency: release the redacted sections of the METR report and related internal Anthropic data.  
- Legislative oversight: consider congressional inquiries or subpoenas (as suggested by Kelsey Piper) to obtain full information from Anthropic and its leadership.  
- Precautionary measures: labs could voluntarily pause or slow AI‑driven R&D to mitigate risk, though the feasibility of such actions is uncertain.  

## Key Quotes
- “We are running out of time.” – METR report summary.  
- “If (If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies) Then Don’t Let Anyone Build It.” – Ethical warning from the report’s authors.  

## Overall Assessment
- The METR investigation indicates a significant acceleration in AI capabilities driven by AI‑assisted research, with limited evidence of imminent bottlenecks.  
- The combination of rapid progress, high uncertainty, and potential existential stakes makes the outlook “not good,” prompting calls for increased oversight and transparency.
