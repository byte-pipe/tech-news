## The rise of “big math”
- “Big math” refers to mathematical methods that underpin everyday technologies such as communication, transport, shopping, and healthcare.  
- Regina Barzilay’s post‑cancer work is highlighted: she applied machine‑learning models to mammograms to predict five‑year breast‑cancer risk, showing how AI can move from entertainment recommendations to life‑saving diagnostics.  

## Historical foundations
- Early examples include Chinese mathematicians solving rice‑yield problems with linear equations, matrices, and vectors—precursors to the matrix operations central to generative AI.  
- James Joseph Sylvester coined the term “matrix” and envisioned it as a generator of other mathematical objects, a view that anticipates today’s massive matrix manipulations in AI.  
- Leon Festinger’s 1949 insight that repeated matrix‑vector multiplications reveal hidden structures foreshadowed modern network analysis.  
- Simple games illustrate complex concepts:  
  - Snakes and Ladders demonstrates a Markov chain, a model later used in Google’s early search algorithms.  
  - George Dantzig’s accidental solution of two unsolved equations in 1939 exemplifies how unexpected encounters can lead to breakthroughs.  

## AI’s impact on mathematics
- AI has become a powerful problem‑solver, finding solutions to equations and conjectures that remained open for decades.  
- The article stresses that while AI accelerates discovery, mathematicians must remain vigilant to prevent misuse or over‑reliance on black‑box methods.
