# Switzerland's Federal Government is Replacing Microsoft on 3,000 Computers

## Pilot phase overview
- The Federal Chancellery invests CHF 9 million to roll out an open‑source workplace for 3 000 federal employees (≈7 % of the total 54 000 workstations).  
- The pilot follows a successful proof‑of‑concept (PoC BOSS) with 172 employees testing the openDesk suite.  
- During the pilot the new system runs alongside Microsoft 365; a full switch‑over is planned for the end of 2027 if the trial succeeds.  

## Reasons for the migration
- **Foreign access risk:** U.S. cloud legislation could allow foreign authorities to view Swiss government data.  
- **Service continuity risk:** Dependence on a single foreign vendor creates operational vulnerabilities.  
- **Cost escalation:** Proprietary licensing fees are rising without Swiss bargaining power.  

## Military acceleration
- The Swiss Cyber Command aims to replace Microsoft 365 entirely with openDesk by October 2026, citing the same sovereignty and security concerns.  

## Legislative and strategic background
- The EMBAG Law (effective 2024) mandates that federal agencies publish government‑developed software as open source by default, promoting digital sovereignty.  
- In December 2025 the Federal Council declared digital sovereignty a primary focus, defining it as the ability to fulfill essential missions without reliance on external suppliers or countries.  

## Wider context
- Neighboring France and Germany are pursuing similar moves away from U.S. tech giants.  
- Microsoft is countering with a CHF 325 million investment in AI and cloud infrastructure in Switzerland, attempting to mitigate the sovereignty argument.  

## Outlook
- If the pilot proves successful, the migration could extend to all 54 000 federal workstations, setting a precedent for other nations seeking greater digital independence.
