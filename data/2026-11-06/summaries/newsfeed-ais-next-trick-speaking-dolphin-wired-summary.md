# AI’s Next Trick? Speaking Dolphin | WIRED

## Overview
- Large language models are now being explored as tools to communicate with dolphins.  
- Denise Herzing, founder of the Wild Dolphin Project, has spent 40 years studying dolphin communication and is now leveraging AI to decode it.  
- The research was presented at the WIRED World Fair.

## Research Approach
- Collected extensive video and audio recordings of dolphins in the wild.  
- Partnered with Georgia Tech and Google DeepMind to apply AI for pattern detection in dolphin sounds.  
- Developed an underwater wearable computer that can emit dolphin-like sounds linked to specific objects (e.g., a scarf) or habitats (e.g., sargassum).  
- Conducted experiments where researchers model behavior after playing a sound, then observed dolphin responses.

## Findings & Challenges
- AI successfully identifies recurring patterns in dolphin vocalizations, offering a structural map of their “language.”  
- Distinguishing genuine understanding from simple mimicry remains difficult, especially in uncontrolled wild settings.  
- Controlled environments or sanctuaries may provide clearer insights into functional meanings.  
- While patterns are emerging, a true two‑way conversational ability is still far off; cultural context of dolphin communication is largely unknown.

## Implications
- Decoding dolphin communication could deepen scientific knowledge of dolphin social hierarchies and behavior.  
- Insights may reflect human attitudes toward the ocean, as many people imagine dolphins would ask, “Why are you messing up our ocean?”  
- Advancing interspecies communication may also prompt broader reflections on humanity’s relationship with the planet.
