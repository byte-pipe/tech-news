# Summary of “A new bill could change how the US builds energy infrastructure”

## Overview
- The Senate will vote on the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (BAAJA) on Nov 9, a permitting‑reform bill aimed at speeding up U.S. energy‑infrastructure projects.  
- The legislation seeks to reduce the lengthy, complex federal permitting process that currently protects air, water, and land under laws such as NEPA and the Clean Water Act.  

## Key Provisions
- Narrows the categories of projects that must obtain federal permits.  
- Sets statutory deadlines to shorten review times that can now take years.  
- Includes specific measures for transmission planning, mining, and other sectors.  
- Technology‑neutral: applies to both fossil‑fuel and renewable projects.  

## Support and Opposition
- **Sponsors:** Two Republicans and two Democrats in the Senate.  
- **White House:** Council of Economic Advisors backs the bill, citing faster reviews and GDP gains.  
- **Clean‑energy groups (e.g., American Clean Power Association):** Favorable, estimating up to 350 GW of new clean capacity.  
- **Environmental groups (e.g., NRDC):** Mixed view—support transmission benefits but warn that reduced environmental reviews could harm public and private lands.  

## Emissions Impact Estimates
- **Rhodium Group (best case):** 4 % reduction in economy‑wide emissions, $78 annual household bill savings, 200 GW of clean energy by 2040.  
- **Rhodium Group (worst case):** Emissions remain roughly unchanged.  
- **Resources for the Future:** 6 % cut in power‑sector emissions by 2041 and >$350 billion saved in system costs by mid‑century.  
- **Purdue model (Kevin Kircher):** If BAAJA had been in effect in 2025, sector emissions would have been higher than baseline, with increased health and mortality risks from air pollution.  

## Political Outlook
- Senate vote scheduled before the next Congress takes control; outcome may hinge on Democratic strategy.  
- Even if passed, the bill would still require President Trump’s signature, limiting potential amendments.  

## Trade‑off Considerations
- Faster permitting could accelerate both clean‑energy and natural‑gas development.  
- Lower electricity prices may boost demand, potentially offsetting emissions gains.  
- The net climate effect depends heavily on which types of projects are actually built.  

## Conclusion
- Simplifying the permitting system is broadly seen as beneficial, but the bill’s technology‑neutral language creates a dilemma: it could unlock substantial clean‑energy capacity while also encouraging new fossil‑fuel infrastructure, with uncertain overall emissions outcomes.
