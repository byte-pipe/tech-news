# 2026 iPad mini review: Maximizing the mini - Ars Technica

# 2026 iPad mini review – key takeaways

## Overview
- Continues to target users who want a small, portable tablet.
- Upgrades make it more competitive, but price is only $50 below the 11‑inch iPad Air, leaving its value proposition unclear.

## Display
- New 8.5‑inch OLED panel (2316 × 1512, 326 ppi) replaces the 8.3‑inch IPS screen.
- Brightness up to 600 nits (full‑screen) and peak HDR brightness 1,200 nits; contrast ratio 2,000,000:1.
- Bezels reduced by 24 %, allowing a slightly smaller body (7.69 × 5.22 × 0.21 in, 0.56 lb).
- Refresh rate remains 60 Hz; MPRT ~17 ms, comparable to previous IPS models.
- Optional nano‑texture glass (extra $100) available on 256 GB and 512 GB models for reduced glare.

## Processor
- Equipped with Apple A20 Pro (2 nm) – successor to the A17 Pro used in 2024.
- ~60 % faster overall, 50 % more memory bandwidth, boosting graphics performance.
- GPU: seven‑core integrated; 512 GB model adds a sixth CPU core and a second 16‑core Neural Engine.
- Handles photo editing, light video work, and multitasking smoothly; still outpaced by iPad Pro (M5) and iPad Air (M4) for heavy workloads.
- Supports Apple Intelligence generative AI features; tasks like object removal take ~20 seconds.

## Audio
- Two woofers and custom tweeters deliver louder volume and stronger bass than previous mini.
- No visible speaker holes; contributes to first iPad with IP67 rating (dust‑tight, water‑resistant).

## Camera and other changes
- 12 MP front and rear cameras unchanged; front camera relocated to the long side, causing off‑center framing in portrait mode.
- Back camera gains Adaptive True Tone flash that adjusts LED pattern for better document scans.
- Color options: “midnight” and lighter “starlight”.

## Pricing and positioning
- Base price $50 lower than the 11‑inch iPad Air, yet still struggles to justify purchase over the larger, more powerful model.
- Best suited for media consumption and light productivity on a truly compact form factor.

## Verdict
- OLED display, current‑gen chip, and improved speakers make the 2026 mini the strongest version yet.
- Limitations: 60 Hz refresh, modest camera repositioning, and price proximity to the iPad Air keep it in a niche role rather than a mainstream replacement.
