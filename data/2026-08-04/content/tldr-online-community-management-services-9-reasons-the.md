Enterprise communities fail quietly. There’s rarely one dramatic moment, just a slow drift toward flat engagement, burnt-out community managers, and stakeholders losing faith. Emilie, community expert at Standing on Giants, has identified nine often-overlooked reasons online community management services underperform and the fix for each one.

## 1. A vague community purpose

If the value proposition of your online community management services isn’t clear to everyone involved, the community team, stakeholders, and members, it creates misaligned decisions and wasted effort. Define what the community is for, who it serves, and what success looks like before anything else.

## 2. The wrong tools for the job

Effective enterprise community management depends on havingthe right techstack for your specific needs. Tools that work for one community won’t necessarily work for another. Regularly audit your tools against your actual requirements, for member engagement, metrics tracking, reporting, and driving quality discussions, and update when they no longer fit.

## 3. Weak discoverability

A community that your target audience can’t find will stagnate. New members are the lifeblood of brand community engagement. Make sure your community appears at the right moments: website touchpoints, onboarding flows, email outreach, and search results for your audience’s most common queries.

## 4. No agreed priorities

Online community strategy covers a wide range of programmes and initiatives. Trying to do everything at once means doing nothing well. Agree on a focused set of priorities, keep the whole team aligned on them, and revisit regularly. When everything is a priority, nothing is.

## 5. Weak company commitment

Community has become a buzzword. Some organisations launch one because it sounds good, without the internal motivation or commitment to sustain it. Without genuine organisational backing, a community becomes an island rather than the connected hub it could be. Real commitment means time, resources, and people willing to champion it from the inside.

## 6. Under-resourced community teams

Community managersare jacks of all trades, and that makes burnout a constant risk. As communities grow and initiatives multiply, workloads rarely shrink. If your community team is already drowning, the damage to your community and your people is already underway. Resource planning needs to stay ahead of growth, not react to it.

## 7. Siloed social media and community management

Social media community managementand brand community forums are often run as separate operations. They shouldn’t be. Members expect a consistent experience wherever they encounter your brand. When teams work in silos, the experience fractures, feedback goes unshared, and brand tone drifts. Align your social and community teams on messaging, tone, and member experience.

## 8. Tracking the wrong metrics

It is easy to default to industry-standard metrics or the ones that are simplest to pull. But if your performance indicators are not tied to your community’s actual goals, they are not telling you much. Start with what success looks like for your community, then choose the metrics that genuinely represent it.The right datadrives better decisions.

## 9. Over-moderation

The biggest draw of an online community is organic, human conversation. Members come for real, first-hand perspectives, not polished FAQs. Heavy-handed community moderation kills that. It also has an AEO cost: authentic member discussions are more likely to be cited by AI engines like ChatGPT and Perplexity, driving new traffic and visibility. A light-touch approach works best. Invest in clearcommunity guidelines, intervene only when necessary, and educate members gently over time.

The common thread

Most underperforming online community management services fail not because of one catastrophic mistake, but because of accumulated small misalignments, unclear goals, mismatched tools, stretched teams, and strategies that never quite join up. Each of these nine issues is fixable with the right focus.
