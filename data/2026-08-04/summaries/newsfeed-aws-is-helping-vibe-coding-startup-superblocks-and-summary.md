# AWS is helping vibe‑coding startup Superblocks, and the implications are big

## Partnership details
- Multi‑year joint marketing agreement between Superblocks and Amazon Web Services (AWS).  
- Superblocks’ vibe‑coding tool can be embedded in the private clouds of AWS customers.  
- Applications use Amazon Aurora databases within the enterprise’s private cloud, preventing external data transmission.  
- Integration with Amazon Bedrock provides AI model inference and app development capabilities.  
- Resulting apps are managed and secured by the enterprise IT department rather than operating as rogue applications.

## Statements from leadership
- CEO Brad Menezes explains that data never leaves the private cloud and benefits from AWS auditing, encryption, and network controls.  
- An AWS spokesperson says the company supports partners that meet strong customer demand and align with enterprise building preferences.

## Market context
- AWS currently lacks a dedicated vibe‑coding agent; its existing agents (Kiro for developers, Quick for business users) serve different purposes.  
- Superblocks has about 50 employees and raised $60 million in a Series A round (May 2025) from Spark Capital, Kleiner Perkins, Meritech Capital, and Greenoaks.  
- The agreement illustrates a growing trend of hyperscalers encouraging enterprises to run AI models and orchestration on their own clouds instead of relying on frontier AI providers.

## Industry dynamics
- Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella promotes multi‑model strategies to reduce costs, avoid lock‑in, and mitigate trust concerns with AI labs.  
- Enterprises are increasingly adopting multiple models, including open‑weight and Chinese open‑source options.  
- Open models accounted for 29 % of traffic through Vercel’s AI gateway last month, a popular enterprise tool for managing multi‑model AI use.  
- CIOs view a multi‑model approach as essential for coding, customer service, HR, and sales automation.

## Outlook
- Menezes predicts that executives who rely on a single model provider may be replaced.  
- AWS sees the introduction of vibe‑coding for business users in private, secure clouds as a second wave following AI coding agents for developers.  
- AWS describes this as an emerging category with real momentum and the type of innovation it supports.
