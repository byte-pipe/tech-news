# Base Power raises $1 billion to expand backyard battery storage

## Company strategy
- Targets residential backyards instead of large land parcels near grid connections.  
- Aims to alleviate grid strain by installing home batteries that can also sell stored electricity back to the grid.  
- Operates primarily in Texas and Illinois, regions that include parts of the PJM grid.

## Recent funding
- Closed a Series D round of $1 billion, valuing the company at $13 billion post‑money.  
- Investors include Ribbit, Addition, Valor Equity Partners, JPMorgan Chase’s Strategic Investment Group, Altimeter, D1 Capital Partners, Sands Capital, Coatue, Layer Global, Energy Impact Partners, Thrive Capital, a16z, Lightspeed, Trust Ventures, CapitalG, among others.  
- Co‑founder and CEO Zach Dell (son of Michael Dell) leads the company; Michael Dell did not participate.

## Product launch
- Introduced the “Base Core” home battery, built in Austin, Texas.  
- Stores 39.2 kWh of electricity, larger than most competitors.  
- Customers can purchase one or two units; each unit can power a home for a day or more during outages.

## Installation pace and scale
- Has installed over 500 MWh of storage in recent years.  
- Currently installs about 100 batteries per day (≈8 MWh daily) and plans to double that rate by year‑end.

## Business model
- Offers batteries on a subscription basis rather than large upfront costs.  
- Example (Houston): $695 installation fee, $19 monthly fee, plus $0.131 /kWh for electricity usage.  
- In regulated markets, partners with utilities to place batteries where they relieve local grid stress; in both regulated and deregulated markets, homeowners gain backup power.

## Market context
- U.S. electricity demand is rising sharply due to broad electrification and rapid AI data‑center construction.  
- Grid expansion after years of stagnation is lagging behind demand, especially in PJM, which hosts many data centers and includes parts of Illinois where Base Power operates.
