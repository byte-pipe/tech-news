# FDA clears blood test to aid evaluation for Alzheimer’s disease – WashU Medicine

## Overview
- The FDA has cleared PrecivityAD2, a blood‑based diagnostic test created by C2N Diagnostics, a Washington University (WashU) startup.  
- The test identifies amyloid plaques and tau protein biomarkers linked to Alzheimer’s disease using high‑resolution mass spectrometry.  
- It offers a less invasive alternative to cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) testing and brain imaging.

## Clinical Significance
- Provides early detection of Alzheimer’s pathology with >90 % accuracy, comparable to CSF tests and brain scans, even in patients with mild cognitive symptoms.  
- Early diagnosis is crucial because newly approved disease‑modifying drugs work best when treatment starts promptly.  
- FDA clearance allows broader marketing, improves insurance coverage prospects, and makes the test more affordable and accessible to clinicians and health systems.

## Technology and Development
- Developed from foundational research by Randall J. Bateman, MD and David M. Holtzman, MD at WashU Medicine.  
- Measures the blood ratio of amyloid‑beta (Aβ42/Aβ40) and two tau forms (p‑tau217 and total‑tau217).  
- Patents were filed through WashU’s Office of Technology Management; C2N Diagnostics holds exclusive commercial licenses and has optimized the assay for clinical use.

## Impact and Future Directions
- Expected to transform Alzheimer’s diagnosis and management by delivering an accurate, reliable test from a single blood draw.  
- C2N employs more than 120 researchers, physicians, and professionals and is expanding its headquarters in St. Louis’ Cortex Innovation District.  
- Ongoing collaboration between WashU Medicine and C2N aims to accelerate commercialization and support the development of additional Alzheimer’s therapies.
