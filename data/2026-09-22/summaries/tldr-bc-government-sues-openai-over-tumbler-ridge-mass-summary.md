# B.C. government sues OpenAI over Tumbler Ridge mass shooting - The Globe and Mail

# Summary of “B.C. government sues OpenAI over Tumbler Ridge mass shooting”

## Main facts
- The British Columbia Attorney General, Niki Sharma, announced a lawsuit filed in U.S. District Court (San Francisco) against OpenAI and its CEO Sam Altman.
- The suit alleges OpenAI’s chatbot ChatGPT interacted with the shooter, 18‑year‑old Jesse Van Rootselaar, in a manner that was “so alarming” it triggered an internal review but was never reported to the RCMP.
- The February 10, 2024 shooting in Tumbler Ridge, B.C., left eight people dead (five secondary‑school students, an educational assistant, the shooter’s mother and brother) and caused the shooter’s suicide.

## Claims in the lawsuit
- OpenAI was negligent and “aiding and abetting” the mass shooting by failing to warn authorities.
- The company designed an “unsafe product” and provided a defective service that allowed the shooter to continue using ChatGPT via a second account after his original account was banned in 2025.
- The lawsuit seeks:
  - A jury trial.
  - Compensation for known and foreseeable harms, including reimbursement for rebuilding the demolished secondary school.
  - Injunctive relief to prevent similar tragedies.

## OpenAI’s response
- CEO Sam Altman expressed “deep regret” that the company did not alert law enforcement.
- OpenAI confirmed the conversation prompted an internal review but declined to release the chat logs to provincial officials.

## Additional legal actions
- Survivors, witnesses, pupils, and staff from Tumbler Ridge Secondary School have filed separate lawsuits against OpenAI.
- The B.C. government is urging federal changes to Canada’s Criminal Code to remove any “AI exemption” and hold AI firms accountable for criminal outcomes linked to their technologies.

## Rationale for filing in California
- OpenAI’s knowledge of the concerning chat occurred while the company operated out of California.
- Filing in the state where many tech firms are headquartered is intended to create broader pressure for AI safety reforms.
