# 7 Weak Product Management Signs - by David Pereira

# 7 Weak Product Management Signs – Summary

## Overview
- The article revisits a list originally written in 2021, reflecting on how the signs of weak product management have become even more prevalent in 2026.
- The author, David Pereira, uses personal experience as a former head of product to illustrate each sign.
- The central claim: the cost of ignoring these signs has increased; weak product management now feels fast and rewarding because AI‑driven prompts make shipping easy, even when the work is hollow.

## The 7 Signs

### 1. Solutions over Problems
- Teams focus on building tools or features without clearly defining the problem they address.
- The ease of creating solutions (e.g., weekend prototypes) masks a lack of problem understanding.
- The author often asks for problem statements and meets eye‑rolling resistance.

### 2. Output over Outcome
- Discussions start with “what should we build next?” rather than “what impact do we want to create?”
- Despite many talks, books, and conferences, the behavior has not shifted; output remains the primary metric.

### 3. Roadmap over Goals
- Roadmaps are still treated as fixed feature lists, while strategic goals are missing.
- The AI hype illustrates the issue: many raise hands for AI on the roadmap, but fewer believe it’s the right priority.
- People follow roadmaps even when they don’t trust the direction, often to protect their jobs.

### 4. Immortal Features
- Features are released, rarely measured, and seldom retired, leading to product bloat.
- The speed of feature addition has accelerated, making “immortal” features more common.
- The author rarely encounters teams that have actually killed a feature.

### 5. Stakeholders’ Wishes over User Feedback
- Stakeholders now bring prototypes or “built” solutions, reducing the need for product teams to create from scratch.
- Real customer interaction has declined; reliance on synthetic users or internal data points grows.
- The old adage “Nothing Important Happens In The Office” still holds despite new chatbots.

### 6. Certainty over Assumptions
- LLMs (e.g., ChatGPT, Claude) quickly validate assumptions, giving a false sense of certainty.
- The author shares a whimsical example of a moon‑sausage strategy generated by AI.
- Critical thinking must remain human; AI can be a cheerleader but not a substitute for reality checks.

### 7. Two Drivers: Product Owner and Product Manager
- In 2021 the split between PO and PM caused confusion; by 2026 roles have blurred, with designers, engineers, and PMs all making product decisions.
- The “everyone has a hand on the wheel” model works only if one person (or clear ownership) maintains end‑to‑end direction.
- Titles matter less; ownership of vision matters more.

## The One Thing That Changed
- The signs themselves have not changed, but ignoring them now leads to rapid, seemingly productive output that lacks real value.
- Weak product management in 2026 feels comfortable because each prompt produces visible progress, even if it’s superficial.

## Call to Action
- Identify which of the seven signs appear in your current work.
- Address them one at a time, using the list as a personal checkpoint to stay honest.
