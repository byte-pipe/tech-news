# China’s answer to ASML runs on Zeiss lenses, Financial Times reports

## Overview
- Huawei is acting as the project‑management coordinator for China’s deep‑ultraviolet (DUV) lithography push.  
- The flagship domestic DUV machine still depends on projection lenses made by German optics firm Zeiss, obtained on the secondary market in China.

## Key Players
- **Yuliangsheng** – Shanghai equipment maker, spun out of SiCarrier, developing the first Chinese‑made advanced DUV machine; SMIC is testing it.  
- **Huawei** – Invests across the lithography supply chain, denies formal ties to SiCarrier, but its venture arm Habo and Huawei‑backed funds hold stakes in:
  - **Fujian Zhiqi Photonics** – ultra‑precision optics maker, aiming to replace Zeiss lenses.  
  - **Beijing RSLaser Opto‑Electronics** – domestic DUV light‑source developer.  
- **Zeiss** – Officially sells lithography optics only to ASML; lenses appear in China via secondary channels.  
- **ASML** – Dominates the global market for both projection lenses and high‑power DUV light sources (through its Cymer unit).  

## Technical Bottlenecks
- **Projection lenses** – Current domestic machines rely on Zeiss lenses, which are not sold directly to China.  
- **Light source** – High‑power, stable DUV light sources are dominated by Japan’s Gigaphoton and ASML’s Cymer; domestic effort led by RSLaser aims to create a 28 nm‑class source.

## Progress & Timeline
- Yuliangsheng targets production of 12 DUV machines by the end of the year.  
- SMIC pairs the domestic tool with multi‑patterning to achieve 7 nm chips, but overall performance remains far behind ASML’s current generation.  
- UBS analysis places Chinese lithography capability at roughly the level of ASML in 2004; several years are expected before parity.  
- Chinese fabs continue to run production on ASML equipment; ASML’s China sales represent about one‑fifth of its total revenue.

## Market Dynamics
- ASML is advancing High‑NA technology and 12‑inch photomasks, shifting the target for the highest‑end chips while China works on 28 nm DUV tools.  
- U.S. export restrictions may unintentionally speed up China’s domestic supply chain by forcing fabs to adopt locally sourced DUV components despite yield risks.

## Watch‑Points
- Whether the planned 12 machines are delivered and which fabs receive them.  
- Success of Fujian Zhiqi Photonics in supplying a domestic replacement for Zeiss lenses.  
- Trends in ASML’s China revenue as an indicator of the domestic tools’ impact on the market.
