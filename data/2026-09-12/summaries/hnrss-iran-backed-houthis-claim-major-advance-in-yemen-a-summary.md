# Iran‑backed Houthis claim major advance in Yemen, seizing island in key shipping route

## Key developments
- Houthis say they have taken control of Perim (Mayyun) Island, which sits in the Bab al‑Mandab Strait – the narrow gateway between the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden.  
- The group also reports capturing the Red Sea port city of Mokha and expelling “Saudi‑backed forces” from Yemen’s western coast.  
- In a statement, the Houthis claim that maritime navigation is safe for all vessels except Saudi ships, which they say are under a blockade.  
- Their military spokesman describes the operation launched on 3 September as a “large‑scale success,” citing the expulsion of Saudi troops from six districts, attacks on seven Saudi divisions, the release of prisoners, and the downing of nine aircraft.

## Strategic significance
- Perim Island controls access to the Bab al‑Mandab, a vital route linking the Suez Canal with the Indian Ocean and Europe; disruption could affect global trade.  
- Saudi Arabia increasingly depends on the Red Sea for oil exports after the US‑Israel‑Iran conflict has limited the Strait of Hormuz.  
- Iran’s support (weapons, training, technology) has strengthened the Houthis, aligning their interests with Tehran’s broader regional strategy to pressure shipping lanes on both sides of the Arabian Peninsula.  
- The nearby Djibouti coast hosts military bases of the US, China, France, Japan and Italy, underscoring the international stakes surrounding the waterway.

## Humanitarian impact
- The United Nations reports that more than 46,000 people have been displaced since the latest round of fighting began.  
- Residents of Mokha describe rapid government withdrawals, shop closures and families fleeing the city.  

## Regional reactions
- Saudi Arabia has responded with airstrikes, including attacks on Mokha airport, but has not yet issued a detailed public statement on the capture of Perim Island.  
- The escalation marks a shift from a largely frozen conflict (since 2022) to active fighting in one of the world’s most critical maritime chokepoints.
