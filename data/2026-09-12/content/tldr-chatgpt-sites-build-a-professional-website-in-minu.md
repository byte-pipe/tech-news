## How to Make the Most of ChatGPT Sites

1. Get started.Select theSitesmode in ChatGPT. (Screenshot)
2. Specify your goal. Describe a landing page, dashboard, guide, or app you want to build.Tip:mention who it’s for. That will help shape the interface.
3. Add context.If you’re working within a ChatGPT Project, it will already have relevant context. It may have your style guide, logos, mission statement, and other files. If not, consider uploading whatever is relevant.
4. Provide visual guidance.Note preferences you have for style, colors, fonts, or imagery. Consider adding reference images or screenshots of sites whose design you like.Tip:Tell ChatGPT to ask you questions before finalizing its approach. That helps you steer the plan.
5. Draw something if you want.If you have a mockup in mind you can draw on paper, snap a photo of it and upload it as part of your query.
6. Give feedback.As soon as ChatGPT gives you its first draft, reply with comments about what you’d like to change. Use the annotate tool in the sidebar to mark specific parts of the site to fix. Note where you want a color or font to be changed, a different image, or revised text.
7. Publish and share.When your site is ready, publish the link to share it with others. Or keep it private if it’s a personal resource.

## Pick a Project to Try

* Make a new landing page or portfolio.If you don’t have a page showcasing your work, build one so people so people can learn more.
* Make a community resource.Create a guide or topic overview based on your own insight or research. Share resources with students, friends, or the public.
* Make a useful app for yourself. Address your own need. I made a private app to map out what I’m working on, pegged to publishing dates.
* Make a game.Create something to make learning fun, or remake an old arcade/trivia title you loved. Customize its design and features so it’s distinct.

## Caveats

* It’s not yet available on free ChatGPT plans.
* If you use ChatGPT Sites heavily, you might hit a beta limit at some point. That will temporarily prevent you from creating new Sites or keeping high-usage Sites publicly available. I haven’t encountered that limit yet.
* Sites don’t appear instantaneously. Depending on the complexity of what you’re building, expect to wait 10 or 15 minutes after submitting a prompt for your site to be ready.
* Help resourcesare limited. For more guidance, the OpenAI Academy has a fewskills workshop recordings. Or watch this helpfulvideoby Paul Lipsky.

## Alternatives

* I usedTildayears ago to make my ownpersonal site. It has excellent templates and gives you more manual control than any AI-first tool. It’s modular: swap in and adapt sections of your site without building from scratch. Iwroteabout it in the early days of this newsletter.
* LovableandBoltare AI-first tools designed specifically for building sites. They’ve gotten easier to use and more flexible since I firstwroteabout vibe coding sites in 2024. If you’re already paying for ChatGPT, you may not need these other subscriptions since you can now build with ChatGPT Sites.
* Claudecan make professional, interactive sites withArtifactsandClaude Design. It’s a superb choice if you’ve already provided Claude with deep context about your work and if you have a paid account. I’ve made lots of simple sites with Claude, and continue to use it alongside ChatGPT Sites.
