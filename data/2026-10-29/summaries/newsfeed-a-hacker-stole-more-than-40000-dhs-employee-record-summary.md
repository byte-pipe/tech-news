# A hacker stole more than 40,000 DHS employee records

## Key Details
- A breach exposed names, job titles, phone numbers, email addresses, and office locations of over 41,000 Department of Homeland Security (DHS) employees.  
- The data was obtained through Okta, the identity‑and‑access management service used by DHS.  
- The hacker group calling itself “Department of Peace” released the information.

## Affected Agencies
- Customs and Border Protection (CBP): ~21,000 records  
- Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE): ~7,000 records  
- Additional records from Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS), and Homeland Security Investigations (HSI).

## Hacker Motive & Statement
- The group claimed the leak was retaliation for DHS efforts to conceal the identities of CBP and ICE agents and to track online critics.  
- Quote: “They have our information, so it’s only fair that we have theirs.”

## Leak Distribution
- Researcher maia arson crimew received the dataset and passed it to Distributed Denial of Secrets (DDoSecrets).  
- DDoSecrets is hosting the “Icepick” dataset, which also lists DHS groups with access to surveillance tools such as the facial‑recognition app Mobile Fortify and the phone‑data analysis tool PenLink.

## DHS Response
- The Department of Homeland Security had not responded to The Verge’s request for comment at the time of publication.
