# Unveiling The Grid: PCBs made in America, twice as fast as Shenzhen

## The problem
- Over the past three decades, electronics manufacturing moved from the West to low‑wage regions because assembly was labor‑intensive.
- Automation now exists but only for very high‑volume products (e.g., SpaceX Starlink), not for the typical “high‑mix, low‑volume” needs of most hardware companies.
- Frequent design changes require hours of setup and manual changeovers, pushing many companies to overseas quick‑turn shops or expensive domestic services.

## What The Grid is
- A single, large robot in Massachusetts that uses levitating movers to transport each board through stations for solder paste printing, component placement, reflow, soldering, and inspection.
- AI agents, learned models, and classic optimizers schedule the workflow, giving every board a unique route with no changeovers or per‑design setup.
- Handles prototypes and production batches on the same equipment, making one unit of 100 different designs as easy as 100 units of one design.
- Already used by major EV makers, top AI labs, and well‑known tech companies for low‑volume production, test stations, tooling, and R&D.

## What it means for a real board
- Example: 4‑layer motor controller (118 parts, 38 unique) with a quantity of five, parts included, delivered to a US address.
- **The Grid**: 4‑day delivery, cost $630, no customs or tariff risk.
- Typical Shenzhen quick‑turn shop: 9‑day delivery, cost ≈ $465 after shipping and US tariffs.
- Trade‑off: pay about $165 more to receive boards five days faster and with a reliable domestic supply chain—value that many hardware teams consider worth the extra cost.

## It’s not a prototype
- The Grid has been operational for five years, generating tens of millions of dollars in annual revenue.
- Across CircuitHub: over 2.2 million boards shipped, more than 150 million parts placed, serving over 26 000 engineers.

## What’s next
- Current demand exceeds existing capacity; plan to increase capacity fivefold this year, with another major expansion in 2027.
- Long‑term vision: make hardware manufacturing as fast as design, proving that Western electronics can be automated at the volumes most companies need.
