# AI execs may be too out of touch to build for the general public | TechCrunch

# Summary of “AI execs may be too out of touch to build for the general public”

## Main incident
- Former Yahoo CEO Marissa Mayer promoted her new AI assistant Dazzle, which learns from a user’s camera roll.  
- In an interview she claimed Dazzle helped her draft three bullet‑point descriptors for her 10‑year‑old daughter’s birthday presentation (“empathetic, creative, and an animal lover”).  
- Critics called the description vague and likened it to a generic horoscope.  
- Mayer later clarified on X that Dazzle was merely resurfacing old photos of her daughter’s projects to demonstrate the prototype’s capabilities.

## Public backlash
- Progressive activist Leah Greenberg highlighted the interview as evidence of a growing disconnect between Silicon Valley culture and broader society.  
- The backlash echoed previous reactions to statements by other AI leaders, such as OpenAI CEO Sam Altman’s claim that he could not imagine raising a newborn without ChatGPT and his suggestion to use ChatGPT for planning children’s activities.  
- Both Mayer and Altman faced negative comments, with Altman being “ratioed” for suggesting AI could replace direct parent‑child conversation.

## Industry perspective
- Mayer suggested Dazzle could outsource mundane parenting tasks: planning birthday themes, playdates, vacations, Halloween costumes, and even coordinating with a spouse’s AI assistant.  
- When asked if a frictionless, AI‑planned life was depressing, she admitted she hadn’t considered it.  
- Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg echoed similar optimism, predicting AI therapists and AI romantic partners will become socially acceptable as people seek more “meaningful” friendships.

## Public opinion data
- A Pew Research Center survey found 50 % of U.S. adults believe AI will diminish the ability to form meaningful relationships, while only 5 % think AI will make such connections easier.

## Cultural critique
- The article argues that tech industry values efficiency and bio‑hacking (e.g., sleep trackers, unregulated peptide stacks) do not translate well to human relationships, which are inherently messy and essential for meaning.  
- Delegating emotional interactions to AI risks surrendering the fulfilling, though difficult, experience of being human.
