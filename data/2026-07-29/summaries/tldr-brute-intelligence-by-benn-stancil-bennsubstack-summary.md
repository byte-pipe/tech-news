# Brute intelligence

## Overview
- The article discusses a new form of “brute intelligence,” where advanced language models act as autonomous researchers, iterating hypotheses, testing them, and refining results without extensive human guidance.  
- This approach is likened to industrialized brute force: instead of random typing, AI agents perform focused, intelligent searches across many “labs” simultaneously.

## Illustrative Cases
- **Terence Tao & Levent Alpöge**: Using Anthropic’s Fable model, Alpöge discovered a counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture. Tao’s blog notes the result seemed “miraculous” and unlikely via pure brute force, though AI assistance was crucial.  
- **Dmitry Rybin**: With a brief three‑message prompt to ChatGPT (“You should do a breakthrough…”), the model produced a complete unconditional counterexample to another conjecture, requiring minimal human direction.

## Key Observations
- AI can generate and test dozens or hundreds of hypotheses rapidly, turning problems with verifiable answers into tasks suitable for automated exploration.  
- The speed of AI‑driven search can make the distinction between “fast” and “smart” blur; a fast brute‑force system may achieve results comparable to human insight.  
- Not all work is amenable to this method; tasks requiring physical experimentation, human interaction, or subjective judgment remain outside its scope.

## Implications for Work and Industry
- **Problem Reformulation**: To leverage AI, many tasks may need to be reframed as mathematically structured problems that can be iterated upon by machines.  
- **Infrastructure Needs**: Current AI models are likened to “decent cars” lacking proper roads; building data pipelines, codebases, and workflows optimized for AI agents is essential.  
- **Potential Domains**: Mathematics, software development, drug discovery, and certain finance problems could see accelerated progress through brute intelligence.

## Future Outlook
- The author suggests a shift toward designing problems and systems that are “AI‑friendly,” allowing machines to handle the exhaustive reasoning and testing phases while humans focus on creativity, interpretation, and areas requiring human judgment.  
- The rise of brute intelligence may lead to a division of labor: human intuition and subjective decision‑making on one side, and AI‑driven exhaustive computation on the other.
