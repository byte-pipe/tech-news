# After fleeing Sudan’s war, refugees battle thirst in Chad’s camps

## Water crisis in Goudrane camp
- The camp of about 50,000 Sudanese refugees had no water for four days until a truck arrived with 6,000 L, which was taken in ten minutes.
- Families scramble for jerrycans; many women collapse and children push forward.
- Residents survive on less than half the UNHCR minimum daily water requirement.
- The only doctor in the camp stresses that medicines are ineffective without clean water.

## Scale of displacement
- Over 938,000 Sudanese have fled to Chad since the war began in April 2023.
- Most live in overcrowded, arid‑east camps such as Goudrane and Adre.
- Existing boreholes, pipelines, sanitation and treatment facilities cannot keep up with growing demand.

## Humanitarian response
- Solidarités International, operating in Chad since 2008, calls the current water emergency one of its most urgent.
- Funding shortfalls limit the ability to provide safe water for all refugees.
- UN agencies note that water needs now outpace available resources.

## Personal testimonies of the escape
- **Rokhia Ibrahim**, 43, former schoolteacher, recounts RSF attacks that left her husband blind and her baby injured; she now watches children leave the camp with empty buckets.
- **Zahra Khamis**, Masalit survivor, describes rape, drone attacks, loss of her child, and a journey where she witnessed many bodies before her son was shot.
- **Fatne Adam**, 53, lives in Metche camp; she lost her husband and son to snipers and says she can only return to Sudan when peace returns.

## Outlook and hope
- The war is now in its fourth year, spreading across Sudan and creating the world’s largest displacement crisis.
- International diplomatic efforts have made little progress; foreign backers of both warring sides face increasing scrutiny.
- For refugees in Chad, the prospect of returning home grows dimmer as the conflict continues.
