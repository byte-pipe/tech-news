# New HIV vaccine shows unprecedented success in preclinical study

## Highlights of the research
- Developed by La Jolla Institute for Immunology (LJI), Scripps Research, and IAVI; aims to protect humans from HIV infection and AIDS.  
- First vaccine to generate high levels of “broadly neutralizing” antibodies (bnAbs) in non‑human primates.  
- Human clinical trials have already begun (HVTN 144 and IAVI G004 Phase 1 studies).  

## Vaccine strategy and mechanism
- Targets the early “germline” stage of B‑cell development, guiding naive B cells toward bnAb production (“germline targeting”).  
- Uses engineered immunogens that mimic conserved HIV envelope antigens, providing repeated “boot‑camp” exposures to steer B‑cell maturation.  
- Booster (“shepherding”) shots reinforce the desired antibody pathway, converting ultra‑rare responses into common ones.

## Preclinical testing in rhesus macaques
- Prime‑boost regimen administered to rhesus macaques at Emory National Primate Research Center.  
- Approximately 44 % of animals produced abundant bnAbs detectable in the bloodstream.  
- Antibodies matched those found in the rare human donors who naturally develop bnAbs, indicating the vaccine can elicit the correct specificity.  
- Protection against infection was not directly tested, but the presence of circulating bnAbs suggests potential efficacy.

## Next steps toward human use
- Ongoing optimization of booster schedules to increase response rates toward 100 % in animals.  
- The priming immunogen has already been evaluated in humans (HVTN 144) and is in Phase 1 testing (IAVI G004).  
- Plans are underway to assess the full immunization regimen in future human clinical trials, leveraging favorable immunogenetics for broader success.
