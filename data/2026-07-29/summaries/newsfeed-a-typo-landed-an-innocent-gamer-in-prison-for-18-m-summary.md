# A Typo Landed an Innocent Gamer in Prison for 18 Months | WIRED  

## Background  
- In 2018 a 12‑year‑old girl in Madison, Wisconsin, exchanged Kik messages with an adult using the username **“fus__ro_dah”** (two underscores).  
- The girl’s mother reported an “inappropriate” photo to police, prompting a forensic review of the phone.  

## Investigation Mistake  
- Dane County investigators subpoenaed Kik for the username **“fus_ro_dah”** (one underscore).  
- Kik returned an email address linked to a Canadian IP, which led Halifax Regional Police to Brandon Klayme, a Nova Scotia resident.  
- The single‑underscore error was never noticed during the investigation or trial.  

## Arrest, Trial, and Imprisonment  
- Klayme’s home was searched; no relevant evidence was found on his devices, and there was no proof he used Kik during the relevant period.  
- He was charged with luring a minor, providing sexual material to a child, and possession of child pornography.  
- A 2023 trial found him guilty; he served a full 18‑month sentence in 2024.  

## Appeal and Overturn  
- While preparing an appeal, Klayme’s team discovered the subpoena typo.  
- Prosecutors acknowledged that the correct username would have pointed to a different individual (a “Jay” in California).  
- The Nova Scotia Court of Appeal acquitted Klayme on all charges, vacating his conviction and probation.  

## Key Takeaways  
- A single character error in a subpoena can redirect an entire criminal investigation.  
- The mistake persisted through investigation, trial, and sentencing without detection.  
- The case highlights the importance of meticulous digital forensics and verification of evidence in cyber‑crime prosecutions.
