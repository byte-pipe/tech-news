# Summary of “Don’t wait until you want revenge” – why prenups are on the rise

## Key findings
- Younger couples are increasingly signing prenuptial agreements, even when there is little wealth disparity.  
- In the US, about 20 % of married couples have a prenup; among millennials the figure is close to 50 % and over one‑third of Gen Z report having one.  
- In the UK roughly 1 in 10 couples have a prenup, with about 1 in 3 married under‑35‑year‑olds doing so.  
- The rise is driven by a desire to discuss finances early, protect inherited assets, and avoid the emotional and financial fallout of divorce.

## Case studies
- **Celine Tsoi & Charles Mock (California)** – engaged after eight months; both wanted a clear financial plan. Charles, a nurse, earns more; Celine, a PhD student, wanted the house guaranteed to her in a split. The prenup cost a few hundred dollars online and eliminated financial arguments.  
- **Becca Gibson & Dave Howell (West Sussex)** – Dave earns £130,000; Becca runs a lash‑extension business. After a difficult first marriage, Becca opted for a prenup to protect her future inheritance and ensure each partner leaves with what they started with. Dave switched from a £5,000 traditional solicitor fee to a £1,600 online service (Wenup).  

## Statistical trends
- US courts traditionally uphold prenups, encouraging their use among millennials and Gen Z.  
- UK legal reforms have increased recognition of prenups, prompting higher uptake among younger couples.  
- The “Great Wealth Transfer” – large inheritances moving to the next generation – is prompting heirs to ring‑fence assets through prenups.

## Legal and market insights
- London lawyer Mark Gilmartin (JMW Solicitors) notes a shift from the “billionaire‑and‑barmaid” stereotype to demand from start‑up professionals, entrepreneurs, and influencers.  
- Online platforms (e.g., HelloPrenup, Wenup) make prenups faster and cheaper, expanding accessibility.  
- Traditional solicitor fees (£4‑5 k) are being replaced by fixed‑price digital services (£1‑2 k).

## Motivations and perceived benefits
- **Fairness:** Couples view prenups as a way to split assets equitably, especially when earnings differ.  
- **Protection of inheritance:** Young adults expecting sizable parental gifts want those assets insulated from future divorce settlements.  
- **Emotional safety:** Discussing finances early reveals partner values and reduces future conflict.  
- **Pragmatism:** Awareness of high divorce rates leads couples to treat marriage as a partnership that should be financially transparent from the start.  
- **Gender equity:** Modern prenups are seen as tools that can empower women by clarifying rights rather than limiting them.  

## Overall impression
- Many couples consider the cost of a prenup a worthwhile investment—often cheaper than the wedding itself—and appreciate having “hard conversations” out of the way, leading to fewer financial arguments after marriage.
