# Tim Curry, star of The Rocky Horror Picture Show and Stephen King’s It, dies aged 80  

## Death  
- Tim Curry died peacefully at his home in Los Angeles, aged 80.  
- His manager confirmed the death to Variety; the cause has not been disclosed.  

## Career highlights  
- Renowned for physically transformative roles:  
  - Dr Frank‑N‑Furter in *The Rocky Horror Show* (stage 1973, film 1975).  
  - The Lord of Darkness in Ridley Scott’s *Legend* (1985).  
  - Pennywise the clown in the TV miniseries *It* (1990).  
- Maintained a parallel career across film, television and voice work.  

## Stage work  
- Graduated from the University of Birmingham (Drama and English, 1968).  
- First major role in the original West End production of *Hair* (1968), where he met Richard O’Brien.  
- Performed as Tristan Tzara in Tom Stoppard’s *Travesties*.  
- Played Mozart in the Broadway transfer of *Amadeus* (1980).  
- Portrayed King Arthur in the Broadway run of the Monty Python musical *Spamalot*.  

## Film and television  
- Film roles included Robert Graves in *The Shout* and Jeremy in *The Ploughman’s Lunch*.  
- Appeared in comedies such as *Home Alone 2: Lost in New York*, *Muppet Treasure Island*, *Scary Movie 2* and *McHale’s Navy*.  
- Provided voice work for animated titles including *Garfield: A Tail of Two Kitties*, *A Turtle’s Tale: Sammy’s Adventures* and *Ribbit*.  
- Television credits:  
  - William Shakespeare in John Mortimer’s six‑part biography (1978).  
  - Bill Sikes in the US TV adaptation of *Oliver Twist* (1982).  
  - Wizard Trymon in the adaptation of Terry Pratchett’s *The Colour of Magic* (2008).  
  - Guest appearances in series such as *Psych*, *Poirot* and *Criminal Minds*.  
  - Voice roles in *Duckman*, *Sonic the Hedgehog*, *The Mask* and *Star Wars: The Clone Wars*.  

## Health and later life  
- Suffered a stroke in 2012 while receiving a massage; required brain surgery, resulting in mobility and short‑term memory challenges.  
- Published memoir *Vagabond* in 2025.  
- In a 2025 Guardian interview he reflected on his longevity, saying, “I’ve had the opportunities, and I’m still showing up.”  

## Tributes  
- Luke Evans (recent Broadway Dr Frank‑N‑Furter) called Curry “a force, a bright fierce flame… an inspiration.”  
- Carol Burnett praised his ability to play “lovable villains” and remembered him as a dear friend.  
- Michael McKean, co‑star from *Clue*, recalled their final conversation about “life and love and laughter,” describing Curry’s passing as a blessing.
