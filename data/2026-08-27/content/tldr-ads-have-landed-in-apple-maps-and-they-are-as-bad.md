# Ads have landed in Apple Maps, and they are as bad as expected

 

Ben Lovejoy
 | Aug 26 2026 - 5:31 am PT																

Whenwe ran a polllast month, the overwhelming majority of9to5Macreaders agreed thatAppleshould exit the ad business.

Ads have now landedinApple Mapsin the US and Canada, and my view is that they are every bit as bad as expected …

Apple warned usback in March that we would begin seeing ads in the company’s Maps app at some point in the summer. We first spotted them ourselves yesterday, andthe company confirmedthat the rollout began in the last few days. Within a few weeks, everyone in the US and Canada will be seeing them.

1. Before searching: Suggested Places in the search screen will feature an ad.
2. After searching: Ads will appear in search results when relevant.

The company says that privacy is a major focus.

The new Maps ads build on Apple’s existing privacy-first approach to advertising. A user’s location and the ads they see and interact with in Maps are not associated with their Apple Account. Personal data stays on the user’s device, is not collected or stored by Apple, and is not shared with advertisers or other third parties.

I’m sure all of that is true, but I still think it’s a terrible look for two reasons.

First, while Apple isn’t doing any kind of tracking in order to deliver personalized ads, almost every other app and website is. Most people are going to assume the iPhone maker is doing the same, and – however unfair the perception might be – that damages the company’s reputation for protecting our privacy.

Second, it simply looks cheap. Ads are a necessary evil in both the app and web publishing ecosystems: they are what keep the lights on when an app or website is free to use. But Apple doesn’t need ad revenue to pay its bills: it already charges a premium price for premium hardware. Ads simply don’t fit with the company’s image.

I think it was John Gruber who likened ads in Apple apps to those awful Intel Inside stickers you got on cheap Windows laptops. Sure, we understand that companies aiming to provide budget laptops need every revenue stream they can find, but we would never have expected Apple to cheapen the look of a MacBook by adding them. Why, then, should we see ads in the stock apps on our Apple devices?

My view very much remains that Apple has no business being in the ad business.
