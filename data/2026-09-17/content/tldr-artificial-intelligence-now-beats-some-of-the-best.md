## gist

An Economist Science & Technology report (read from Hindustan Times syndication after the live Economist page returned 403 and no public archive.is/ph snapshot was found) covers the Summer 2026 Metaculus Cup: for the first time an AI took first place, with other AIs second and fifth and humans third and fourth. LLM-based forecasters now approach superforecaster parity on evolving question sets, while startups add multi-model debate, paywalled data, and memory wipe for counterfactuals. Caveats remain: the cup is four months, entrants are not necessarily the world’s best, and multi-year horizons may still favor humans.

## ideas

* Metaculus Cup milestone.An AI won the seasonal cup for the first time; AIs also took second and fifth, leaving third and fourth to humans across hundreds of September-resolved questions.
* LLM forecasters, not narrow models.Unlike weather-style specialized systems, these bots use general LLMs to read news and data broadly and explain reasoning—useful when persuading decision-makers.
* Startup tricks on top of scale.Multi-model investigation and debate, paywalled esoteric datasets (Mantic), pundit track-record scoring (Preseen), and memory wipe for counterfactual re-runs sit atop frontier LLM scale.
* Solo tinkerer can still win.Jeffrey Liang’s bot beat better-funded startups with under 150 hours and a couple thousand dollars of compute, and leads an AI-only Metaculus contest.
* Limits and price collapse.Four-month cups and non-elite human fields bound the claim; multi-year forecasting may still favor humans. Superforecaster work can cost over $10,000 and a week; FutureSearch sells minutes and a few dollars.
