# Summary of “PS5 Linux lead quits as open‑source projects have become ‘a bunch of noobs using LLMs’ that ‘they don’t even understand’ – FRVR”

## Key developments
- Andy “TheFlow0” Nguyen, renowned PlayStation hacker and creator of the first Vita kernel exploit, announced he is abandoning the PS5 Linux project and stepping away from the PS5 homebrew scene.  
- Nguyen had invested years of work, including plans for PS5 Pro support and a 2027 release, but cites the rise of AI‑generated “vibe‑coding” contributors as the primary reason for quitting.  

## Reasons for departure
- Recent bans by the RPCS3 emulator team on “vibe‑coding” participants highlight a trend of contributors submitting code they do not understand, often produced with large language models (LLMs).  
- Nguyen describes the current community as “a bunch of noobs using LLMs and writing hacks they don’t even understand,” contrasting it with the earlier era of highly skilled researchers.  
- AI‑assisted coders (“slop kiddies”) discovered a remaining hypervisor bug, reported it to Sony for a bounty, and accelerated the bug’s disclosure, undermining further development.  

## Impact on PS5 Linux
- The project aimed to turn the PS5 into a Steam‑Machine‑style platform, enabling Linux installation, dual‑booting, and broader game compatibility.  
- The last official release under Nguyen’s leadership is **Version 2.5**, supporting PS5 “Phat” and “Slim” models on firmware 3.00‑7.61.  
- Future updates for newer firmware or PS5 Pro are now uncertain due to the halted development.  

## Nguyen’s final remarks
- He urged the AI‑driven contributors to delay reporting the bug until after the release of GTA 6, to give users a chance to legally purchase the game and enjoy Linux, but the request was ignored.  
- The combination of misunderstood AI‑generated code and premature bug reporting led Nguyen to deem further work “down the sink.”
