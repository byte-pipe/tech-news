# VAR controversy: Pressure for quick decisions obvious in rushed derby review

## Overview of the incident
- Video assistant referee Matt Donohue hurried the review of Erling Haaland’s winning goal in the Manchester derby.  
- He and assistant VAR Blake Antrobus failed to notice that Enzo Fernandez was three yards off‑side before attempting a header.  
- The goal was allowed and Manchester City beat Manchester United 1‑0.

## Speed versus accuracy debate
- The Premier League’s season principle is a “more efficient application of VAR to reduce delays.”  
- Average VAR delay fell from 46 seconds per game to 36 seconds season‑on‑season.  
- Critics argue that the push for speed contributed to this error and a similar mistake in the Arsenal‑vs‑Aston Villa penalty incident.

## Process failures
- Donohue stopped the replay at the moment Josko Gvardiol touched the ball and immediately used semi‑automated off‑side technology, bypassing a full review of the cross and Haaland’s header.  
- This prevented the VAR team from seeing Fernandez’s off‑side position.  
- AVAR Antrobus only gave a brief “no, yeah” about a possible Fernandez touch, missing the crucial check.  
- The support VAR, who can intervene on protocol issues, took no action despite the unfolding error.

## Reactions
- Wayne Rooney described the refereeing decisions as “baffling.”  
- City manager Enzo Maresca acknowledged the difficulty of making decisions in 10‑20 seconds and accepted that mistakes happen.  
- Referees’ chief Howard Webb noted the need to balance quicker reviews with correct outcomes.

## VAR staffing and training
- Professional Refereeing (Pro Ref) has invested heavily in VAR training; Donohue is a qualified VAR, on his 65th match and listed for UEFA competitions.  
- Only a few VARs, such as Nick Hopton and Neil Davies, have clear records over recent seasons.  
- Former full‑time VARs Lee Mason and Mike Dean were dismissed after a series of errors.  
- Even leagues with dedicated VAR teams, like Spain’s La Liga, experience similar controversies.

## Potential fixes
- Calls for dedicated VAR specialists and stricter review protocols to prevent rushed decisions.  
- Emphasis on ensuring that speed improvements do not compromise the thoroughness of the review process.
