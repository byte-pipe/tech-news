# Original Sony PlayStation 2 security chip ‘broken wide open’ after 26 years — chemical decapping and four years of reverse engineering unlocks MechaCon secrets

## Main achievement
- Canadian retro‑hardware enthusiast DiscoStarslayer successfully reverse‑engineered and dumped the CXP102064 “MechaCon” security chip from the original PS2 “Fat” console.  
- The work spanned four years and combined chemical decapping, optical dumping, and a software exploit uncovered from the optical data.

## Function of the MechaCon chip
- Controls the PS2’s optical and flash‑drive mechanics.  
- Handles Sony’s disc‑security features, including MagicGate memory‑card encryption and KELF executable decryption.  
- Also appears in arcade boards such as Namco System 246/256 and Konami Python 1, where it authenticates encrypted game data.

## Methods used
- Chemically removed the chip’s package to expose the silicon die.  
- Utilized microscopes and optical probing to read the circuitry.  
- An accidental exploit enabled full data extraction via software.

## Implications
- Improves preservation, repair, and emulation of PS2 hardware and games.  
- Supports development of homebrew software and more accurate emulators.  
- May allow replacement of aging PS2 optical drives.  
- Provides a pathway for archival work on arcade machines that used the same chip.
