# Chop up your books

## Main point
- The author urges readers to physically divide oversized books into smaller, more manageable volumes, arguing that this improves comfort and portability.

## Why it matters
- Large books (e.g., an 850‑page paperback like *Lonesome Dove*) strain the hands, are awkward to read in bed, and take up excessive space in bags or on planes.
- Splitting them creates “reasonable‑sized” sections that are easier to handle and transport.

## How to do it
- **Buy a personal copy** – avoid using library books.
- **Choose the format** you prefer (paperback is easiest, hardback works too) and note page size, margins, and type.
- **Find natural break points** such as parts or chapter divisions.
- **Bend the spine** at the first break point until you can see the glue strip; for hardbacks look for seams between stitched signatures.
- **Cut with an X‑acto blade** directly through the glue, separating the sections cleanly.
- **Create a new cover** for each split section; a manila folder works well because it is sturdy yet inexpensive.
- **Glue the new spine** using bookbinder’s glue or regular Elmer’s glue; clamp with binder clips and let dry.
- **Label each volume** with a Sharpie or similar marker.
- **Read the smaller books** and enjoy the convenience.

## Additional notes
- The practice is intended for personal use; authors are unlikely to object.
- No legal issues arise from cutting your own purchased copy.
- The method requires only basic tools: a sharp blade, glue, a folder, clips, and a marker.
