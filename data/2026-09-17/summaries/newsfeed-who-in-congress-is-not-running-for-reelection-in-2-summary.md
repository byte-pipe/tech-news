# Who in Congress is not running for reelection in 2026?

## Overview
- At the close of the 2026 primary season, NPR’s retirement tracker shows **99 seats** (83 in the House, 16 in the Senate) will have a different member when the 120th Congress convenes.  
- This represents **nearly one‑in‑five** lawmakers who began the 119th Congress.

## Sources of Turnover
- **Retirements or runs for other offices:** 13 senators and 60 representatives have announced they will not seek reelection to their current seats.  
- **Deaths or resignations:** 16 incumbents (6 members of the House, 2 senators, plus 8 others) left office during the term.  
- **Primary defeats:** 13 incumbents were defeated in their party primaries.  
- Additional changes are possible as many incumbents face competitive general‑election races.

## Notable Departures and Office Changes
- **Senate:**  
  - Mitch McConnell (KY) – retiring.  
  - Amy Klobuchar (MN) and Marsha Blackburn (TN) – presumptive gubernatorial candidates, likely to resign if elected.  
- **House:**  
  - Nancy Pelosi (CA) – announced retirement.  
  - Several long‑time members (e.g., Rep. John Larson of CT) lost to younger challengers.  

## Primary Upsets and Competitive Races
- **Texas:** Rep. Dan Crenshaw was the first incumbent voted out; Senator John Cornyn also lost in a runoff.  
- **Louisiana:** Rep. Julia Letlow (Trump‑backed) won the runoff after Sen. Bill Cassidy’s primary loss.  
- **Kentucky:** Rep. Thomas Massie lost to Trump‑backed challenger Ed Gallrein.  
- **New York:** Rep. Adriano Espaillat and Rep. Dan Goldman were defeated in Democratic primaries backed by Mayor Zohran Mamdani.  
- **Colorado, Michigan, Connecticut, Florida:** Additional incumbents (e.g., Rep. Diana DeGette, Rep. Shri Thanedar, Rep. Cory Mills) were ousted by progressive or younger challengers.  

## Historical Context
- The 1992 election cycle previously held the record for retirements (65 House members, 9 senators).  
- The 2026 wave is driven by:  
  - Unpopularity of the incumbent Trump administration.  
  - A Democratic push for younger, more diverse candidates.  
  - Early retirement announcements, many occurring earlier than in past cycles.  

## Expected Political Impact
- Republicans, despite leading in the number of retirements, are expected to lose ground in the midterms, following the typical pattern of the party in power.  
- Redistricting (e.g., Tennessee’s elimination of a Memphis‑based seat) has reshaped several races, contributing to incumbent defeats.  
- The final composition of Congress could shift further if additional incumbents lose in November’s general election.
