# A Complex Legacy: Unpacking The Life of Sarah Forbes Bonetta

• Born in 1843 in Òkè-Òdàn, Nigeria, Sarah Forbes Bonetta (known as Àìná) was kidnapped by King Ghezo's forces and held at his court as a young girl.
• Her family was killed in 1848, and she was taken to England, where she was adopted by Frederick Forbes, a member of the Royal Navy's West Africa Squadron.
• In 1850, Forbes petitioned King Ghezo to sign an anti-slavery treaty, and in the process, he became friends with Queen Victoria and obtained permission to bring Bonetta back to England under the guise of a diplomatic gift.
• The book "Becoming Sarah Forbes Bonetta: A Yorùbá Woman in Queen Victoria's Court" is the first to extensively explore this complex relationship and the events that led to Bonetta's life in England.
• As a member of queen's court, Bonetta was married off to a wealthy man, and later gave birth to a son, Prince Frederick Louis.
• However, this domestic arrangement was actually a move by Queen Victoria to secure Bonetta's status as a "diplomatic gift" from an African king, and her ultimate control over her life.
• After Prince Frederick Louis' death, Bonetta was effectively indentured to Queen Victoria, with her being declared the monarch's property, and her marriage and life subject to the monarch's approval.
• Despite having agency, Bonetta was never truly free, and her complex life in England was a strategic move by the British monarchy.
• The life of Sarah Forbes Bonetta remains a testament to the complex and often fraught history of the British imperial project and its interactions with African cultures.
