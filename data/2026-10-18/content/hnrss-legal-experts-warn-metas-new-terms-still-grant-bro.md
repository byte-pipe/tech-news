## Legal experts warn Meta’s new terms still grant broad scraping permissions, despite language changes

Allowing scraped content to be used for all “advertising and business products” is a substantial expansion of Meta’s permissions.

 

Meta-owned platforms include Facebook, WhatsApp, Oculus, Threads, Instagram, and Messenger. 
(Adobe Stock)

By 
Andrew Deck
 
and
 
Hanaa' Tameez

 Oct. 17, 2026, 2:49 p.m. 

Meta has removed the word “train” from its upcomingterms of servicefor business accounts. The company revised its terms after Nieman Lab’s story on Thursdayexposedthat the new terms would permit broad scraping of websites “associated with” business accounts, and possibly allow Meta to use that scraped content to train its AI models.

In its revision, published Friday afternoon, Meta removed the word “train” from a list of allowed use cases and limited those uses to “advertising and business products,” rather than all Meta products. But the revised terms will still allow for broad scraping of websites, potentially superseding robots.txt files, according to legal experts.

On Saturday, a Meta spokesperson said the company revised the terms because “it wasn’t clear in the initial consolidation of terms that robots.txt preferences are respected. News sites, like all other content owners, still have the ability to disable the crawler via robots.txt flags. That has been the case, and it has not changed.”

Meta has not included any specific language about respecting robots.txt preferences in its revised terms of services.The new terms are slated to go into effect on October 30. There is no option for business accounts to opt out of the new terms before then.

RELATED ARTICLEMeta’s new commercial terms will allow the scraping of news sites, legal experts sayAndrew DeckOctober 15, 2026“I think publishers should remain concerned,” saidMatt Topic, a media and intellectual property attorney at Loevy & Loevy. “The revised version of the new terms still allows Meta to use that scraped content to develop any ‘business product.’ That doesn’t seem to be a defined term, and presumably Meta is developing AI business products. I think ‘develop’ is broad enough to include ‘train.'”

Meta currently lists examples of its business products as “ads, Meta Business Tools and Meta Business Suite.”

Virtually every news organization has at least one Meta business account, which allows companies to manage Facebook Pages, Threads accounts, Instagram profiles, and WhatsApp channels from one place.

those preferences.”Meta’s current terms permit it to scrape websites associated with business accounts, but only in a very specific scenario. That language is found in its terms of service for when accounts useMeta’s generative AI features to run advertisements. The scraped content can only be used to “optimize your ads.”Adding this language to more general terms of service, and allowing scraped content to be used for all “advertising and business products,” is a substantial expansion of Meta’s scraping permissions.A Meta spokesperson said the company is incorporating its “Ad Creative Generative AI Terms” into its general commercial terms to make them easier to understand.Three legal experts Nieman Lab consulted were skeptical about the revised terms of service and Meta’s most recent statements.“[The update] doesn’t address my concerns,” saidJames Grimmelmann, a professor of digital and information law at Cornell Law School. “While it adds more language about the permitted uses, [the] ‘develop…advertising and business products’ [language] is still broad enough to drive a truck through. This does nothing to restrict the ‘associated with your products or services’ [language] that would let Meta scrape entire sites.”University of Wisconsin law professorBenjamin Sobelexplained that robots.txt indicates a website’s preferences, but compliance with those is completely voluntary. He likened it to a homeowner putting up a sign saying, “Don’t look at my house.” Putting up that sign doesn’t give the homeowner an enforceable legal right to stop people from looking at the house.“You can still express the preference, and unless it’s a legally enforceable preference, it can still be ignored all the same,” he said.RELATED ARTICLEFederal appeals court upholds Thomson
