# Bespoke DNA vaccine offers hope for treatment of notorious brain cancer

## Key points
- An early‑phase clinical trial tested a personalized DNA vaccine in patients with glioblastoma, a deadly brain tumour with a median survival of less than 18 months.  
- The vaccine was administered safely and succeeded in activating the patients’ immune systems against the tumour.  
- The study demonstrates the feasibility of a bespoke, patient‑specific immunotherapy approach for this aggressive cancer.  
- Findings are reported in Nature Cancer (doi: 10.1038/s43018‑026‑01163‑w, 2026).  

## Context
- Glioblastoma remains one of the most challenging cancers, with current therapies offering limited extension of life expectancy.  
- Tailoring vaccines to each individual’s tumour antigens aims to overcome the tumour’s heterogeneity and immune evasion mechanisms.  

## Implications
- Successful immune stimulation suggests that DNA‑based, personalized vaccines could become a component of multimodal treatment strategies for glioblastoma.  
- Further trials are needed to assess long‑term efficacy, optimal dosing, and combination with other therapies.
