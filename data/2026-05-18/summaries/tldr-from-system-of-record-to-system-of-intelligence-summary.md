# From “System of Record” to “System of Intelligence”

## Overview
- The article argues that the next wave of enterprise value will shift from owning the **system of record (SoR)**—traditionally the CRM database—to owning the **system of intelligence (SoI)** that sits above the database and orchestrates AI agents.
- This reasoning layer treats the database as infrastructure, providing context, actions, and orchestration for sales and GTM workflows.

## Evolution of the CRM
- Historically, the CRM (e.g., Salesforce, HubSpot) has been the “sticky” asset for go‑to‑market software because it accumulates valuable data (call notes, pricing, contacts) that creates high switching costs.
- The CRM’s value has been compared to a “Cadillac” prize; the database is where most of the value resides.
- As AI tools become widespread, CRM usage is actually **rising** because agents enrich the data, making the system more useful for reps.

## From Friend Graph to News Feed Analogy
- Social media’s friend graph was once the core asset; the news feed later became the primary consumer layer, using the graph as just one input.
- Similarly, the CRM is moving from being the core product to becoming an input for AI‑driven “newsfeed” experiences that surface context and recommended actions.

## The Rise of AI Agents and Orchestration
- AI agents now perform tasks such as:
  - Researching 10‑Ks and earnings calls before meetings.
  - Coaching reps on objections in real time.
  - Listening to calls and automatically writing structured notes into the CRM.
- These agents view the CRM simply as a **structured data source**, not as a UI‑centric application.
- Orchestration—pulling signals from CRM, calendar, email, Slack, billing, telemetry, etc.—becomes the new “gravity well” that keeps users within a platform.

## Implications for Switching Costs
- Switching costs will transition from “all our data is in Salesforce” to “all our workflows, reasoning, and institutional context live in our AI layer.”
- The SoI will become the primary lock‑in mechanism, while the SoR remains a backend infrastructure component.

## Outlook
- Companies that build robust reasoning/orchestration layers on top of existing databases are poised to capture the majority of enterprise GTM software value in the coming decade.
- Traditional SoR incumbents (Salesforce, HubSpot) are already adding AI‑first, API‑first features, but the strategic focus is shifting toward the intelligence layer that consumes the database.
