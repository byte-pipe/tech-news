# AI IPOs and Frontier AI as a Prisoner's Dilemma

* Paul Kedrosky

### What Happened

After last week's outbreak of"Hey maybe unfettered AI advances will kill us all", and a subsequentindustry coalescencearound Anthropic head Amodei;'s idea of perhaps, you know,slowing down a tad, there is a new and predictable argument making the rounds in the AI safety debate. It says that if frontier labs like OpenAI and Anthropic really believe frontier models are dangerous, they should simplyslow down themselves.

No cartel. No special antitrust treatment. No new regulatory regime. If themodels are scaryenough to justify slowing the frontier, then thefrontier labs should just do it, perhaps because they are trying tolimit litigation risk. Anything else, to this way of thinking, starts to look likeregulatory capture dressed up in an AI safety frock.

Frontier labs do, of course, have obvious incentives to support rules that make life harder for smaller competitors, and “safety” can become a convenient way to turn incumbency into regulation.But that doesn't mean all calls for safer AI are thinly-disguised anti-competitiom.

### What It Means

The problem is thatAI safety is almost definitionally a collective-action problem.

OpenAI may prefera world in which every frontier lab slows down.Anthropicmay prefer the same thing. (Let's not bring Musk's xAi into this, because, trust me, it just makes it even harder.) Neither prefers a world in which it slows while everyone else keeps racing, hiring its researchers, taking its customers and widening the capability gap.

That is not hypocrisy. It is anequilibrium problem,straight out ofJohn Nash.
