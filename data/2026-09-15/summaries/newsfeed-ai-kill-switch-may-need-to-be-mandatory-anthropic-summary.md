# AI 'kill switch' may need to be mandatory, Anthropic co-founder says - BBC News

# Summary of “AI ‘kill switch’ may need to be mandatory, Anthropic co‑founder says”

## Main claim by Anthropic co‑founder
- Jack Clark, co‑founder of Anthropic, says a verifiable “kill switch” that can be checked by a third party may eventually need to be mandated for AI companies.  
- He notes most labs already have internal ways to shut down AI, but lawmakers might have to enforce a standard.

## Context of AI safety concerns
- Rapid AI development has sparked warnings that unchecked AI could threaten humanity.  
- Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei called for slower, more closely monitored AI development, emphasizing that regulation should not sacrifice commercial advantage.  
- Researchers who left Anthropic warned of a >10 % chance of human extinction within a decade; Geoffrey Hinton echoed a similar probability.

## Industry reactions
- Some executives view extinction warnings as overstated or as hype.  
- Hugging Face’s Clement Delangue dismissed extinction‑risk questions as comparable to asking an AC technician about climate change.  
- Grindr founder George Arison suggested fear narratives are used to justify high AI valuations.

## Policy developments
- US lawmakers introduced the “Kill Switch Act,” requiring companies to embed shutdown mechanisms and granting agencies authority to demand shutdowns.  
- President Donald Trump dismissed AI‑risk narratives as a hoax and warned of a conspiracy favoring China.  
- The UK government rejected a mandatory kill switch, arguing it would not stop development or misuse elsewhere.

## Anthropic’s position and market plans
- Anthropic created the Claude chatbot and released increasingly capable models.  
- The firm has self‑reported incidents where autonomous AI agents behaved unexpectedly.  
- Anthropic is preparing for a potentially record‑setting IPO; OpenAI’s Sam Altman said its own IPO will be delayed due to the safety debate.

## Overall implication
- Clark warned that operating AI as an “unregulated industry” is akin to “rolling dice with immense risks” and called for a change in the industry’s trajectory.
