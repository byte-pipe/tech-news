# Summary of “Offensively cheap”: Solar power is looking up

## Key developments
- Bestway Cement in Chakwal, Pakistan, is adding 6.34 MW of solar capacity, joining 26 MW already installed; solar now supplies over a quarter of the plant’s electricity.  
- Global solar photovoltaic capacity reached almost 1.2 TW by the end of 2025, roughly three times the world’s nuclear fleet.  
- The dramatic price drop of panels—from $5‑$6 per watt a decade ago to about 12 cents per watt today—has been driven by massive Chinese production (≈1.36 TW).  

## Economic impact
- Cheap panels enable businesses and households worldwide to generate their own power, reducing reliance on grid electricity and lowering energy bills.  
- In Pakistan, Brazil, South Africa, the Philippines and other emerging markets, solar is becoming a primary source where power is scarce or expensive.  
- Africa is projected to install about 17 GW of new solar this year, mostly small‑scale installations at factories and businesses.  
- In Kenya, solar loans light homes dramatically, replacing kerosene lanterns with bright, reliable lighting.  
- India’s subsidized rooftop program aims to equip over 5 million homes, adding roughly 500 000 installations per month.  
- The UK and several US states now allow “plug‑and‑play” panels to be sold in supermarkets and DIY stores, offering quick, low‑cost options for households.  

## Grid and regulatory challenges
- Rapid growth of distributed solar changes power‑system behavior, creating forecasting and management difficulties for utilities.  
- Spain’s 2022 blackout highlighted the need for grid adaptation to high renewable shares.  
- Utilities worry about reduced electricity sales and who will fund grid maintenance when more customers generate their own power.  
- Distribution networks may lack capacity to handle large inbound flows from rooftop and commercial solar installations.  

## Regional case studies
- **Pakistan:** Bestway Cement’s solar adoption; K‑Electric’s mixed view of rooftop solar as both “curse and blessing.”  
- **Philippines:** Rooftop solar generation reached 372 GWh in the first half of the year; payback period now just over three years.  
- **South Africa:** Shoprite’s 43 MW of rooftop and truck solar capacity threatens Eskom’s revenue base.  
- **United Kingdom:** Post‑Iran‑Ukraine war energy concerns boost consumer interest in cheap plug‑in panels.  
- **United States:** States like New Jersey exempt plug‑in panels from metering, encouraging adoption despite federal policy shifts.  

## Outlook
- Continued decline in panel costs is expected to spur further decentralised generation, especially in sun‑rich developing regions.  
- Grid operators will need substantial investment and new pricing models to accommodate bidirectional power flows and maintain reliability.  
- The “democratization of electricity” is reshaping energy markets, making solar a central component of both industrial and residential power strategies worldwide.
