# AI IPOs and Frontier AI as a Prisoner's Dilemma

## What Happened
- Recent AI‑safety debate revived after a “hey maybe unfettered AI advances will kill us all” alarm and Anthropic’s call for a modest slowdown.
- A common argument: if frontier labs (e.g., OpenAI, Anthropic) truly view frontier models as dangerous, they should voluntarily slow themselves down without needing cartels, antitrust exemptions, or new regulations.
- Critics claim that calls for regulation can be a way for incumbents to protect their market position, but not every safety appeal is merely anti‑competition.

## What It Means
- AI safety is fundamentally a collective‑action problem: each lab benefits when all competitors also slow down.
- OpenAI, Anthropic, and others would each prefer a world where every frontier lab eases the pace, avoiding a race that widens the capability gap.
- No lab wants to be the only one that slows while rivals continue to race, hire talent, and capture customers.
- This situation mirrors a Nash equilibrium, not hypocrisy: the optimal outcome requires coordinated restraint, which is hard to achieve unilaterally.
