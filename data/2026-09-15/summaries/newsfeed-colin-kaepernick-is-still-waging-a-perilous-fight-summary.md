# Colin Kaepernick is still waging a “Perilous Fight,” 10 years after taking a knee  

## Background  
- Adopted as a baby into a white family and grew up in a nearly all‑white town.  
- At age 8 he was called the n‑word and threatened with a lynching‑style attack; he never told his parents, but the incident shaped his sense of Black identity.  
- Football became a space to explore his Blackness; teammates and their mothers shared experiences of racism and offered guidance on dealing with police.  
- Played college football at the University of Nevada, Reno, and was drafted by the San Francisco 49ers in 2011.  

## Protest and Aftermath  
- After the fatal shootings of Mario Woods, Alton Sterling, and Philando Castile, Kaepernick began protesting police violence.  
- At the start of the 2016 season he knelt during the national anthem, saying he could not show pride in systems that “continue to kill us, target us, and discriminate against us.”  
- The protest sparked intense criticism; he never played another NFL game after the 2016 season.  
- Filed a grievance alleging collusion by NFL owners; the case settled in 2019.  

## The Perilous Fight memoir  
- In his new memoir, *The Perilous Fight*, Kaepernick retraces his journey to the NFL and explains why he still hopes to return.  
- He argues the league tried to punish him for protesting and vows to change that narrative.  

## Interview highlights  

### Player autonomy and NFL culture  
- Kaepernick recounts a teammate saying, “I need to ask my owner,” illustrating the NFL’s expectation of obedience and the fear players have of losing what they’ve earned.  

### Media coverage  
- He believes media focus on “respect for the flag” and “proper protest methods” distracted from the core issue of police brutality.  
- Acknowledges some journalists covered the protest’s intent faithfully while exposing broader media manipulation.  

### NFL’s response  
- Describes the league’s reaction as revealing its political stance against calls to stop police killings of Black people.  

### Public perception and personal passion  
- Counters the narrative that he no longer wants to play; he affirms his love for football and ongoing pursuit of a career.  

### Views on youth football  
- Cites concerns about chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE); would delay his own child’s participation in contact football until high school.
