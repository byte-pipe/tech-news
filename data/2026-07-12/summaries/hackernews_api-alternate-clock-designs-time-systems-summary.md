## The 24‑hour clock
- Preference for a single‑cycle display over AM/PM.  
- Hour hand makes one full rotation per day; minute hand provides needed accuracy.

## The decimal (base 10) clock
- Divides a day into 10 hours, each hour into 100 minutes, each minute into 100 seconds.  
- Seconds are 86.4 % of a conventional second, making them shorter.  
- Reading the time is straightforward; calculations involve simple decimal shifts.

## The binary (base 2) clock
- Intended for enthusiasts of binary representation.  
- Each “binary second” spans three conventional hours, so the clock updates only every three hours, limiting accuracy.

## The hexadecimal (base 16) clock
- Uses 16 hours, 128 minutes, and 128 seconds.  
- A hexadecimal second is one‑third of a conventional second.  
- Hour labels range from 1 to F.

## The 36‑hour (0‑degree) clock
- Features 36 hours, 60 minutes, and 60 seconds.  
- With more hours, minutes and seconds are two‑thirds the length of standard units.
