## Background
- Norway, with a population similar to Scotland, has become a football powerhouse at the 2026 World Cup.  
- The team reached the quarter‑finals, beating Ivory Coast and Brazil, and will face England.  
- Scotland, despite a similar size, exited at the group stage, highlighting a widening gap between the nations.

## Key Factors of Success
- Two‑decade strategic plan initiated by the Norwegian Football Federation (NFF).  
- Investment in artificial pitches (2000‑2010) and a coaching revolution through the National Team School (NTS).  
- Strong economic base, partly funded by oil wealth, and a unique sports‑funding model using gambling proceeds.

## Investment in Facilities
- Over 5,000 artificial pitches built and 586 renovated between 2016‑2025.  
- Year‑round playing conditions transformed football from a summer sport to a year‑round activity.  
- In 2026, state‑owned Norsk Tipping contributed more than 2 bn NOK (£152.7 m) to sports facilities.

## National Team School (NTS)
- Established in 2013 after Norway missed Euro 2012.  
- Not a centralized academy; it links grassroots clubs, districts, top clubs, and the federation.  
- 14 of the 15 players in the 2‑1 win over Brazil had been part of the NTS pathway.  
- Emphasises collective responsibility: “Everyone’s in it together.”

## Player Development Philosophy
- Children stay with grassroots clubs until about age 12, avoiding early academy selection.  
- Focus on love for the game and personal ownership rather than pure physical metrics.  
- Examples:  
  - Erling Haaland entered national talent camps at 14, later becoming a world‑class striker.  
  - Martin Ødegaard identified at 11, inspired the NTS ethos, signed by Real Madrid at 16.  
- Core values taught: safety, security, togetherness, and team‑first mentality.

## World Cup Performance
- 17 of the 26‑man squad play in Europe’s top four leagues (Premier League, Bundesliga, La Liga, Serie A).  
- Success attributed to technical style fostered by artificial surfaces and unified development system.  
- Team’s unity symbolised by Viking rowing displays and a squad photo in their first‑club kits.

## Impact on Domestic League
- Only four players in the World Cup squad play for Norwegian clubs; three from Bodø/Glimt, whose Champions League run hints at league growth.  
- NFF goal: produce and sell talent to major leagues while strengthening the domestic competition.  
- Manager Ståle Solbakken notes a balanced squad age range and credits club and federation effort.

## Future Outlook
- The NTS model aims to sustain Norway’s status as a football nation and continue feeding talent to top European leagues.  
- Continued investment in facilities and the collaborative development structure are seen as essential for long‑term success.
