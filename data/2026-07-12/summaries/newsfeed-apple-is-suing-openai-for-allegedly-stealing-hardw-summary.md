# Apple Is Suing OpenAI for Allegedly Stealing Hardware Secrets

## Background of the Lawsuit
- Apple filed a complaint in U.S. District Court in San Jose against OpenAI and its chief hardware officer Tang Tan.  
- The suit alleges that OpenAI obtained Apple trade secrets, including unreleased parts, prototypes, confidential designs, and documents about stealth projects.  
- Apple claims OpenAI encouraged departing Apple employees to bring proprietary technology and coached them on evading Apple’s security protocols.

## Alleged Actions by Tang Tan and OpenAI
- Tan, who spent 24 years at Apple overseeing iPhone product design, allegedly told job candidates to bring actual Apple components (batteries, logic boards, shields) to “show‑and‑tell” interview sessions.  
- He is accused of providing an internal Apple security‑procedure document to departing workers, advising them on how to hide their exit, retain system access, and avoid signing exit paperwork.  
- Tan reportedly instructed recruits on how to bypass Apple’s data‑security measures and to bring confidential Apple parts to OpenAI interviews.

## Other Defendants and Evidence
- Former Apple engineer Chang Liu is named; the lawsuit says he downloaded dozens of confidential hardware files and coached another Apple employee on copying files without detection.  
- Apple’s investigation used data and messages collected from employee devices, discovering Liu retained access to Apple’s internal file‑sharing system via a bug that has since been fixed.  
- io Products, acquired by OpenAI for $6.5 billion and co‑founded by former Apple executives (including Tan, Scott Cannon, Evans Hankey, and Jony Ive), is also named as a defendant.

## Supplier Misconduct Claims
- OpenAI’s io unit allegedly approached at least two Apple suppliers, misrepresenting that Apple had approved projects, to replicate a trade‑secret metal‑finishing technique and to gather battery‑related information.  

## Context of the Apple‑OpenAI Relationship
- The companies partnered in 2024 to distribute ChatGPT on iPhones, MacBooks, and iPads.  
- The partnership has soured; Apple is increasingly relying on Google’s Gemini AI for its in‑house models.  
- Both firms are expected to compete fiercely in the emerging market for AI‑powered consumer devices.

## OpenAI’s Response
- Spokesperson Drew Pusateri said OpenAI has “no interest in other companies’ trade secrets” and is focused on building innovative technology.  
- OpenAI has hired more than 400 former Apple employees and plans to launch its first hardware products no earlier than April 2027, including an AI‑powered tabletop puck.

## Legal Demands
- Apple seeks an injunction to stop the alleged theft, monetary damages, and the return of any pilfered property and data.  

## Potential Significance
- The case could become the most high‑profile Silicon Valley intellectual‑property dispute since Waymo’s 2017 lawsuit against Uber, which settled for $245 million.
