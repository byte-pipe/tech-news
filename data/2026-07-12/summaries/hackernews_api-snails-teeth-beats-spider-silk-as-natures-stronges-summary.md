# Summary of “Snails’ Teeth Beats Spider Silk As Nature’s Strongest Material”

## Main Findings
- Marine limpets can clamp onto rocks with 75 psi using their muscular foot and secretions.  
- Their radula (tooth‑studded tongue) contains teeth that are, on average, five times stronger than most spider silk.  
- Laboratory tests show the teeth can endure pressures that would convert carbon into diamond.  
- A single tooth could support roughly 3,300 one‑pound bags of sugar, comparable to a strand of spaghetti holding that load.

## Material Composition
- The teeth consist of goethite (an iron‑containing crystal) nanofibers embedded in a protein matrix.  
- This natural composite gives the teeth exceptional tensile strength, surpassing Kevlar and approaching the performance of high‑quality carbon fibers.

    - *Lonsdaleite* (hexagonal carbon) formed from meteorite impacts, about 58 % stronger than diamond.  
- The snail tooth material ranks above Kevlar and is on par with the best carbon fibers.

## Potential Applications
