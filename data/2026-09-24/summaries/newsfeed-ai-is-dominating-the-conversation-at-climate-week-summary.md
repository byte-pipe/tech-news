# AI is dominating the conversation at Climate Week | MIT Technology Review

**AI and Climate: A Intersection of Concerns**

This week at the UN General Assembly, world leaders have converged on Manhattan for Climate Week, bringing together investors, policymakers, advocates, and journalists. The pressing issue of climate change is front and center, bringing to the forefront the intersection of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and climate.

**Key Points:**

* The UN General Assembly and Climate Week highlight the growing tension between AI's potential benefits for climate research and its environmental impacts.
* Funding and attention from Big Tech companies are flowing into energy startups, but there is also concern about the emissions-heavy natural gas buildout needed to meet energy demand.
* The world is facing increased pressure to meet its climate goals, with a report by the UN Environment Program indicating that the target of keeping warming below 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels may no longer be achievable.
* AI can help address the emissions problem by supporting renewable energy and energy efficiency, but also by facilitating carbon removal and grid resilience.

**In Focus: Key Developments and Outcomes**

* **Increased investment in energy technologies**: $26 billion in global climate-tech investment, a 55% increase from last year, with companies like Google, Meta, and others investing in data centers powered by AI and energy solutions.
* **Missing targets**: The world has slipped out of reaching the 1.5°C target due to the missed deadlines, emphasizing the urgency of addressing climate change.
* **Sectoral implications**: Carbon management and low-carbon fuels are crucial solutions for addressing climate change, but are seeing less investment than expected.
* **Challenges and opportunities**: AI can help address emissions, facilitate energy efficiency, and support carbon removal, but also carries risks if not managed responsibly.
