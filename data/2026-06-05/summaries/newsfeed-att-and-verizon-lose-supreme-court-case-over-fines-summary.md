# AT&T and Verizon Lose Supreme Court Case Over Fines for Selling Location Data
## Summary

The US Supreme Court has ruled that the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) can impose fines on telecommunications companies like AT&T and Verizon without granting them a jury trial, as they did in 2024 over violations related to selling users' location data without consent. 

## Key Points

* The High Court accepted the FCC's argument that carriers could have obtained jury trials if they refused to pay the fines.
* Carriers challenged the fines through circuit appeals courts, where judges ruled on their cases but ultimately fell short of achieving a full refund of payments (estimated at $104 million in total).
* Chief Justice John Roberts wrote the majority opinion, citing FCC forfeiture proceedings as being consistent with Seventh Amendment precedents.
* The court's decision rejects carriers' claim that fines imposed by the government must be granted a jury trial.

## Facts

* AT&T and Verizon were fined a total of $104 million by the Federal Communications Commission in 2024 for violating new location data rules.
* The companies challenged their fines in circuit appeals courts, where judges ruled on their cases, but ultimately the FCC deemed them non-binding until enforcement through court action.
* The Supreme Court accepted the government's argument that they can impose punishments directly due to nonbinding forfeitures.

## Context

The decision maintains the power of the FCC while reaffirming its ability to investigate and propose penalties for infractions. However, it also serves as an important safeguard against punitive actions by regulatory agencies.
