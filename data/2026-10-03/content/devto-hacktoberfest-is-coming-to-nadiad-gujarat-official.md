Organized entirely by a solo student

Most students hear about hackathons, open source and developer communities in theirthird or fourth year. By then, a lot of the best chances to use them are gone.

I'm a final-year student at Dharmsinh Desai University in Nadiad, and this October I'm doing something about that.

## 🎉 I'm bringing Hacktoberfest to Nadiad.

🎟️ Register for free

## 🪞 Why I'm doing this

I spent my entire first year doing what I thought I was supposed to do: attending lectures, reading theory, giving exams, and scoring a 9+ CPI.

I had never heard of MLH, DEV or Hacktoberfest. Not once.

I found all three only in the last 3-4 months, and it changed how I see college. I started writing on DEV, built a presence there, won theGitHub Finish-Up-A-Thonchallenge, became aDEV Trusted Member, and now I'm organizing an MLH-approved event. And I'm not even a member of any college club. It's just me, trying to make sure you don't miss this like I did.

Two of the things we'll cover, open-source AI models and running Claude Code on free open models, weren't even a thing when I was a first-year. So this session is made of everything I wish I'd had back then.

## 🌍 What is Hacktoberfest?

Hacktoberfest is a month-long global celebration of open source, run every October byMajor League Hacking (MLH)with partners including DigitalOcean and DEV. This year the theme isopen-source AI, and hundreds of local events (called Fests) are happening around the world.

Ours is one of them, listed on the official Hacktoberfest site:
