# Californian accused of shipping $300M worth of Nvidia chips to China without Uncle Sam’s approval

## Key Facts
- Greg Lui, 38, a California business owner, was arrested on Thursday and charged with illegally exporting Nvidia hardware to China.  
- The alleged value of the hardware is about $300 million and includes high‑end GPUs such as A100, H100, PNY GeForce RTX 4090 and GeForce RTX 5090.  
- The hardware was ordered through Lui’s company, Earthmade Computer Inc., and shipped to Malaysia and Singapore, where no U.S. export license is required, before being forwarded to China.  

## Alleged Scheme
- The operation began around October 2023 and continued until at least 12 August 2026.  
- Lui received more than $176 million from two Malaysian transshipment firms in exchange for introducing them to U.S. suppliers of Nvidia equipment.  
- Export documents show 92 servers sent from San Francisco International Airport to Kuala Lumpur, with the final consignee listed as a Chinese customer in Hong Kong.  
- Prosecutors say Lui directed a U.S. front company to falsify shipment records, listing “Jackie Lui, CEO of Topmost” (a California‑registered firm) as the recipient.  
- In 2021, Lui allegedly purchased false identity documents that were used to conceal the true destination of the shipments.  

## Legal Charges and Potential Penalty
- One count each of violations of the Export Control Reform Act and the Export Administration Regulations.  
- Additional counts for outbound smuggling and money laundering.  
- The combined offenses carry a maximum possible sentence of 50 years in federal prison.  

## Official Statements
- John A. Eisenberg, Assistant Attorney General for National Security, emphasized the importance of protecting “super intelligence” technology from adversaries.  
- Roman Rozhavsky, Assistant Director of the FBI’s Counterintelligence and Espionage Division, described the operation as selling “hundreds of millions of dollars’ worth of American Super Intelligence technology” to the Chinese government.  

## Responses and Comments
- The Register reached out to Earthmade Computer Inc. and Nvidia for comment; no responses were received at the time of publication.  
- The case underscores the U.S. government’s focus on preventing advanced AI hardware from reaching rival nations and reinforces ongoing enforcement of export‑control laws.
