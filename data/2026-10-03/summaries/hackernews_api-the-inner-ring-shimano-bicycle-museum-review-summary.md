# The Inner Ring | Shimano Bicycle Museum Review

## Overview
- The museum is a modern glass‑and‑steel building in Sakai, near Osaka and Kyoto, focused on bicycles rather than Shimano’s corporate story.  
- Bilingual signage (Japanese and English) greets visitors with a welcome message about the many ways to enjoy cycling.

## Exhibits
- **Chronology Hall (upstairs, darkened room)**
  - Spotlit display of bicycles from hobby horses to modern machines.  
  - Functions as an auditorium; educational films cover bicycle history, sport, war, industrialisation, and consumerism.  
- **Bright White Room**
  - Open‑access bikes on small stages: Freddy Maerten’s steel Flandria, early pro‑peloton Shimano Crane rear mech, Paralympic‑modified bikes, a Breezer mountain bike with a Campagnolo headset, etc.  
  - Humdrum examples: Swedish postal Crescent bike with studded tires, dust‑covered Cannondale from a 55,000 km world tour, scratched 2021 Olympic Merida road bike, various kids and shopping bikes.  
- **Materials & Weight Stations**
  - Hands‑on comparison of steel, aluminium, and titanium tubing.  
  - Opportunity to lift a racing bike and feel its lightness.  
- **Component Displays**
  – Exploded view of a Dura‑Ace groupset (≈3,200 parts).  
  – Few non‑Shimano parts (e.g., Campagnolo headset, Mavic rear mech).  
- **Flagship Groupset Gallery (top floor)**
  – Sequential displays of Dura‑Ace and XTR groupsets from 1973 to 2021, with empty cases reserved for future models (2027 onward).  

## Shimano History
- Minimal coverage: brief note that Sakai’s gun blacksmiths repaired imported bikes in the late 19th century, later producing Shimano’s first product, a freewheel.  
- Mention of founder Shozaburo Shimano and the link to fishing gear, but no fishing equipment is shown.  

## Facilities
- Library with extensive books, brochures, and catalogues; a locked archive for researchers.  
- No gift shop; visitors can wander freely after the tour.  

## Verdict
- An enjoyable celebration of bicycles that puts the machine first; very little corporate promotion.  
- Provides a comprehensive visual history, with most bikes accessible for close inspection.  
- Compared favorably to smaller hobby‑type bicycle museums; spacious (4,223 m²) and peaceful.  

## Access
- Located in Sakai, ~15 km south of Osaka.  
- About a 30‑minute train ride from Osaka Central Station to Sakai or Sakaihigashi stations (the latter nearer the museum).  
- Bike parking available.  

## Admission
- Listed price: JPY 500 (under €3).  
- Possible price increase noted, but still inexpensive even if doubled.
