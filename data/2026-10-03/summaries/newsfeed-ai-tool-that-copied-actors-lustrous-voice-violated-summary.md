# AI tool that copied actor’s ‘lustrous’ voice violated his rights, Tokyo court rules  

## Main ruling  
- Tokyo District Court declared that a human voice is protected under publicity rights in Japan.  
- The court held that unauthorised use of a performer’s voice can infringe those rights, likening a voice to a portrait as a symbol of personality.  
- The judge dismissed Tsuda’s request to have TikTok remove the videos because the user deleted them after the suit was filed.  

## Parties involved  
- **Plaintiff:** Kenjiro Tsuda, voice actor known for Kento Nanami in *Jujutsu Kaisen*.  
- **Defendant:** TikTok, represented by the anonymous account that posted the AI‑generated videos.  
- **Judge:** Aya Takahashi.  

## Facts of the case  
- An anonymous TikTok account uploaded 188 videos between July 2024 and September 2023, using AI‑generated speech that sounded “deep” and “lustrous,” matching Tsuda’s baritone.  
- The account paired the voice with images of urban legends, occult topics, and conspiracy theories, and its avatar resembled Tsuda’s anime character.  
- At its peak the account had over 200,000 subscribers and was estimated to earn ¥500,000–¥75,000 per month.  
- TikTok argued the voice was a “generic male voice” and that any similarity was subjective.  

## Industry context  
- This is the first Japanese lawsuit seeking protection of vocal identity against AI cloning.  
- Voice actors in Japan, whose fame often rests on vocal performance, fear AI mimicry threatens their livelihoods.  
- The Japan Actors Union highlighted that a voice reflects years of training and apprenticeship.  
- In 2024, Japanese actors launched the “No More” campaign to oppose unauthorised AI use of their faces and voices.  

## Reactions  
- Tsuda’s legal team described the TikTok content as “sordid” and argued the account’s popularity stemmed from cloning his “attractive” voice.  
- TikTok said it would respect the judgment while reviewing the ruling’s details, emphasizing a balance between technology development and rights protection.  

## Legal significance  
- The decision extends publicity rights to vocal characteristics when used commercially to exploit an individual’s appeal.  
- It sets a precedent for future disputes over AI‑generated reproductions of personal attributes in Japan.
