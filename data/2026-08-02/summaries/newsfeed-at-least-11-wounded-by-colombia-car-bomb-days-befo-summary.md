# At least 11 wounded by Colombia car bomb days before presidential election

## Incident details
- A car bomb exploded in Cúcuta, a city on the Colombian‑Venezuelan border, on Saturday.  
- The blast injured eight police officers and three civilians, totaling at least 11 wounded.  
- No group claimed responsibility; active actors in the area include the ELN, dissident FARC factions, and the criminal clan Clan del Golfo.  

## Official reactions
- Security secretary George Quintero reported the injuries to the press.  
- President‑elect Abelardo de la Espriella condemned the attack on X, calling it “cowardly terrorist” and stating that terrorism will not intimidate Colombia or undermine its pursuit of peace and security.  

## Political and security context
- Colombia has endured a six‑decade internal armed conflict involving left‑wing rebels, right‑wing paramilitaries, government forces and criminal organisations.  
- The country’s status as the world’s top cocaine producer has been a central issue in recent elections.  
- The election campaign was marked by political violence, including bombings and drone attacks attributed to rebel groups.  
- In 2025, right‑wing candidate Miguel Uribe Turbay was assassinated; his mother, journalist Diana Turbay, was killed in 1991 during the conflict.  

## President‑elect’s platform
- De la Espriella, a millionaire lawyer with no prior public office, promises a hard‑line crackdown on rebels and drug‑trafficking networks.  
- His campaign received backing from the United States under former President Donald Trump, who promotes a militarised “narcoterrorist” approach.  
- He pledges to deepen Colombia’s military alliance with the U.S., build megaprisons, and order the Air Force to shoot down drug‑laden planes and the navy to sink drug‑laden boats.  

## Transition to office
- De la Espriella is scheduled to assume the presidency on 7 August.  
- He had considered being sworn in at a military garrison in Cauca, but the plan was not approved; the inauguration will take place at Bogotá City Hall with approximately 11,000 soldiers and police officers in attendance.
