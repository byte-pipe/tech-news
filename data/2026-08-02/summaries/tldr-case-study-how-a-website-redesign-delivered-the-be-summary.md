# Case Study: How a website redesign delivered the best year ever for Churnet Valley Railway  

## Overview  
- CRE used a research‑led, data‑driven redesign process to turn the railway’s website into its biggest growth lever.  
- The project delivered the railway’s best year ever, sold‑out the Christmas event, and increased overall bookings.  

## Client Background  
- Churnet Valley Railway (CVR) is a heritage steam railway in the Staffordshire Moorlands, UK.  
- Attracts hundreds of thousands of visitors annually and holds a 4.8‑star Trustpilot rating.  
- Operating Director Greg Wilson wanted to grow bookings but feared a full redesign could jeopardise existing sales.  

## CRE’s Data‑Driven Redesign Strategy  
1. **Research first** – Identify real problems and highest‑impact opportunities before any design work.  
2. **Controlled A/B testing** – Every change runs as a test against the current page; winners are kept, losers are discarded.  
3. **Iterative learning** – Test results dictate the next page or element to tackle, creating a reusable template of proven design patterns.  

## Key Research Findings  
- Users described the existing site as “outdated,” “boring,” and “poor design,” while competitors were seen as “modern,” “fresh,” and “family‑friendly.”  
- Specific problem areas:  
  * Homepage highlighted a niche Whisky Experience, confusing the core audience.  
  * Dining listing page suffered from “Paradox of Choice”; < 25 % of visitors scrolled to the bottom.  
  * 23 % of exit‑survey respondents were “researching options,” indicating insufficient information on availability and dates.  
  * Repeated customer‑service questions (inclusions, group bookings, dietary needs) were not answered early enough on the site.  

## Project Plan (Template‑First Approach)  
1. Redesign and test the high‑traffic homepage.  
2. Use the winning homepage as a template for the next high‑value page (e.g., Breakfast Train booking page).  
3. Add new design patterns to the template library as needed, testing each new page.  
4. Cascade proven patterns across all high‑value pages, refining continuously.  
5. Apply insights to remaining site sections for ongoing growth.  

## Homepage Test (First High‑Impact Page)  
- **Control issues:** CTA for “Book tickets” had low visual weight; niche event placed above family‑friendly messaging.  
- **Variations introduced:**  
  * Hero section with embedded calendar showing available dates.  
  * Navigation updated to highlight primary visitor intents (Rides, Dining, Events).  
  * Menu items made clearer and more prominent.  
  * Redundant sections removed.  
  * Strong steam‑rail imagery placed front‑and‑center.  
- **Result:** +5 % conversion lift on the homepage, confirming the redesign approach and shifting focus to the next page.  

## Leveraging the Winning Pattern  
- The homepage’s successful elements (live calendar hero, clearer navigation, focused imagery) formed a reusable design pattern.  

### Breakfast Train Page (+10 % conversion)  
- Original page sent users to an external site for dates, creating friction.  
- Applied the homepage pattern: integrated calendar, streamlined copy, and clearer CTA.  
- Achieved a 10 % increase in bookings for the Breakfast Train.  

### Additional Wins (summarized)  
- Dining experiences page redesign reduced choice overload and improved booking rates.  
- Christmas event page was optimized, leading to a sold‑out event and the most profitable day of the year.  
- Overall site modernization maintained existing sales while unlocking new growth.  

## Outcomes  
- CVR recorded its best financial year ever, driven largely by website‑generated bookings.  
- The Christmas event sold out, delivering the highest profit margin of the year.  
- Ongoing template library enables continuous, low‑risk optimization across the site.  

## Lessons for Other Markets  
- Start with rigorous user research to validate the need for a redesign and pinpoint exact problems.  
- Break a large redesign into small, testable experiments to minimize risk.  
- Use winning designs as templates to accelerate improvements on other high‑value pages.  
- Data‑driven iteration turns a risky “big bet” into a series of confident, revenue‑positive moves.
