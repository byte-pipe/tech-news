# Big-World Intuitions – Summary

## Definition of big‑world heuristics
- A “big‑world” intuition is a rule‑of‑thumb that works when the environment is vastly larger than the individual’s influence.
- In such settings, the optimal strategy is to follow general principles rather than calculate the precise impact of each action on the environment or on other agents.
- The approach is essentially non‑consequentialist: you can ignore the immediate, specific effects of your actions because they are negligible in the grand scale.

## Typical situations where big‑world intuitions apply
- **Startup in a large market** – focus on growth and customer value instead of obsessing over competitors.
- **Small trader in a deep market** – bid your true price; your trades do not move the market.
- **Early stage of a game** – concentrate on accumulating resources, largely independent of opponents’ moves.
- **Species with low resource demand relative to carrying capacity** – maximize offspring without worrying about scarcity.
- **Repeated long‑term interactions** – maintain trustworthiness; short‑term betrayals are outweighed by reputation costs.
- **Hard technical problems with uncertain societal impact** – prioritize doing good work and sharing knowledge rather than over‑weighing speculative future harms.

## Why they are useful
- **Robustness to uncertainty**: Even if you misjudge your significance, acting on generally good heuristics tends to produce favorable outcomes.
- **Simplicity**: Removes the need for complex, situation‑specific strategic calculations.
- **Scalability**: Works for the vast majority of people who have limited power or influence.

## Contrast with “end‑game” or small‑world contexts
- In tight, high‑stakes environments (e.g., chess endgame, oligopolistic markets) the impact of each move is large.
- Here you must engage in explicit strategic reasoning: model how others will respond, compute “if‑then” trees, and continuously update your plan.
- The non‑consequentialist heuristics can fail dramatically when the world is not “big” relative to you.

## Limitations of big‑world intuitions
- They break down when an individual or group becomes **super‑powerful**, **close to winning**, or operates in a **bizarre scenario** where standard heuristics lead to harmful outcomes.
- In such cases, more detailed, adversarial strategic thinking is required.

## Analogy to physics (optional)
- The concept resembles **mean‑field theory**, where a single particle’s behavior is modeled against an approximately fixed background created by many other particles, each having negligible influence on the whole.
