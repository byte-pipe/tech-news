# At least 57 people die as tens of thousands cross to Spanish enclave of Ceuta from Morocco

## Main developments
- Over 50,000 migrants entered the Spanish enclave of Ceuta from Morocco within 24 hours, overwhelming the city of 85,000 residents.  
- At least 57 people died during the crossing, many by drowning or crush injuries at the Tarajal breakwater.  
- Spain’s prime minister Pedro Sánchez called the influx a “violation of Spain’s territorial integrity” and blamed human‑trafficking mafias exploiting a recent Supreme Court ruling that barred summary returns of intercepted migrants.  
- Sánchez announced the creation of a temporary reception centre for registration, triage and expedited expulsion of those without legal claim, and the deployment of a visible buoy barrier at Tarajal beach to mark the border.  

## Government and EU response
- Spain deployed additional police and soldiers; more than 48,300 migrants voluntarily returned to Morocco by the evening of Friday.  
- The Irish EU presidency convened an Integrated Political Crisis Response meeting on Saturday, with a follow‑up EU ambassadors’ meeting scheduled in Brussels.  
- Italy, led by Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni, threatened to suspend Spain from the Schengen zone, citing Spain’s migration regularisation scheme as an incentive for trafficking.  
- The European Commission noted that a member state may temporarily reintroduce internal border controls in exceptional cases but must notify the Commission; Italy has not yet formally done so.  

## Context and background
- The crossing dwarfs a May 2021 incident when 10,000 migrants entered Ceuta after Morocco relaxed border controls.  
- Tensions between Spain and Morocco have previously risen over the Western Sahara issue, but Morocco’s government described the surge as driven by “criminal organisations” and affirmed continued cooperation with Spain.  
- Ceuta and Melilla are the EU’s only land borders with Africa and periodically face large migration surges.  

## Humanitarian impact
- Thousands of migrants, including unaccompanied children, were left without shelter, sleeping in parks and on pavements.  
- Local Guardia Civil officer Rachid Sbihi described the situation as a serious humanitarian crisis and noted chaotic conditions on the ground.  

## Political reactions
- Spain’s foreign minister José Manuel Albares summoned Italy’s ambassador after Italy’s call for Schengen suspension, labeling the remarks “unbecoming” and “partisan demagoguery.”  
- Sweden’s prime minister Ulf Kristersson called for a coordinated European response, while other EU members discussed possible support for Spain.
