# Danube's record low levels force shutdown of Hungary's only nuclear plant  

## Key events  
- Record‑low water levels on the Danube have prevented the suction nozzles of the Paks nuclear plant from reaching the river, forcing a complete shutdown for the first time.  
- Romania’s Cernavoda plant also shut down one of its two 706 MW reactors for the same reason; the second reactor faces possible closure.  
- The low flow affects the Danube in 19 countries, with levels dropping to the lowest seen in 30 years in several sections and to a third of the July average in Serbia.  

## Impact on energy supply  
- Hungarian Prime Minister Péter Magyar warned of a “critical” energy situation from Monday, citing the Paks shutdown and an ongoing heatwave with temperatures around 37 °C.  
- Citizens were asked to limit high‑energy activities (e.g., electric‑car charging, air‑conditioning) between 17:00 and 22:00.  
- A “rotational shutdown regime” may be introduced, and the government will compile a list of electricity and water users whose consumption could be curtailed.
