# Astronomers Detect First Exomoon Candidate

## Discovery Overview
- An international research team reports evidence of a moon‑like body orbiting the brown dwarf **CD‑35 2722 B**, located 73 light‑years from Earth.  
- This marks the first robust detection of a satellite outside our Solar System, though its classification as a true “moon” remains debated.

## Target System
- **CD‑35 2722 B**: a brown dwarf with a mass ≈ 37 times that of Jupiter.  
- It orbits the star **CD‑35 2722** (≈ 40 % of the Sun’s mass) at an apparent separation of about 2.8 arcseconds.  
- Brown dwarfs occupy the mass range between planets and stars, lacking sufficient mass for sustained nuclear fusion.

## Observations & Method
- Data collected over 23 nights from October 2023 to February 2026 using the **CRIRES+ infrared spectrograph** on the European Southern Observatory’s Very Large Telescope.  
- Employed the **Doppler (radial‑velocity) method** to detect the wobble of CD‑35 2722 B caused by the gravitational pull of an orbiting companion.  
- Direct spectroscopy was possible because the brown dwarf is sufficiently distant from its host star, yielding measurements up to **100 times more precise** than previous attempts.

## Detected Satellite Characteristics
- **Orbital period:** roughly 170 days.  
- **Minimum mass:** ≥ 0.9 Jupiter masses, about **2.5 %** of the brown dwarf’s mass.  
- This mass ratio is larger than any moon‑planet pair in the Solar System (Earth‑Moon ≈ 1.2 %).  
- Calculations of the **Roche limit** and **Hill radius** confirm the satellite’s orbit is dynamically stable.

## Validation Checks
- Researchers ruled out alternative explanations such as:
  - Errors in correcting for Earth’s orbital motion.  
  - Seasonal atmospheric variations.  
  - Effects of the brown dwarf’s own rotation.

## Terminology & Classification
- The object is referred to as an **“exosatellite.”**  
- No official definition for exomoons exists yet; the paper notes uncertainty about whether the object will meet future moon criteria.

## Implications
- Provides a new data point for theories of planet formation and celestial mechanics.  
- Suggests that smaller, rocky moons around brown dwarfs could experience tidal heating, potentially creating habitable environments even far from their host stars.  
- Future facilities, such as the **39‑meter Extremely Large Telescope**, may enable detection of much smaller exomoons.

## Context & Related Work
- Earlier observations of the **HD 206893** system hinted at a satellite but lacked definitive confirmation.  
- This study delivers the first clear evidence of a satellite orbiting a brown‑dwarf companion using the Doppler technique.
