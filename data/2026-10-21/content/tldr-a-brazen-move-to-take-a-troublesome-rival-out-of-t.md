Main content
 

 

During the latest quarterly Salesforce earnings call, Chief Operating and Finance Officer Robin Washington made a rather telling remark, noting “early signs of recovery in Marketing”. She said:

While it is too soon to call this a sustainable trend, we're very encouraged by the success enterprise customers are having on our next-generation Marketing product.

That can certainly be read as an acknowledgement of steady decline in revenue growth in the Marketing & Commerce category of Salesforce’s portfolio.

While this was coming in at 10.4% in Q2 fiscal 2024, by Q4 of fiscal 2026, that had fallen to 1.5%. Now that Marketing & Commerce is no longer broken out as a revenue line, replaced by a catch-all Agentforce Apps category, the most recent quarter’s comparable rate is unknown.

But the decline since 2024 has, with a couple of brief upticks along the way, been pretty consistent. In constant currency terms, subscription and support revenue for Agentforce Marketing & Agentforce Commerce in the most recent quarter declined one percent year-on-year, an improvement on the two percent decline reported in the previous quarter, but still a far cry from the glory days.

## Buying in

So what’s to be done to turn this around? Yesterday we got a clue in the form of the announcement that Salesforce is undertaking a $5.66 billion takeover of Braze, a loss-making Customer Engagement platform provider which has increasingly posed a competitive threat to Salesforce’s own Marketing offering in this space.

Acquisitions to flesh out the Salesforce portfolio are nothing new, of course. Marketing Cloud itself made its debut back in 2013, built on tech brought on board as part of takeovers of Radian6, BuddyMedia, and ExactTarget.

Will Braze be absorbed in the wider Salesforce Marketing whole, like the above three, or remain a standalone brand, like Slack or MuleSoft? Time will tell, but the wording in the formal announcement is interesting - Salesforce says that Braze’s real-time engagement and AI decisioning capabilities will complement all four layers of Salesforce’s architecture — Data 360, Customer 360, Agentforce, and AIforce.

As per the announcement, this latest buy-in will extend customers ability to:

* Act on customer behavior in real time. Braze’s event-streaming architecture turns live customer signals and data from cloud data warehouses and other platforms into personalized messages and coordinated journeys, helping Marketers adapt engagement as customers browse, buy, and use products.
* Deliver richer experiences across channels. Braze’s deep mobile and app capabilities, including in-app messages, personalized content feeds, and live updates, work alongside email, web, and messaging channels to create more connected customer experiences.
* Make personalization more intelligent. Braze’s AI decisioning helps marketers optimize content, offers, channels, and timing. Bringing these capabilities together with Salesforce’s customer context and Agentforce would help businesses make engagement more relevant and connect marketing with the broader customer relationship across Commerce, Sales, and Service.

## You pays your money...

So what is Salesforce getting for its money here, besides taking out a rival who had parked its Customer Engagement tanks on Marc Benioff’s lawn? If you can’t beat them, buy them, sniped a number of commentators online following the announcement of the planned acquisition.

Well, according to Patrick Stokes, President, Applications and Marketing, Salesforce:

Every interaction is an opportunity to wow your customers. Most of that wow comes from knowing your customer, and responding at just the right moment. Braze was built to turn live customer behavior into personalized engagement at scale. Bringing that capability together with Salesforce Marketing Cloud, CRM, and Data 360 will deliver that wow moment in every customer interaction.

Less colorfully perhaps, Braze does bring with it 2,790 customers, as per last official count, with 361 of those spending more than $0.5 million per annum. That total represents at 12% annual growth rate. According to CEO William Magnuson last month, there’s more to come:

The biggest thing about our pipeline is that there's still a huge amount of addressable market that is living on legacy technology and Braze is their AI bet.

He added:

The biggest underlying trend that we're seeing is that organizations are seeing that they need to have the combination of being able to have the full first-party context that is actionable with advanced AI within a platform that provides them the right guarantees and scalability and security and performance to be able to bring to life these more advanced AI capabilities and drive new strategies within their organizations.

And customers and prospects are getting over any nervousness they might have around AI adoption, he suggested:

We certainly have run into a number of organizations either in different geographies that are a little bit more reticent to adopt AI quickly or large enterprises that might have AI adoption consoles or other sorts of heavier weight processes.

But once people start to get a taste for it, they're really running at it.

If Magnusson’s thesis is correct and his expectations around Braze’s pipeline are realistic, then removing this player from the board does look like sound game play on Salesforce's part.

Magnusson's own pitch for Braze’s USP is concise:

Customer Engagement is a team sport that crosses marketing, data and engineering and supporting how those teams work together is where we see the next wave of value.

Releasing that value has been a challenge for too long, he suggested:

Marketing technology has always forced a trade-off. Every gain in power came with added complexity and every ambitious idea took specialists, time and patience. We believe that we are now breaking that trade-off. Braze is getting both more powerful and easier to use at the same time...When a marketer watches an idea, go from creative spark to working prototype in just minutes, they remember why they fell in love with this work. And that's the flywheel that we're building.

nd now one that Salesforce hopes it has brought inside the Ohana tent.

## My take

Man, it's like it's only upside now.

In light of yesterday’s announcement, an interesting comment from Magnusson at this year’s Citi Global TMT Conference only a few weeks ago.

Braze’s board has signed up for the Salesforce acquisition, but it will still need shareholder approval - highly likely - and, of course, regulatory approval - riskier, but the odds here are in Salesforce’s favor. The whole thing will take time though, with completion unlikely until the start of Salesforce’s fiscal 2028 period next year.

While Braze’s share price was well up on news of the deal, Salesforce’s was down, suggesting that investors may need to be won over here. Mind you, Wall Street pretty unanimously declared that buying Slack was a bad idea and look how that turned out...
