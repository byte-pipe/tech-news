# A reason to eat carbs | MIT Technology Review

## Main Findings
- A high‑fat, low‑carbohydrate (ketogenic) diet, while useful for weight loss and protective against colon cancer, increased the incidence of tumors in the small intestine of mice.
- Tumor growth was driven by fatty‑acid oxidation, a metabolic pathway that activates proteins causing intestinal stem cells to multiply more rapidly.
- An abundance of active stem cells improves repair after injury but also raises the risk of tumor formation.
- The protective effect against colon cancer does not involve ketone bodies such as β‑hydroxybutyrate (BHB); these molecules act as metabolic by‑standers.
- Commercial ketone supplements are unlikely to have significant health effects, either beneficial or harmful.

## Researchers’ Comments
- Omer Yilmaz (director, MIT Stem Cell Initiative) warns against generalizing the effects of ketogenic diets across different parts of the gut.
- Postdoc Fangtao Chi highlights the need to understand why the same diet produces opposite outcomes in adjacent sections of the gastrointestinal tract.

## Implications
- Dietary guidelines should account for organ‑specific responses to ketogenic diets.
- Ongoing research will aim to uncover the mechanisms behind the divergent effects on the colon versus the small intestine.
