# Somewhere, Coding Stopped Being Allowed to Be Fun - DEV Community

## Introduction
- The author feels a growing pressure to stop enjoying programming for its own sake.
- A recent post by Chris Kiehl (“It’s Still OK to Enjoy Programming”) sparked the realization that enjoying coding doesn’t need economic justification.

## The doom stream doesn’t argue with you. It soaks you.
- Developer feeds constantly repeat narratives: AI will replace us, the craft is dead, junior paths are gone.
- This saturation subtly convinces developers that pure‑pleasure coding is pointless.
- Disconnecting from the feed removes the negativity; the problem is the ambient influence, not a single argument.

## The lie hidden in the despair
- The underlying assumption: value equals economic value.
- If a machine can do something cheaper, doing it yourself is deemed inefficient.
- This logic is absurd when applied to other activities (running, cooking, music) but has been internalized for programming.
- The market has convinced many that programming must be defensible on an invoice to be worthwhile.

## The best thing I built this year was worthless
- The author’s proudest recent projects are intentionally “worthless”: tiny personal tools, deliberately slow solutions, hand‑crafted games.
- The reward lies in the process—struggling, figuring things out—not in the final artifact.
- Economic metrics miss the “aliveness” and personal satisfaction these projects provide.

## The quiet rebellion
- The most radical act for developers now is to keep building for joy, not profit.
- Embrace weekend projects that a machine could solve instantly, simply because the struggle is enjoyable.
- This stance isn’t anti‑AI; the author still uses AI tools but separates work (value‑producing) from personal exploration (joy‑producing).

## It’s still OK to love this
- Reiterates Kiehl’s message: loving programming is fine without utility or monetary payoff.
- Closing the doom‑stream tab and opening an editor restores the intrinsic worth of coding.
- The decision of what is “worth doing” belongs solely to the individual.

## Call to action
- Readers are invited to share the most economically worthless things they’ve built or learned purely for personal satisfaction.
