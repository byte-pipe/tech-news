## The First Contact (1996)  
- While a Ph.D. student at Syracuse, I received a call from “Bob Cringely,” who praised my Java book (which I had thought was only the second on the market).  
- I was surprised he knew my name correctly and that he claimed to have read seven Java books, which didn’t exist.  

## Early Collaboration  
- Bob asked if I did Java programming; I said yes (a slight exaggeration).  
- He hired my partner and me to build “Pronto,” a system that pre‑downloaded magazine PDFs over idle dial‑up connections and printed them at 2 am.  
- Pronto received funding (from a Japanese source) and was paid for development, but it never saw a meaningful release.  

## Ongoing Relationship  
- Bob added me to his “startup‑idea” mailing list; over 13 years I exchanged roughly 320 emails (about three per year).  
- His emails typically began with a detailed startup concept followed by precise revenue projections.  
- I participated in two of his ventures (home‑account and Sonisafe) and saw many pitch decks that never became public.  

## Bob’s Personality and Public Image  
- Charismatic on camera, good‑looking, disarming, and comfortable with “nerds.”  
- Desired to be a superstar startup founder, though his strongest talent was storytelling and media presence.  
- I suggested he start a YouTube interview series; he was generally too absorbed in his current ideas to respond.
