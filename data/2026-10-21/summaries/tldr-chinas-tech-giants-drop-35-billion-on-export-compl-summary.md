# China’s tech giants spend $35 billion on export‑compliant Nvidia RTX Pro 5500 GPUs

## Main points
- ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent have placed orders for more than 3 million Nvidia RTX Pro 5500 workstation GPUs, valued at roughly $35 billion.  
- The RTX Pro 5500 is fully compliant with U.S. export restrictions, making it one of the few AI‑capable GPUs China can legally import.  
- Nvidia has told its customers it can only fulfil about 5 %–10 % of the demand, far below the companies’ expectations.  
- Production capacity is limited to around 3 million RTX Pro 5500 cards globally by 2027.  

## GPU details
- Workstation GPU with the same CUDA‑core count as the flagship RTX 5090.  
- Equipped with 84 GB of GDDR7 memory, suited for AI workloads.  
- Less powerful than Nvidia’s enterprise AI GPUs (H200, B300), which remain banned or heavily restricted for export to China.  

## Pricing and order scale
- Each RTX Pro 5500 is priced at approximately $11,400.  
- Total order value reaches about $35 billion for the three Chinese firms combined.  

## Nvidia’s response
- Nvidia confirmed “unprecedented” growth in demand from China since the 2022 export restrictions.  
- The company cited outdated U.S. export controls on older gaming products and China’s own import limits as factors affecting supply.  

## Context
- Trade tensions between the United States and China continue, but demand for Nvidia GPUs in China remains strong.  
- Despite export bans, some restricted AI chips still reach China through workarounds, undermining the intended effect of the restrictions.
