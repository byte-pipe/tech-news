# Sixteen Years After Deepwater Horizon, Deep Sea Life Shows Few Signs of Recovery

## Main Findings
- Acoustic surveys and thousands of net samples across the Gulf of Mexico reveal a ≥60 % decline in octopus, squid, krill, shrimp, and lanternfish populations during the first few years after the 2010 spill.  
- The decline is described as the “fastest and most severe” observed in the ocean layer from ~600 ft to >2 miles deep.  
- Researchers stop short of definitively attributing the loss to the oil spill but identify it as the most parsimonious explanation.  

## Evidence of Ongoing Contamination
- Oil‑related compounds have been detected in the tissues of squid and octopus and in deep‑water fish eggs.  
- Such contaminants can impair reproduction, development, and survival, and may disrupt the olfactory cues deep‑sea fish use to locate mates.  

## Genetic Consequences
- The study reports reductions in genetic diversity among affected species.  
- Lower genetic variability could limit the ability of populations to adapt to a rapidly changing environment.
