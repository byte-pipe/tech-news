# After deadly Kyiv strike, Ukraine warns interceptor shortage is costing lives

## Key events of the night attack
- Around midnight Moscow launched 115 drones and 24 ballistic missiles at Kyiv.  
- Most drones were shot down, but none of the ballistic missiles were intercepted.  
- The strike killed 21 people, injured dozens, and caused extensive damage to railway infrastructure and nearby warehouses.

## Human and material impact
- At Kvitneva railway station workers moved white body bags; eight victims were identified by shoes, glasses and clothing.  
- A residential house was heavily damaged, losing its roof and third floor, though the occupants survived.  
- Storage facilities, including an online‑retailer warehouse and a postal sorting centre, were destroyed or left smouldering for many hours.

## Shortage of Patriot interceptor missiles
- Ukraine relies on US‑provided Patriot missiles to counter Russian ballistic missiles.  
- US stockpiles have been depleted by other conflicts, and recent political statements have limited further transfers.  
- Each incoming missile requires at least two Patriot interceptors; Russia typically fires 20‑30 missiles per large‑scale strike, outstripping Ukraine’s available supply.  
- Experts compare the situation to having a high‑performance car without fuel: the Patriot systems exist, but lack the missiles needed to operate.

## Political and military responses
- President Volodymyr Zelensky linked the high casualty rate directly to the interceptor shortage and urged European and American partners for decisive action.  
- He discussed the issue with NATO chief Mark Rutte, emphasizing daily dependence on external decisions.  
- Military analyst Ivan Stupak warned that Patriot systems themselves could become Russian targets unless relocated or concealed until more interceptors arrive.  

## Ongoing recovery efforts
- Ukrainian medics and repair crews quickly cleared the railway site and restored power lines to resume train services, illustrating the country’s rapid response capability despite persistent aerial threats.
