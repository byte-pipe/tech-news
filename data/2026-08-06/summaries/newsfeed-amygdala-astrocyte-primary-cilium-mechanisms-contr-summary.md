# Amygdala astrocyte primary cilium mechanisms contribute to stress behaviours

## Abstract
- Stress‑related behavioural changes are linked to alterations in amygdala astrocytes.  
- During stress, amygdala astrocytes show reduced expression of primary‑cilium‑related proteins and possess shorter primary cilia.  
- G‑protein‑coupled receptors (GPCRs), especially sphingosine‑1‑phosphate receptor 1 (S1PR1), regulate astrocyte cilium length and molecular profile.  
- Chemogenetic activation or pharmacological targeting of S1PR1 restores cilium length, normalizes astrocytic gene expression, and improves stress‑related behaviours in mice.  
- Human amygdala astrocytes also express cilium‑related genes, many of which are dysregulated in stress‑related brain disorders; S1PR1 is highly expressed in these cells.  
- Genetic ablation of astrocyte primary cilia in mice modifies specific stress‑related behaviours and alters gene expression in astrocytes and surrounding cells, confirming a functional role for astrocytic cilia.

## Introduction
- The basolateral amygdala (BLA) is central to emotional learning and is activated by chronic stress.  
- Prior research has focused on neuronal mechanisms; the contribution of non‑neuronal cells, particularly astrocytes, remains underexplored.  
- Astrocytes are essential for neural circuit function and are altered in psychiatric conditions.  
- Primary cilia act as cellular antennae for extracellular signaling; their role in astrocytes is poorly understood.  
- This study investigates how amygdala astrocyte primary cilia are affected by stress and whether targeting cilium‑associated GPCR signaling can mitigate stress‑induced behavioural deficits.

## Experimental Design
- Mouse models: chronic restraint stress (CRS), water‑avoidance stress (WAS), and spared nerve injury (SNI) to represent chronic, psychological, and pain‑related stress.  
- Assessments: physiological metrics (body weight, adrenal/thymus weight, corticosterone), behavioural tests (open‑field, sucrose preference, forced‑ swim), immunohistochemistry for neuronal activation (FOS) and astrocyte markers (GFAP, SOX9), and molecular profiling of astrocytes.  
- Interventions: chemogenetic activation of astrocyte GPCR pathways, pharmacological activation of S1PR1, and astrocyte‑specific genetic deletion of primary cilia (Ift88 knockout).

## Key Findings
- **Stress‑induced astrocyte changes**  
  - CRS and WAS reduced expression of cilium‑related genes and proteins in amygdala astrocytes.  
  - Primary cilia in astrocytes became significantly shorter after stress exposure.  

- **GPCR signaling as a therapeutic target**  
  - Activation of astrocyte GPCRs, particularly S1PR1, increased cilium length and restored normal expression of cilium‑associated genes.  
  - Chemogenetic stimulation of astrocyte GPCRs improved performance in stress‑related behavioural assays (increased sucrose preference, reduced immobility in forced‑ swim).  

- **Human relevance**  
  - Single‑cell RNA‑seq data show abundant expression of cilium‑related genes in human amygdala astrocytes.  
  - Many of these genes are dysregulated in post‑mortem brains from individuals with depression or anxiety.  
  - S1PR1 is highly expressed in human amygdala astrocytes, mirroring mouse findings.  

- **Genetic disruption of astrocyte cilia**  
  - Astrocyte‑specific deletion of Ift88 (essential for cilium formation) led to altered stress‑related behaviours, such as increased anxiety‑like activity and impaired reward processing.  
  - Gene‑expression analysis revealed downstream changes in both astrocytes and neighboring neuronal/glial populations, indicating broader circuit effects.

## Conclusions
- Amygdala astrocytes undergo structural and molecular remodeling of their primary cilia in response to diverse stressors.  
- Restoring cilium integrity through GPCR (S1PR1) activation reverses stress‑induced molecular signatures and ameliorates behavioural deficits in mice.  
- The conserved expression of cilium‑related pathways in human amygdala astrocytes suggests translational potential.  
- Targeting astrocyte primary cilium mechanisms represents a novel therapeutic avenue for stress‑related psychiatric disorders.
