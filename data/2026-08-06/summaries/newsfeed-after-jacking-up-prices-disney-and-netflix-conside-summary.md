# After jacking up prices, Disney+ and Netflix consider offering free alternatives

## Disney’s exploration of a free tier
- CEO Josh D’Amaro confirmed Disney is “exploring a free product” for streaming customers.  
- Goal: attract price‑sensitive users, a strategic priority for the company.  
- Expected benefits: increase ad inventory and revenue, and drive top‑of‑funnel Disney+ subscriber growth.  
- No specifics on format or launch timeline were provided.  
- Business Insider reported internal discussions about making some Disney+ content available without a paywall; chief product officer Adam Smith mentioned the idea in a streaming‑team meeting but gave no timeline or scope.

## Netflix’s cautious stance
- Co‑CEO Greg Peters said a free offering could make sense in certain markets but warned about cannibalizing paid tiers.  
- Emphasized the need for a differentiated product and a robust ad‑supported business to make the economics viable.  
- Netflix has no near‑term plans to launch a free tier but will continue to evaluate the option.

## Context: recent price hikes and market reaction
- Disney+ (131.6 M subscribers) raised U.S. prices twice in 2024, most recently adding $2 to ad‑supported and $3 to ad‑free plans in October.  
- Netflix (325 M+ subscribers) also raised prices twice, with the latest increase of $1 (ad‑supported) and $2 (ad‑free) in March 2024.  
- Repeated hikes have spurred churn, frustration, and growing interest in free ad‑supported streaming (FAST) services.  
- FAST usage: 46 % of U.S. internet households regularly watch long‑form video via FAST (Q3 2025, Parks Associates).  
- Survey data (Q4 2025, U.S. & Canada) shows 54 % use ad‑supported subscription tiers, with overall AVOD/FAST adoption at 70 %, up five points year‑over‑year.

## Irony and industry shift
- The same companies that have driven subscription‑based streaming growth are now contemplating free, ad‑supported models that they helped popularize.
