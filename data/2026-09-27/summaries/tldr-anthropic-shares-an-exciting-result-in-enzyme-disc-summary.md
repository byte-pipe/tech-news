# Summary of “Anthropic shares an exciting result in enzyme discovery – and an exercise in public's perception of science and AI”

## Main observations
- I am a former wet‑lab chemist now working in chem‑biosecurity and AI evaluation; I am not affiliated with Anthropic.
- Anthropic announced that their Life Science team, using Claude, identified a previously unknown enzyme system in bacteriophage DNA.
- The reported computational effort (≈950 agents, 210 M tokens, 21 h) is modest, but details on models, search space, and compute are missing, making the claim hard to assess.
- The press release provides very little information about experimental validation, robustness of results, or which aspects remain speculative.

## Concerns about communication
- The announcement blurs two very different statements:
  1. “Biologists used Claude” – AI assisted hypothesis generation and candidate selection.
  2. “Claude discovered …” – AI is portrayed as the sole discoverer.
- Using these interchangeably can mislead the public and the scientific community about the current capabilities of AI in life sciences.
- Over‑emphasising Claude’s role may create the impression that AI can already outperform expert human researchers in complex, time‑intensive fields, which I believe is premature.

## Possible reasons for limited detail
- Anthropic may be:
  - Pursuing patent protection.
  - Conducting additional experiments.
  - Exploring related scientific questions.
  - Preparing a manuscript for peer‑review.
  - Choosing to withhold certain data for strategic reasons.
- These motives are normal in both industry and academia.

## Positive aspects
- Claude appears to have contributed substantially to hypothesis generation and candidate prioritisation, while humans performed experimental validation and interpretation.
- This hybrid workflow already shortens discovery timelines and demonstrates a valuable use case for AI‑augmented research.

## Recommendations
- Clearly distinguish between AI‑assisted hypothesis generation and actual experimental discovery in future communications.
- Provide more transparent details on validation protocols and the extent of AI involvement to avoid overstating capabilities.
- Maintain careful framing to prevent misconceptions about AI’s current limits in biology.
