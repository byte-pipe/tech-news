# AI: Banks scramble to lend $10B to Anthropic

## Overview
- Anthropic is arranging a revolving credit facility (RCF) expected to exceed $10 billion ahead of a planned IPO.
- The IPO is projected to raise around $75 billion, potentially matching SpaceX’s historic offering.
- Major banks are committing large sums to the RCF to secure a role in the upcoming underwriting mandate.

## Credit Facility Details
- Top‑tier banks asked to commit roughly $1.25 billion each; second‑tier banks about $1 billion; smaller participants $750 million or less.
- The RCF functions like a massive corporate credit card: Anthropic can draw funds as needed and pays a spread on drawn amounts plus a fee on undrawn commitments.
- Illustrative economics (not actual terms):
  - 25% utilization, 1.5% spread on drawn funds, 0.25% fee on undrawn amount.
  - Approx. $5 million interest on drawn $310 million and $2.3 million fee on undrawn $940 million → ~ $7 million gross annual revenue before costs.

## IPO Potential and Underwriting Rewards
- If Anthropic’s IPO reaches $75 billion, underwriting fees could range from $500 million to $2 billion, depending on negotiated spreads.
- SpaceX’s IPO achieved a sub‑0.75% spread, still generating about $500 million in fees due to size.
- Winning the IPO mandate would also open opportunities for follow‑on equity, debt, derivatives, treasury services, and future lending.

## Banking Competition and Relationship Strategy
- Banks view the RCF as a relationship‑building tool that improves chances of winning the IPO mandate.
- Economic models suggest the incremental expected value from a higher probability of winning the underwriting fee can dwarf direct lending revenue (e.g., $20 million expected uplift vs. $7 million from interest/fees).
- Historical research shows pre‑IPO banking relationships reduce IPO underpricing by ~17% and increase likelihood of securing underwriting business.

## Legal Constraints
- U.S. banking regulations prohibit conditioning credit on the borrower purchasing other bank services (e.g., “give us the IPO, we’ll give you the loan”).
- Banks may still cultivate relationships with the expectation of future business, but must keep credit decisions independent of such expectations.

## Market Context
- EY’s Global IPO Trends indicates a surge in mega‑IPOs and AI‑related offerings in H1 2026: 12 companies raised >$1 billion, up from four in the same period of 2025.
- Concentration of capital formation in a few large deals makes league‑table rankings increasingly dependent on participation in these marquee transactions.
