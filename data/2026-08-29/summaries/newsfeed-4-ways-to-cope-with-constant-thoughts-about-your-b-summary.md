# 4 ways to cope with constant thoughts about your body image

## Overview  
- Psychologist Diana Hill (acceptance and commitment therapy) introduces **body‑image flexibility** – the ability to respond to negative thoughts without letting them dominate your life.  
- The article offers four practical exercises to shift attention, re‑frame thinking, and reclaim energy for activities you value.

## 1. Focus your attention inward  
- Practice “flexible attention”: notice negative body thoughts and gently redirect them.  
- Turn focus to internal sensations (interoceptive awareness): hunger, fullness, tension, emotions, breathing.  
- Benefits include better hunger cues, empathy, and overall mindfulness.

## 2. Appreciate what your body can do  
- Shift attention outward to the body’s functions: seeing, hearing, breathing, moving.  
- Verbally acknowledge these abilities (“My eyes are seeing a tree; my lungs are breathing”).  
- Reinforces a sense of connection to the world rather than appearance.

## 3. Interrogate your intentions  
- When you catch yourself altering posture (e.g., sucking in the stomach), pause and ask:  
  - When do I do this more or less?  
  - Is this behavior driven by cultural messages I truly endorse?  
  - How does it affect my daily life (clothing comfort, confidence, social participation)?  
- Recognizing biological, psychological, and social influences helps break automatic “shut‑off” habits.

## 4. Do an energy audit  
- Honestly assess how much time/energy you devote to body‑related activities (selfies, beauty products, diet tracking).  
- Identify energizing alternatives (outdoor movement, playing with a pet, hobbies).  
- Replace some image‑focused time with activities that celebrate what the body enables you to do, freeing creative and relational energy.
