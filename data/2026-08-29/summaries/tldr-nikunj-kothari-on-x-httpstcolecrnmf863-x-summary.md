# Nikunj Kothari on X: "https://t.co/LECRNMF863" / X

# Summary of Nikunj Kothari’s X post “being legible to capital”

## Market context
- The current market is highly consensus‑driven: hot startups get hot deals, cold ones get ignored.  
- Most VCs rely on signals and peer notes rather than deep independent analysis.  
- Early‑stage valuations (Series A) are being shaped by this “legibility” effect.

## Fundraising strategy
- Success depends on how clearly you present yourself to investors.  
- The process is an art; there is no exact formula, but preparation and awareness of signals matter.

## Who to talk to at a firm
- **Associates and principals** are valuable contacts; review their recent investments to gauge influence.  
- Secure a warm introduction; a direct line to a partner (GP) is far more effective than cold outreach.  
- Angels who have backed you can fast‑track conversations with partners.

## Asking the right amount
- Over‑asking raises skepticism; under‑asking signals lack ambition.  
- Aim slightly lower than your true target to increase the chance of closing the round, but avoid later “down‑selling” which erodes confidence.  
- The amount you state publicly sticks; changing it later creates doubt about judgment.

## Pricing and dilution
- VCs are wary of deals that appear too favorable; they expect a reasonable price and ownership stake.  
- Expect a floor of ~10 % dilution from a lead fund unless you have extraordinary leverage.  
- Comparing your terms to a competitor’s deal is risky; you rarely know the full context of their agreement.

## Story and traction
- Revenue alone no longer secures a Series A; VCs look for obvious, defensible advantages in product, technology, or go‑to‑market.  
- Highlight unique insights that give you an edge over existing competitors.  
- Keep traction slides current (e.g., show the latest month’s numbers) to avoid appearing stale.

## Hiring and “Mandate of Heaven”
- New, high‑quality hires dramatically improve a fund’s perception of downside risk.  
- Showcase recent talent acquisitions in the pitch; many founders overlook this lever.

## Small details that get read
- Update metrics to the most recent month; stale data signals inactivity.  
- When asked about timelines, default to “two weeks” as a standard response.  
- Remember that decks circulate widely; never share information you wouldn’t want public.

## Vibes and authenticity
- Genuine enthusiasm and clear articulation of where you need help resonate more than forced hype.  
- “Negging” or pretending to be someone else is less effective than being yourself.

## Outlook for non‑hot sectors
- Even strong companies can struggle in this consensus market (e.g., Anthropic).  
- Position yourself as a compelling contrarian bet; investors balance portfolios and may champion unexpected plays.  
- Focus on maintaining runway and ambition rather than chasing headline valuations.

**Bottom line:** One clear, authentic “yes” can carry you through; make sure every signal you send—from who you talk to, to the amount you ask, to the story you tell—is legible and compelling.
