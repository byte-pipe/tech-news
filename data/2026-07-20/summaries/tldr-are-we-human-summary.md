# Are we human?

**Are We Human?**

* The concept of humanity has been debated throughout history and across cultures. Philosophers such as René Descartes have argued that we are human because our minds contain a sense of self, but modern scientists argue that the question is moot in the context of evolutionary biology.
* From an anthropological perspective, humans are culturally constructed beings with varying norms, values, and meanings assigned to us through social interactions. This suggests that what it means to be human can vary greatly across cultures and societies.
*[**Main Idea 1: Debating Humanity's Definition**]

* In the field of philosophy, there are different arguments regarding humanity. Some argue that humans share a common essence, while others see humanity as solely physical in nature.
* According to David Halberstam, author of "The Undocumented Americans," we can define what it means to be human through our relationship with other people and our capacity for love and empathy.

**Key Conclusion:**

Humans have been multifaceted concepts throughout history and continue to be debated by philosophers, anthropologists, and scientists. While there are varying definitions of humanity, commonalities often emerge when examining our experiences, emotions, and social roles.

* **Summary Points:**
    + Definition of humanity varies across cultures, philosophies, and scientific perspectives.
    + Humans can refer to ourselves through interactions with others, indicating empathy and love.
    + Our self-worth is tied to social relationships and capacity for human connections.
